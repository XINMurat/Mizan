# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**Verdict: not ready for sign-off.** Only claim 3's descriptive decline is proven. The rest are unproven, and two of them (2 and 6) are presented as fact when they are not.

## 0. Audit declaration

- **Scope:** 6 atomic claims. All 6 are checkable in principle, but I had only the summary text. I did not inspect the HR export, the external audit report, the vendor white paper, or the recruiter meeting notes. Every verdict below rests on how the summary describes those sources, not on the data.
- **Retrospective:** No claim was preregistered. The summary was written after the outcomes it reports.
- **Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims assessed; 0 of 4 source artifacts inspected.

## 2. What survives

- **Claim 3, descriptive decline:** The data source is a census from the system of record, not recollection, and the volume check (212 vs 208) is the right instinct. The fall can be stated as a fact, with the attribution removed.
- **Claim 4, design:** An external, blinded audit with human baselines is the correct design. What is missing is the uncertainty and an outcome measure, not the structure.

## 3. Structural diagnosis

The summary puts four kinds of evidence at one visual level: testimony (claim 1), a vendor description (claim 2), an estimate (claim 6), and a system-of-record measurement (claim 3). The board cannot tell them apart. This is tier drift inside a single document, not an accusation of bad faith. The missing card is the failure record: applicants the tool rejected who would have succeeded, recruiters who overrode it and were right, and any group-level outcome data.

## 4. Replacement lines for tomorrow

- "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days across all 14 departments, comparing two quarters before and after rollout (HR system records). We have not yet tested how much of that the tool caused."
- "On 600 blinded applications, the tool's shortlist matched the expert consensus 81% of the time; two human panels matched each other 79%. We are computing uncertainty and have not yet measured accuracy against outcomes."
- Move claims 1, 2, 5, and 6 to a slide titled "hypotheses under test," each with the test that will decide it.

## 5. Next steps (ordered by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Claim 2, fairness (highest stakes):** Where lawfully held, compute selection rates by group from the 600-application set and report the adverse-impact ratio. Until that exists, strike "unbiased" from the summary. A hiring-discrimination exposure outweighs every other item here.
2. **Claim 4:** Get the external firm's shortlists and compute a paired confidence interval on the 2-point gap. This takes days.
3. **Claim 3:** Confirm the metric definition and whether 41 and 29 are means or medians. Compare departments or roles the freeze did not touch. This takes days.
4. **Claim 6:** Run a two-week time log, or count screening actions from ATS logs and multiply by timed durations. This takes weeks.
5. **Claim 1:** Collect a six-month manager rating of hires, blinded to tool use, and compare against pre-tool hires. This takes quarters.
6. **Claim 5:** Preregister the 12-month retention test now, with its comparator and a review date.
