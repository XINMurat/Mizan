**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims are checkable at the level of the summary text. 0 of 6 were checked against source data (HR extracts, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, panel scoring sheets). Every tier below is conditional on the summary describing its evidence accurately.

**Mapping to your binary:** PROVEN means `[K]`. None of the six reaches `[K]` on the evidence the summary presents, so I cannot sign off on this set as written.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** `[S]`
The only support is "hiring managers say the new hires feel stronger." That is an unmeasured impression. No quality-of-hire outcome (performance rating, ramp time, probation pass rate) is reported, and no comparison group exists. The 81% audit overlap in claim 4 measures agreement with panels, not hire quality. Verdict: NOT PROVEN.

**2. "It is unbiased."** `[Y]`
The white paper says the model was trained on "diverse data." Training-data diversity is an input property. Unbiasedness is an outcome property: selection rates by group, adverse-impact ratios, and error rates by group. None of those appears here, and the summary contains no demographic breakdown at all. The wording moves from an input claim to a conclusion, which is the misleading pattern. Verdict: NOT PROVEN. A vendor white paper is also an interested-party document, so it cannot serve as the arbiter.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** `[KKE]`
The drop is measured in HR-system data across 14 departments, which is a good instrument. The board framing, though, sits this claim under the tool's benefits, and the attribution is not supported:
- **No control group.** The before/after comparison has no concurrent comparator, so secular trends, seasonality, and other process changes all remain live.
- **The hiring freeze is an unaddressed confound.** Equal open-role volume (212 vs 208) does not show equal hiring pace or applicant pool. Freezes change who is still in the pipeline.
- **Right-censoring.** If time-to-hire is computed only for requisitions closed within each window, slow requisitions still open at the end of the "after" window drop out. That biases the after-period mean downward. The summary does not say whether it is a mean or median, or how open requisitions were handled.
- **Rollout timing** against the 2-quarter windows is not stated.

Verdict: NOT PROVEN as a tool effect. The descriptive fact that the HR-recorded figure fell could be restated as `[K]` once the censoring check is run and the wording drops the attribution.

**4. Blinded external audit: tool 81% vs panels 79%.** `[KKE]`
This is a real strength: external, blinded, n=600. But the comparison is asymmetric:
- The tool's 81% is overlap with a consensus that is partly built from the two panels. The 79% is panel-vs-panel. These are different quantities.
- No shortlist size, no confidence interval, and no pre-stated parity threshold are reported. A 2-point difference on 600 applications is well within sampling noise for a typical shortlist.
- Shortlist overlap measures agreement, not validity. Even a perfect match would show the tool reproduces human judgment, including whatever biases those humans carry.

The supportable claim is "the tool's shortlists are roughly as consistent with the panels as the panels are with each other." Verdict: NOT PROVEN as stated. The missing control is a bootstrap CI on both numbers, plus the same consensus-construction applied to one panel against the other.

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** `[S]`
A forecast. The first cohort started 3 months ago, so the summary itself says it cannot be measured. There is no threshold, no comparator, and no kill condition. Verdict: NOT PROVEN. It should be recorded as a preregistered prediction with a locked threshold and a comparison group, not presented as a result.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** `[KKE]`
The arbiter is the recruiters' own estimates, given in a team meeting. That is `author`-class arbitration, which cannot reach `[K]` under the method. No time-logging, no pre/post measurement, and no comparison. Verdict: NOT PROVEN.

## Patterns and the missing card

- **Tier drift:** the vendor's input claim ("diverse data") becomes the board's output claim ("unbiased") with no new evidence. The anecdote in claim 1 is presented as a result.
- **Missing card:** the summary contains no failures, no costs, no adverse-impact data, no quality outcome, and no rollout details. A maximally honest version of this board slide would show the audit's near-tie, the censoring question, and the blank in the fairness row.
- **Hit rate:** no prediction record exists in this document, so no hit rate can be computed.
- **Domain probe (checklist item 12):** no domain scenarios were supplied by anyone with hiring-operations knowledge, so this probe is unanswered. Questions to ask the HR owner include: what happens to candidates flagged by the tool who appeal, how cancelled requisitions are scored, and how the tool treats internal transfers and rehires. Until those are answered, this set has not been tested for coverage gaps. (A flag like this would also be recorded as W6 in the registry.)
