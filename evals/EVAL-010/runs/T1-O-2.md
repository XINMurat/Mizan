# Audit: Support chatbot board summary

**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The claims were selected by the team lead for a board deck, and I audited their wording against the descriptions given. I did not open the ticketing data, the RCT output, or the vendor dashboard, so every tier below rests on the text as written.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims read. 1 of 6 has a stated design that I could check for internal consistency. 0 of 6 were checked against source data, because I have no access to it here.

**Bottom line:** One claim survives as written (claim 4). Two need a rewording or one check before the board sees them (claims 1 and 3). Three are not evidence for what they say (claims 2, 5, 6). The team lead's confidence is not supported by the summary as written.

## Claim by claim

**1. Median first response fell from 4.2 h to 40 s.** `[KKE]`
- Source: ticketing timestamps, 38,000 tickets, 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by the data platform team. This is a strong instrument, independent of the bot team.
- The confound that could flip the meaning: "first response" may include the bot's automatic greeting. If so, the 40 s is an acknowledgement, not help, and the board will read it as help.
- Also uncontrolled: the before and after periods are not matched for ticket mix or season, and the hiring freeze started in the same month.
- What would change this to `[K]`: a query showing whether the first response is a human reply or a bot message, reported separately, plus the median time to first *human* response.

**2. Customers prefer the bot to human agents.** `[Y]`
- Source: product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.
- Thirty transcripts cannot establish a preference. There is no comparison arm, no denominator, and no record of how the 30 were chosen. Reading transcripts shows what happened in those chats, not what customers would choose.
- What would change this: a preference measure (a rating after each channel, or a choice offered to a random sample) with a stated denominator.

**3. The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own.** `[Y]`
- Source: vendor dashboard. "Resolved" means the customer closed the chat window.
- Closing a window is abandonment as often as resolution. The metric cannot distinguish a solved problem from a customer who gave up. The vendor also defines the metric, so it is not independent.
- Partial cross-check: claim 4's repeat-contact result points the same way (fewer repeats), which is weak evidence that not all closures are failures. It does not validate 70%.
- What would change this: resolution defined as no repeat contact within 7 days and no escalation, measured by your own data team.

**4. The bot reduced repeat contacts.** `[K]`
- Source: randomized assignment, 5,000 customers, bot-first vs human-first for 6 weeks, analysis by the analytics team, not the bot team.
- Checked: the reported figures are internally consistent. 9.1% − 11.4% = −2.3 points, which is the midpoint of the stated CI (−3.0 to −1.6). The CI excludes zero.
- Scope to state on the slide: the effect is for the bot-first routing policy, over 7 days after contact, over 6 weeks, in this customer population.
- Remaining check (not a blocker): confirm that repeat contacts were counted through the same channels in both arms. If bot-first customers contact by a different channel that is not counted, the effect is overstated.
- Also confirm the analysis was specified before the data came in. The summary does not say.

**5. It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year.** `[S]`
- Source: vendor sales deck. This is a forecast with no model, no baseline, and no stated assumptions.
- The hiring freeze also changes headcount, so any cost reduction is confounded with it.
- Presented as a projection, it is a plan. It cannot be "proven" tomorrow.

**6. Agents are happier since launch.** `[S]`
- No survey was run. Attrition is a different construct from happiness, and it fell in the same month as a hiring freeze, which changes who leaves and who can.
- What would change this: a validated agent survey, before and after, with a response rate reported.

## The missing card

This format shows only the bot's best numbers. It does not show:
- escalated tickets and whether agents now handle harder cases with less context,
- customers who gave up (which claim 3 may be counting as resolved),
- the bot's running cost against the headcount savings in claim 5,
- any failure in the 30 transcripts that were not chosen.

## Recommended next steps, by priority

1. **Before the board (one query, claim 1):** confirm whether "first response" includes bot greetings. Report the median time to first human response alongside it. Impact: this decides whether the headline is a real improvement.
2. **Before the board (wording):** rewrite claim 3 as "the bot closed 70% of chats; closure is not resolution, and we are measuring resolution separately." Remove "resolves" from the slide.
3. **Before the board (wording):** drop claims 2, 5 and 6 from the proven list. Present 5 as a forecast with its assumptions, and 2 and 6 as open questions.
4. **Lead with claim 4.** It is the strongest evidence you have. State its scope and the channel-counting check.
5. **After the board:** set up a tracking registry for the bot (see below), so the next claim is tested before it is written.
