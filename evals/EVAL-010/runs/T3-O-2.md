---

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** `[S]`
- Evidence: managers say new hires "feel stronger." No quality-of-hire measure (performance rating, probation pass, retention) is reported.
- Item 4 measures agreement with human panels. Agreement is not quality.
- Confound: managers who adopted the tool may be the ones who already liked it.
- Would change the tier: a preregistered quality-of-hire outcome, compared against a matched pre-tool or non-adopter cohort.

**2. "It is unbiased."** `[Y]`
- Evidence: the vendor white paper says the training data was "diverse." That describes an input. It does not show an output with no disparate impact. Calling it "unbiased" converts a data description into an outcome claim, which is tier drift from the source.
- No selection-rate-by-group analysis (for example, the four-fifths rule) appears anywhere in the summary.
- Item 4 has the same gap. Even a perfect match with human panels would reproduce whatever bias the panels have.
- Would change the tier: a selection-rate analysis by protected group, run by a third party, with a locked threshold.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** `[KKE]`
- Evidence: HR-system dates for every requisition, two quarters before and after, 14 departments. Volume was stable (212 vs 208). This is the strongest data in the summary. Source not in hand.
- The causal reading ("the tool did this") has not been tested. Open checks:
  - **Censoring.** If the after-period counts only requisitions that closed inside the window, slow requisitions still open at the cutoff drop out. That alone can shorten the average. Include open requisitions as right-censored, or report the share still open.
  - **Seasonality.** Compare the same two quarters from the prior year.
  - **Mix.** The freeze stabilized volume, not role mix. Check whether the hardest roles were frozen out of the after-period.
  - **Adoption timing.** Confirm the tool went live at the boundary, not partway through.
- Would change the tier: the censoring-robust figure and a same-season prior-year comparison both showing the drop. Then `[K]` for the descriptive change. The causal attribution would stay `[H]` without a non-adopter comparison.

**4. Blinded external audit: 81% tool vs 79% human-panel agreement.** `[H]`, source not in hand
- If the report confirms the numbers, the description is accurate and the audit design is independent, which is the strongest design in the summary.
- But the 2-point gap is inside sampling noise at n = 600. Roughly, each overlap rate carries about ±3–4 points at 95%, and the gap is smaller than that.
- The two comparisons may not match. "Tool vs consensus" and "panel vs panel" are different constructions. Consensus depends on how it was built. Check that before comparing.
- What it shows is parity with human agreement, not superiority, and not validity against outcomes.

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** `[S]`
- A forecast. The summary itself says it is too early, and the first cohort is 3 months old. The sentence is honest. The board headline ("will improve") is not.
- Would change the tier: a preregistered comparison (tool cohorts vs matched prior cohorts at 12 months), with a locked threshold and a stated direction for a null result.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** `[KKE]`
- Evidence: estimates in a team meeting. The arbiter is the people who benefit, and no baseline was measured. Self-estimates of time saved are known to run high.
- Would change the tier: time logs or ATS activity timestamps, before and after.
