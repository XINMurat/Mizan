## The arithmetic on claim 4

For two arms of 2,500 customers each (5,000 total), with 9.1% and 11.4% rates:

- Standard error of the difference ≈ √(0.091·0.909/2500 + 0.114·0.886/2500) ≈ 0.86 points
- 95% CI half-width ≈ 1.68 points, so the CI should be about **−4.0 to −0.6**.
- The reported CI (−3.0 to −1.6) has a half-width of 0.7, about **2.4× narrower** than the stated sample supports.

Possible explanations: the analysis counted contacts rather than customers, the arms were unequal, covariate adjustment was applied and not disclosed, or the CI was computed incorrectly. I can't tell which from here. The direction probably survives, since even the wider CI excludes zero. But a board number whose precision can't be reproduced from its own sample size should not be presented as proven.

## What survives, specifically

- **Claim 1:** A full-population timestamp measurement from a team independent of the bot. The 4.2 h → 40 s change is real in the data. Only its label needs fixing.

The effect is a 2.3-point drop on a roughly 10% base, about 20% relative. It is the claim most worth preserving, once its CI is reconciled and the abandonment control is run.
