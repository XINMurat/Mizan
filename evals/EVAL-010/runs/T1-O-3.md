# Mizan audit: support chatbot board summary

**Declaration:** This audit is retrospective. I reviewed the six claims as written in the summary. I could not open the ticketing data, the vendor dashboard, the RCT protocol, or the transcripts, so every tier rests on the provenance the summary states. The claims were not selected by me; I audited all six. Coverage: 6 of 6 claims assessed, 0 of 6 checked against raw sources.

## Claim table

| # | Claim | Tier | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Median first response fell 4.2 h → 40 s | **[K]** | Full population (38,000 tickets), system timestamps, independent data team, 10 weeks each side. Descriptive, so no causal attribution is needed. **Open check:** does "first response" include the bot's automatic acknowledgment? If yes, 40 s measures the bot, not agent responsiveness. The board wording must match the definition. |
| 2 | Customers prefer the bot | **[S]** | Preference was never measured. 30 transcripts, selection method unknown, read by the product team (author-arbitrated). Transcripts show behavior at most, and no denominator exists. |
| 3 | Bot resolves 70% on its own | **[Y]** | The vendor defines "resolved" as the customer closing the window. Closing a window is not resolution: abandonment, giving up, and a successful answer all look the same. The arbiter is the vendor, who is interested in the number. The figure is technically what the dashboard shows, but "resolves" implies more than the definition supports. |
| 4 | Bot reduced repeat contacts | **[K]** | Randomized, n = 5,000, fixed 6-week window, 95% CI (−3.0 to −1.6) excludes zero, analytics team independent of the bot team. Two checks before the board: (a) confirm the randomization balance and that contacts are logged identically in both arms. Bot-first chats that never create tickets could undercount repeat contacts in one arm only, which would flip the result to [KKE]. (b) Confirm the protocol was registered before data collection. The hiring freeze hits both arms equally under randomization, so it does not threaten this result. |
| 5 | Will cut headcount cost by a third next year | **[S]** | A future projection from the vendor's sales deck. No model, no baseline, no test. |
| 6 | Agents are happier | **[KKE]** | Happiness was never measured (no survey). Attrition is a proxy with a live confound: a hiring freeze started the same month. The control that could flip it (attrition in comparable non-bot teams, or the same quarter in prior years) has not run. Not [K] until that control runs. |

## Counter-example sweep

No pattern claims in the summary ("always," "consistently") — so no sweep was needed. Claim 3 is a definitional question, not a pattern.

## Hit rate

Not applicable. The summary praises no one's judgment or predictions.

## The missing card

A board summary of a support bot cannot show: (a) escalations the bot handed to humans and what those cost in agent time; (b) customer satisfaction or CSAT, which the summary never mentions; (c) the 30 transcripts that went badly; (d) the cost of the vendor contract against the savings; (e) the freeze's effect on human capacity in the control period. The first response figure is a best case, not the whole customer experience.

## Structural diagnosis

Each claim is one step above its evidence. Claim 1 is a clean measurement that reads as a service-quality claim. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 are a product team, a vendor, and a vendor's sales deck supplying the numbers. Nobody here intended to inflate the summary. The format rewards a headline over a denominator, and the team lead's confidence came from the format, not from the evidence.

## What survives

- **Claim 1**: the speed improvement is real in the data as described, subject to the definition check.
- **Claim 4**: the strongest evidence in the set. Independent randomized test, pre-specified window, and a confidence interval that excludes zero. Present it as the lead result.

## Verdict on the team lead's "all proven"

Two of six pass. Claim 4 is the one you can defend in the room.
