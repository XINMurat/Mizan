# Audit: Support chatbot board summary

**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective. I reviewed a summary written after the results were known, and this review is also after the fact.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims assessed. I could not open any underlying source (ticket timestamps, the randomized-test data, the vendor dashboard, the transcripts, or the sales deck), so every tier below is based on the provenance and internal consistency stated in the summary. Nothing here is a re-measurement.

**Bottom line:** Claims 1 and 4 can go to the board, with the wording changes below. Claims 2, 3, 5 and 6 cannot be presented as proven. The team lead's "all proven" is not supported. I would not sign off on the slide as written.

## Claim-by-claim

| # | Claim | Tier | Reason | What would change the tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Median first response fell 4.2 h → 40 s | **[K]** for the measured quantity | Full population (38,000 tickets), system timestamps, named source. The drop is real as measured. The risk is definitional: if the bot's instant auto-greeting counts as "first response," the 40 s figure measures the greeting, not help. A before/after comparison over 10 weeks also has no control for volume or staffing changes. | Confirm that "first response" is defined identically before and after, and report automated and human first response separately. |
| 2 | Customers prefer the bot | **[Y]** (evidence supports [H] at most) | 30 transcripts read by the product team. No denominator, no selection method, no comparison arm, no preference question asked. "Prefer" is a comparative claim with no comparison. | A preference survey or a randomized satisfaction measure, bot vs. human, with a stated sample. |
| 3 | Bot resolves 70% on its own | **[Y]** | "Resolved" = customer closed the window. Closing a window is not resolution; customers also close windows when they give up. The source is the vendor's own dashboard, so the arbiter is interested. | An independent resolution definition (no contact within N days, or a post-chat check) and a third-party or internal measurement. |
| 4 | Bot reduced repeat contacts | **[K]** (directional) | Randomized, 5,000 customers, 6 weeks, analytics team (not the bot team). The effect is 9.1% vs. 11.4%, and both interval endpoints are negative. **Arithmetic check:** with about 2,500 per arm and rates near 10%, the 95% CI on the difference is about ±1.7 points, roughly −4.0 to −0.6. The stated CI (−3.0 to −1.6, ±0.7) implies about 14,000 per arm. Either the denominator is not 5,000 customers (for example, it is contacts, or a subset), or the interval is wrong. The direction survives both versions. | Analytics team to supply per-arm denominators and the unit of analysis. Also confirm that bot-deflected contacts are logged as contacts in both arms. If bot-handled contacts are logged differently, the arms are not measured the same way. |
| 5 | Cut support headcount costs by a third next year | **[S]** | A forecast from the vendor's sales deck. No model, no baseline, no assumptions shown. It is also entangled with claim 6: any headcount effect is confounded with the hiring freeze. | A cost model with the baseline, the deflection rate from a verified claim 3, and a stated hiring plan. Until then it is a vendor projection. |
| 6 | Agents are happier | **[Y]** | No survey was run. Lower attrition coincides with a hiring freeze, which changes the labour market, workload and open roles. Attrition is at best a weak proxy for happiness, and this quarter's number has an obvious rival explanation. | An agent survey with a pre-launch baseline, or attrition compared across matched teams with and without the bot. |

## What the summary cannot show (the missing card)

The format shows only wins: speed, a preference, a deflection rate, a favourable experiment, a cost forecast. It cannot show:
- The share of customers who abandoned the bot and never reached a human, which the 70% figure conceals.
- Wrong or harmful bot answers, and how often they were corrected later.
- Escalation rates, and the time customers spent in the bot before reaching a human.
- Results for segments the randomized test may not represent (languages, enterprise accounts, complaints, billing disputes).
- Cost of the bot itself, including licensing, integration and maintenance.

Some of these are probably in the data already. I have not checked, so they are gaps in this summary, not confirmed failures.

## Counter-examples searched

- Claim 1: I could not access the timestamp data, so no counter-example search was possible. The check is definitional, not a search.
- Claim 3: The randomized test (claim 4) does not contradict the 70% figure directly, but it cannot confirm it either, since it does not measure resolution.
- Claim 6: I searched the summary for any survey or direct agent measure. None is present.

## Recommended board wording

- **Keep:** "Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds across 38,000 tickets (10 weeks before and after launch). This includes automated responses; human first-response time is [to be added]."
- **Keep, with the CI check cleared first:** "In a 6-week randomized test of 5,000 customers, bot-first routing reduced 7-day repeat contacts from 11.4% to 9.1%. Run by the analytics team."
- **Reword to a hypothesis:** "The bot appears to resolve a substantial share of tickets. We are measuring resolution independently; the vendor's 70% figure counts closed windows, not resolutions."
- **Remove or mark as a projection:** the headcount-cost claim (until a model exists) and the agent-happiness claim (until surveyed). The customer-preference claim should be reworded to "early transcript review suggests…", or removed.
