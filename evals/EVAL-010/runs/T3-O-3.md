# Mizan audit: hiring screening tool board summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). This is a retrospective audit, and it selected its claims after seeing the summary. The HARKing status is declared, and it applies to this report too.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims are in principle checkable. I checked none directly. I had only the summary text, with no access to the HR export, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, or the meeting notes. Every tier below rests on how the claim is written, not on its source. No counter-examples were searched because no sources were available.

**Format conflict:** The requested verdict block is binary, and the tier table is not. I map PROVEN to `[K]` and NOT PROVEN to every other tier (`[S]`, `[Y]`, `[KKE]`, `[H]`). The block is the only place the binary format applies. The per-claim tiers below carry the actual findings.

**Bottom line for tomorrow:** I cannot confirm this as "all proven." One claim survives in narrow form. Five do not, and two of those are the headline claims the board will remember. The team lead's confidence is not evidence.

---

### 1. "The tool picks better candidates." — `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative
- **Evidence in the summary:** managers say new hires "feel stronger." That is impression, not an outcome.
- **Missing:** any outcome measure (rating, probation pass, ramp time), any comparison group, any threshold.
- **Tier drift:** testimony in the notes became a headline fact on the board slide.
- Claim 4 measures agreement with human panels. It cannot show "better."

### 2. "It is unbiased." — `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading, with `[KKE]`
- "Trained on diverse data" describes an input. It says nothing about the outputs.
- "Unbiased" requires an output test: selection rates by group on the tool's actual decisions (adverse-impact ratio or equivalent, under the rules that apply to you).
- The only evidence comes from the vendor, which makes the vendor the author and arbiter. Under R-rules that caps it at permanent `[KKE]`.
- The 600-application audit could support this test if it reported results by group. The summary does not say it did.
- Consult counsel on which rules apply. Do not claim compliance.

### 3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days." — `[KKE]`, descriptive part `[K]`-grade
- **What holds:** the decline is observed in full-population HR data (every requisition, 14 departments, two quarters either side).
- **What does not:** the summary places this under the tool, so the board will read it as the tool's effect. The data cannot separate the tool from other causes.
- **Missing controls:**
  - Was the clock definition (open date, close date, offer or start) unchanged across the adoption date?
  - Was the decline already under way before adoption? (A pre-trend would produce the same drop.)
  - Did all 14 departments adopt at the same time? If so there is no unexposed comparison group.
  - Headcount was equal (212 vs 208), but role mix was not checked. A hiring freeze changes which roles get filled, and role mix changes time-to-hire.
- Two-sidedness: if the tool did nothing, this data would look the same. It cannot be called evidence for the tool yet.

### 4. Blinded external audit: 81% vs 79% overlap — `[K]` for parity only
- **Holds:** an external firm, blinded, n=600. The tool agrees with the panels about as well as the panels agree with each other.
- **Does not hold:** "better." Overlap measures similarity to human judgment, not accuracy.
- **Statistics:** each proportion has a standard error of about 1.6 points at n=600. The 2-point gap is within noise. "Comparable" is supported. "Higher" is not.
- **Missing:** how the consensus was built (did the tool's scores feed into it?), shortlist size, confidence intervals, and whether the overlap was paired.
- **Verdict scope:** PROVEN for parity. The board sentence should not imply superiority.

### 5. "First-year retention will improve." — `[S]`
- A forecast with no baseline and no threshold. A three-month cohort cannot show first-year retention.
- Retention depends on many factors besides screening, so even a later improvement would need a comparison to attribute it.

### 6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week." — `[S]`
- Estimates given in a team meeting. No time logs, no baseline, no counterfactual.
- Self-report makes the author the arbiter, so this cannot reach `[K]` without a measurement.

### Missing card (what this format cannot show)
The summary has no failures. It has no false negatives (good candidates the tool rejected), no subgroup results, no cost, no candidate complaints or appeals, and no account of what the tool replaced. Those are the cards a maximally honest version would include.

### Domain probe (not done)
I have not run the domain probe. Hiring has edge cases I cannot list myself: internal transfers, re-applicants, career gaps, accommodation requests, roles with zero or one applicant, who may see scores, and candidate requests for review. HR and legal owners need to supply these. Until they do, record `supplied_by: none`, and do not claim full coverage.

### Recommendations, ordered by criticality × impact / effort
1. **Reword the slide tonight.** Claims 1, 2, 5, and 6 become "not yet measured." Claim 3 becomes "observed decline; attribution under review." Claim 4 becomes "parity with human panels (81% vs 79%), not superiority."
2. **Run an adverse-impact analysis on the 600 applications by group.** It is cheap, uses data you already have, and bears the most risk.
3. **Pull the time-to-hire series** with the adoption date, the clock definition, and at least four pre-adoption quarters, and check the role mix during the freeze.
4. **Define a quality outcome** (e.g., 90-day manager rating or probation pass) and preregister a threshold and a comparison group before collecting it.
5. **Run a 2–4 week time log** for recruiters, before and after adoption, to replace the meeting estimate.
