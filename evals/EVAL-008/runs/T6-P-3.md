## Failures in detail

**Claim 4 [R]: Refuted as written.** The summary says coverage was "complete" with 48 services scanned. Its own Appendix A lists 53 production services. The mechanism is probably a scanner asset list that does not match the CMDB, or a 48-count taken before the inventory date. The impact is 5 unscanned services, about 9.4% of the inventory. This entry stays on record as refuted. It can be revised only by a new entry with reconciliation evidence, not by editing this one.

**Claim 2 [Y] + [KKE]: The "0 critical" result is conditional, and the summary drops the condition.** The result applies to 48 scanned services, not 53. It also assumes the scanner detects what it should, which has never been tested. A scanner that misses criticals will also report zero. The board sentence "the platform has no critical vulnerabilities" is not supported.

**Claim 5 [Y]: Independence and scope problems.** The platform team built the systems it tested. Without a scope document, tester qualifications, and a list of the attack paths tested, "found no way" cannot be distinguished from "did not look." This is the most overclaimed sentence in the summary.

**Claim 7 [Y]: Completion is not behavior.** The summary moves from "99.7% completed training" to "phishing risk is negligible" with no measurement in between. Phishing simulation click and report rates are the measurement that would support the conclusion.

**Claim 3 [KKE]: Likely real, not yet proven.** Stating the severity definitions is good practice. The open issues are: (a) if high-severity tickets were reclassified, closed as accepted risk, or left open and dropped from the average, the drop can be an artifact; (b) the sample sizes are not given, so 41 vs. 12 has no uncertainty attached.

**Claim 8 [KKE]: Strong candidate, but the test is weaker than the claim.** A policy with 0 exceptions does not show that every admin account is covered. Accounts can sit outside the IdP, such as local admin accounts on services, service accounts, or legacy auth paths, and none of those would appear in the export. Credit: citing an export is the correct form of evidence. The gap is the population being tested.

## Next steps (in priority order)

1. **Reconcile scanner coverage with the CMDB.** Identify the 5 services not scanned. Either scan them or restate the result as "48 of 53 (90.6%)." Seed a known-vulnerable test host to measure scanner recall before reporting "0 critical."
2. **Pull the ticket export.** Report high-severity counts per year, open-at-cutoff tickets and their age, a sample of closure timestamps matched to actual deployment dates, and closure reasons. Recompute MTTP with still-open tickets counted at the report date.
3. **Get the pentest scope document.** Identify the testers and their independence from the platform team. Reword the finding to "no escalation paths found within scope X, using methods Y." Commission an independent test if the board needs the absolute claim.
4. **Replace training completion with a behavioral measure.** Use phishing simulation click and report rates, before and after training.
5. **Join the admin-role principal list against MFA registration and sign-in logs.** Include local admin accounts on the 53 services, not only IdP accounts.
