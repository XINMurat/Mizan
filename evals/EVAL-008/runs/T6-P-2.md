## Findings in detail

### Claim 4 — [R] Reddedildi / Refuted: "Scan coverage was complete"
- **Source conflict:** Claim 4 says 48 services were scanned. Claim 6 says production has 53. That leaves 5 unscanned, or 9.4% of the inventory. Coverage is 48/53 = 90.6%, not complete.
- **Possible explanations to check, not assume:** the CMDB export date differs from the scan date; some CMDB entries are decommissioned or non-production; the scanner skipped services for a technical reason. Each one changes the story. Only the first two could legitimately reduce the gap. Any of them would need to be shown, not asserted.
- **Record:** Keep the "complete" claim on the record as refuted. Do not revise it to "complete" once the explanation is found. Record the explanation next to it.

### Claim 2 — [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading: "No critical vulnerabilities"
- **Mechanism of error:** A scanner result (zero critical findings in what it scanned) became a statement about the whole platform. Two gaps: the 5 unscanned services (claim 4), and scanner recall. Zero findings is evidence of absence only to the extent the scanner can detect what is present. No recall check, such as seeded test vulnerabilities or a known-vulnerable canary, is reported.
- **Quantitative impact:** At least 5 of 53 production services (9.4%) have no scan result behind this claim.
- **Tier drift:** The claim moved from a scanner observation [K] to a platform-wide conclusion [Y] without new evidence. Flag it.
- **Preregistered refutation (proposed):** The claim is refuted if any of the 53 services has a critical finding. Lock this before scanning the 5 gaps.

### Claim 3 — [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing: "Mean time to patch dropped from 41 to 12 days"
- **This is the one flattering surprise, so it gets the symmetric check first.** A 71% reduction (29/41 days) is large and should be tested before it headlines anything.
- **Checks not yet run:**
  - **Censoring:** If high-severity tickets still open are excluded from the mean, the metric improves mechanically. Include open tickets at their current age.
  - **Closure reason:** If "won't fix," "risk accepted," or "duplicate" count as closed, the drop may reflect reclassification. Report counts by closure reason.
  - **Outliers:** A mean is sensitive to a few long-tail tickets. Report the median and the count alongside it.
  - **Severity definitions:** "Same definitions" is asserted, not shown. Confirm that no tickets were re-scored between years.
- **Tier:** Stays [KKE] until these run. It can move to [H] or [K] only after they run.

### Claim 5 — [H] Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis: "No way to escalate privileges"
- **Arbiter problem:** The team that built the platform tested it. That is the author as judge. Independence is required before this reads as an assessment.
- **Negative-result problem:** "Found no way" is a claim about what a time-boxed test did not find. Scope, method, dates, and tester qualifications are needed to judge how much that means.
- **Next step:** An independent test with a stated scope. Until then, the claim can be stated as "a platform-team test with scope X found no escalation paths," not as a platform fact.

### Claim 7 — [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading: "Training completion → phishing risk negligible"
- **Mechanism:** Completion measures whether people finished training. It does not measure whether they resist phishing. The inference from completion to negligible risk does not hold, whatever the completion rate.
- **Missing denominator:** The 0.3% who did not complete is not quantified. Report the headcount.
- **Outcome evidence needed:** A phishing simulation with a click rate and a report rate. Set the threshold for "negligible" before running the simulation. A threshold chosen after seeing results is a moving goalpost.

### Claim 8 — [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing: "MFA enforced on all admin accounts, 0 exceptions"
- **Policy versus enforcement:** An identity-provider policy export shows that a policy exists with no listed exceptions. It does not show that every admin account has an enrolled MFA factor and is covered by the policy.
- **Scope gaps the policy may not cover:** Local admin accounts outside the IdP, service accounts, break-glass accounts, and legacy authentication protocols that bypass conditional access. Any of these can make "0 exceptions" true on paper and false in practice.
- **Needed check:** An account-level export of all admin accounts, with MFA factor status, reconciled against the full admin population from the directory.
- **Attachment:** I did not review the attached export. Its contents may narrow this, but the policy-versus-account gap applies regardless.

### Claim 1 and claim 6
- **Claim 1 [H]:** Harmless context, but unsourced. Attach a roster or drop it. Note that a six-person team responsible for 53 services is a capacity fact the board should see, and it bears on the patch-time result in claim 3.
- **Claim 6 [K]:** Accepted as a cited count. The CMDB may itself be incomplete, so the true production population could exceed 53. Ask for the inventory reconciliation method.

## Suggested board-safe wording

Replace the eight points with statements at the level of the evidence:

- *Scanner results: zero critical findings in 48 of 53 production services. The remaining 5 are not yet scanned; results pending.*
- *Mean time to patch high-severity issues: 41 → 12 days (median and open-ticket treatment pending verification).*
- *Privilege escalation: a platform-team test with scope [X] found no escalation paths. Independent testing is scheduled.*
- *Security training: 99.7% completion. Phishing resilience has not yet been measured.*
- *MFA: policy export shows enforcement across the IdP with no listed exceptions. Account-level verification is in progress.*
