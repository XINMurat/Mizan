# Review of Checkout Outage Postmortem, 2026-09-18

## Claim-by-claim assessment

**1. Paged at 14:05.**
*Partly supported.* A paging-system timestamp would support this, but the postmortem doesn't cite one. It also leaves open how the outage start (14:03) was established, since the page came two minutes later. Cite the pager log.

**2. Outage lasted 47 minutes, 14:03 to 14:52.**
*Not supported as written.* 14:03 to 14:52 is 49 minutes. Either a timestamp or the duration is wrong. The postmortem also never defines "outage." Is it any error, the error rate crossing a threshold, or a failed checkout? The start time depends on that definition.

**3. Error rate peaked at 38% of requests (load balancer logs).**
*Mostly supported.* The source is specific. Still missing: the aggregation window (a one-minute peak and a five-minute peak differ), whether the load balancer sees all checkout traffic or only some of it, and how the 38% compares with the baseline error rate.

**4. Root cause: the cache eviction change in release 8.2. Rolled back at 14:50, errors stopped shortly after.**
*Not supported. This is the central claim and the weakest.*
- **Confounded by the DNS incident.** Claim 6 says the DNS provider's incident was resolved at 14:49, one minute before the rollback at 14:50. Errors stopping "shortly after" 14:50 is equally consistent with DNS recovery. The postmortem doesn't address this overlap.
- **No causal evidence.** The postmortem gives no mechanism linking cache eviction to checkout failures, no reproduction, and no evidence that the errors began when 8.2 reached production. Showing that errors started after the 8.2 deploy would help. So would showing that hosts or regions still on the old version were unaffected, or that the error rate dropped on rolled-back hosts before the DNS fix took effect.
- **"Shortly after" is vague.** Give the actual time the error rate returned to baseline.
- **Who determined this?** The postmortem doesn't say whether anyone other than the author reviewed the attribution.

At most, the evidence supports "8.2 is a candidate contributing factor, and the rollback coincided with recovery." It does not support "root cause."

**5. No customer data was affected. We checked the three services we suspected might have written partial orders.**
*Overstated.* The check covers three suspected services, so the strongest claim the evidence supports is "no partial order writes were found in the three services checked." Whether other services that write orders were in scope isn't stated. The method (queries, audits, reconciliation against the payment provider?) isn't described. "Partial order" is also undefined. Rewrite the claim to match the scope actually checked.

**6. The DNS provider's status page records an incident, resolved at 14:49.**
*Supported as a fact about the third party's page, but its relevance is ignored.* The status page is an external record, not evidence about your own impact. You need to show whether your checkout traffic actually used the affected DNS resolution path and whether your own logs show DNS failures during 14:03–14:49. Without that, this claim is an unexamined confounder and should be analyzed, not just listed.

**7. New alert fires when error rate exceeds 5% for 2 minutes. Replayed against this incident, it fires at 14:06.**
*Partly supported, with limits.*
- A replay against one incident shows the rule would have fired then. It does not show the rule is well calibrated. Thresholds chosen after looking at this incident's data may be overfit.
- The replay doesn't report false positives. Run the rule against normal periods to check how often it would fire without an incident.
- The 14:05 page must have come from something other than this alert, since the alert would not have fired until 14:06. Say what paged the engineer, and whether the new alert would have caught the incident before that page.
- Confirm the alert is deployed and live, not just that it was tested.

**8. This will not happen again.**
*Unsupported.* No evidence can establish this. Given that the root cause is uncertain (claim 4) and the DNS dependency is unaddressed, the claim is also unjustified on its face. Replace it with specific corrective actions, owners, and deadlines, and state residual risk honestly.

**9. The team held a retrospective on Friday.**
*Supported, but not a finding.* The date 2026-09-18 is itself a Friday, so "Friday" is ambiguous: it could mean the same day as the incident or the following Friday. Give the actual date. This claim doesn't bear on the postmortem's conclusions.

## Missing from the postmortem

- **Mitigation time.** The engineer was paged at 14:05 and the rollback happened at 14:50, about 45 minutes. Why did mitigation take that long? Was the rollback the first action tried? This is probably the most actionable part of the incident and it isn't discussed.
- **Customer impact.** There is no count of failed checkouts, lost revenue, or affected users. The 38% error rate is a request-level metric, not an impact measure.
- **Detection.** Detection depended on a page, and the new alert didn't exist yet. The analysis of why detection was slow is absent.
- **Alternative explanations.** Beyond DNS, none are considered, so the postmortem can't rule them out.
- **Independent review.** No one other than the author is named as having verified the timeline or root cause.

## Summary

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Paged 14:05 | Plausible; cite the source |
| 2. Duration 47 min | **Arithmetic error** (49 min) |
| 3. Peak 38% | Supported; define window and scope |
| 4. Root cause 8.2 | **Not supported**; confounded with DNS recovery |
| 5. No customer data affected | **Overstated**; scope limited to three services |
| 6. DNS incident 14:49 | Fact is supported; **causal relevance unexamined** |
| 7. Alert fires at 14:06 | Partly supported; one-incident replay, no false-positive check |
| 8. Won't happen again | **Unsupported** |
| 9. Retro on Friday | Supported; date ambiguous |

**Adequately supported:** claims 1 (once sourced), 3, and 9 (once dated). Claim 7 is supported as a narrow statement about one replay.

**Before publishing:** fix the duration, determine whether 8.2 or the DNS recovery explains the recovery (for example, by checking error rates on hosts that never ran 8.2, or on requests that bypassed the failing DNS path), narrow claim 5 to what was checked, replace claim 8 with concrete actions, and add the mitigation-time analysis.
