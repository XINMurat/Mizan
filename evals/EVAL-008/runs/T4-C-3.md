# Review: Checkout Outage Postmortem, 2026-09-18

**Bottom line:** Only a few claims are adequately supported. The most serious problems are an arithmetic error, a root-cause claim that a concurrent DNS incident could explain, a scoped data-impact claim written as a blanket statement, and the closing "will not happen again," which nothing here supports.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. Paged at 14:05.** *Partly supported.* The time is plausible, but the source (pager log, for example) isn't cited. The postmortem also doesn't discuss the two-minute gap between onset (14:03) and paging, which is a detection-latency finding worth stating.

**2. Outage lasted 47 minutes, 14:03 to 14:52.** *Not supported as written.* 14:03 to 14:52 is 49 minutes. Either the duration or one endpoint is wrong. The postmortem also doesn't say how the start and end were defined (for example, first error above some threshold, and recovery below what level?). Both endpoints depend on that definition.

**3. Error rate peaked at 38% (load balancer logs).** *Supported as a measurement, with gaps.* The source is named. Missing: what counts as an error (5xx only, or timeouts too?), the aggregation window for "peak," and the normal baseline error rate, so readers can judge how abnormal 38% is.

**4. Root cause: cache eviction change in 8.2; rolled back at 14:50, errors stopped shortly after.** *Not supported as a root-cause claim.*
- The evidence is temporal association. Nothing shows a mechanism, reproduction, or log or trace linking cache eviction to the failing requests.
- The rollback at 14:50 coincides with the DNS provider's resolution at 14:49 (claim 6). A one-minute-earlier external recovery is a serious confounder, and the postmortem doesn't address it.
- "Shortly after" is unquantified. A per-minute error series around 14:49–14:52 is needed.
- Missing: when 8.2 was deployed. If it went out hours before onset, the link is weaker; if it went out at 14:00, it's stronger.
- The claim should read as "the most likely cause, pending confirmation," or the postmortem should show the evidence that separates the two explanations.

**5. No customer data affected; checked the three services we suspected might have written partial orders.** *Overstated.* The check covered only services suspected in advance, so it cannot support a blanket "no customer data was affected." Also missing: how the check was done (database queries, reconciliation against payment records?), who performed it, and whether partial writes could have occurred in unsuspected services. Accurate wording would be: "No partial order writes were found in the three services examined, using method X."

**6. DNS provider's status page records an incident, resolved 14:49.** *Supported as a fact about the status page only.* It establishes that the provider reported an incident, not that it affected this company's traffic. The postmortem doesn't say whether the team's own DNS resolution failed, which is the question that matters. Time zones on the status page should also be confirmed. This claim is the main confounder for claim 4 and needs to be analyzed, not just listed.

**7. New alert (>5% for 2 min) replayed against this incident fires at 14:06.** *Supported as a replay, but weaker than it appears.*
- The replay is in-sample. The threshold was presumably chosen with this incident in view, so it proves little about generalization.
- No false-positive check over normal traffic is given. A 5%/2-minute rule may page constantly on noisy services.
- Most importantly, 14:06 is after the actual page at 14:05. In this incident the alert would not have improved detection. The postmortem should say so.

**8. "This will not happen again."** *Not supported.* No prevention measure is verified. The rollback restores service, and the alert shortens detection, but neither stops a bad cache change from shipping. Nothing addresses review, canary, or test coverage for the cache eviction code. This should be replaced with specific, owned follow-up actions and an honest statement of residual risk.

**9. Retrospective held on Friday.** *Adequately supported as a process fact, but not a finding.* It's unverifiable from the text, and it's ambiguous: 2026-09-18 is itself a Friday, so state the date of the retro. It also says nothing about what the retro concluded.

## Missing from the postmortem overall
- Named reviewer(s) and who made the judgments above.
- Deployment timestamp for release 8.2 and the pre-outage error baseline.
- A per-minute error timeline, ideally overlaid with the rollback and DNS resolution times.
- Confirmation of whether the DNS incident affected this system at all.
- Specific, owned action items with due dates.

## Summary
- **Adequately supported:** claim 3 (with minor definitional gaps) and claim 9 (as a process fact).
- **Supported with caveats:** claims 1, 6, and 7 (the last is an in-sample replay that would not have beaten the human page).
- **Not supported as written:** claim 2 (arithmetic), claim 4 (root cause, confounded by DNS), claim 5 (scope overstated), and claim 8 (unsupported guarantee).
