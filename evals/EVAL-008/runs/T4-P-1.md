# Mizan Audit: Checkout Outage Postmortem, 2026-09-18

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **Coverage:** 9 of 9 claims are assessed. I checked internal consistency, logic, and whether each cited source supports its claim. I did not have access to the load-balancer logs, pager records, release diff, or retrospective notes, so `[K]` below means "source cited, not re-verified."

**Verdict:** The outage, the 38% peak, and the rollback are reasonably supported. The root cause is not yet established, the duration contradicts the timestamps, and the "will not happen again" conclusion is not a testable claim.

## Errors, located

**Claim 2: duration contradicts timestamps.** 14:03 to 14:52 is 49 minutes, not 47. The 47 matches 14:05 to 14:52, which suggests the start time was taken from the page rather than from the first error. If the outage actually began at 14:05, the page had zero detection lag, and claim 1 changes meaning. Mechanism: the start time was likely mixed up with the page time. Impact: a 2-minute error in the headline figure, and an unknown shift in the detection-lag story.

**Claim 4: the root cause has an unaddressed confound.** The DNS provider recorded its incident as resolved at 14:49 (claim 6). The rollback happened at 14:50. Errors "stopped shortly after," which is vague. The data cannot separate these explanations:
- (a) The 8.2 rollback fixed the errors.
- (b) DNS recovery fixed the errors, and the rollback was coincidental.
- (c) Both contributed.

If (b) holds, the 8.2 change may not be the root cause, and the planned fix would not prevent recurrence. Mechanism: no minute-resolution error timeline is shown, and the DNS incident is absent from the causal analysis. Impact: the root-cause attribution could be wrong, and the entire remediation depends on it. This is the critical control: a minute-by-minute error rate for 14:45–14:55, compared against the DNS resolution time and the rollback completion time. Also check whether DNS errors appear in the logs before 14:49.

**Claim 5: scope overreach.** The check covered three suspected services. "No customer data was affected" is a system-wide claim. Any service not on the suspect list is unexamined. Also, the check method is not described. Rewrite as: "Three services suspected of writing partial orders were checked. [Method]. Found: [result]. Other services: not checked." Coverage of this claim is 3 of N services.

**Claim 7: the alert is fitted to the incident.** The threshold was chosen, or at least confirmed, against this incident's logs. A replay on the same data that motivated the threshold cannot show it generalizes. There is also no false-positive count over normal traffic. Mechanism: in-sample fitting plus a missing baseline. Impact: the alert may be too noisy to use, or may have been tuned to fire exactly here. Also note that 14:06 is one minute after the recorded 14:05 page. Unless the page came from the new alert, this alert gains zero minutes for this incident. Next step: replay the threshold against 30+ days of non-incident traffic and report the false-alert rate, and state the threshold's origin and date.

**Claim 8: not a conclusion.** "This will not happen again" has no refutation condition and no measurable outcome. Replace it with a falsifiable prediction, for example: "A regression in cache eviction will be detected by the 5%/2-min alert within 3 minutes, and no 8.x release will produce a >5% error episode over the next 90 days. Refuted if either fails."

## Conclusions that survive

- The outage occurred, and the error rate peaked at 38% (claim 3, source cited).
- A rollback of release 8.2 was performed at 14:50 (claim 4, the event itself, not its causal effect).
- The DNS provider recorded an incident resolved at 14:49 (claim 6).
- A check of three suspected services was performed (claim 5, scoped).

## Next steps before publishing

1. **Fix the duration** and confirm the true outage start time from the first error in the logs.
2. **Build a minute-level timeline** of error rate, DNS resolution (14:49), and rollback completion (14:50). Rewrite the root cause as "probable" until it survives this control.
3. **Scope claim 5** explicitly, or extend the data check to all services that write orders.
4. **Replace claim 8** with a falsifiable prediction and a refutation condition.
5. **Qualify the alert** with its origin date and a false-positive rate over non-incident days.
6. **Add a retrospective date and attendees**, and clarify whether the meeting was on the incident day.

Two questions that would change the verdict: Did the 14:05 page come from the existing monitoring or from the new alert? And do the error logs show any errors continuing after 14:50?
