**Mode:** Audit (retrospective), with Security Probe (Mode 7) elements applied to the pentest, scanner, and MFA claims.

## 1. Verdict

The summary's four headline conclusions do not hold as written:

- "The platform has no critical vulnerabilities" is `[Y]`. The scanner data supports a narrower claim about 48 services.
- "Scan coverage was complete" is `[R]`. Item 6 contradicts it: 53 services in the inventory, 48 scanned.
- "No way to escalate privileges" is `[KKE]`. A self-run pentest finding no path is silence, not a pass.
- "Phishing risk is now negligible" is `[Y]`. Training completion is not a phishing-resistance measurement.

The MFA item is the strongest in the document because it names a verifiable artifact. Even it stops short of what the sentence claims, because a policy export shows a policy, not that every admin account is covered.

## 2. Claim-by-claim

**Claim 1: "Our security team has 6 people."**
Tier: `[H]`. No source is attached, and no claim depends on it. Headcount is context. It is relevant to the coverage question: six people cannot triage findings across 53 services plus the MFA and pentest workload. The document never connects capacity to the coverage claims.

**Claim 2: "The scanner reported 0 critical findings across the services it scanned, so the platform has no critical vulnerabilities."**
Split into two claims:
- (a) *0 critical on scanned services*: `[KKE]`. The scanner's own output is not attached, and no detection calibration is cited. A scanner returning zero findings has not been shown to be capable of returning a finding. Run it against a seeded known-vulnerable service and record whether it fires. Until that check runs, "0" is a reading from an uncalibrated instrument (R24).
- (b) *Therefore the platform has no critical vulnerabilities*: `[Y]`. This is an inference from a subset (48 of 53, see Claim 4) to the whole platform, and from "scanner found none" to "none exist." Scanners detect known signatures. They do not detect misconfiguration, logic flaws, or secrets in unscanned places.

**Claim 3: "Mean time to patch high-severity issues dropped from 41 to 12 days (ticket system, same severity definitions both years)."**
Tier: `[KKE]`.
- Credit: the severity definitions are stated as constant across years, and the instrument is named. That is more than most summaries provide.
- Mechanism of error: the metric is computed from the ticket system, so it measures ticket closure, not patch deployment. More importantly, a mean over *closed* tickets falls whenever long-open tickets are excluded or the backlog ages out. If high-severity tickets were left open or closed as "won't fix" or "risk accepted," the mean improves without any change in patching speed.
- Missing control: open high-severity count and age distribution at both dates, plus the mean computed with open tickets included as censored (age to date). The document does not say whether this was done.
- Also unstated: mean versus median. A few very old tickets move the mean a lot.

**Claim 4: "Scan coverage was complete: 48 production services were scanned."**
- The count "48" is `[H]`, from the summary text.
- "Complete" is `[R]`. Item 6 lists 53 production services in the CMDB export dated 2026-09-01. That is 48 of 53, or 90.6%. Five services are unscanned.
- Possible explanation, unverified: the scan ran before those five services were added. If so, the claim should say "complete as of [scan date]," and the scan date is missing from the document. Even then, the five unscanned services are not named, so the gap cannot be sized.
- Second-order question: the CMDB may over-count (decommissioned or duplicate entries). If so, 53 is wrong and the coverage denominator needs reconciling. Either way, "complete" does not survive the document's own appendix.

**Claim 5: "A penetration test by our platform team found no way to escalate privileges."**
Tier: `[KKE]`, with a permanent `[KKE]` ceiling.
- Arbiter class: `author`. The platform team builds and operates the system under test. A team judging its own platform cannot return a `[K]`.
- Silence, not a pass: "found no way" is a negative search result. Under R26, a failed attempt or clean run is silence. Only a named control on every privilege path promotes a claim.
- Scope is not stated: which services, which roles, which trust boundaries, time-boxed or exhaustive, and whether the five unscanned services were in scope.
- Missing: an independent test, or at minimum a scope document and a list of privilege-escalation paths the team tried.

**Claim 6: "Appendix A — production service inventory: 53 services (CMDB export, 2026-09-01)."**
Tier: `[H]`. The appendix was not provided to me. The claim is a count from a CMDB export, which is a cited source for "the CMDB says 53." It is not verified against runtime. It is the claim that refutes Claim 4, so it is important to resolve it before the board relies on either.

**Claim 7: "99.7% of employees completed security training, so phishing risk is now negligible."**
- Completion rate: `[H]`, from an LMS. Not verified.
- "Phishing risk is negligible": `[Y]`. Completion measures attendance. Phishing risk depends on click rate and report rate under realistic simulation, and on whether the 0.3% who did not complete include high-privilege staff. The inference jumps from one measurement to a risk level, with no threshold.
- Locked threshold missing. A claim of "negligible" needs a number, for example a simulated-phishing click rate below X% in a defined population, measured before and after training.

**Claim 8: "MFA is enforced on all admin accounts, verified by an identity-provider policy export showing 0 exceptions (attached)."**
Tier: `[KKE]`.
- Credit: this is the only claim that names a specific instrument, a specific artifact, and a specific result. It is the right kind of evidence.
- Gap: a policy export from the IdP shows that a policy exists and what it excludes. It does not show that every admin account is subject to it. Admin accounts that never pass through the IdP are invisible to it: local administrator accounts on servers, database admin accounts, service accounts, break-glass accounts, and any admin roles inside the five unscanned services.
- Missing control: enumerate admin-capable accounts from each system's native role store and join them against IdP enrollment. The number that matters is "admin-capable accounts without enforced MFA," not "policy exceptions."

## 4. The missing card

This summary format cannot show:
- Incidents: detections, time to detect, time to contain. Absent entirely.
- Medium and low backlog. Only high-severity timing is reported.
- Scanner false-negative rate, and which vulnerability classes the scanner cannot see.
- The five unscanned services, and any services in the inventory that a scanner cannot reach.
- Third-party and vendor-hosted services, if any.
- Cost and staffing against the workload.
- Remediation outcomes for the pentest and scanner findings, if any were produced.

The format selects for positive, countable, pre-packaged numbers. That is structural, not necessarily intentional (mechanism, not motive).

## 5. Domain probe (Mode 7, checklist item 12)

I cannot write the scenarios for this domain alone, and I have not invented them. Scenarios I list below are prompts for the interview with the security team, drawn from the eight classes in checklist item 12. None has been confirmed as occurring in this organisation.

- Two at once: a service is added to the CMDB and a privileged account is created on it the same day, before any scan.
- End of life: a service is decommissioned but still runs, or still appears in the scan list.
- Crossing a boundary: an admin role is granted through a federated or external identity that never touches the policy export.
- The person leaves: a contractor or ex-employee retains admin credentials on an unscanned system.
- Zero, one, many: a service with no admin accounts versus one with hundreds of service accounts.
- Undo and exit: a break-glass or emergency account used during an incident and never revoked.
- Out of order: a patch applied before the ticket was opened, or the ticket closed before the patch was verified.
- Who may see it: does the board pack reveal the 5 unscanned services' names to readers who should not know them?

`probes.domain`: `supplied_by: none` (self-report). Under R19, no tier-K coverage claim may rest on this probe until the security team supplies its own scenarios.

## 6. Conjunction audit (checklist item 13)

Features and guarantees that can interact:

| Pair | Guarantee reached | Order | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scan coverage × "no critical vulns" | Derived absence signal: the zero count's meaning depends on the denominator | Coverage first | **Fail.** The five unscanned services are unknown; the zero is not transferable. |
| Scan coverage × MFA enforcement | Both checks are blind to the same class: systems outside the IdP and outside the scan list | Either | **Unchecked.** Plausibly overlapping. Test by listing admin-capable accounts on the five unscanned services. |
| Pentest scope × MFA exceptions | Pentest was run by the platform team; admin accounts outside IdP may be out of its view | Pentest first | **Unchecked.** Scope not stated. |
| Mean time to patch × open backlog | Closed-ticket mean is biased by censored open tickets | Backlog first | **Fail as stated.** Open-inclusive recalculation required. |

Pairs marked unchecked block a tier-K coverage claim (R20). None are tier K in this report, so the block is not triggered, but the board document should not claim coverage.

## 7. What survives

- The headcount, the 53-service CMDB count, and the training completion rate are plausible as reported facts. None is verified here.
- The scanner, if calibrated, is a legitimate instrument for the 48 services it covered, for known signature classes.
- The MTTP metric has stated severity definitions, which is the right discipline. The calculation needs the open-ticket check.
- The MFA claim names a verifiable artifact. Once the account-level enumeration is done, it could become `[K]` for the scope it covers.

## 8. Recommended next steps

Ordered by criticality × impact / effort:

1. **Correct before the board reads it.** Replace "complete" with "48 of 53 production services (90.6%) scanned as of [date]," and remove "no critical vulnerabilities" or restate it for the 48. Effort: minutes. Impact: high.
2. **Name the five unscanned services and scan them.** Reconcile the CMDB, including decommissioned and duplicate entries. Effort: days. Impact: high.
3. **Enumerate admin-capable accounts from native role stores, not from the IdP,** and count those without enforced MFA. Include service, break-glass, and local accounts. Effort: days. Impact: high.
4. **Commission an independent pentest with a written scope** covering privilege-escalation paths and the unscanned services. Effort: weeks. Impact: high.
5. **Recompute MTTP with open high-severity tickets included as censored,** and report median alongside mean. Effort: hours. Impact: medium.
6. **Replace "phishing risk negligible" with a simulated click-rate metric** and a locked threshold, measured per population including privileged users. Effort: weeks. Impact: medium.
7. **Calibrate the scanner** against a seeded known-vulnerable service and record whether it detects it. Effort: hours. Impact: medium.
