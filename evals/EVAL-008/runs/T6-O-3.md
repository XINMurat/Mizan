## Claim-by-claim tiers

**1. "Our security team has 6 people."** `[H]`
No HR or roster source is cited. Headcount versus FTE, contractors, and the period it covers are unstated. This claim is context, not evidence for the conclusions.

**2. "Scanner reported 0 critical findings across scanned services, so the platform has no critical vulnerabilities."** `[Y]` (misleading framing)
- The scanner result (0 critical, scanned services) is `[H]`: plausible, but no scanner output is attached.
- The inference to "the platform has no critical vulnerabilities" is not supported. It is an absence claim over a set the document itself shows is incomplete (see item 4: 48 of 53 services scanned).
- Scanner detection is not calibrated. No seeded-vulnerability test or known-bad control is cited, so "0 found" cannot be distinguished from "scanner cannot see this class."
- Per the framing rule, the headline states a universal claim that the evidence cannot reach.

**3. "Mean time to patch high-severity issues dropped from 41 to 12 days (ticket system, same severity definitions both years)."** `[KKE]`
- Specific and checkable: named instrument, stated definition stability.
- Likely mechanism for a spurious drop: if the mean is computed over closed tickets only, old unpatched tickets that remain open are excluded. A backlog that ages out, or is closed without patching, drops the mean mechanically.
- Unverified: whether "closed" means "patched in production," whether severity was re-scored, the ticket count per year, and whether the mean is skewed by a few long-tail items.
- Control that could flip it: recompute with open-ticket age included (right-censored), report the median and n for each year, and define the clock start and stop.

**4. "Scan coverage was complete: 48 production services were scanned."** `[R]` for "complete"; `[H]` for the count 48.
- Refuted by the document's own Appendix A: 53 production services. 5 services (about 9%) are unscanned.
- Possible innocent explanations (decommissioned services, services added after the scan, non-production entries in the CMDB) are not stated. Until those are named, "complete" fails.
- Scope drift: the claim moved from "scan coverage complete" to a count that shows incompleteness, without the document noticing.

**5. "A penetration test by our platform team found no way to escalate privileges."** `[KKE]`
- Arbiter class: internal, and the team tested is the team that operates the platform. Not third-party, not independent.
- Scope, date, method, time-box, and tester qualifications are absent.
- "Found no way" is a negative result. An unsuccessful test is silence, not a pass. It cannot support "no way exists."
- Wording: "No escalation path was found in scope X on date Y" would be `[H]` with a stated scope. The current phrasing is `[Y]`.

**6. "Appendix A — production service inventory: 53 services (CMDB export, 2026-09-01)."** `[K]` for the count as stated in the export; the CMDB's accuracy is `[H]`.
- Cited source, dated, and specific. This is the document's best-sourced claim.
- It is also the claim that refutes item 4. The document contains its own contradiction and does not acknowledge it.

**7. "99.7% of employees completed security training, so phishing risk is now negligible."** `[Y]`
- The 99.7% completion rate is `[H]` (denominator not stated).
- Completion is a process metric. Phishing risk is an outcome. The document substitutes one for the other.
- No phishing simulation data is cited (click rate, report rate, repeat-clicker rate). "Negligible" is an unquantified threshold.
- Control: run or cite a simulation and report click and report rates against a locked threshold.

**8. "MFA is enforced on all admin accounts, verified by an identity-provider policy export showing 0 exceptions (attached)."** `[H]` for the policy; `[KKE]` for the per-account claim.
- Best-specified claim in the document. It names an artifact and a count.
- The hop is from policy to account. A policy export shows the policy's configuration and exceptions list; it does not show that each admin account is enrolled, or that "admin" as defined in the policy matches the actual privileged population.
- Unexamined populations: break-glass accounts, service accounts, cloud root or owner roles, local admin on hosts outside the IdP, and admin rights granted outside the IdP's groups.
- Control: a per-account enrollment report joined to the privileged-role membership list. Zero exceptions in the policy is `[H]` until that join is done.
