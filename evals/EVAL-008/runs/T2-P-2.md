## Claim-by-claim

**1. Headcount 28 → 41** `[H]`
Plausible and checkable against payroll/HRIS. The summary does not say whether this counts FTEs, heads, or contractors. Growth is +46%, which matters for the burn argument in the round.

**2a. Revenue $2.4M → $3.0M** `[H]`
Plausible. Not sourced. "Revenue" needs a definition: recognized revenue, ARR, or bookings. The three differ a lot for a company with annual plans, and annual billing moves cash and bookings ahead of recognized revenue.

**2b. "40% increase"** `[R]`
Refuted by its own inputs. $3.0M / $2.4M = 1.25, so growth is **25%**. The headline overstates growth by **15 percentage points**. A 40% figure would require $3.36M. The number may come from another period, another metric, or a typo, but as written it fails. Do not use 40% in the round. Record it as a refuted figure, not a corrected one.

**3. NPS 31 → 44** `[KKE]`
*What survives:* Sampling noise does not explain this. With n ≈ 1,100 per wave, the standard error of an NPS is about 2–3 points. The standard error of the difference is about 2.7 points under a typical distribution, and about 4.3 points in the worst case (when the variance is maximized). A 13-point change is roughly 3 to 5 standard errors. It is real movement in the measured sample.
*What is missing:* No response-rate comparison between waves. No check that the population surveyed is the same. Since March, 60% of new customers are on annual plans, so the wave-2 respondent pool may be a different mix of tenure and plan. Causal attribution to integrations or annual plans is not supported yet. The `[KKE]` tag stands until the segment cut is run (see next steps).

**4. "Integration users have 2× LTV, so pushing everyone onto integrations doubles LTV"** `[Y]` + `[S]`
The correlation may be real. The causal claim is not. Customers who adopt integrations are probably already more engaged, bigger, or more technical. The 2× gap mostly measures who chose to adopt, not what adoption does. If selection explains all of it, forcing integrations on everyone changes LTV by roughly zero. The "doubles" claim is an untested intervention, so it is `[S]` at best.
*Mechanism of error:* selection bias plus reverse causation (engaged users adopt integrations, and engagement drives LTV). No LTV figures are shown.

**5. Churn 3.1% → 2.2% after annual plans** `[Y]` + `[KKE]`
The drop may be produced by the metric itself. Annual-plan customers do not churn monthly. They churn at renewal, once a year. A blended "monthly churn" figure will fall as the annual share of the base grows, without any change in customer behavior.
*Quantitative impact:* The ratio 2.2 / 3.1 = 0.71 means a drop of about 29%. That is exactly what you would see if about 29% of the base were on annual plans with no in-month churn, and monthly-plan churn stayed at 3.1%. Since 60% of *new* customers since March are annual, a 29% share of the total base is plausible. So the observed drop could be fully mechanical.
*Also:* annual churn is deferred, not eliminated. It shows up at renewal, probably 12 months out. The March cutoff also overlaps the integrations push and onboarding tests, so the "after we introduced" timing does not isolate the plan change.
*Control not run:* churn split by plan type, and renewal-cohort retention for annual customers.

**6. Office move in June** `[S]`
No claimed effect, no metric, no test. It is not a growth claim. If the company wants it counted as a possible confound (for example, if the June office move coincided with a survey wave or a sales disruption), it needs a stated hypothesis. Otherwise remove it from the summary.

**7. Activation improved, p = 0.04, out of 20 onboarding metrics** `[R]`
Refuted under multiple-comparison correction.
*Mechanism:* 20 tests on a null effect give a 64% chance (1 − 0.95²⁰) that at least one falls below p = 0.05 by chance alone. The Bonferroni threshold for this family is 0.05 / 20 = 0.0025. Holm's first step uses the same cutoff. The p = 0.04 result fails by a factor of 16. It was never a preregistered test, so it cannot pass as a confirmatory result. Calling it "significant" is `[Y]`.
*Record:* I am not rewriting the threshold to make this pass. It is a near-miss on an exploratory search, and the record should say so.
*Missing:* effect size, confidence interval, sample size, and which metric it was.

**8. Gross margin 78%, from audited statements** `[H]`
The source is named, which is better than most. The figure is not shown, and I have not seen the statements. Check: (a) revenue in the margin calculation matches the $3.0M used above; (b) COGS includes hosting, support, and payment fees, consistent with prior periods; (c) whether FY2026 is actually closed and audited, since a round in progress may be using a draft.
