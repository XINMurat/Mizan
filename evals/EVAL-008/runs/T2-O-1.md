## Cross-claim findings (conjunctions)

1. **Headcount vs. revenue.** Revenue per head fell from $85.7k ($2.4M/28) to $73.2k ($3.0M/41), a 15% drop. Growth so far is in cost, not efficiency. This is `[K]` arithmetic from the summary's own figures, and it is absent from the summary.
2. **Annual billing × churn metric.** The churn metric's meaning changed when annual plans launched. Comparing 3.1% to 2.2% as the same quantity is invalid until the metric is split by plan type.
3. **Annual billing × revenue.** If annual invoices are booked as revenue at billing, then 60% annual adoption inflates revenue growth in exactly the period that matters. Ask for the recognition method. This pair is `unchecked`.
4. **Integrations × NPS.** If integration adopters are over-represented in the survey wave, the NPS rise could be a population effect. Unchecked.

## The missing card

This format shows only favorable summary metrics. It cannot show:

- **Burn and runway.** Headcount +46% with revenue +25% implies rising burn. For a raise, this matters more than any metric listed.
- **Unit economics.** CAC, payback period, and the cost of building and promoting integrations.
- **Cohort retention.** Monthly churn blended across plan types hides the annual cohort's behavior.
- **The 19 onboarding metrics not reported.** Their results are the denominator for claim 7.
- **Cash vs. revenue.** Annual upfront payments improve cash position even when revenue growth is weaker.

## Next steps (criticality × impact / effort)

1. **Correct the revenue growth figure before anything goes to investors.** Effort: minutes. Impact: a 15-point overstatement in the headline growth number. The raise cannot proceed on 40%.
2. **Remove or rewrite the activation "significance" claim.** Recommendation: report it as an exploratory finding, state the 20-test family, and run a preregistered follow-up. Cost if wrong: investors who run the same arithmetic will discount the whole deck.
3. **Reframe the LTV claim** as a correlation, and run a randomized integration nudge before forecasting a doubling. Recommendation: a 10–20% holdout on the next onboarding flow. Cost if wrong: a forecast built on selection bias.
4. **Split churn by plan type and report the annual share of the base.** Effort: one query. Impact: decides whether the churn story survives at all.
5. **Attach the audited statements and confirm the COGS definition.** Recommendation: send the auditor's report with the deck. Cost if wrong: a margin claim that fails diligence.
6. **Pull NPS response rates and cohort composition** for both waves.
7. **Add burn, runway, and revenue per head** to the summary.
