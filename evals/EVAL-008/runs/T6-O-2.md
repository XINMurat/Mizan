I had no access to the ticket system, the scanner output, the CMDB export, the IdP policy export, or the LMS. Everything below is judged from the text and its internal consistency.

## Findings by mechanism

**Finding 1: Coverage claim refuted by the document's own numbers (claim 4 vs. 6).**
- Mechanism: The summary reports the scanned subset as if it were the population. "Complete" describes the scan scope, not the inventory.
- Impact: 5 of 53 services (9.4%) have no scan evidence. Any critical in those 5 would not appear in the headline.
- Alternative explanation to rule out: The CMDB is stale and 5 services were decommissioned. If so, claim 6 is wrong, and the 53 figure should be corrected. Either way, the summary cannot say "complete" until one of these is shown.
- Further caveat: The CMDB export is a lower bound on the real estate. Services absent from the CMDB (shadow or unregistered) are invisible to both the scanner denominator and the audit.

**Finding 2: "No critical vulnerabilities" is a scanner statement, presented as a platform statement (claims 2a, 2b).**
- Missing control: no calibration of the scanner against a seeded known-vulnerable target. Without a planted-positive test, "0 criticals" cannot distinguish "none exist" from "the scanner did not detect them." This is the same instrument-calibration gap that R24 targets.
- Scope: scanners detect known package CVEs well. They detect misconfigurations, authorization flaws, and logic flaws poorly. The summary's wording, "no critical vulnerabilities," covers all of these. The scanner does not.
- Silence on highs: The headline reports criticals only. Claim 3 tracks high-severity patching, so highs exist in the process. The count of open highs is absent from the summary.

**Finding 3: The patch-time improvement may be a population change (claim 3).**
- Mean time to patch is computed over closed tickets. If the open backlog grew, or old long-running tickets were closed, or new tickets were opened late, the mean moves without any faster patching.
- Required checks (not run):
  1. Count of open high-severity tickets at both dates, with ages.
  2. Median alongside mean. The mean is sensitive to a few very old tickets.
  3. Whether the clock starts at scanner detection or ticket creation, and whether that definition is the same in both years.
  4. Whether the 5 unscanned services produce any tickets. They produce none, so they are absent from the denominator.
  5. Whether scanner coverage in the prior year was higher, lower, or equal. If coverage changed, the populations differ.
- Current tier stays `[KKE]`. The result may be true; the control has not run.

**Finding 4: The penetration test cannot be used as independent assurance (claim 5).**
- Arbiter class: `author`. The platform team tested the platform it built. Under the rules, this claim carries a permanent `[KKE]` and cannot be promoted to `[K]` regardless of effort.
- Scope unknown: Which services, which escalation paths, which accounts, which time box? A test that did not include the 5 unscanned services cannot speak to them.
- A "no finding" result is only informative if the test could have found something. No evidence that it could (for example, a known seeded path) is given.

**Finding 5: A policy export is not account-level enforcement (claim 8).**
- Policy ≠ enforcement. Typical gaps: break-glass accounts, service accounts with admin rights, legacy protocols that skip MFA, and admin rights granted outside the IdP (local admin, cloud IAM roles, direct database roles).
- The "0 exceptions" figure counts exceptions configured in the policy. It does not count admin accounts that were never in the IdP.
- Required checks (not run): (a) reconcile the admin population from all sources (IdP groups, cloud IAM, local, service accounts) against the IdP list; (b) sign-in logs showing an MFA claim for each admin role over a stated window; (c) enumeration of exempted groups and their members.

**Finding 6: Activity is reported as risk reduction (claim 7).**
- Training completion is an input. Phishing risk is an outcome. The summary reports the input and asserts the outcome. The outcome measure exists in most programs (simulation click and report rates, incident counts) and is absent here.

## The missing card (what this format cannot show)

The summary is built entirely from favorable headline metrics. It cannot show:
- Which 5 services were not scanned, and why.
- How many high-severity findings are open, and how old they are.
- Any finding that was not patched, accepted, or deferred.
- Phishing simulation results, or any phishing incident.
- Admin accounts outside the IdP, or service accounts with elevated rights.
- The scope and independence of the penetration test.
- The scanner's detection rate on known-vulnerable targets.

The structure shows a clean set of metrics because it has no slot for failures. That is a format property, not necessarily an intent.

Unscanned services create no tickets, so their findings never enter the metric. | Asymmetric: the metric is fine only if the scanned population is the population | **Fails.** The metric is computed over a subset and presented as the whole. |

## Recommendations (criticality × impact / effort, highest first)

1. **Correct claims 2b and 4 before the board sees them.** Low effort, highest impact.
   Replace with: "Zero critical findings in the 48 of 53 production services scanned (90.6%). Five services were not scanned; remediation is in progress."
   Replace "platform has no critical vulnerabilities" with a sentence that names the scope.
2. **Identify the 5 unscanned services.** Either scan them or document why each is excluded. If they are decommissioned, correct the CMDB.
3. **Reconcile the admin population (claim 8).** Pull admin holders from all sources and compare to the IdP. Attach sign-in evidence, not only the policy export.
4. **Recompute MTTP with open tickets and medians (claim 3).** Report open high-severity counts and ages alongside the closed-ticket mean.
5. **Commission an independent penetration test (claim 5)** with a stated scope that includes the 5 unscanned services.
6. **Replace "phishing risk negligible" with an outcome metric (claim 7).** Use simulation click and report rates, or drop the sentence.
7. **Calibrate the scanner.** Seed a known-vulnerable target and confirm detection before citing "0 criticals" again.
