# Mizan Audit Report: FY2026 Growth Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective. The claims were written before this review, and I selected the examples from the document. I did not write any probe scenarios here, so none were written after a gap was found. The audit also has no access to source data, so every check below is internal consistency, arithmetic, or reasoning about confounds, not verification against records.

**Coverage:** 8 claims. 4 are checkable by internal arithmetic or reasoning (claims 2, 3, 5, 7). 0 of 8 are verified against source data (HR, billing, survey exports, financial statements, experiment logs). Claim 8 names an audited source, which would make it checkable by the investor directly.

## Claim-by-claim tiering

| # | Claim (quoted) | Tier | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Headcount grew from 28 to 41." | `[H]` | Plausible, unverified. Needs a point-in-time definition (end of period or average?) and whether contractors are included. |
| 2 | "Revenue grew from $2.4M to $3.0M... a 40% increase." | `[R]` on the 40% | ($3.0M − $2.4M) / $2.4M = **25%**, not 40%. The stated percentage is refuted by its own inputs. Overstated by 15 points, or 60% relative to the true figure. The dollar figures stay `[H]` until matched to a source. |
| 3 | "NPS rose from 31 to 44 (same vendor, same question; n = 1,120 and 1,085)." | `[KKE]` | A 13-point change is large relative to sampling noise. Response rate, survey timing, and the mix of new annual-plan customers versus tenured customers are all unreported. |
| 4 | "Integration users have twice the LTV... pushing every customer onto integrations will double LTV." | Association: `[KKE]`. Causal framing: `[Y]`. Forecast: `[S]` | The 2× gap is an observational comparison. Customers who adopt integrations are probably more engaged, larger, or longer-tenured already. Reverse causation (customers who stay longer adopt more) is equally plausible. No pre-adoption comparison, matched control, or LTV definition is given. The "will double" forecast has no test designed. |
| 5 | "Monthly churn fell from 3.1% to 2.2% after annual plans... 60% of new customers chose annual." | `[KKE]` | The drop exists in the numbers. A mechanical explanation has not been excluded (see finding B). |

## Findings

**A. The revenue growth figure is wrong (highest priority).** The document states 40% growth. Its own inputs produce 25%. In a fundraising document, this is the claim an investor will check first, and the error is visible without any data access. Possible explanations include a different period, ARR or bookings rather than recognized revenue, or a transcription error. The audit cannot tell which. The mechanism matters for the fix: if the 40% refers to a different metric, the summary has mixed two metrics and both need labels.

**B. The churn drop may be a metric artifact.** Annual-plan customers cannot churn on a monthly cycle during their term, so they fall out of the monthly churn numerator. Whether they also fall out of the denominator depends on how churn is defined. If annual customers are excluded from the base, the blended rate falls mechanically.
- Quantified: if monthly-plan churn were unchanged at 3.1%, a blended rate of 2.2% requires that roughly **29%** of the measured base be on annual plans. The document reports 60% of *new* customers since March, not the share of the base, so this is plausible but unverified.
- This matters beyond churn. If LTV is computed as ARPU ÷ monthly churn, the same artifact inflates LTV by 3.1/2.2 ≈ **41%** with zero change in retention.
- Annual plans may also carry discounts, which lower ARPU. That would show up in revenue and margin, not in churn.

**C. The LTV comparison may be the same selection problem.** Integration adoption and annual-plan choice may correlate. If so, the 2× LTV figure partly reflects the annual-plan effect in finding B. The doubling forecast extrapolates from adopters to all customers, which assumes the effect is causal and holds for marginal customers. Neither assumption is tested.

**D. The activation result fails under multiple comparisons.** The document reports the one favorable metric from 20 tested, with no pre-registered primary metric, no effect size, and no correction. This is a search result. It can be promoted only by a pre-registered confirmatory test on fresh cohorts.

**E. The NPS change is probably real in the survey, but the population may have changed.** A 13-point move from the same vendor and question is hard to explain as noise. The open question is whether the respondents are comparable. New annual-plan customers in their first months may answer differently from tenured customers, and the sample mix has likely shifted since March.

## The missing card

This summary is a format of wins. It cannot show:
- **Cash and runway**: burn, cash balance, and months of runway. For a round, these are the central numbers, and none appear.
- **Cost of growth**: CAC, payback, and the cost of the annual-plan discount. The 41% headcount growth has no revenue-per-head or burn context.
- **Net revenue retention and ARR**: the revenue line is ambiguous (finding A), and the churn story is incomplete without expansion and contraction.
- **Failures and reversals**: no declined experiments, no segments that got worse, no customers lost to the annual-plan change.
- **Definitions**: LTV method, churn denominator, NPS response rates, and the audit period.

Sketch from available evidence: the only numbers that describe the cost side are the headcount change and the office move, both of which increase burn, and neither is quantified.

## Domain probe (step 7) and conjunctions (step 8)

**Domain probe:** `probes.domain.supplied_by: none` (self-report). R19 blocks any tier-K coverage claim over this. I did not write scenarios. The questions to ask the founders, in the eight-class seed from checklist item 12:
- *Two at once:* a customer on an annual plan who also adopts an integration. Which cohort does the LTV figure count them in?
- *End of life:* what happens at the first annual renewal, and is that before or after the period reported?
- *Crossing a boundary:* do annual customers see different churn or NPS treatment?
- *Zero/one/many:* how many customers are on annual plans right now, as a share of the base?
- *Undo and exit:* can an annual customer switch to monthly, and how is that counted?

**Conjunction pairs (`probes.conjunction.pairs`):**

| Feature | Guarantee it can reach | Order | Outcome |

## Credit where it is due

- The headcount change is internally consistent and needs only a definition.
- The NPS sample sizes are large enough that the sampling error does not explain the change. The open question is composition.
- The gross margin claim names an audited source. That is the right instinct, and it is checkable.
- The office move is stated as a fact and makes no causal claim.

## Next steps (criticality × impact / effort)

1. **Fix the revenue growth figure before any investor sees the document.** Highest criticality, lowest effort. Correct it to 25%, or relabel the 40% with its metric and period.
   *Recommendation:* one line in the summary with the metric name. *Cost if wrong:* if 40% is a valid figure for a different metric, the summary still has to say which metric.
2. **Recompute churn on monthly-plan customers only, and report annual share of the base.** High impact because it feeds LTV, and the cost is one query.
   *Recommendation:* show churn by plan type alongside the blended figure. *Cost if wrong:* if the blended figure holds on the monthly base, the claim gets stronger, and the round can use it.
3. **Restate LTV with a stated method and a pre-adoption comparison for integrations.** Do this before saying "double."
   *Recommendation:* drop "will double" from the document until a staggered or randomized rollout exists. *Cost if wrong:* a weaker growth story, but no false promise in diligence.
4. **Retract "significantly" for activation or pre-register a confirmatory test.** Cheap to do honestly.
   *Recommendation:* report it as exploratory, with the 20-metric count stated. *Cost if wrong:* the result may be real, and the confirmation takes one cohort.
5. **Break NPS out by tenure and plan type, and report response rates.** Lower priority, because the change is probably real.

**Re-audit trigger:** before the data room opens, and again at the next fiscal close. The next close is when the first annual renewals begin to show, and it is when the churn question can be settled with data rather than arithmetic.
