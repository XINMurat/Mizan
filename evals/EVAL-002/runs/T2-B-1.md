# Mizan Audit Report: "Growth summary, FY2026"

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective).
**HARKing status:** The document was written after the outcomes were known, and it chose its own metrics. This audit is retrospective too: I picked the checks after reading the claims.
**Coverage:** I atomized 8 numbered items into 11 claims. I checked 4 arithmetically or logically from the document alone (the revenue %, the churn mechanism, the multiple comparisons, the LTV inference). None of the underlying data (financials, cohort tables, survey exports) was available, so every factual claim is capped at `[H]` until a source is supplied.
**Constraint:** This was a single-pass written audit with no access to company data. The domain probe (step 7) was not supplied by a domain owner, so the coverage statement cannot reach `[K]`.

## 1. Claim-by-claim tiers

| # | Claim (quoted/atomized) | Tier | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Headcount grew from 28 to 41" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible | This is easy to verify through payroll, but no source was given. It is also a **cost**, not growth. See the "missing card" section. |
| 2a | "Revenue grew from $2.4M to $3.0M" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible | Unsourced. It should reconcile with the audited statements cited in #8. |
| 2b | "…a 40% increase" | `[R]` Reddedildi / Refuted | (3.0 − 2.4) / 2.4 = **25%**, not 40%. The stated figure overstates growth by 15 percentage points, which makes it 1.6× the true growth. No base in the document produces 40%. Even the reverse calculation, 0.6/3.0, gives 20%. This is a hard arithmetic error in the headline growth number. |
| 3 | "NPS rose from 31 to 44 (same vendor, same question; n=1,120 / 1,085)" | `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing | Credit where due: this is the best-controlled claim in the document (same instrument, disclosed n). With n≈1,100 the standard error of NPS is about ±2.5–3 points per wave, so a 13-point move is far outside sampling noise. What is still missing is the **sampling frame**. Who was surveyed? If churned or unhappy customers left the base (see #5), or if the survey went only to active or annual customers, NPS rises through survivorship without any customer changing their opinion. Response rate and frame for both waves are needed. |
| 4a | "Integration users have 2× the LTV of non-users" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible | Unsourced, and LTV is usually a modeled figure. The formula and cohort window need to be stated. |
| 4b | "…so pushing every customer onto integrations will double LTV" | `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading | This is a correlation presented as a causal effect. Selection is the obvious rival: larger, more committed, more technical customers both adopt integrations and stay longer. Forcing adoption on customers who didn't self-select won't transfer their LTV. There is also a structural ceiling: customers who already use integrations can't be "doubled" again. The causal claim needs an experiment or a quasi-experiment, for example randomized integration onboarding, or within-customer before/after compared against matched non-adopters. |
| 5a | "Monthly churn fell from 3.1% to 2.2% after annual plans in March" | `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading | This is a **mechanical artifact**, not evidence of retention. Customers on annual plans billed once a year *cannot* churn monthly until their renewal date. Since 60% of new customers since March chose annual, a large share of the base is structurally unable to show up as churn for up to 12 months. Most of the first renewal cohort (March 2027) hasn't come due yet. The drop is at least partly deferral, not reduction. |
| 5b | "60% of new customers chose annual" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible | Unsourced, and it may be true. It is the fact that undermines 5a. |
| 6 | "We moved to a new office in June" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible | Probably true, but irrelevant as growth evidence. It is a cost item presented as a signal. |
| 7 | "Tested 20 onboarding metrics; activation improved significantly (p = 0.04)" | `[R]` Reddedildi / Refuted, as a claim of significance | This is a textbook multiple-comparisons problem. At α = 0.05 over 20 tests, the expected number of false positives is 1.0, and P(≥1 false positive) = 1 − 0.95²⁰ ≈ **64%**, assuming the tests are independent. One hit at p = 0.04 is what pure noise looks like. With a Bonferroni correction the threshold is 0.0025, and 0.04 fails it. Under Holm or BH it also fails, unless other metrics had smaller p-values, which aren't disclosed. The activation change itself stays `[S]` until it replicates on a preregistered single metric. |
| 8 | "Gross margin 78%, from audited financial statements" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible; eligible for `[K]` | This has the strongest source type of any claim here, because an audit is a `third_party` arbiter. It moves to `[K]` once the audited statements are produced, the cost of revenue definition is checked (e.g., hosting, support, and onboarding staff included or not), and revenue reconciles with #2a. |

## 2. Conjunction checks (the pairs)

- **#1 × #2, revenue per employee:** $2.4M/28 = $85.7k, and $3.0M/41 = $73.2k. That is a **15% decline** in revenue per head. Headcount grew 46% while revenue grew 25%. The document puts these side by side as two growth signals, but together they show falling efficiency.
- **#5 × #3, churn mechanics × NPS:** if the pre-March base lost dissatisfied customers while new customers are locked into annual plans, the NPS population changed composition. The two "improvements" may share a single cause: who is left in the base.
- **#5 × #2, annual prepay × revenue:** cash billed upfront for annual plans can inflate "revenue" if bookings or billings are reported instead of recognized revenue. Confirm that #2a is GAAP-recognized revenue (it should be, if it ties to #8's audited statements).
- **#4 × #5:** are integration users disproportionately on annual plans? If so, the LTV gap in #4 is partly a billing-term artifact too.

## 3. Hunting for counter-examples

The pattern claim is "everything improved." The document contains its own counter-examples: revenue per employee fell, and 19 of 20 onboarding metrics were apparently not significant. The format discloses neither. I found no other counter-examples, because no data beyond the document was available.

## 4. The missing card

A "growth summary" format structurally omits the following:
- **Burn and runway:** a 13-person hiring wave plus an office move is a large increase in cost. Neither is quantified.
- **The 19 null onboarding tests.**
- **Cohort retention**, as opposed to blended monthly churn.
- **CAC and payback period.**
- **Net revenue retention** and expansion revenue.
- **Absolute counts behind the percentages**, for example how many customers the 2.2% churn applies to.

## 5. Mechanism vs. motive

The errors fit **format selection pressure** (a fundraising summary rewards favorable framing) and common analytical mistakes (misreading billing terms, confusing correlation with causation, testing many metrics at once). Intent is not evidenced. The 40% figure could be a calculation slip, for example a different base or a mix-up with bookings. Ask before assuming it was deliberate.

## 6. Next steps (ordered by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Correct the revenue growth figure to 25%**, or produce the calculation that gives 40%. This takes almost no effort and has a large credibility impact. Investors will catch it.
2. **Restate churn on a like-for-like basis.** Report monthly-plan churn only, for both periods. Report annual-plan logo retention separately once renewals come due. Do not claim a churn improvement until the March 2027 renewal cohort resolves.
3. **Drop "p = 0.04" as a significance claim.** Report all 20 metrics with a correction, or preregister activation as the single primary metric and re-test it.
4. **Reframe #4 as correlational**, or run a randomized integration-onboarding test with a preregistered LTV proxy, such as 6-month retention. Suggested kill condition: an adoption lift that produces less than 10% retention lift.
5. **Produce the audited statements** to promote #8 (and #2a) to `[K]`.
6. **Disclose the NPS sampling frame and response rates** for both waves to resolve the `[KKE]` on #3.
7. **Add burn, runway, CAC and revenue per employee**, which is the missing card.

## 7. Recommendation to the recipient (investor)

**Recommendation:** Treat this summary as unverified. Request items 1–3 and 5 before diligence proceeds. **Why:** 4 of 11 atomized claims are misleading or refuted, including both of the headline performance metrics (growth rate and churn). **Cost if wrong:** you delay a deal whose fundamentals may be fine, since recognized revenue did grow 25% and the NPS signal is reasonably controlled. That cost is small compared with pricing a round on 40% growth and falling churn that don't hold.
