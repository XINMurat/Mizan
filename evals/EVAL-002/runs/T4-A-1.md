# Postmortem review — checkout outage, 2026-09-18

**Summary:** Several of the factual records hold up. The two biggest conclusions do not: the root cause (item 4) and "no customer data affected" (item 5). Item 8 should be removed. Item 2 has an arithmetic error. Fix these before publishing.

## Claim-by-claim

| # | Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paged at 14:05 | **Supported (record)**, assuming it comes from the paging system's log. Cite the source. | Plain timestamp. |
| 2 | 47 minutes, 14:03–14:52 | **Internally inconsistent.** | 14:03 to 14:52 is **49** minutes. Either the duration or one of the timestamps is wrong. Also say how "start" and "end" were defined (first error? error rate over a threshold?). |
| 3 | Peak error rate 38% (LB logs) | **Supported**, and it names a source. | Say whether 38% is the peak per minute or over some other window. |

Confounded.** | Item 6 records a DNS provider incident that was resolved at **14:49**, one minute *before* the rollback. Recovery follows both events, so the timing cannot tell them apart. Also check whether 8.2 was deployed around 14:03. If it went out much earlier, that weakens it as the cause. Downgrade to "suspected contributing cause" until you have discriminating evidence, such as error types (DNS resolution failures vs. cache or backend errors), per-host timing, or a replay of 8.2. |

## Required changes before publishing
1. Fix the duration/timestamp mismatch in item 2.
2. Rewrite item 4 as an open question with two candidate causes (release 8.2 vs. the upstream DNS incident). List the evidence that would separate them and assign someone to gather it.
3. Narrow item 5 to what was actually checked, or widen the check to every service that writes orders.
4. Validate the alert in item 7 on data outside this incident, and report its false-positive rate.
5. Delete item 8.

**Conclusions that are supported:** 1, 3, 6 and 9 (records of what happened), with sources to be cited. **Not supported as written:** 2 (arithmetic), 4, 5, 7 and 8.
