# Mizan Audit — Postmortem: checkout outage, 2026-09-18

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **HARKing status:** The postmortem was written after the outcome was known, and so was this audit. I had no logs, deploy records, pager history or DNS provider data. The only evidence was the document itself. **Coverage:** 9 of 9 claims were tiered. Only internal consistency (arithmetic, timeline ordering) could actually be checked. The rest are tiered by how strong the evidence the document cites is.

**Verdict:** Do not publish as written. The headline conclusion (root cause = release 8.2) has an unexamined confound in the document's own item 6. The duration figure contains an arithmetic error. The data-safety claim and the prevention claim go further than their evidence.

## Missing card (what this format cannot show)
- **Customer impact:** failed checkouts, lost revenue, retries or duplicate orders. The postmortem only gives an error percentage.
- **Detection gap:** 14:03 to 14:05 was fast. Time to mitigation was 47 minutes. What happened between 14:05 and 14:50, and why did rollback take ~45 minutes?
- **Rejected hypotheses:** Was DNS considered during the incident and ruled out? If so, on what evidence? Negative results belong on record.
- **Action items with owners and dates.**

## Conjunction check
- Release 8.2 × DNS incident: the two may have *interacted*, e.g. the new eviction policy caused more cache misses, which caused more upstream lookups that hit the degraded DNS. In that case both are contributing causes and neither alone is "the root cause". This cannot be settled from the document. It is recorded as unchecked.

## Next steps (criticality × impact/effort)
1. **Get the DNS incident start time** from the status page (cheap, decisive). If it started around 14:03, root cause 4b drops to "undetermined, two candidates".
2. **Fix the 47/49-minute error** (trivial).
3. **Symmetric control for 8.2:** redeploy 8.2 to staging or canary with DNS healthy, and check the error rate. It is preregistered to refute 8.2 if the error rate stays <1% at comparable load.
4. **Widen the data check** to every service that writes order or payment state, or narrow the claim's wording.
5. Reframe item 7 honestly and add a false-positive replay.
6. Replace item 8 with owned action items.

## Registry seed (surviving hypotheses)
- **H-1 (bug):** Mechanism: the 8.2 cache eviction change caused the checkout errors. **Rivals:** DNS provider incident; interaction of the two. **Refutation:** 8.2 on canary with healthy DNS shows error rate <1% over 30 min at ≥50% production load. **Arbiter:** instrument (LB error rate). **Tier:** `[KKE]`.
- **H-2:** No customer data was corrupted. **Refutation:** any partial or orphaned order in *any* order- or payment-writing service in 14:03–14:52. **Tier:** `[H]` until the full service list is checked.

**Trigger to re-audit:** before internal publication, and again once steps 1 and 3 have results.

**What survives now:** items 1, 3, 4a, 6 and 9 as plausible factual records (they need sources attached), plus the alert's existence (7). The root cause, data-safety and "never again" conclusions are **not supported** in their current form.
