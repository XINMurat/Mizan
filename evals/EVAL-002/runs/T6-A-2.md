# Evaluation: Annual security assessment summary

**Bottom line:** The summary's own data contradicts two of its conclusions. Claim 4 and Claim 6 conflict, which also undercuts Claim 2. Claims 5 and 7 go further than their evidence. Claims 3 and 8 are reasonably supported, and Claim 1 is a plain fact.

## Claim by claim

| # | Claim | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Team has 6 people | Accepted (low stakes) | Simple fact. Easy to check, and no conclusion depends on it. |
| 2 | 0 critical findings, so there are no critical vulnerabilities | **Not supported** | The absence of findings is not proof that no vulnerabilities exist. The claim only covers "the services it scanned", which is 48 of 53 (see 4 and 6). Scanners also miss whole classes of flaws, such as logic bugs, authentication and authorization flaws, and misconfigurations outside the scanner's scope. At most, the evidence shows the scanner found no known critical issues in 48 services. |
| 3 | Mean time to patch high-severity issues fell from 41 to 12 days | Mostly supported | It uses the same data source and the same severity definitions in both years, which is good. Still to check: whether the number of high-severity issues changed a lot, whether any are still open (a mean over closed tickets only drops the slow ones), and whether "patched" means the same thing both years. The median and the count of open items should be reported too. |
| 4 | Scan coverage was complete: 48 services scanned | **Contradicted** | Appendix A (Claim 6) lists 53 production services. 48 of 53 is about 91% coverage. Five production services were not scanned at all. |
| 5 | Pen test by the platform team found no privilege escalation | Weakly supported | The test was not independent. The platform team is testing its own systems. Scope, method, duration and tester qualifications are not stated. "Found no way" reflects only this test's effort, not a guarantee. An independent third-party test is needed before this can be offered as assurance. |
| 6 | Inventory: 53 services (CMDB, 2026-09-01) | Accepted as data | This is the reference point that exposes the gap in Claim 4. If the scan ran before 2026-09-01, some of the gap could come from new services. The report does not say so, and those services would still be unscanned. |
| 7 | 99.7% training completion, so phishing risk is negligible | **Not supported** | Completing training measures attendance, not behaviour. Evidence of reduced risk would be phishing-simulation click and report rates, real incident trends, or MFA and email-filtering controls. "Negligible" is not justified: one click can be enough. |
| 8 | MFA enforced on all admin accounts, with an IdP export showing 0 exceptions | Supported, with a scope caveat | The attached policy export is strong direct evidence. Check that "all admin accounts" includes admin accounts outside the IdP: local, break-glass, cloud-root and service accounts. Also check whether the MFA methods are phishing-resistant. |

## What the board should take away

- **Headline correction:** coverage is 48 of 53 services, not complete. Five production services have no scan data. The "no critical vulnerabilities" conclusion does not cover them. Even for the 48 scanned services, the evidence supports only "no critical findings from the scanner".
- **Real evidence of progress:** the patch-time improvement (Claim 3) and MFA enforcement (Claim 8) are the best-supported claims.
- **Overstated assurance:** the privilege-escalation claim rests on a self-assessment (Claim 5). The phishing claim rests on training completion rates (Claim 7).

## Recommended actions before the board relies on this
1. Identify the 5 unscanned services, scan them, and explain why they were left out.
2. Change Claim 2 to say "0 critical scanner findings across 48 of 53 services".
3. Commission an independent penetration test with a defined scope.
4. Report phishing-simulation metrics in place of, or alongside, training completion.
5. Add the median patch time, the ticket counts and the open high-severity items to Claim 3.
6. Confirm that the MFA scope includes admin accounts outside the IdP.
