# Review: Checkout outage postmortem, 2026-09-18

**Verdict:** Not ready to publish. The main conclusion, the root cause in claim 4, is not established, and the postmortem's own claim 6 is a competing explanation it never deals with. Claims 2, 5, 7 and 8 have specific problems. Only claims 1, 3 and 9 are adequately supported, and they are the simple factual ones.

## Claim by claim

**1. Paged at 14:05: supported (pending citation).** This is a simple fact that the paging system's log can confirm. Cite that log entry.

**2. "47 minutes, from 14:03 to 14:52": internally inconsistent.** 14:03 to 14:52 is **49 minutes**, not 47. Either the duration or one of the timestamps is wrong. The postmortem also doesn't say how the start and end were defined (the first error above baseline? a threshold?) or where they come from. Fix the number and state the definition and the source.

**3. Error rate peaked at 38% (load balancer logs): supported.** It names a source and a measurement. Minor points: give the denominator and the time window of the peak, and say whether LB-level errors capture all checkout failures. For example, client-side failures or requests that never reached the load balancer would not show up there.

**4. Root cause is the cache eviction change in 8.2: NOT supported. This is the central problem.**
- The only evidence is timing: errors stopped "shortly after" the 14:50 rollback. That is correlation, and it is weak here because of claim 6.
- **Confound:** the DNS provider's incident was resolved at **14:49**, one minute *before* the rollback and three minutes before the outage ended. The DNS recovery explains the timeline at least as well as the rollback does. Recovery around 14:49–14:52 fits either cause, or both.
- Missing evidence that would separate the two causes:
  - When did 8.2 deploy? Did the errors start at deploy time or at the DNS incident's start?
  - What do the errors look like? Cache misses and timeouts to the backing store point to 8.2. Resolution failures point to DNS.
  - Is there a mechanism explaining how the eviction change produces checkout errors?
  - Can the failure be reproduced by re-enabling 8.2 in staging or on a canary?
  - What were error rates in services that don't use the cache but do depend on DNS?
- "Shortly after" is vague. Give the timestamp.
- Supported wording: "Errors subsided between 14:49 and 14:52, coinciding with both the 8.2 rollback (14:50) and the upstream DNS provider's recovery (14:49); the cause is not yet determined."

**5. No customer data affected: overclaims.** The team checked only "the three services we suspected." That shows those three are clean. It does not show that *no* data was affected. The claim also rests on the root cause, which is unproven: if DNS was the cause, the failure paths may differ from the ones they suspected. It also doesn't say *how* the services were checked (a reconciliation query? who ran it? what counted as "partial"?). Either do a full reconciliation of orders and payments for 14:03–14:52 or restate it as: "No partial orders found in services A, B and C; other services not checked."

**6. DNS provider incident resolved at 14:49: supported as a fact, ignored as evidence.** This is a third-party record and credible as a timestamp. However, the postmortem lists it without any analysis, even though it directly undermines claim 4. Add the DNS incident's start time and compare it with the 14:03 onset.

**7. New alert would fire at 14:06: supported as a replay, but it shows no benefit.** The replay is a reasonable check. However, the engineer was already paged at **14:05**, so the new alert would have fired a minute *later* than existing paging. It adds no detection speed for this incident. The postmortem also doesn't check the alert's false-positive rate: has 5% for 2 minutes been crossed on normal days? Replay it against a few weeks of normal traffic. If the alert is kept, justify it on another basis, such as coverage of a gap. It is not an improvement for this outage.

**8. "This will not happen again": unsupported. Remove it.** No evidence can support this. The root cause is uncertain, and the only action item is an alert that detects the problem later than current paging. Replace it with concrete action items that have owners, for example:
- Settle the root cause.
- Canary or gate cache changes.
- Monitor DNS dependencies or add resolver fallback.
- Run the data reconciliation.

**9. Retrospective held on Friday: supported but uninformative.** Give the date and list the attendees and outcomes. Did the retro weigh the DNS explanation?
