# Mizan Audit Report — Annual Security Assessment (board summary)

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective) with Mode 7 (security-probe) evidence rules applied to security claims.
**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The document's claims were written after outcomes were known; this audit is also retrospective and chose its checks after reading the document. No thresholds were preregistered by the authors.
**Coverage:** 8 of 8 claims atomized and tiered. Checkable against evidence inside the document: 4 (claims 2, 4, 6 cross-checked against each other; 8 against its cited export). Not independently verifiable from here: ticket-system data (3), headcount (1), pentest report (5), training records and phishing data (7). The attachments (CMDB export, IdP policy export, scanner output) were **not available to me**; tiers that depend on them are conditional on them saying what the summary says.
**Constraint note:** Only the summary text was available. No scanner configs, pentest scope, or ticket exports were opened.

## Headline

The board conclusions are **not supported as written**. Two of the load-bearing conclusions ("no critical vulnerabilities", "phishing risk negligible") overreach their evidence, and the document **contradicts itself** on scan coverage: it claims complete coverage of 48 services while its own Appendix A lists 53 production services. At least 5 services (≈9.4%) were never scanned, so "0 critical findings" says nothing about them. The operational metrics (patch time, MFA) are the strongest parts and survive, with caveats.

## Conjunction check (step 8)

- **Claim 2 × claim 4 × claim 6:** individually each looks fine; together they break the headline. "0 critical" (2a) + "complete coverage" (4) → "no critical vulnerabilities" (2b). Remove the false coverage claim and the headline has no support over the 5 unscanned services — which, being outside the normal pipeline, are *more* likely than average to be unmaintained.
- **Claim 5 × claim 8:** "no privilege escalation" leans on MFA for admins, but MFA protects login, not escalation paths from a non-admin foothold. The two do not compound into assurance.
- **Claim 3 × claim 4:** MTTP is measured only on found issues; unscanned services generate no tickets, so the metric is silent on them.

## Counter-example search

- "Complete coverage": counter-example found in the document itself (53 vs 48).
- "No critical vulnerabilities" / "no privilege escalation": no counter-example available in the text, but the evidence type (scanner silence, in-house pentest) cannot produce one reliably — reported as absence of search capability, not absence of vulnerabilities.

## Missing card (what this format cannot show)

The summary reports only green metrics. It structurally omits: incidents and near-misses in the year; findings that are open, risk-accepted, or past SLA; what was out of scope (the 5 services; pentest exclusions); third-party/supplier risk; detection and response capability (MTTD/MTTR for incidents). A board summary with no open items should itself be read as a selection effect, not as a clean year.

## Domain probe (step 7) — not performed

I did not have a domain owner to supply real threat scenarios; I have not invented them. Recorded as `supplied_by: none`. The coverage claim of this audit therefore stays `[H]`.

## Mechanism (not motive)

The skew is consistent with format pressure — an annual board summary rewards confident conclusions and has no slot for "we don't know" — rather than intent. The pattern is uniform: accurate narrow measurements (2a, 7a) followed by an inference that drops the qualifier (2b, 7b).

## Next steps (criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Reconcile 48 vs 53 before the board relies on this** (low effort, high impact). Name the 5 services; scan them or document why they are excluded. Retract "complete".
2. **Reword claim 2** to "0 critical scanner findings across 48 of 53 services" and drop "no critical vulnerabilities".
3. **Reword claim 7**; replace "negligible" with an outcome metric — phishing-simulation click and report rates vs last year, with a locked target.
4. **Commission an independent pentest** (external or separate internal red team) with written scope and starting-privilege assumptions; until then, report claim 5 as "platform team self-test found no escalation path within scope X".
5. **Add controls to claim 3:** ticket volume and severity mix both years, median alongside mean, and exclusion of risk-accepted closures.
6. **Confirm MFA scope** covers non-IdP admin accounts (break-glass, cloud root, local).
7. Add an "open findings / incidents / out of scope" section to future board summaries.

## Registry seed (surviving `[H]` claims and recurring failure classes)

The organization is ongoing, so these are written as first entries for a security-claims registry, re-run trigger: **each quarterly board report and every change to the CMDB service count.**

- **SEC-001 [H]** Scanner coverage equals production inventory. Metric: scanned services / CMDB services (instrument: scanner asset list vs CMDB export, same date). Threshold: ≥ 100% or every gap has a written exclusion. Refutation: any unexplained gap. Arbiter: `instrument`. Data status: current value seen (48/53) — this is postdiction for this cycle; prospective from next quarter.
- **SEC-002 [H]** High-severity MTTP improvement is real, not a mix effect. Metric: median MTTP per severity-and-asset-class stratum. Threshold: median improves ≥ 30% within strata that hold ≥ 20 tickets both years. Exclusion rule: risk-accepted closures excluded, and counted separately. Refutation: improvement disappears after stratification. Arbiter: `instrument` (ticket system).
- **SEC-003 [H]** Training reduces phishing susceptibility. Metric: simulation click rate (instrument: phishing-simulation platform). Threshold: click rate ≤ 50% of prior-year baseline with n ≥ 500 sends. Refutation: reduction < 20%. Arbiter: `instrument`.
- **SEC-004 [KKE]** No privilege-escalation path exists. Arbiter must be `third_party` (independent tester); remains `[KKE]` permanently under the current in-house test.
- **Failure class FC-1:** "narrow measurement → broad conclusion with the qualifier dropped" (2b, 7b). Check every future summary for it.
- **Failure class FC-2:** internal inconsistency between body and appendix (4 vs 6). Cross-check all counts against their appendices before release.

**Recommendation:** return the summary to the security team for correction of items 2, 4, 5 and 7 before it is treated as the board's record. If the team disagrees on item 4, the cheapest resolution is to name the 5 services — that one list either restores the claim or confirms the refutation.
