# Evaluation of the FY2026 Growth Summary

The 8 claims fall into four groups. Two are simple facts that can be checked. One contains an arithmetic error. Three rest on flawed inference. Two hold up reasonably well.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. Headcount 28 → 41.** This is a simple fact, and HR or payroll records can confirm it. It says nothing about growth quality. Note that headcount grew 46% while revenue grew 25%, so revenue per employee fell from about $86k to about $73k. A funder should ask about that.

**2. Revenue $2.4M → $3.0M, "a 40% increase."** The arithmetic is wrong. (3.0 − 2.4) / 2.4 = **25%**, not 40%. The dollar figures may be correct, but the headline growth rate is overstated by 15 points. This error is disqualifying until it is corrected and explained. Also ask whether this is recognized revenue or bookings. Question 5 matters here, because annual plans billed upfront can inflate bookings and cash compared with recognized revenue.

**3. NPS 31 → 44.** This is the best-supported claim. Vendor and question were the same, and both samples are large (n ≈ 1,100 each). With samples that size, the standard error of NPS is roughly 2.5–3 points per wave, so a 13-point change is very unlikely to be noise. Remaining checks:
- Were the sampling frame and timing the same? A shift toward annual-plan customers or newer cohorts could change who responds.
- What were the response rates?
- Were detractors under-sampled?

The claim is likely real, but it shows sentiment, not revenue.

**4. Integrations double LTV, "so pushing every customer onto integrations will double LTV."** This is invalid causal inference. The observed 2x difference is a correlation and is almost certainly confounded by selection. Larger, more engaged, longer-tenured customers are the ones who adopt integrations. Reverse causation is also possible: customers who stay longer have more time to set integrations up. Forcing integrations on customers who don't need them won't give them the traits of self-selected adopters. The "double" projection has no support. To support it, the company would need an experiment that randomly encourages integration adoption, or at least a comparison that controls for size, tenure and plan.

**5. Monthly churn 3.1% → 2.2% after annual plans were introduced.** This is most likely a measurement artifact. Customers on annual plans billed once a year can't churn in a given month until their renewal date. With 60% of new customers on annual plans since March, those customers are structurally locked in for up to 12 months. That alone lowers measured monthly churn without any change in retention. The real test is:
- churn on monthly plans only, comparing like with like before and after March
- the first annual renewal rates, which don't exist yet for plans started in March 2026

Until then, the churn improvement is unproven and may simply be deferred churn. The timing also means "after" does not establish "because of."

**6. New office in June.** This is a fact with no bearing on performance. It adds costs. Including it pads the list.

**7. "Tested against 20 onboarding metrics; activation improved significantly (p = 0.04)."** This is a textbook multiple-comparisons problem.
- With 20 metrics at α = 0.05, you'd expect about 1 false positive by chance. The chance of at least one is about 64%.
- After a Bonferroni correction (α ≈ 0.0025), p = 0.04 is not significant.
- Activation also appears to have been picked after the results were seen (HARKing, or hypothesizing after the results are known).

Treat this as a hypothesis for a new, preregistered test, not as a result.

**8. Gross margin 78% from audited statements.** This is credible and checkable, and audited financials are strong evidence. Confirm two things:
- whether hosting, support and onboarding staff costs sit in COGS (cost of goods sold) consistently with SaaS peers
- which period the audit covers, since FY2026 may not be audited yet

## Summary table

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1 | Headcount 28→41 | True-if-verified; irrelevant to traction (revenue/employee fell) |
| 2 | Revenue +40% | **Wrong**: actual growth is 25% |
| 3 | NPS 31→44 | Likely real; the strongest claim |
| 4 | Integrations → 2x LTV | **Unsupported causal leap** (selection bias) |

| 8 | Gross margin 78% | Credible (audited); verify cost classification |

## Bottom line

Only claims 3 and 8 give real positive evidence. The revenue headline has an arithmetic error that inflates growth from 25% to 40%. Of the three most impressive-sounding operational claims, 4 is correlation, 5 is a billing artifact and 7 is multiple comparisons, and none shows what it asserts.

Before investing, ask for:
- a corrected growth figure and the revenue recognition basis
- monthly-plan-only churn
- a cohort retention table
- LTV by integration status with controls for customer size and tenure
- a preregistered replication of the activation result

The pattern of errors, all of which flatter the company, is itself a diligence signal.
