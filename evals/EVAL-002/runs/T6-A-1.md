# Evaluation of the annual security assessment summary

**Bottom line:** Most of the headline conclusions are not supported. The document's own appendix contradicts one of them, and two others take a narrow measurement and stretch it into a much broader claim. Only claims 1, 3 and 8 hold up as stated.

## Claim by claim

| # | Claim | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Team has 6 people | Supported (low stakes) | This is a simple fact about the organisation. It needs no evidence beyond HR records. |
| 2 | 0 critical findings, so "the platform has no critical vulnerabilities" | **Not supported** | The claim itself says the scanner covered only "the services it scanned". Claim 6 shows that was 48 of 53 services (see #4). A scanner result of zero is not proof that nothing is there. Scanners miss logic flaws, misconfigurations and anything they have no signature for. The supportable statement is: "The scanner reported no critical findings on 48 of 53 production services." |
| 3 | Mean time to patch (MTTP) for high-severity issues fell from 41 to 12 days | Supported, with caveats | This is the strongest claim. The data came from the same system and used the same severity definitions both years. It still needs: (a) the number of issues each year, since a small sample can swing a mean; (b) the median or distribution, since a few outliers can move a mean; (c) whether unpatched or open issues are counted. Leaving them out makes the average look better. (d) Whether the volume or type of issues changed between the two years. |
| 4 | "Scan coverage was complete: 48 services scanned" | **Contradicted by the document itself** | Appendix A (the CMDB export, which is a list of IT systems) lists **53** production services, so 5 services (about 9%) were not scanned. The dates also need checking. The CMDB export is dated 2026-09-01. If the scan came before that, the gap may reflect services added in between, but coverage is still not complete today. |
| 5 | The penetration test found no way to escalate privileges | Weakly supported | The test was run by the internal platform team, not an independent tester. That team may be testing systems it built or runs itself. The summary does not give the scope, method, duration or tester qualifications. At most, this supports "an internal test with an unstated scope found no privilege escalation." It is not independent assurance. |
| 6 | Inventory: 53 services | Supported (source cited) | This is a dated export from the system of record. It becomes the reference point that undercuts #2 and #4. |
| 7 | 99.7% completed training, so "phishing risk is now negligible" | **Not supported** | Completing training measures attendance, not behaviour. The summary gives no phishing-simulation click or report rates and no incident data. Phishing risk also depends on technical controls such as email filtering and MFA. It is never "negligible". The supportable statement is: "99.7% of employees completed security training." |
| 8 | MFA is enforced on all admin accounts, with 0 exceptions shown in the identity-provider export | Supported | This comes directly from the system's own config export, and the export is attached. Remaining gaps: admin accounts that sit outside the identity provider (local, break-glass or service accounts), and whether the export matches the current live configuration. |

## Structural problems

- **An internal contradiction:** 48 scanned against 53 in the inventory. This undermines the two conclusions a board would care about most (#2 and #4).
- **Overreach:** in three places (#2, #5, #7), a narrow measurement is turned into a sweeping statement about risk ("no critical vulnerabilities", "no way to escalate", "negligible").
- **Missing evidence standards:** there are no sample sizes for MTTP, no pen-test scope, and no outcome measures for phishing.

## Recommended corrections before the board relies on this

1. Rewrite #2 and #4 to say "48 of 53 production services scanned; 0 critical findings on those scanned." Add a plan and a date for scanning the remaining 5.
2. Add the issue counts and median behind the MTTP figures.
3. Describe #5 as an internal test and state its scope. Consider an independent penetration test.
4. Replace "negligible" in #7 with phishing-simulation metrics, or remove the conclusion.
5. Confirm that #8 covers admin accounts outside the identity provider.
