# Mizan Audit: Annual Security Assessment (Board Summary)

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective), with Mode 7 (security probe) evidence rules for security claims.
**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The document picked its examples after seeing outcomes, and this audit is retrospective too. No thresholds were locked in advance.
**Coverage:** I tiered 8 of 8 items. For none of them could I open the underlying evidence (scanner output, ticket export, pentest report, CMDB export, IdP export). I checked each claim against the document itself: internal consistency, whether the inference is valid, and whether the arbiter is independent. Every tier below is therefore capped by "evidence as described, not inspected."
**Constraint:** I had no host tools. This is a text-only audit and I did not run any verification.

## Headline

Two of the board-level conclusions do not follow from their own evidence. "No critical vulnerabilities" (item 2) and "phishing risk negligible" (item 7) are both unsupported. The document also contradicts itself: item 4 says scan coverage was "complete" at 48 services, but its own Appendix A (item 6) lists **53** production services. Five services (about 9.4%) went unscanned, and that gap directly undercuts item 2. Items 3 and 8 are the strongest parts of the report.

## Conjunctions (step 8)

- **Items 2 × 4 × 6:** The "no criticals" guarantee depends on the coverage claim, and the coverage claim fails against the inventory. The three items look fine on their own and fail when read together. This is the report's most important finding.
- **Items 5 × 8:** A privilege-escalation test is not independent of admin controls. If the pentest scope excluded non-IdP admin paths, neither item covers them.

## Missing card (step 6)

The format shows only achievements. It leaves out incidents and breaches during the year, open or accepted risks, findings from the unscanned services, third-party/supplier risk, pentest scope exclusions, and any target that was missed. An annual assessment with zero stated failures is itself a selection-bias signal.

## Domain probe (step 7)

I did not run the domain probe. The scenarios have to come from the security owner, not from me. Supplied by: none. Seed questions for the security owner: shadow/unregistered services, departed-employee access, third-party access, incident count.

## Score

- Supported: 8 (`[K]`, conditional) and 3 (`[H]`, strong).
- Unsupported or misleading: 2b, 4, 7b (`[Y]`) and 5 (`[KKE]`).
- **Both of the report's risk conclusions (2 and 7) fail.** The board should not read this document as "no critical vulnerabilities, negligible phishing risk."

## Next steps (criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Reconcile 48 vs 53.** Name the 5 unscanned services and either scan them or document why they are excluded. This is low effort and high impact.
2. **Reword item 2** to "0 critical findings on 48/53 scanned services; scanner detection not independently validated." Add a seeded-vulnerability calibration test for the scanner.
3. **Commission an independent (third-party) penetration test** with a documented scope.
4. **Replace item 7's conclusion** with outcome metrics: phishing simulation click rate and report rate, with thresholds locked before the next campaign.
5. **Add controls to item 3:** ticket counts per year, median, and closure reasons.
6. **Extend item 8** to non-IdP admin, root and break-glass accounts.

**Recommendation:** Send the board a corrected summary with items 2, 4 and 7 revised before this report is relied upon. If the recommendation is wrong (for example, the 5 services were legitimately out of scope), the cost is one extra reconciliation line in the report.
