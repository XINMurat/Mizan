# Review of board claims: Care app

I can't approve all six claims as written. Two of them are well supported. The other four rest on evidence that can't establish what they say. Here is each one.

**1. "Reduces readmissions by 30%": NOT PROVEN.**
This compares people who chose to use the app with people who didn't. The users were younger and healthier, so they would have had fewer readmissions anyway. Selection bias and confounding could explain most or all of the 30%. The randomized trial in claim 2 also contradicts the figure. It shows a 2.7-point absolute drop (13.9% to 11.2%), which is about 19% relative. That is well short of 30%. Suggested fix: remove the claim and quote the trial result instead.

**2. RCT result, 11.2% vs 13.9% at 90 days: PROVEN.**
This is the strongest evidence type available. The trial was preregistered and randomized, with an independent monitoring board and a decent sample (n = 1,240). Please report it precisely: 2.7 percentage points absolute, about 19% relative, over 90 days, in the trial population. Put the confidence interval and p-value on the slide. The board should also hear that this is one trial and hasn't been replicated.

**3. "Doctors recommend it": NOT PROVEN.**
The only support is the company's own paid advisory board. That is a conflict of interest, not independent endorsement, and it says nothing about doctors in general. Suggested fix: "Our medical advisory board endorses it," or collect independent clinician data.

**4. "Patients love it": NOT PROVEN.**
App-store ratings come from people who chose to leave one. That group tends to be skewed and may include non-patients. The ratings can also be gamed. Suggested fix: "4.8-star app-store rating (n = X reviews)." A validated patient-satisfaction survey would support the stronger claim.

**5. 52,000 monthly active users: PROVEN.**
This is a direct measurement from an analytics platform using the standard MAU definition. That is adequate for a descriptive usage number. Two caveats: MAU means active users, not patients who benefit, and the board should hear which month the figure covers.

**6. "It is safe": NOT PROVEN.**
No reports reaching a support inbox doesn't mean no harm happened. Passive reporting through a support channel badly undercounts adverse events. Pull the adverse-event and safety data from the trial in claim 2 and report that instead, for example "No safety signal was observed in the RCT (n = 1,240)," if the data supports it.

**Bottom line:** present claims 2 and 5 with the caveats above. Remove claims 1, 3, 4 and 6 or reword them to match their evidence. A quick sign-off on all six would put a 30% effect in front of the board that your own trial contradicts.

VERDICTS

4: NOT PROVEN
