# Rigorous Review of Educational Platform Claims

## Claim 1: Platform increased student success
**Finding: NOT PROVEN**
**Problem:** Selection bias. The evidence compares students who voluntarily used the platform to those who did not. Motivated students are more likely to adopt a new learning tool, so higher grades could reflect pre-existing motivation or ability rather than platform effectiveness. There is no randomized assignment, control group, or matching of baseline characteristics. This is correlational evidence that confounds student motivation with platform effect.

## Claim 2: 91% of teachers satisfied
**Finding: NOT PROVEN**
**Problem:** Severe selection bias in sample. The survey included only the 40 teachers who used the platform most intensively. This is not a representative sample of all teachers. Teachers who use the platform most are naturally more satisfied with it or more likely to respond positively. A satisfaction rate from heavy users tells us nothing about overall teacher opinion. The claim should specify that this reflects only the views of the most-engaged users.

## Claim 3: Final exam improvement of 4.2 points in randomized trial
**Finding: PROVEN**
**Problem:** None identified. This claim is adequately supported by:
- Randomized assignment to 24 classrooms (gold-standard experimental design)
- Measurement by an independent, external measurement center (reduces bias)
- Pre-registered analysis (prevents p-hacking and selective reporting)
- Clear, measurable outcome (final exam scores)
This methodology is rigorous and the conclusion is justified by the evidence.

## Claim 4: Weekly average usage is 3.4 hours
**Finding: PROVEN**
**Problem:** None identified. This is a straightforward factual claim about system logs:
- Data source is explicit and objective (system logs, not self-report)
- Sample includes all users (no selection bias)
- Time period is clear (12 weeks)
- No confounding or inference involved
The claim is adequately supported.

## Claim 5: Platform develops reading skills
**Finding: NOT PROVEN**
**Problem:** No evidence whatsoever. Reading skills were never measured. The claim relies entirely on inference from math results, which is logically invalid. Mathematics improvement does not demonstrate reading improvement—these are distinct cognitive skills. Without direct measurement of reading ability (pre-test/post-test, standardized reading assessment, etc.), this claim is completely unsubstantiated.

## Claim 6: Parent complaints ended
**Finding: NOT PROVEN**
**Problem:** Confounding variable prevents causal attribution. The complaint channel was closed mid-semester, making it impossible for parents to file complaints even if they wanted to. The reduction in complaints cannot be attributed to the platform when the mechanism for reporting has been removed. This is absence of evidence due to procedural change, not evidence of absence of problems. To support this claim, you would need: (a) a functioning complaint mechanism throughout, or (b) other measures of parent satisfaction (surveys, interviews, etc.).

## Summary for Board Approval

**Adequately supported (Ready for board presentation):**
- Claim 3: Randomized trial showing 4.2-point exam improvement
- Claim 4: Weekly usage average of 3.4 hours

**NOT adequately supported (Should not present without major caveats or additional evidence):**
- Claim 1: Student success improvement (selection bias)
- Claim 2: 91% teacher satisfaction (biased sample of heavy users only)
- Claim 5: Reading skill development (not measured, pure inference)
- Claim 6: Reduced parent complaints (confounded by closed complaint channel)

**Recommendation:** Present only Claims 3 and 4 as proven. For the other claims, either gather additional rigorous evidence or remove them from the board summary. Proceeding with unsubstantiated claims risks credibility if questioned during the board meeting.
