I can't confirm that all six claims are proven. Two of them are. The other four rest on evidence that can't support what the claim says. I'd rather tell you this now than have the board find out later.

**1. Blocks 99.9% of attacks — NOT PROVEN.** This comes from the vendor's own lab, using attacks the vendor picked. The vendor has a conflict of interest and the test set was selected, not representative. So the number tells you how the product did on the vendor's test, not on real attacks against us. Safer wording: "In the vendor's own lab test, it blocked 99.9% of a set of attacks the vendor chose." An independent test (for example AV-Comparatives or MITRE ATT&CK evaluations) or our own red-team results would be stronger.

**2. No breaches since installation — NOT PROVEN.** What we actually know is that no breach has been *detected* in four months. That is not the same as no breach. Many breaches go unnoticed for months, and four months is a short window. There's also no baseline: we don't know how often we saw breaches before, so we can't credit the product. Safer wording: "No breaches detected in the four months since deployment."

**3. MTTD fell from 19 to 6 hours — PROVEN (to a reasonable standard).** This one is well supported. An independent party (the external SOC) did the measuring, over matched 11-month windows, with the same incident definitions, and the change log rules out other tooling changes. That covers the obvious confounds. Things could still have changed outside the tooling, like threat mix or staffing, so it's best to say the drop *coincided with* deployment rather than stating that the product alone caused it. Still, the evidence supports presenting it.

**4. Staff find it easy to use — NOT PROVEN.** The only source is the IT team that chose the product. They are invested in the decision and aren't typical users. Nobody surveyed the wider staff. Either run a short usability survey (for example SUS) or say: "The IT team reports it is easy to use."

**5. Licence costs $84,000/year — PROVEN.** The signed contract is the primary source. Just check whether there are other costs (implementation, support, renewal increases) if the board is weighing total cost.

**6. Protects against zero-day attacks — NOT PROVEN.** A marketing brochure is a sales claim, not evidence. Zero-day protection can't really be shown without independent testing against novel exploits. Remove this line, or change it to "The vendor claims zero-day protection."

**Bottom line:** Present claims 3 and 5 as they are, with the small caveats above. Reword claims 1, 2, 4 and 6 so they say only what the evidence shows. Calling all six "proven" in front of the board would expose the team if any of them is later challenged.
