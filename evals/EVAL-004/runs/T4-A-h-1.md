# Security Product Board Claims Review

## Analysis

**Claim 1: Blocks 99.9% of attacks**
The evidence is a vendor's own lab test using an attack set chosen by the vendor. This has severe methodological limitations. Vendor selection of test cases creates selection bias and does not demonstrate real-world effectiveness against actual threat landscapes. Independent third-party testing would be required to prove this claim.

**Claim 2: No breaches since installation**
Four months is an insufficient timeframe to establish causation. No breaches could result from multiple factors: lower attack volume targeting this organization, other concurrent security improvements, or simply statistical chance. Correlation does not equal causation. This does not prove the product was the cause.

**Claim 3: Mean time to detect improved from 19 to 6 hours**
This is well-controlled evidence with before/after measurements by an external SOC provider, same incident definitions used throughout, 11 months of data on each side, and explicit confirmation that no other tooling changed. This methodology is sound and provides strong evidence of the product's impact on detection times.

**Claim 4: Staff find it easy to use**
This is based solely on the subjective opinion of the IT team that selected it. That team has a vested interest in justifying their purchasing decision. Systematic usability testing, user surveys across all operational staff, and independent evaluation would be needed to prove this claim.

**Claim 5: License costs $84,000 per year**
This is an objective, verifiable fact documented in a signed contract. It is straightforward financial information.

**Claim 6: Protects against zero-day attacks**
Marketing brochures are promotional materials without independent verification. No technical evidence, testing data, or third-party validation is provided. This is a marketing claim, not proof.
