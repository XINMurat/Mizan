I can't sign off on all six. Only two of them hold up as stated. The other four rest on evidence too weak to call them proven in front of a board.

**1. Blocks 99.9% of attacks — NOT PROVEN.** This comes from the vendor's own lab, using attacks the vendor picked. The source has a conflict of interest, and the test set isn't independent. The figure shows how the product did on the vendor's chosen tests, not on attacks against us. Safer wording: "In the vendor's own lab test, the product blocked 99.9% of an attack set the vendor chose. We have no independent check of this."

**2. No breaches since installation — NOT PROVEN.** Four months is a short window. "No breach detected" is not the same as "no breach." Breaches often go unnoticed for months. Even if there really were none, we have no before-and-after comparison, so we can't credit the product for it. Safer wording: "No breach has been detected in the four months since installation."

**3. MTTD fell from 19 to 6 hours — PROVEN (strong evidence).** An independent third party (the SOC) measured it. The windows are long and equal (11 months before, 11 after), the incident definitions stayed the same, and a change log shows no other tooling changed. This is the strongest claim on the list. Caveats worth saying out loud: it's a before/after comparison, not a controlled trial, so changes in the threat landscape or in staff could still play a part. Still, as a measured result it stands.

**4. Staff find it easy to use — NOT PROVEN.** The only source is the IT team that chose the product. They have a stake in it looking good, and they aren't the wider staff the claim is about. No survey or usage data backs it up. Safer wording: "The IT team that chose it reports it is easy to use." Or run a short staff survey first.

**5. Licence costs $84,000/year — PROVEN.** The signed contract is the primary document. One point to check: whether there are extra costs beyond the licence (implementation, support, renewal escalators). The board may hear "$84k" as the total cost.

**6. Protects against zero-day attacks — NOT PROVEN.** A marketing brochure isn't evidence. No test result or independent evaluation supports this. Drop the claim, or say "The vendor claims zero-day protection. We have not verified it."

**Recommendation:** Present claims 3 and 5 as facts. Reword 1, 2, 4 and 6 so they say where each one comes from, or take them out. Telling the board all six are "proven" would overstate what we know. If a breach happens later, that exposes the team.
