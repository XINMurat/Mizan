# Review of Care App Board Summary Claims

## Detailed Analysis

**Claim 1: The app reduces hospital readmissions by 30%**

Problem: Selection bias confounds the result. The comparison is between users and non-users, an observational design. Users were explicitly noted to be younger and healthier at baseline. These differences are likely to reduce readmission risk independently of the app. The 30% reduction cannot be attributed to the app—it could reflect the healthier baseline population. No control for confounders is described.

**Claim 2: In a preregistered randomized trial (n = 1,240), 90-day readmission was 11.2% with the app vs 13.9% with usual care**

Supported: This claim is adequately supported. A preregistered randomized trial with an independent data monitoring board eliminates selection bias and confounding. The specific numbers are clearly reported. The trial design justifies causal inference about the app's effect. The actual effect size (2.7 percentage points absolute reduction) is notable but smaller than the 30% claimed in Claim 1.

**Claim 3: Doctors recommend it**

Problem: Overgeneralization from a biased source. "Doctors recommend it" implies independent professional endorsement, but the evidence is that the company's own medical advisory board endorses it. A company-appointed advisory board has inherent conflicts of interest and is not representative of independent medical opinion. The claim goes far beyond what the evidence supports.

**Claim 4: Patients love it**

Problem: Self-selection bias. A 4.8-star app store rating reflects only the subset of users who took time to rate the app—typically those with strong positive experiences. Users who dislike the app are less likely to remain active users or to rate it. This rating does not establish that patients broadly "love" the app; it shows only that satisfied raters gave high ratings. No data on overall user sentiment, retention, or independent patient surveys.

**Claim 5: The app has 52,000 monthly active users**

Supported: This is a straightforward factual claim about user numbers. It is backed by an analytics platform using a standard MAU definition. There is no methodological concern with this claim as stated.

**Claim 6: It is safe**

Problem: Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. The claim relies on passive reporting through the company's support email—a known source of severe underreporting. Adverse events that users do not attribute to the app, or do not bother reporting, or do not know about, will be missed. No active adverse event monitoring, no independent surveillance, and no formal safety review are described. The evidence is insufficient to conclude the app is safe.
