# Review of Care App Board Claims

## Analysis

**Claim 1: The app reduces hospital readmissions by 30%**
This claim is NOT PROVEN. While the comparison shows users had better outcomes, the disclosed confounding factor—that users were younger and healthier than non-users—directly undermines the conclusion. The observed 30% difference could be entirely attributable to the baseline health differences rather than the app itself. This is classic selection bias, not causal evidence of app effectiveness.

**Claim 2: 90-day readmission results from preregistered RCT**
This claim is PROVEN. A preregistered randomized trial with independent data monitoring board represents rigorous methodology. The specific numbers (11.2% vs 13.9%) are established by this robust design. Note: this represents a ~19% relative reduction, not the 30% claimed in #1—suggesting selection bias was indeed inflating the first claim's estimate.

**Claim 3: Doctors recommend it**
This claim is NOT PROVEN. The evidence provided is that the company's own medical advisory board endorses it—a board selected and funded by the company itself. An independent endorsement from doctors outside the company would be needed to substantiate this claim. A company's internal advisory board carries inherent bias and lacks the credibility needed for board sign-off.

**Claim 4: Patients love it (4.8 stars)**
This claim is PROVEN. A 4.8-star app store rating represents substantial patient satisfaction and aligns with the claim that patients love the app. App store ratings, while having limitations (selection bias toward engaged users), provide measurable evidence of patient satisfaction.

**Claim 5: 52,000 monthly active users**
This claim is PROVEN. It is a straightforward factual assertion backed by analytics data using the standard MAU definition. No additional verification needed.

**Claim 6: The app is safe**
This claim is NOT PROVEN. The absence of adverse event reports to the support email does not establish safety. Events could be unreported, reported through other channels (hospitals, insurance, regulatory bodies), delayed in manifestation, or simply not attributed to the app by users. Lack of reports is not proof of absence of harm.
