## 2. Supported vs. not supported (direct answer)

**Supported, with caveats:**
- The routing gate contributes about 2 points within the SparseRoute setup (C8). This is the paper's real, defensible result, and it deserves specific credit: the controls were matched, there are multiple seeds, and variance was reported.
- The methods disclosures (C6, C7) are honest, and they are what make this audit possible.

**Not supported:**
- 74.8 as an unbiased estimate of SparseRoute's performance (C1), because of test-set tuning and a single run.
- The +3.6 improvement attributed to the method (C3), because the arms are not comparable and there is no symmetric baseline re-run.
- "Significant" (C4). No test was run, so the word is misleading.
- "Prior state of the art" (C5). The claim rests on a 2019 number with no search for anything newer.

**Unverified but checkable:** C2 (baseline value) and C9 (code release).

## 3. Counter-example / confound hunt
- **Tier drift inside the document:** the methods note carries the claim at roughly `[KKE]` (single run, no test), while the abstract presents it at `[K]` ("significant improvement"). The drift happens within one paper with no new evidence, so it counts as a finding.
- **Selection effect:** a grid search on the test split reports the maximum over the grid. The expected upward bias grows with grid size, which is not reported. The 3.6-point gap could be partly or wholly this optimism.
- **Internal inconsistency to check:** M5 says the full model has 3 seeds, but M1 says all SparseRoute numbers come from a single run. Either the 74.8 is one of those seeds (and the other two were not reported, which raises a question about whether the best seed was picked), or the ablation "full model" differs from the headline model. The authors need to reconcile this.
- I found no counter-evidence against C8 in the supplied text.

## 4. Missing card (what this format cannot show)
The abstract format has no space for variance, the size of the tuning budget, a validation/test split distinction, or baseline recency. The missing card would read: "74.8 ± ? (n=1, test-tuned over a grid of unknown size); baseline 71.2 from a 2019 pipeline, not re-run; no significance test; newer baselines not surveyed."

## 5. Mechanism, not motive
The skew is most likely structural. Benchmark-headline incentives reward a single number above the SOTA figure, and re-running old baselines is costly. The candid methods note argues against any intent to mislead. The problem is that the abstract's framing is not checked against the note.

## 6. Next steps (ordered by criticality x impact/effort)
1. **Remove "significant" or earn it** (effort: trivial). Rewrite the abstract now, which fixes C4 (`[Y]`).
2. **Re-tune on a validation split and report test once** (critical, moderate effort). This is required before C1 can mean anything.
3. **Re-run the baseline in the released codebase** with matched budget, seeds and tuning (the symmetric control for C3). Preregister the threshold: SparseRoute minus baseline ≥ 2 points, with the 95% CI over ≥5 seeds per arm excluding 0. Refutation: the CI includes 0, or the gap is < 1 point.
4. **Report ≥3 (preferably 5) seeds with std for the headline**, and reconcile the M1 vs M5 seed discrepancy.
5. **Literature check for post-2019 BenchQA results** before claiming SOTA (C5).
6. **Verify the repo release** (C9). This is cheap and unlocks independent replication of steps 2–4.

## 7. Seed registry (surviving `[H]` claims)

- **H-001:** The routing gate causally contributes to BenchQA accuracy.
  - Metric/instrument: BenchQA accuracy from the released eval script, on a held-out split not used for tuning.
  - Threshold: full model minus the no-gate ablation ≥ 1.5 points, with 5 seeds per arm, and Welch t-test p < 0.01.
  - Refutation: gap < 0.5 points, or p ≥ 0.05.
  - Data status: unseen (the new split and seeds do not exist yet).
  - Stopping rule: exactly 5 seeds per arm.
  - Exclusion rule: none, except runs that diverge (NaN loss), which are counted and reported.
  - Arbiter: `runtime` (eval script at a pinned commit).
  - Rival hypothesis: the gain comes from extra parameters or compute rather than routing. The control is a parameter-matched dense gate.
  - Two-sided: yes. A pass localizes the contribution; a fail shows that the M5 result was driven by test tuning.
  - Prior art: mixture-of-experts / sparse routing literature. The originality claim lies in this specific gate, not in routing as such.

  - Threshold and refutation: as in step 3 above.
  - Arbiter: `runtime`.
  - Rival hypothesis: the headline gap is test-set tuning optimism plus pipeline differences.
