# Mizan Audit Report — SleepWell evidence summary (partnership decision)

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective), with a seed registry for the partnership due diligence.
**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective. The startup picked its claims after seeing its own data. My tier choices were also made after reading the claims. No threshold here was preregistered by SleepWell.
**Coverage:** 6 documents atomized into 11 claims. I could independently verify **0 of 11** because no raw data, survey instrument, analytics export or endorsement source was attached. Tiers are therefore judgments about how well each claim is *supported as stated*, plus what would verify it. Nothing was confirmed against primary sources.
**Constraints:** I had no access to SleepWell's data, to Dr. Chen, or to the national statistics source cited. The domain probe (step 7) was **not run**: no domain owner (for example, your sleep-medicine advisor) supplied scenarios. `supplied_by: none`, so no tier-K coverage claim is possible.

## 1. Claim-by-claim tiering

| # | Claim (atomized) | Tier | Justification / mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | "87% of users who completed the 8-week program said they sleep better" | `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis | The number is plausible as a self-report figure but has no source. We need the survey instrument, the n, and the response rate. |
| 1b | "SleepWell improves sleep quality" (inferred from 1a) | `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading | **Survivorship:** only people who *completed* the program were counted, so dropouts (likely the non-responders) are excluded. There is **no control group**, so regression to the mean (people install when sleep is at its worst), placebo/expectancy and the natural passage of time can't be ruled out. "Said they sleep better" is self-report, not measured sleep quality (e.g., PSQI, actigraphy). A single-arm, completers-only self-report cannot support a causal claim. Impact: the true effect could be anywhere from zero to substantial. |
| 2a | "Our users sleep 7.3 h/night on average" | `[H]` | We don't know how this was measured (phone sensor? self-log?). Phone-based sleep estimates usually overestimate sleep because they count time in bed. |
| 2b | "National average is 6.8 h" | `[H]` | No source or year given. Survey figures vary by source (roughly 6.8–7.0 h in commonly cited US polls). The measurement method differs from 2a. |
| 2c | "The app adds half an hour of sleep" | `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading | **Selection confound plus instrument mismatch.** People who install a sleep app are not a random national sample (age, income, health-consciousness). There's also no pre-install baseline for the same users. The 0.5 h difference compares two different populations measured with two different instruments. The correct evidence would be a within-user before/after comparison against a matched control. |
| 3 | "Dr. Maria Chen, a leading sleep researcher, endorses SleepWell" | `[H]`, with `[KKE]` on its evidential weight | Unverified: no affiliation, publications, statement or date given. Missing control: **conflict of interest** (is she paid, an advisor, or an equity holder?). Even if true, an endorsement is authority, not evidence of efficacy. It should carry no weight in the efficacy question. |
| 4a | "Daily log-in users sleep better than weekly users" | `[H]` | Plausible as a correlational finding. The metric, effect size and n aren't stated. |
| 4b | "…which shows daily use causes better sleep" | `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading | **Reverse causation and confounding.** People who sleep well may be the ones with the stable routines that produce daily logins, and conscientiousness drives both. Engagement is self-selected. The company itself admits in claim 6 that the data is observational, so the causal wording contradicts its own claim 6. **This is an internal contradiction in the document.** |
| 5 | "30-day retention 41% (n=9,850, Jan–Mar cohort, analytics platform)" | `[H]`, the most verifiable claim in the set | Credit where it's due: this claim is specific, scoped and has a named source and n, which makes it checkable. Two things need verifying: the retention definition (any open on day 30? active in days 28–30?) and whether the analytics export confirms it. It's a business metric, not an efficacy metric. Note also that **59% had churned by day 30**, which puts the 8-week "completers" in claim 1 into perspective: completers are likely a small minority of installs. |
| 6a | "SleepWell has not been evaluated in an RCT" | `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven (as a self-disclosure) | It's an admission against interest, which is the most credible kind of statement. It is accepted. |

**Distribution:** `[K]` 2 · `[H]` 6 · `[Y]` 3 · `[KKE]` 1 (overlay on 3) · `[R]` 0 · `[S]` 0.
No `[R]` means none of the claims was refuted. It does not mean they held up: without data, nothing could be tested to failure.

## 2. Counter-example / pattern hunt
- Pattern claim "the app improves sleep": the obvious counter-evidence is the non-completers and the 59% who churned by day 30. **Their outcomes aren't reported.** Searching the document for any failure or null result turned up **none in 6 claims checked**. A summary with no negatives is a selection signal in itself.

## 3. The missing card
This format can't show: **dropouts and their outcomes, the survey response rate, any pre-install baseline, adverse effects (e.g., sleep anxiety or orthosomnia from tracking), negative or abandoned analyses, and who funded or paid Dr. Chen.** From the evidence available, the one number we can reconstruct is this: if about 41% remain at day 30 and retention decays further by week 8, completers may be around 20–30% of installs or fewer (post-hoc estimate, not preregistered). So "87%" may describe roughly a quarter of users or less.

## 4. Conjunction check (step 8)
- Claims 1 × 5: the completer framing (1) combined with high churn (5) means the headline 87% applies to a heavily filtered subgroup. Neither claim is misleading alone; together they show the survivorship. **Finding.**
- Claims 4 × 6: causal language (4) alongside the observational admission (6) is an internal contradiction. **Finding.**
- Claims 2 × 5: the average of 7.3 h is presumably computed over retained, tracking users only, so the same survivorship applies. **Unchecked:** the denominator wasn't given.

## 5. Mechanism, not motive
The skew fits normal startup pitch incentives (show wins, use causal verbs because they sell) better than it fits intent to deceive. The honest disclosure in claim 6 argues *against* bad faith. The problem is structural: the summary puts causal headlines on top of an observational dataset.

## 6. Verdict for the partnership decision
- **Business traction (claim 5):** credible pending verification. It is the strongest claim.
- **Efficacy (claims 1–4):** **not established.** The honest current tier for "SleepWell improves sleep" is `[H]` at best. The causal framings are `[Y]`.
- **Recommendation:** don't make the partnership contingent on, or co-market, any efficacy claim ("improves sleep", "adds 30 minutes") until the data requests below are met. A partnership limited to distribution or engagement is defensible now. An efficacy-branded or clinical partnership is not. *If this recommendation is wrong,* the cost is a delayed deal with a genuinely effective product, which is recoverable. Co-marketing unsupported health claims carries reputational and possibly regulatory risk (advertising-substantiation rules), which is harder to reverse.

## 7. Next steps (criticality × impact/effort)
1. **Request the raw retention export and the definition** (low effort; verifies the one strong claim).
2. **Request the survey instrument, the n and the response rate for claim 1, plus outcomes for non-completers** (low effort; decides whether 1b is `[Y]` or salvageable).
3. **Ask for Dr. Chen's statement, affiliation and compensation disclosure** (low effort; resolves `[KKE]`).
4. **Request within-user pre/post sleep data** (validated scale such as PSQI or ISI) for 2c and 1b (medium effort).
5. **Condition any efficacy-branded partnership on a preregistered RCT or waitlist-controlled trial** (high effort, decisive), using the registry below.
6. **Have a sleep-medicine domain owner supply probe scenarios** (e.g., users with insomnia disorder, shift workers, users on sleep medication, adverse effects of tracking) so step 7 can actually run.
