# Evaluation of Security Assessment Claims

## Adequately Supported Claims

**Claim 3: Mean time to patch dropped from 41 to 12 days**
- Supported by: Ticket system data with consistent severity definitions across both years
- Evidence is appropriate and verifiable
- Caveat: This shows a change occurred, not whether 12 days is adequate or whether the improvement was due to security team actions vs. other factors

**Claim 8: MFA is enforced on all admin accounts**
- Supported by: Identity-provider policy export showing 0 exceptions
- Verification is from authoritative source
- Adequately supported for the specific claim made

---

## Inadequately Supported or Problematic Claims

**Claim 2: "Platform has no critical vulnerabilities"**
- Problem: Incomplete scan coverage. Only 48 of 53 production services were scanned (per Claim 6). The conclusion extends beyond the evidence.
- Should read: "The 48 scanned services have no critical vulnerabilities" or acknowledges that 5 services remain unscanned.

**Claim 4: "Scan coverage was complete"**
- Direct contradiction with Claim 6: 48 services scanned vs. 53 in the inventory
- Coverage is incomplete by definition (5 services missing)

**Claim 5: "Penetration test found no way to escalate privileges"**
- Who judged: Internal "platform team" conducted the test, creating a conflict of interest. No independent third-party verification.
- Scope unclear: No definition of what was tested (which systems, which privilege levels, attack vectors considered)
- False negative risk: "Found no way" does not mean "no way exists"—it reflects the testers' skill and methodology, not the actual security posture
- No baseline or control: No comparison to previous years or industry standards

**Claim 7: "99.7% training completion means phishing risk is now negligible"**
- Causation not established: Training completion ≠ phishing resistance or behavior change. No evidence linking the completion rate to reduced phishing risk.
- No baseline provided: Unknown if 99.7% is an improvement
- No measurement of actual outcome: Did phishing click rates, reporting, or compromise attempts actually improve?
- Overstated conclusion: "Negligible" is not supported by a training completion metric alone
- No control group: How would you know the improvement wasn't due to other factors (e.g., improved email filtering)?
- Who judged: No independent assessment of phishing susceptibility

---

## Summary

Two claims are adequately supported. Six claims have significant gaps:
- Two directly contradict each other on scan coverage (Claims 2 & 4)
- One relies on internal-only assessment with no third-party verification (Claim 5)
- One draws a causal conclusion unsupported by the evidence provided (Claim 7)
