# Evaluation of Startup Metrics Summary

## Overview
This metrics summary contains one critical error, one concerning logical fallacy, one potential statistical artifact, and several solid claims. Below is a detailed evaluation of each claim.

---

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### 1. Headcount Growth (28 → 41)
**Assessment: Credible.** The company grew its team by 46% ($46\%$), representing 13 new hires. This is a straightforward factual claim with no mathematical or logical issues.

---

### 2. Revenue Growth (40% YoY)
**Assessment: FALSE CLAIM.** 
- Stated growth: 40%
- Actual growth: $\frac{3.0 - 2.4}{2.4} = \frac{0.6}{2.4} = 25\%$

This is a 15 percentage point discrepancy. Either the startup made an arithmetic error or is deliberately misrepresenting growth. This is a significant red flag and undermines credibility.

---

### 3. NPS Improvement (31 → 44)
**Assessment: Credible, with minor qualifications.**
- Improvement of 13 points is substantial
- Sample sizes (n=1,120 and n=1,085) are large and consistent
- Using the same survey vendor and question ensures methodological consistency
- With sample sizes this large, a 13-point improvement is almost certainly statistically significant and represents genuine improvement

The claim is sound, though the startup could strengthen it by explicitly stating statistical significance.

---

### 4. Integration LTV Correlation → Causation
**Assessment: LOGICALLY FALLACIOUS.**

The claim: "Customers who use integrations have 2x LTV, therefore pushing every customer onto integrations will double LTV."

**Problem:** This commits the classic correlation-causation error. Possible confounding variables:
- Higher-value customers may naturally gravitate toward integrations (selection bias)
- Customers who are more sophisticated or engaged may both adopt integrations AND generate higher LTV independently
- The causal direction is unclear: integrations might enable higher value, or high-value customers might be attracted to integrations

**Conclusion:** This inference is unsupported. The startup should not expect 2x LTV improvement from mandatory integration adoption.

---

### 5. Churn Improvement (3.1% → 2.2%)
**Assessment: Credible claim, but context-dependent.**
- The 0.9 percentage point reduction (29% relative improvement) is meaningful
- Introducing annual plans as a retention mechanism makes logical sense
- The fact that 60% of new customers chose annual plans shows strong product-market fit for this pricing option

**Caveat:** The causality requires careful interpretation:
- The monthly churn rate improvement might reflect a changed customer mix (annual plan customers excluded from monthly cohort)
- Seasonal effects or other concurrent changes could drive churn improvements
- Only 10 months of data with the new model (March–December) limits confidence in trend durability

Overall, the claim is reasonable but could be strengthened with more detailed cohort analysis.

---

### 6. Office Move (June)
**Assessment: Neutral fact.** This is a logistical event with no inherent business claim. No evaluation needed.

---

### 7. Onboarding Metrics Testing (20 metrics tested; p = 0.04)
**Assessment: STATISTICALLY UNRELIABLE.**

**Critical issue:** Multiple comparisons problem.
- The company tested 20 different metrics against significance threshold (p = 0.05)
- With 20 tests, random chance alone predicts ~1 metric (5%) will appear "significant" by accident
- No mention of correcting for multiple comparisons (Bonferroni, FDR, or pre-registration)
- The reported p = 0.04 result is likely a false positive

**Conclusion:** This finding should not be trusted without multiple-comparisons correction or evidence of pre-registration. The startup should report either the corrected p-value (Bonferroni: p < 0.0025) or acknowledge the limitation.

---

### 8. Gross Margin (78%)
**Assessment: Credible.** Backed by audited financial statements, this is the most trustworthy claim in the summary. No concerns.

---

## Summary of Credibility

| Claim | Credibility | Issue |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| Headcount growth | ✓ Strong | None |
| Revenue growth | ✗ False | Off by 15 percentage points (25%, not 40%) |
| NPS improvement | ✓ Strong | None |
| Integration causality | ✗ Fallacious | Correlation ≠ causation |
| Churn improvement | ✓ Good | Minor context caveats |
| Office move | – | Neutral fact |
| Onboarding metrics | ✗ Unreliable | Multiple comparisons problem |
| Gross margin | ✓ Strong | Audited |

**Overall Assessment:** The startup's summary contains one outright false claim (revenue growth), one invalid inference (integration causality), and one methodologically flawed finding (onboarding metrics). Investors should request clarification on the revenue figure, be skeptical of integration ROI projections, and ask for statistically corrected onboarding results. The solid claims (headcount, NPS, churn, margin) provide genuine evidence of business health, but the errors raise questions about analytical rigor or integrity.
