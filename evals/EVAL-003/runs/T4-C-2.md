# Postmortem Review: Checkout Outage 2026-09-18

## Critical Issues

### Claim 4: Root cause attribution (NOT SUPPORTED)
**Problem:** The postmortem attributes the outage to the cache eviction change in release 8.2, but the evidence does not support this conclusion. Claim 6 reports that the upstream DNS provider had an incident resolved at 14:49—only one minute before the rollback at 14:50. This creates a confounding variable: errors could have stopped due to the DNS provider's fix rather than the 8.2 rollback. The postmortem provides no evidence (such as traced requests, error logs, or timing analysis) that definitively links the cache eviction change to the 38% error rate. The phrase "errors stopped shortly after" is too vague to establish causation and ignores the competing hypothesis that DNS resolution was the actual problem or a contributing factor.

### Claim 5: Data impact assessment (NOT SUPPORTED)
**Problem:** The conclusion that "no customer data was affected" is based on checking only three services that the team "suspected might have written partial orders." Critical information is missing: (1) How many services total can write orders? (2) On what criteria were these three selected as suspects? (3) Were all services that touched order state during the 47-minute window actually inspected? Checking only three services of an unknown total number does not justify a blanket "no customer data" statement. This is insufficient evidence for such a broad claim.

### Claim 2: Outage duration math (INCORRECT)
**Problem:** The stated duration of 47 minutes does not match the timestamps given. From 14:03 to 14:52 is 49 minutes, not 47. Additionally, the outage allegedly started at 14:03, but the on-call engineer was paged at 14:05—a two-minute gap that is not explained. What defined the 14:03 start time? This needs clarification.

### Claim 8: Preventive guarantee (NOT SUPPORTED)
**Problem:** The statement "This will not happen again" is an absolute claim unsupported by any evidence. No specific preventive measure, architectural change, process change, or root-cause fix is identified that would guarantee recurrence prevention. This claim goes far beyond what the evidence allows and should be removed or replaced with concrete mitigation steps.

## Moderate Issues

### Claim 3: Error rate peak context (INCOMPLETE)
**Problem:** While the load balancer logs are cited as evidence for the 38% peak, no baseline is provided. What is the normal error rate for the checkout service? Is 38% a severe spike or a minor deviation? Without a control or baseline, the severity cannot be objectively assessed.

### Claim 6: DNS incident relevance (UNADDRESSED)
**Problem:** The DNS provider's incident (resolved at 14:49) is mentioned as a fact but not integrated into the root-cause analysis. This timing directly contradicts the root-cause claim in Claim 4 and should be explicitly addressed: Was DNS ruled out? Did both issues occur? The postmortem presents this as a separate detail rather than analyzing its causal role.

## Adequately Supported Claims

- **Claim 1:** The on-call engineer was paged at 14:05. This is a factual timestamp.
- **Claim 7:** The alert threshold is reasonable and would have improved detection. Replaying it against historical logs is solid validation for a forward-looking control.
- **Claim 9:** The team held a retrospective. This is factual but lacks detail on outcomes.

## Recommendations

Before publishing this postmortem:
1. Resolve the competing root-cause hypothesis: DNS incident vs. cache eviction change. Provide traced evidence linking the error messages to the cache change, or acknowledge the DNS incident as the likely cause.
2. Expand the data impact assessment to include all services that could have modified order state, not just three suspected ones, or clearly document scope limitations.
3. Correct the 47-minute calculation and explain the 2-minute gap between outage start and paging.
4. Remove Claim 8 or replace it with specific, evidence-based prevention steps.
5. Provide normal baseline error rates to contextualize the 38% peak.
