### Claim 2: "The vulnerability scanner reported 0 critical findings across the services it scanned, so the platform has no critical vulnerabilities"
**Status:** `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing  
**Atomized into two sub-claims:**
- **(2a)** Scanner result: 0 critical findings in scanned services
- **(2b)** Conclusion: Platform has no critical vulnerabilities

**2a — Scanner result:** `[K]` Proven  
Verifiable from scanner output. Assumes scanner is calibrated and was run per configuration.

**2b — Logical inference:** `[KKE]` Critical control missing  
This conclusion requires evidence that:
- All production services were included in the scan [**BLOCKED — see Claim 4**]
- The scanner's absence of findings indicates actual absence of vulnerabilities, not scanner blindness [**UNCHECKED**]
- No critical vulnerabilities exist in unscanned services [**UNCHECKED**]

The claim conflates "scanner silence on the tested subset" with "platform has no critical vulnerabilities." The missing control is **coverage completeness** (addressed in Claim 4 below) and **scanner calibration** — the report does not verify that the scanner is properly tuned or that its zero findings on comparable past runs or benchmarks was accurate.

### Claim 4: "Scan coverage was complete: 48 production services were scanned"
**Status:** `[R]` Reddedildi / Refuted  
**Atomized:**
- **(4a)** Statement: "48 production services were scanned"
- **(4b)** Conclusion: "coverage was complete"

**4a — Scanner count:** `[K]` Proven  
Verifiable from scanner logs or report.

**4b — Completeness claim:** `[R]` **Refuted**  
Claim 6 reports "production service inventory: 53 services (CMDB export, 2026-09-01)."

**Calculation:** 48 ÷ 53 = 90.6% coverage, not complete.

**Five services are unscanned.** The word "complete" is false on its face. This is not a minor gap — Claim 2 depends entirely on the assumption that all services were tested. The unscanned 9.4% includes the attack surface most likely to harbor vulnerabilities (services may be unscanned because they are deprecated, legacy, unstable, or inaccessible — all risk factors).

**Counter-example search:** Checked Claim 6 (service inventory) against Claim 4 (scan count). Found direct contradiction.

### Claim 5: "A penetration test by our platform team found no way to escalate privileges"
**Status:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis  
**Justification:**  
- Penetration test was conducted by an internal team, not an independent third party. Internal testing has structural bias: the testers have limited adversarial motivation and bounded threat modeling (the team tests scenarios they expect, not scenarios an external adversary would try).
- "Found no way to escalate" is a negative result, which is weaker evidence than a positive one. A penetration test's null result is credible only if:
  - The testing methodology is preregistered (threshold for "comprehensive" before results seen) — report does not state this.
  - The tester is independent (structural incentive against finding nothing to report) — report does not state this; internal team has reputational incentive to report clean results.
  - Scope is explicit (which escalation paths, which starting conditions, which tools) — report does not state this.
- No mention of tooling or methodology. A thorough pen test by professionals differs sharply from a basic privilege-escalation check.
- Moves to `[H]` not `[K]` because the mechanism is present (privilege escalation was tested) but the evidence is weaker than an independent audit or a failure/success on a predetermined benchmark.

### Claim 7: "99.7% of employees completed security training, so phishing risk is now negligible"
**Status:** `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading  
**Atomized:**
- **(7a)** Fact: 99.7% of employees completed security training
- **(7b)** Conclusion: Phishing risk is now negligible

**7a — Training completion:** `[K]` Proven  
Verifiable from training system records. Near-universal completion is factually supported.

**7b — Phishing risk inference:** `[Y]` Misleading  
This conclusion requires bridging evidence:
- **Training efficacy:** Does completion of security training reduce phishing susceptibility? The literature shows completion rates ≠ behavioral change. A two-hour annual training does not eliminate phishing risk.
- **"Negligible" threshold:** No metric is provided. What counts as negligible? < 0.1% of staff compromised per campaign? A specific hold-out rate? No definition.
- **Baseline for comparison:** What was phishing risk before? Has it been measured? Report does not provide click rate, compromise rate, or other empirical metric.
- **Selection of metric:** The report cites training completion (an input), not a phishing-resistance outcome (a result). A staff that completes training can still click malicious links at high rates.

The claim is technically true (high completion happened) but **frames** the input as evidence for an outcome that was not measured. This is threshold theatre — a precise-looking number (99.7%) applied to an unmeasured construct ("negligible risk"). The statement is misleading because it implies a causal link and a quantified outcome without evidence for either.

## Missing Card: What the Summary Format Cannot Show

This assessment omits:
1. **Unscanned services (5 units, 9.4% of inventory):** What are they? Why unscanned? Are they production or legacy? A service excluded from scanning is often the highest-risk service (deprecated, no maintainer, hard to instrument).
2. **Vulnerability age distribution:** Claims 3 shows patching speed but not where new vulnerabilities originate. Fast patching of 1-2 issues per year differs materially from fast patching of 50.
3. **Phishing incident rate:** The report cites training completion but provides no empirical data on clicks, compromises, or trend. Claimed "negligible risk" has no measurement.
4. **Scanner configuration:** What is the scanner's false-negative rate? How is it calibrated? Is it tuned to the platform's specific tech stack or running in default mode?
5. **Scope of penetration test:** Which services, which account types, which attack surface? "No privilege escalation found" spans a huge range from "we tried common techniques on 3 admin accounts" to "we tried all known techniques on all accounts and found nothing."
6. **Incident history:** Has the platform been breached or had high-severity incidents in the past two years? Context matters — improvement from a disastrous baseline is different from incremental improvement on an already strong baseline.

## Conjunctions: Features That Fail Together

**Pair 1: Scanner silence + Incomplete coverage**  
Claim 2 (no critical vulns) assumes claim 4 (complete coverage). Claim 4 is refuted. Therefore, Claim 2's conclusion (`[KKE]` → cannot promote to `[K]`) remains blocked.

**Pair 2: Training completion + Unmeasured phishing resistance**  
Claim 7b (negligible risk) assumes a causal link between training completion and actual behavior. Report provides only the input (completion), not the outcome (resistance). This pair fails the two-sided informativeness test: if 99.7% completed training and phishing attacks still succeed, the entry provides no information about whether training caused the outcome. Neither success nor failure of a future phishing campaign would speak to the accuracy of "negligible risk."

## Mechanism vs. Motive

The summary's skew is structural, not intentional:
- The ticket system (Claim 3) tracks improvements, not status. Improvement is inherently good news and gets reported; absence of improvement is silence.
- Scan results (Claim 2) are binary: 0 or not-0. Zero is reportable; absence of a scan is not a data point the tool surfaces.
- The format is an improvement summary, not a risk assessment. A format designed to celebrate progress will omit capabilities that were always strong (e.g., if MFA was already enforced last year, improvement is 0, so it does not appear).

Audit conclusion: The summary is not deliberately misleading, but its format is a selection pressure that surfaces improvements and silences status quo. The board is seeing a curated data set, not a risk picture.

## Recommendations (Ordered by Criticality × Impact/Effort)

### Immediate (before this board briefing):
1. **Rescan the 5 unscanned services or document why they are out of scope.** If they are production, they must be included. If they are non-production or decommissioned, state that explicitly and remove from the inventory count in the CMDB. Current claim ("coverage was complete") is false and must be corrected.
2. **Restate Claim 2 to account for gap:** "The vulnerability scanner reported 0 critical findings in the 48 services scanned (90.6% of production inventory). The 5 unscanned services were excluded because [reason]. Scanning and resolution status of those services is tracked as a separate workstream."

### Before next assessment:
3. **Define and measure phishing resistance metric:** clicks per employee per campaign, compromise rate, time-to-report, etc. Tie training completion to behavior, not just attendance. A year from now, report both training completion AND empirical phishing incident data.
4. **Commission an independent penetration test** (not internal team). State scope in advance (which services, which account classes, which tools). Preregister the passing threshold. Report not just "no escalation found" but "tested N vectors on M services, details in appendix."
5. **Document scanner configuration:** Which CVE database, which false-negative rate on benchmark suites, which false-positive rate. Calibrate once against a known-good set, then re-validate annually.

### Governance:
6. **For the next board briefing, include a Data Appendix:** service count in inventory, services scanned, services unscanned (with reason), patch metrics broken down by severity band, and phishing incident counts (if available). The current summary is accurate but incomplete.

## Registry Recommendation

This assessment is a one-shot board briefing, not a living registry. However, to prevent recurrence of these gaps:
- **Create a registry entry for each preregistered claim** (MFA enforcement, patch time targets, training completion %) with thresholds and metrics locked before the next assessment.
- **Track the 5 unscanned services** as a separate entry: mechanism (why unscanned), refutation condition (when do they become scanned), status updates appended.
- **Lock the next penetration test scope** in a registry entry now, before work begins, so the assessment cannot drift.

## Summary for Board

The security team's assessment is **partially supported** but rests on an incomplete claim set:
- ✓ **Solid:** Patch speed improved (Claim 3), MFA enforced (Claim 8)
- ⚠️ **Weak:** Penetration testing used internal team; scope not explicit (Claim 5)
- ✗ **Unsupported:** Claim of complete scan coverage (only 90.6%, Claim 4), and claim of negligible phishing risk (no empirical measurement, Claim 7b)
- ✗ **Blocked:** Conclusion that platform has no critical vulnerabilities (Claim 2b) depends on the false claim of complete coverage.

**Board should ask:** Which 5 services were not scanned, and what is the plan to scan or retire them? If they are production, this is a material gap. If they are non-production, the inventory count should be corrected. After clarification, the team's report will be credible on that point.

**The team should track phishing resistance empirically** (clicks, compromises, time-to-report) in next year's assessment. Training completion is an input; resistance is an outcome. Right now, the report cites the input and calls it proof of the outcome — that is misleading, even if unintentional.

**Overall rating:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez (plausible but incomplete security posture) rather than `[K]` Kanıtlanmış. The team is making progress, but this summary does not carry the evidence density the board needs to rely on.
