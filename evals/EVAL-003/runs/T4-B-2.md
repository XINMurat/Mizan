### 4. Root cause: release 8.2's cache eviction change; rollback fixed it
**Tier: [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing**  
This claim contains two sub-claims:
- *4a: The cache eviction change in 8.2 caused the errors.*
- *4b: Rolling back 8.2 stopped the errors.*

**For 4b (temporal correlation):** Rollback occurred at 14:50; errors stopped "shortly after." Load balancer logs confirm this sequence (verifiable, likely [K]).

**For 4a (causation identification):** This is the weakest link. The postmortem offers no mechanism: *how* does a cache eviction change spike error rates to 38%? What was the behavior? What assumptions did it violate? Without mechanism, we have correlation between one event (rollback) and one outcome (errors stopping). But:

**Critical confound — unaddressed:** Claim 6 states the upstream DNS provider resolved an incident at 14:49 — one minute *before* the rollback at 14:50. If DNS service restored at 14:49, errors would naturally subside whether or not 8.2 was rolled back. **Which fixed it?** The postmortem does not address this temporal coincidence. This is not a minor point; DNS unavailability could easily produce the 38% error spike, and the timing overlap makes the root-cause identification ambiguous.

**Required control:** Separate the effects. Did error rate begin declining *before* 14:50 (supporting DNS as primary cause)? Or did it remain flat until the rollback, then drop sharply (supporting 8.2 as primary cause)? The logs should show this; it must be stated explicitly.

**Tier: [KKE]** — The correlation between rollback and error cessation is documented, but a plausible alternative cause (DNS restoration at 14:49) is contemporaneous and unrefuted. The causal claim cannot be elevated until this confound is controlled.

### 5. No customer data affected; checked three services
**Tier: [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing**  
Quoted claim: "No customer data was affected: we checked the three services we suspected might have written partial orders."

**Framing issue:** The scope is narrowly stated. "We checked three services" is not equivalent to "no services were vulnerable." The word "suspected" signals post-hoc selection: three services were examined *after* the incident, filtered by suspicion. This is classic selection bias in a negative-result context.

**Required clarifications:**
- How many total services exist in the system?
- Were the three services selected before or after the incident resolved?
- What was the selection criterion? "Might have written partial orders" is vague; how was "partial" defined, and how would you detect it?
- Was the absence of partial orders verified with a direct check (querying order state), or inferred from log absence?

**Current tier justification:** A check occurred ([H] or [K] depending on evidence quality), but the coverage claim ("no customer data affected, period") is unsupported. The statement is technically true of the three services checked, but presented as a system-wide finding without justification for the sample.

**Tier: [KKE]** — The negative result exists, but the scope and method that could elevate it to [K] are missing. Reframe as "[checked three services; no partial orders found in those three]" and state why three were sufficient, or revert to [H].

### 8. "This will not happen again"
**Tier: [S] Spekülatif / Speculative**  
Quoted claim: "This will not happen again."  
Source access: No source cited; no mechanism provided.

**Mechanism gap:** What aspect is prevented?
- *Prevention of 8.2's cache eviction bug?* No mention of a fix to the code; only rollback is documented, which is a revert, not a fix.
- *Prevention of detection delay?* The new alert (Claim 7) accelerates detection by ~1 minute; it does not prevent the outage.
- *Prevention of future DNS incidents?* The postmortem has no control over the upstream provider.
- *Prevention of releasing untested changes?* No process change is mentioned; no testing gate is described.

The statement is absolute ("will not happen again") with zero supporting evidence. It is either a confidence statement without basis or a placeholder for an unstated prevention mechanism. In either case, it cannot be tiered above [S] without explanation.

**Tier: [S] / Speculative** — Reframe before publication. Replace "This will not happen again" with either:
- A specific, testable prevention mechanism (e.g., "Cache eviction changes now require load-test pass before release"), which would then be tiered as [H] or [K] depending on whether the control is designed or already running.
- OR: An honest constraint statement, e.g., "We cannot prevent upstream DNS incidents; our control is faster detection via the new alert."

## Missing Information (Structural Omission)

The postmortem format omits several things inherent to incident reports:

1. **Failure mechanism of 8.2's cache eviction change.** What did the code do? What assumption broke? Why errors, not silently degraded performance?

2. **Timeline of detection and response.** Outage start (14:03) → on-call page (14:05) → investigation (?) → rollback decision (?) → rollback execution (14:50). What happened in the 45-minute gap? Who decided to rollback? On what evidence?

3. **Prevention mechanism beyond detection.** The new alert detects; it does not prevent. How will future cache eviction changes be tested before release?

4. **Findings from Friday retrospective.** Was one held? What did it recommend?

5. **Root-cause analysis resolution.** Why 8.2's change was deployed without load testing, and what process change prevents recurrence of *untested releases*, not just cache-eviction bugs.

6. **Quantification of customer impact.** Error rate peaked at 38%, but how many users/transactions were affected? Did the 47 minutes correlate to customer complaints? Revenue impact? Severity tier?

## Recommendations (Ordered by Criticality × Impact/Effort)

### Critical (Blocks Publication)

1. **Resolve the 14:03/14:05 timing discrepancy.** Clarify whether 14:03 is the true error-rate spike start or an estimated root-cause activation. If it is a 2-minute detection delay, state this explicitly; it is a finding, not a bug.
   - Effort: 10 minutes (check logs).
   - Impact: Without this, the outage timeline is ambiguous and undermines all downstream claims.

2. **Rule out the DNS provider's incident as the primary cause.** Plot error-rate curves in 5-minute buckets from 14:00 to 14:55. Explicitly state whether error rate improved before 14:50 (supporting DNS as primary) or remained high until rollback (supporting 8.2 as primary). If both contributed, say so and assign relative weight.
   - Effort: 30 minutes (data extraction + analysis).
   - Impact: Without this, the root-cause identification is ambiguous and the postmortem cannot guide future prevention.

### High (Weakens Findings)

3. **Expand the customer-data audit.** State the total number of services, the selection method, and why three services' data is sufficient evidence for "no customer data affected." If a full check is not feasible, reframe as "[checked three suspected surfaces; no partial orders found]" and tier to [H].
   - Effort: 20–60 minutes (depends on codebase size).
   - Impact: Without this, the "no data affected" claim is a selection-bias finding masquerading as a clean result.

4. **Replace "This will not happen again" with a specific control.** Examples:
   - "Cache eviction changes now require load-test pass before release into production."
   - "Releases affecting hot paths now require review by two senior engineers."
   - State whether this control is already in place or will be implemented by [date].
   - Tier the control as [H] (designed but not run) or [K] (already running, point to evidence).
   - Effort: 15 minutes.
   - Impact: Without this, the prevention mechanism is opaque and the postmortem reads as incomplete.

5. **Add findings from the Friday retrospective.** What did the team discuss? What other improvements were considered and rejected? Record this; it is part of the incident response and belongs in the final report.
   - Effort: 15 minutes (extract from retrospective notes).
   - Impact: Omitting it makes the report feel incomplete and hides decision rationale.

### Medium (Improves Clarity)

6. **Describe the 8.2 cache eviction change's mechanism.** In 2–3 sentences, explain *what* the change did and *why* it produced errors. Example: "The change evicted cache entries older than 30 seconds on each write. This broke assumptions in the order-service cache-layer, causing cache misses that cascaded to database queries, overloading the connection pool."
   - Effort: 15 minutes (ask the engineer who wrote the change).
   - Impact: Helps future readers understand and predict similar failures; supports the root-cause tier.
