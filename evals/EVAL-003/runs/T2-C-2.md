# Metrics Review: FY2026 Growth Summary

## Adequately Supported

**Claim 3: Net Promoter Score rose from 31 to 44**
- Evidence: Same survey vendor, same question, large sample sizes (n=1,120 and n=1,085)
- Control/Baseline: Implicit year-over-year comparison
- Judgment: Consistently measured
- Conclusion: Stays within evidence—no overclaiming about causation, just states the increase
- **Status: SUPPORTED**

**Claim 8: Gross margin is 78% from audited financial statements**
- Evidence: Audited financial statements provide third-party verification
- Baseline: Stated as fact, not a causation claim
- Who judged: Independent auditors
- **Status: SUPPORTED**

**Claim 6: We moved to a new office in June**
- This is a factual statement with no causal claims
- **Status: SUPPORTED**

## Problems Identified

**Claim 2: Revenue grew 40%**
- **Problem: Mathematical error.** $2.4M to $3.0M is a 25% increase, not 40%. ([$3.0M - $2.4M] / $2.4M = $0.6M / $2.4M = 0.25 or 25%)
- This is a significant mistake that undermines credibility.
- **Status: NOT SUPPORTED—INCORRECT CALCULATION**

**Claim 4: Pushing every customer onto integrations will double LTV**
- **Problem: Correlation vs. causation.** The startup observes that customers using integrations have 2x LTV, then concludes forcing all customers onto integrations will double LTV.
- No evidence of causation: No randomized test, no control group, no causal isolation
- Alternative explanations: Customers using integrations may be self-selected (more sophisticated, already high-value, better fit). The integration usage may correlate with LTV rather than cause it.
- Who judged: No independent verification mentioned
- **Conclusion goes far beyond the evidence—this is a classic correlation/causation error**
- **Status: NOT SUPPORTED—UNSUPPORTED CAUSAL CLAIM**

**Claim 5: Churn fell from 3.1% to 2.2% because annual plans were introduced**
- **Problem: No causal evidence.** The startup presents churn reduction after the March annual plan launch, but provides no evidence the annual plan caused the reduction.
- No control: What if churn would have fallen anyway due to product improvements, market conditions, or other factors?
- No isolation: The 60% adoption rate of annual plans is stated as a separate fact, not as evidence that annual plans caused the churn reduction
- **Status: NOT SUPPORTED—UNSUPPORTED CAUSAL CLAIM**

**Claim 7: Activation rate improved significantly (p = 0.04)**
- **Problem: Multiple comparisons without correction.** The startup tested 20 different onboarding metrics. With 20 independent tests and a standard p < 0.05 threshold, approximately 1 false positive is expected by chance (20 × 0.05 = 1).
- Who judged: No mention of independent verification or testing methodology
- No correction applied: Should report Bonferroni-corrected p-value or pre-registered primary metric
- **Status: NOT SUPPORTED—MULTIPLE COMPARISONS PROBLEM NOT ADDRESSED**

**Claim 1: Headcount grew from 28 to 41**
- **Note: Not problematic as stated—this is a factual claim with no causal implication.** However, the startup presents it without context about impact on costs, output, or productivity, so it's unclear why this metric matters for the investor pitch.
- **Status: SUPPORTED AS A FACT, but context is missing**

## Summary

Three claims are adequately supported by evidence (NPS, gross margin, office move). Five claims have significant problems: one contains a mathematical error (claim 2), two make unsupported causal claims (claims 4 and 5), one has a hidden multiple-comparisons issue (claim 7), and one lacks context (claim 1).
