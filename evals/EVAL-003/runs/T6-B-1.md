## Claim-by-claim tier audit

### Claim 1: "Our security team has 6 people"
- **Tier:** [K] — Kanıtlanmış / Proven
- **Evidence:** Administrative fact (verifiable via org chart, payroll).
- **Issue:** Unstated relevance — team size alone says nothing about capacity, skill mix, or coverage adequacy.

### Claim 2: "The vulnerability scanner reported 0 critical findings across the services it scanned, so **the platform has no critical vulnerabilities**"
- **Tier:** [KKE] — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing
- **Mechanism of the error:** 
  1. **Scanner absence-of-findings ≠ absence-of-vulnerabilities.** Scanners have false-negative rates (undetected classes, evasion). A green scan is silence on classes the scanner cannot express.
  2. **Scope is incomplete** (see Claim 4 below: 48 of 53 services scanned). The unmeasured 5 services are not ruled out by the scanner.
  3. **Missing symmetric controls:** No external red-team results, no manual code review, no third-party assessment. Per Mode 7: "only a named control on every path to the asset promotes" a security claim. One vendor's output does not meet this.
- **What survives:** The factual claim "the scanner reported 0 critical findings in its scope" is [K]. The conclusion that the *platform* has no critical vulnerabilities is [KKE].

### Claim 3: "Mean time to patch high-severity issues dropped from 41 to 12 days"
- **Tier:** [K] — if verified against ticket system (same severity definitions, same time windows both years).
- **Limitations:** This measures *patching speed*, not *incidence rate* or *exploit window closed before exploitation*. It is orthogonal to whether high-severity issues exist. Faster patching of a proliferating issue class is not evidence of lower risk.

### Claim 4: "Scan coverage was complete: 48 production services were scanned"
- **Tier:** [R] — Reddedildi / Refuted
- **Counter-evidence:** The assessment itself provides Appendix A: "production service inventory: 53 services (CMDB export, 2026-09-01)." 
- **The finding:** 48 / 53 = 90.6% coverage. **Coverage is incomplete.** The missing 5 services are un-scanned. The assessment's own definition ("complete") does not survive its own data.

### Claim 5: "A penetration test by our platform team found no way to escalate privileges"
- **Tier:** [KKE] — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing
- **Mechanism of the error:**
  1. **Arbiter is internal.** The pentesting team is part of the platform organization, not an independent adversary or third party. Internal testing is calibrated against internal threat models (which are often optimistic). Mode 7 requires the control to be external or third-party to constrain attacker knowledge parity.
  2. **Absence-of-exploitation in one test ≠ absence-of-capability.** The test may have been incomplete (limited attack surface explored, limited techniques tried, limited time). Per Mode 7 rules: a test result is [KKE] until a *control* (independent, resource-bound, methodology pre-registered) passes.
  3. **No scope statement.** Which privilege targets were tested? Escalation to what level? From which starting points?
- **What survives:** The platform team has tested *some* escalation scenarios and found no success in that bounded test. That is [K]. The claim that escalation is impossible platform-wide is [KKE].

### Claim 6: "Production service inventory: 53 services (CMDB export, 2026-09-01)"
- **Tier:** [K] — Kanıtlanmış / Proven (inventory fact, not a security claim, but a data point).
- **Relevance:** This contradicts Claim 4 ("complete coverage" at 48/53).

### Claim 7: "99.7% of employees completed security training, so phishing risk is now negligible"
- **Tier:** [Y] — Yanıltıcı / Misleading
- **Mechanism of the error:**
  1. **Completion ≠ effectiveness.** Training completion is a process metric; phishing susceptibility is a behavior outcome. The claim conflates the two. No data on phishing-click rates, reported-phishing rates, or security-incident rates are provided.
  2. **"Negligible" is unquantified.** What is the target phishing-success rate? How does 99.7% completion lead to "negligible" risk? The threshold is implicit and unstated.
  3. **Missing controls:** No simulated phishing test, no incident rate comparison year-over-year, no user segmentation by role (high-value targets may have low training completion).
- **What survives:** 99.7% training completion is [K] (verifiable from HR system). The causal claim on phishing risk is [Y].

### Claim 8: "MFA is enforced on all admin accounts, verified by an identity-provider policy export showing 0 exceptions"
- **Tier:** [K] — Kanıtlanmış / Proven (for the policy enforcement claim).
- **Limitations:** This verifies *policy exists*, not *enforcement in practice*. No data on:
  - MFA bypass vulnerabilities (vendor issues, implementation flaws).
  - Device attestation or session-binding (MFA alone does not protect compromised devices).
  - Whether the policy export reflects runtime behavior or intended state.
  - Scope: does this include service accounts, CI/CD systems, or only human admins?
- **Separate tier for practice:** [KKE] (symmetric control missing — runtime enforcement audit, device-level telemetry, or attestation).

## Domain probe (Security context)

Per Mode 7, the assessment should be measured against the *actual threats that occur in this field*. The assessment does not provide:

1. **Supply chain risk.** 53 services likely depend on third-party libraries, services, or infrastructure. No mention of SBOM, vendor assessment, or dependency scanning.
2. **Operational security (compromised credentials).** The assessment assumes defenders control admin credentials. No mention of secrets rotation, credential vault auditing, or insider-threat controls.
3. **Data exfiltration / lateral movement.** Privilege escalation is tested; data-access lateral movement (service-to-service, database access) is not mentioned.
4. **Deployment and infrastructure.** 53 services imply deployment infrastructure. No mention of container security, Kubernetes hardening, or infrastructure-as-code scanning.
5. **Incident response / incident detection.** The assessment is preventive. No mention of detection controls (SIEM, EDR, network monitoring) or response readiness.

**Coverage statement:** The assessment claims to audit **preventive security** (scanning, pentesting, training, MFA). It does not audit **detection/response, supply-chain risk, or operational resilience**. These are orthogonal categories; their absence is not claimed, therefore they appear as silence. Per Mode 7 rules, silence on a category blocks a tier-[K] "complete security" claim.

## Conjunctions (Mode 7, step 8)

A vulnerability not found by the scanner + a pentesting team not finding escalation + 5 un-scanned services = ?

- **Pair 1: Scanner (48/53 services) + Pentesting (internal team).** Pentesting was likely informed by scanner results. If the scanner is blind to a class of issue (e.g., authorization logic flaws), the pentesting team may inherit that blindness. No independence check (did the pentest team use different methods, or did it validate the scanner?).
- **Pair 2: Scanner + Training (99.7% completion).** Scanner finds technical flaws; training addresses human flaws. These are independent. No contradiction, but also no redundancy.
- **Pair 3: MFA enforcement + Admin operations.** MFA on all admin accounts is enforced. **Order matters:** Is the policy evaluated at login only, or does session binding persist? Can an MFA-authenticated admin with a compromised workstation pivot to un-MFA'd service accounts? The conjunction is safe in one direction (preventing unauthenticated admin access) but not the other (preventing compromised-admin lateral movement).

**Finding:** The three controls (scanner, pentesting, MFA) are loosely coupled and do not form a defense-in-depth system. A failure in one (e.g., a 0-day scanner-evasion attack) is not redundantly caught by the others.

## Next steps (ordered by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **[Critical, Low effort]** Correct Claim 4: state that 5 of 53 services were not scanned. Name which services and why (if known).

2. **[Critical, Medium effort]** Audit un-scanned services for critical findings using the same scanner or manual methods. This is not optional — the missing 9.4% of your surface is where undetected issues accumulate.

3. **[High, Medium effort]** Conduct an external penetration test by a third party not embedded in the platform organization. Agree on a scope and methodology pre-test. Use this result to promote Claim 5 to [K].

4. **[High, Low effort]** Add a phishing simulation test (red-team email campaign). Correlate click rates with training completion. If click rates are low (< 5%), you have evidence for Claim 7; if not, Claim 7 is demoted to [H].

5. **[Medium, Low effort]** Verify that MFA-enforce policy is enforced at runtime (log audit, not just policy export). Check for bypass paths (service-account workarounds, API token auth without MFA, etc.).

6. **[Medium, Medium effort]** Audit supply-chain risk: SBOM for all 53 services, known-CVE scan of dependencies, vendor security assessment matrix. This category does not appear in the current assessment but is standard in security auditing.
