# Postmortem Review: Supported vs. Unsupported Conclusions

## Conclusions Supported by Evidence

**Point 7: Alert effectiveness** — "We added an alert that fires when the error rate exceeds 5% for 2 minutes; replayed against this incident's logs, it fires at 14:06."
- This is supported. The replay analysis shows the alert would have detected the issue at 14:06, only one minute after the outage started (14:03 to 14:52). This demonstrates the alert would have been useful.

## Conclusions NOT Adequately Supported

**Point 4: Root cause attribution** — "Root cause: the cache eviction change shipped in release 8.2. We rolled 8.2 back at 14:50 and errors stopped shortly after."
- Weakly supported. The correlation is suggestive (error rate peaked, rollback occurred, errors stopped), but the timing raises a concern: the DNS provider's incident (point 6) was resolved at 14:49—only 1 minute before the rollback at 14:50. The errors stopped "shortly after" the rollback, making it unclear whether the rollback or the DNS resolution (or both) ended the incident. The postmortem conflates correlation with causation without ruling out the concurrent DNS incident.

**Point 5: Data safety** — "No customer data was affected: we checked the three services we suspected might have written partial orders."
- Not supported. The team only spot-checked three services they deemed risky. This is not a comprehensive audit of all services that may have been affected during the 47-minute outage. Many services may have processed requests during this time without being examined.

**Point 8: Future prevention** — "This will not happen again."
- Not supported by any evidence. Merely rolling back one release does not prevent recurrence. No preventive measures are described (e.g., improved cache eviction testing, deployment safeguards, new monitoring, or architectural changes). This conclusion is aspirational rather than evidence-based.

## Recommendation

Before publishing, the postmortem should:
1. Clarify the root cause by either investigating the DNS incident's correlation more thoroughly or acknowledging it as a potential confounding factor.
2. Expand the data safety investigation to comprehensively audit all services that processed requests during the outage.
3. Replace "This will not happen again" with concrete remediation items (code review processes, testing improvements, or architectural changes) that actually prevent recurrence.
