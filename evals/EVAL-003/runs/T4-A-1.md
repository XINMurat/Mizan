# Review of Incident Postmortem

## Conclusions That Are Supported

The following conclusions are supported by the evidence presented:

1. **Timeline and Error Rate**: The outage duration (47 minutes, 14:03–14:52) and error rate (peaked at 38%) are supported by data from load balancer logs.

2. **Alert Coverage**: The new alert (>5% error rate for 2 minutes) would have fired at 14:06, demonstrating that adequate monitoring can now catch similar events quickly.

## Conclusions That Are NOT Fully Supported

1. **Root Cause (Release 8.2)**: While the timing of the rollback and error cessation suggests 8.2 was involved, this conclusion is undermined by a critical detail: the upstream DNS provider had an incident resolved at 14:49, which is *before* the rollback at 14:50 but during the active outage window. The postmortem does not establish whether the DNS issue was:
   - A coincidental upstream problem
   - A contributing factor to the outage
   - The actual root cause
   
   The causal chain pointing to 8.2 is circumstantial, not definitive. The concurrent DNS incident creates ambiguity.

2. **No Customer Data Affected**: The postmortem states the team "checked the three services we suspected might have written partial orders." This is insufficient evidence to support the conclusion. The check was limited to three *suspected* services. There is no evidence that:
   - All services that could have written partial orders were checked
   - The check was comprehensive across the system
   
   This conclusion overreaches the scope of the investigation.

3. **"This will not happen again"**: This statement is completely unsupported. The postmortem identifies no preventive measures, process changes, or architectural improvements that would prevent recurrence. A retrospective was held, but no specific outcomes or actions are documented. This is the weakest claim in the postmortem.

## Recommendations Before Publishing

- Clarify the relationship between the DNS provider incident and the 8.2 rollback to establish true causation
- Document the full scope of the data integrity check, not just the three suspected services
- Replace the unsupported assertion with specific, documented remediation actions
