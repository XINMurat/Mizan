# Evaluation of Startup Metrics Claims

## Summary
While several claims are factually accurate, three contain significant errors or misleading reasoning that should concern investors.

---

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### 1. Headcount Growth (28 to 41)
**Status: Accurate** — 46% headcount growth is correctly stated and represents material expansion.

### 2. Revenue Growth Calculation
**Status: MAJOR ERROR** — The claim of "40% increase" is mathematically wrong.
- Calculation: ($3.0M - $2.4M) / $2.4M = $0.6M / $2.4M = 0.25 = **25% growth**
- This is a 15 percentage point misrepresentation. The actual growth (25%) is still respectable, but the misstatement is a red flag for rigor.

### 3. NPS Improvement (31 to 44)
**Status: Likely valid, with caveats** — A 13-point increase with sample sizes of ~1,100 is likely statistically significant and meaningful. Stating "same survey vendor, same question" demonstrates awareness of measurement consistency. However, without visibility into confidence intervals or the full statistical test, we cannot be certain.

### 4. Integration Impact on LTV
**Status: LOGICAL FALLACY** — This confuses correlation with causation.
- The claim: "Customers with integrations have 2x LTV, **therefore** pushing all customers onto integrations will double LTV."
- Actual possibilities:
  - Higher-value customers are more likely to adopt integrations (selection bias)
  - Integrations enable higher LTV (true causal effect)
  - A combination of both
- **Risk**: Rolling out integrations to all customers will not automatically double LTV if the correlation is driven by customer segment rather than the feature itself. This requires causal evidence from experimentation, not observation.

### 5. Monthly Churn Reduction (3.1% to 2.2%)
**Status: CONFOUNDED CLAIM** — The mechanism is misleading.
- The churn fell from 3.1% to 2.2%, but this coincided with introducing annual plans in March.
- Key fact: 60% of new customers post-March chose annual plans.
- **Problem**: Annual plan customers are contractually locked in and report zero monthly churn by definition. The observed churn reduction is partly (possibly entirely) due to the mix shift toward annual customers, not improved retention of monthly customers.
- To assess true retention improvement, the company should report monthly churn *among monthly-plan customers only* before and after March.

### 6. Office Move
**Status: Not a metric** — This is a fact, not a performance claim. Relevance is unclear.

### 7. Onboarding Metrics Testing (p = 0.04)
**Status: STATISTICALLY SUSPICIOUS** — Multiple testing problem.
- The company tested "changes against 20 onboarding metrics" and reports one with p = 0.04.
- With 20 independent tests at α = 0.05, random chance alone predicts ~1 false positive (20 × 0.05 = 1).
- **Without correcting for multiple comparisons**, this result is unreliable and may be the false positive the math predicts.
- A proper approach would apply Bonferroni correction (p < 0.0025 for 20 tests) or pre-register the primary metric.

### 8. Gross Margin (78%)
**Status: Accurate** — Audited financial statements are a reliable source. This is credible.

---

## Overall Assessment

**Red Flags:**
1. The revenue growth error suggests either careless analysis or intentional misrepresentation.
2. The churn claim obscures a confounding variable (annual plan adoption).
3. The integration LTV claim lacks causal evidence.
4. The onboarding p-value likely reflects multiple testing bias, not genuine insight.

**Acceptable Claims:**
- Headcount, NPS, gross margin, and office relocation are straightforward or reliably sourced.

**Recommendation for Investors:**
Request deeper analysis on: (1) monthly churn among monthly-plan customers only, (2) a properly adjusted statistical test for the onboarding metrics, and (3) an A/B test or cohort analysis showing causal impact of integrations on LTV, not just correlation.
