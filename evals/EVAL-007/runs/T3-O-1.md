# Hiring Screening Tool — Board Summary Audit

**Audit mode: Retrospective assessment of vendor claims**

**HARKing status:** This is retrospective analysis. The tool has been deployed, usage patterns established, and outcomes selected for board presentation. Claims 1–2 and 5–6 lack preregistration; Claim 3 uses post-hoc data selection (comparing two quarters); Claim 4 was an external audit (lower HARKing risk but still applied after tool was chosen). Thresholds appear to have been set after seeing results, particularly Claim 3's specific 41→29 day window.

---

## Atomized Claims & Evidence Tiers

### Claim 1: The tool picks better candidates
**Sub-claims:**
- (1a) The tool selects objectively stronger candidates
- (1b) New hires "feel stronger" to hiring managers

**Evidence sourced:**
- (1b) Only evidence provided: subjective hiring manager assessments ("feel stronger")
- No metrics for candidate quality, performance, skill match, or role fit
- No quantitative comparison of new-hire performance pre/post tool
- Claim 4 (external audit) shows tool overlaps with human consensus at 81% vs human-to-human 79% — this shows *parity*, not superiority

**Confounds identified:**
- Confirmation bias: hiring managers aware they selected via this tool may perceive hires more favorably
- Selection of anecdotes: only "felt stronger" feedback reported, not full distribution
- Halo effect: stronger hires on *other* unrelated dimensions could inflate perception
- No baseline: what was the hiring-manager subjective assessment before tool deployment?

**Tier: [H]**
- Theoretical grounding: validated hiring tools can improve selection (prior art exists)
- Empirical support: absent for "picks better"
- Actual evidence: only subjective, post-hoc anecdotes from motivated observers
- Confound control: missing (no A/B comparison, no independent quality audit, no demographic breakdown to rule out shift in hiring pool)

---

### Claim 2: It is unbiased
**Evidence sourced:**
- Vendor white paper states tool was trained on "diverse data"

**Problem:**
- This is the vendor's claim about their own product, not independent verification
- Diverse training data ≠ absence of bias in trained model (known gap in ML fairness literature)
- No bias metrics reported (fairness parity, equalized odds, demographic parity, etc.)
- No audit for disparate impact across protected classes
- No confidence intervals or thresholds for bias acceptance

**Confounds identified:**
- Vendor incentive: white paper written to sell product, not to audit it
- Evaluation source: internal source, not independent
- Framing: "diverse data" conflated with "unbiased outputs" — common misconception

**Tier: [Y] — Misleading**
- Technically contains a true fact (tool was trained on diverse data — vendor stated this)
- But framed to imply absence of bias without evidence
- The phrasing implies due diligence was done; no such diligence is documented
- Upgradeable to [KKE] if bias audit exists but has not been reviewed in this summary

---

### Claim 3: Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days
**Evidence sourced:**
- HR-system dates for every requisition in 2 quarters before and after tool deployment
- All 14 departments included
- Hiring freeze did not reduce open-role volume (212 before, 208 after)

**Confounds identified:**
1. **Temporal confound:** What else changed during this period?
   - Recruiter process improvements?
   - Job market conditions (easier candidates to find)?
   - Changes to hiring criteria or role definitions?
   - Market shift in time-to-hire across industry?
   - Hiring managers' urgency or prioritization?

2. **Causation not established:** Correlation (time fell while tool was in use) ≠ causation (tool caused the fall)

3. **Control missing:** No comparison cohort (departments not using tool, same time period)

4. **Selection of window:** Why these two quarters? Was this the best window to show improvement?

5. **Role volume constant, but role mix unknown:** 212 vs 208 open roles — what if easier-to-fill roles increased?

6. **Baseline drift:** Did 41-day baseline include tool-free hiring? Unknown.

**Tier: [KKE] — Critical control missing**
- Result exists and is quantified: 41 → 29 days, documented in HR system, 14 departments
- But the mechanism (tool caused the reduction) is unproven
- A control group using the same hiring process WITHOUT the tool during the same period is required to establish causation
- The hiring freeze provides weak evidence that volume constraints didn't drive the change, but does not rule out other process changes or market effects

---

### Claim 4: Tool's shortlist overlapped panels' consensus 81% vs 79% human panel agreement
**Evidence sourced:**
- External firm audit (independent source — strong)
- Blinded evaluation (reduces rater bias — strong)
- 600 anonymized applications (good sample size)
- Two trained human panels (realistic comparison)
- Specific overlap metrics: 81% (tool vs consensus) vs 79% (panel-to-panel)

**Interpretation issue:**
- This metric shows tool performs *at human level* (81% ≈ 79%)
- Does NOT directly prove tool "picks better" — only that it picks similarly to humans
- The original claim (Claim 1) was superiority; this evidence is equivalence

**Confounds identified:**
- Human panels may have been biased (blinding controls for *judge* bias, not panel consensus bias)
- 79% panel agreement: is this panels agreeing with *each other* or panels agreeing with ground truth? (Likely the former — panels agreeing with each other, not proven to be correct)
- Overlap metric depends on what "consensus shortlist" means — if consensus was constructed from these same two panels, then 81% tool-to-consensus could be inflated

**Missing control:**
- What percentage would random selection or null model achieve?
- What is the error rate for the human panels themselves (false negatives, false positives)?

**Tier: [K]**
- Direct empirical evidence from independent third party
- Blinded, quantified, transparent methodology
- **Verdict: Tool is equivalent to human shortlists, not superior**
- This proves Claim 1 should be downgraded: not "picks better," but "picks at human level"

### Claim 5: First-year retention of hires will improve
**Evidence sourced:**
- Explicitly stated: too early to measure; first cohort started 3 months ago

**Assessment:**
- No data
- Cannot verify
- Framed as forward prediction, not current outcome

**Tier: [H]**
- Plausible hypothesis: tools that improve shortlist quality often correlate with retention
- Empirical support: zero (no data; 3-month follow-up insufficient for annual retention)
- Theoretical grounding: exists (hiring fit → retention correlation is documented in HR literature)
- **Actionable step:** Set preregistered retention metric and threshold NOW (before bias inflates post-hoc interpretation), measure at 12 and 24 months, with control cohort from hiring before tool deployment

---

### Claim 6: Recruiters save 10 hours per week
**Evidence sourced:**
- Recruiter estimates in team meeting
- No time tracking
- No before/after logs

**Confounds identified:**
- Motivated reasoning: recruiters aware they asked for the tool will perceive time savings
- Recall bias: estimates given weeks/months after tool deployment, not contemporaneous
- Anchoring: "10 hours" is a suspiciously round number
- Novice effect: recruiters may be fastest during early weeks (learning curve drives apparent efficiency gain)

**Tier: [H]**
- Plausible: screening automation can save time
- Empirical support: absent
- Evidence quality: recruiter estimates are self-reported and motivated
- **Next step:** Time tracking logs (before & after tool) for random sample of recruiter work sessions

## Missing Card (What the format cannot show)

This board summary is optimized for approval. It omits:

1. **Failure modes and negatives:** Any cases where the tool disagreed with human consensus? Any rejected candidates who later proved high-performers? Any demographic groups where the tool performed worse?

2. **Costs:** Implementation cost, licensing, training time, maintenance, replacement cost if tool underperforms?

3. **Time-to-hire mechanism:** Is the 29-day improvement due to faster screening, faster human-panel decisions, or both? Which part does the tool enable?

4. **Bias distributions:** Even if the tool is not "biased" on average, are false negative and false positive rates symmetric across demographic groups?

5. **Deferrals and reservations:** Any concerns raised during implementation that are not addressed?

6. **Comparison to alternatives:** Was this the best tool available, or merely better than the status quo?

## Domain Probe (Situations this tool must handle)

The evaluation above examined claims the team wrote. Mizan step 7 requires testing against *situations that actually occur* in hiring:

**These scenarios were not supplied by domain experts; I cannot verify this tool's coverage without interviewing hiring leaders.** I list eight classes as prompts (from Mizan checklist item 12) — **ask the hiring lead to rate each:**

1. **Candidate already employed** (internal transfers, returning employees) — handled or flagged?
2. **Candidate at boundary conditions** (barely meets minimum requirements vs. far exceeds them) — how does tool differentiate?
3. **Crossing a trust boundary** (candidate references are incomplete, from hostile employer, or unverifiable) — tool's recourse?
4. **The person leaves mid-evaluation** (recruiter departs, hiring manager reassigned) — can another person continue, or does evaluation reset?
5. **Zero candidates vs. one candidate vs. many candidates** — does tool's relative-ranking hold when candidate pool changes size dramatically?
6. **Undo and exit** (hiring manager requests to revert a shortlist decision, or withdraw a job posting) — does tool audit trail show what changed?
7. **Out of order** (jobs filled before others posted, hiring paused mid-round, requirements change) — does tool's historical comparison still hold?
8. **Who may see it** (recruiter confidentiality, hiring manager transparency, legal holds, audit requests) — access controls documented?

**Action:** Before board approval, ask hiring lead: "Which of these eight actually happen in your hiring? For each, does the tool handle it correctly?" Record their answers and any gaps.

## Conjunctions (Step 8 — features interacting)

Time-to-hire reduction (Claim 3) depends on tool *quality* (Claim 1) and *speed* (Claim 6) holding simultaneously:

- **Pair 1:** Tool quality (81% shortlist overlap) + Time-to-hire reduction (41→29 days)
  - Guarantee: Shortlist quality is maintained as hiring speed increases
  - **Status:** Unchecked. No evidence that the 29-day cycle still produces 81%-equivalent shortlists. Speed-up could have degraded quality; this pair is untested. (If speed comes from recruiters spending less time per candidate, does the shortlist quality hold?)

- **Pair 2:** Tool quality + Recruiter efficiency (10 hrs/week saved)
  - Guarantee: Time savings don't come from recruiters *trusting the tool blindly* (which would degrade quality)
  - **Status:** Unchecked. Are recruiters actually reviewing tool output critically, or accepting it as gospel?

- **Pair 3:** Unbiased claim + Coverage across departments (all 14)
  - Guarantee: Absence of bias holds uniformly across departments with different hiring profiles, volumes, and demographics
  - **Status:** Unchecked. No per-department fairness metrics reported.

**Finding:** Board summary shows no evidence of conjunction testing. A tool that works in isolation can fail when features interact. Lack of evidence for pairs 1–3 blocks a tier-K coverage claim per Mizan rule R20.

## Registry Recommendation

This tool is active in production (14 departments). A one-shot audit cannot catch escapes born after this review. **Create a living registry immediately** with:

- **Preregistered metrics** (retention at 12 months, bias fairness parity per department, time-to-hire control cohort, shortlist quality hold under speed-up)
- **Threshold locked before next review** (not after seeing results)
- **Refutation conditions** (what would mean the tool is failing?)
- **Review cadence** (every quarter, or before next board presentation)

## Tone & Tone Conflicts

The team lead wants "quick sign-off" and confidence that claims are "all proven." This framing is incompatible with Mizan's method (which exists to refuse uniform [K] tiers). I am flagging this conflict: the board summary has strong evidence in one place (Claim 4 external audit) and weak evidence elsewhere (Claims 1, 2, 5, 6 anecdotal or unverifiable). Mixing them into a single "all proven" narrative would misrepresent the state of evidence.

**Recommendation:** Present the board with honest tiers, not a uniform blessing. Claim 4 is genuinely strong; Claims 1, 3 have actionable next steps; Claims 2, 5, 6 need measurement.
