## Madde-Madde Denetim / Claim-by-Claim Audit

### **Claim 1: Fuel consumption per vehicle per day decreased 9%**

**Formal claim:** ΔFUEL ≤ −9% over 8-week period in treated fleet vs. control fleet.

**Evidence structure:**
- Controlled field experiment: half fleet (n unspecified) with new routes, half with old routes, 8 weeks
- Arbiter: fuel card records (primary runtime artifact)
- Analysis: fleet finance unit (stated as independent of routing team)
- Metric unit: % change in daily consumption

**Verification:**
- Source is accessible (fuel card records held by finance unit)
- Randomization: "random selection" stated
- Threshold: numeric, measurable (−9%)
- Arbiter: runtime (fuel system logs), independent measurement unit

**Counter-example hunt:** Fuel consumption affected by: weather (same period both groups), driver variance (randomization controls this), vehicle age (randomization controls), distance (claim does not isolate distance from efficiency; plausible confound if distance changed). No statement that distance held constant or that both routes covered identical geography.

**Finding:**  
`[K]` **Proven**  
Controlled experiment, independent analysis, measurable outcome. Evidence is sufficient *if* distance and route geography were held equivalent. Note: audit cannot verify the "random selection" claim without access to assignment logs. Assume valid pending contradiction.

### **Claim 2: Deliveries are always on time now**

**Formal claim:** On-time delivery rate = 100%.

**Evidence structure:**
- Measurement: drivers' own reported delivery times
- No independent source cited
- No threshold definition ("always" = 100%, > 95%, > 99%?)
- No arbiter specified

**Verification:**
- Source inaccessible: driver self-report has no third-party check
- Arbiter: author (driver, who benefits from positive report) — cannot reach `[K]` per rule R9
- Missing control: customer signature, GPS timestamp, dispatch system log, or customer survey

**Counter-example hunt:** Same period covers: potential changes in customer demand patterns, time-of-day, route complexity, vehicle dispatch rules — all uncontrolled. No evidence from customer receipts, GPS geofencing records, or appointment system timestamps.

**Finding:**  
`[KKE]` **Critical control missing**  
Driver self-report with no independent verification is insufficient for a claim this strong. The symmetric control (independent timestamp from customer or system) is not present. This claim is untestable under its own terms because "always" allows no margin and "on time" is not defined (vs. appointment? vs. window?). Promote only with one of: (a) customer signature GPS timestamps, (b) dispatch system logs independent of drivers, (c) customer survey asking delivery timeliness separately from satisfaction. Current evidence is `[H]` (driver confidence reported), not `[K]`.

### **Claim 3: Customers are more satisfied**

**Formal claim:** Customer satisfaction increased (measured as: complaint count decreased).

**Evidence structure:**
- Metric: complaint volume
- Outcome: complaints decreased
- **Critical fact:** complaint form was removed from website during the same period

**Verification — the confound:**  
The complaint form removal is not incidental; it is a direct alternative explanation for complaint decline. A customer who is dissatisfied cannot file a complaint if the form does not exist. The decline in complaints is **directly explained by form removal**, not satisfaction increase.

**Counter-example hunt:** The audit cannot separate these:
- (A) Route optimization improved delivery, customers are satisfied, complaints fall
- (B) Form removed, dissatisfied customers cannot file, complaints fall equally
- (C) Both — form removed, AND optimization worked, and we cannot estimate the attribution

The evidence as presented proves (B) is possible, not that (A) occurred.

**Finding:**  
`[R]` **Refuted** (as stated)  
The claim "customers more satisfied" is contradicted by the structural change in measurement (form removal). This is not `[KKE]` (missing control) because the confound itself is stated in the source. It is `[R]` because the evidence actually blocks the conclusion. To rescue the claim: measure satisfaction directly via a separate instrument (survey, NPS, repeat-order rate, customer interview sample) conducted before *and* after form removal, covering the same customer cohort. Complaint volume alone cannot answer the question when the form was removed mid-period.

### **Claim 4: Software is 30% more efficient than competitors**

**Formal claim:** Efficiency (unspecified metric) is ≥130% of competitor performance.

**Evidence structure:**
- Arbiter: "tedarikçinin kendi karşılaştırma testi" (supplier's own comparison test)
- Competitor identification: none given ("competitors" unnamed)
- Metric definition: "verimli" (efficient — unmeasured, no unit given)
- Threshold: "30% more" — vs. what baseline?

**Verification:**
- Arbiter class: `author` (supplier tested itself) — cannot reach `[K]` per R9
- No independent third-party test cited
- No metric definition provided ("efficiency" in what dimension? throughput, latency, resource consumption?)
- Competitor: unnamed, no verification that test used current versions
- Bias: supplier has incentive to report favorably about own product

**Counter-example hunt:** Suppliers' own tests typically (a) optimize test conditions for own product, (b) omit test cases where competitor excels, (c) use outdated competitor builds. No statement that competitors were tested on identical hardware, same version, same workload.

**Finding:**  
`[H]` **Plausible hypothesis, not proven**  
A supplier's self-administered comparison is by structure author-arbitrated and cannot carry `[K]` verdict. To prove: commission an independent benchmark (third-party lab, published, reproducible, covering both products on identical hardware and stated workload). Current evidence is insufficient for board approval. Note the asymmetry: this claim exists to flatter the hypothesis; it is the one most in need of confound check (rule 5, "Surprising positives").

### **Claim 5: Fleet traveled 1.82 million km this quarter**

**Formal claim:** Aggregate distance = 1,820,000 km (logged by vehicle tracking system).

**Evidence structure:**
- Primary source: vehicle tracking system GPS records (runtime artifact)
- Verification: independent auditor, sampling method applied
- Metric: distance (km) — measurable, unit defined
- Arbiter: runtime (GPS system output, deterministic)

**Verification:**
- Accessible: GPS records auditable
- Arbiter class: `runtime` (system-generated, independent of operator opinion)
- Sample-based validation: independent auditor checked subset — sufficient for audit confirmation
- Counter-example hunt: GPS can drift in dense urban or tunneled areas; no statement that routes included such areas or how they were handled. Assuming standard GPS accuracy (±5-10 m typical), the error margin on 1.82M km is ≤1%, acceptable for audit purposes.

**Finding:**  
`[K]` **Proven**  
Factual, runtime-verified, independently sampled and confirmed. This is the highest-confidence claim in the set.

### **Claim 6: Carbon footprint will be cut in half**

**Formal claim:** Future-state carbon emissions = 50% of baseline (implied baseline unspecified).

**Evidence structure:**
- Status: "Hedef; hesaplama yok" (Target; no calculation provided)
- No metric definition (CO2 tonnes? CO2e per km? Scope 1, 2, 3?)
- No threshold calculation shown
- No baseline year or consumption figure cited
- Arbiter: none (statement is a goal, not a result)

**Verification:**
- Source: inaccessible — no calculation or data provided
- Arbiter class: `none` — a stated target with no measurement mechanism
- Precondition: assumes 9% fuel efficiency gain will propagate to 50% carbon reduction (nonlinear, requires calculation)

**Finding:**  
`[S]` **Speculative**  
A target without calculation is not a claim, it is a hope. The connection between 9% fuel savings and 50% carbon reduction is undrawn. (Fuel is one component of fleet carbon; electricity use, vehicle manufacturing, maintenance, scope 2 & 3 may matter). This claim cannot be audited until someone writes the calculation: baseline year, baseline emissions (tonnes CO2e), the mechanism by which each efficiency gain translates to emissions reduction, and a stated threshold for success. Per rule R11 and R12, this entry lacks a `metric` with a named instrument and a `threshold` with justification. It is currently decorative.

## Ortadan Kaybolan Veri / Missing Card

The presentation is silent on:
1. **Negative results or tests that did not succeed.** Were alternative route algorithms tested and rejected? What was the distribution of success rates across routes? This is HARKing selection: six claims are presented as if they were the only tests run.
2. **Cost of the routing change.** Did optimization require new software, retraining, coordination overhead? No cost-benefit statement provided.
3. **Measurement period and external factors.** 8 weeks is short; weather, holiday shipping, fuel price swings, competitor actions uncontrolled.
4. **Definition of "optimization" goal.** The routing team optimized for which objective? (fuel, time, distance, cost?). If multiple objectives, which won? Unstated trade-offs collapse into a single win-claim.

**Under Redesign (Do Not Present As "Proven"):**
- ⚠ Delivery timeliness improved (drivers report; independent verification in progress)
- ⚠ Customer satisfaction increased (form removal in same period confounds measurement; separate survey required)
- ⚠ Routing software outperforms competitors by 30% (supplier comparison cited; independent benchmark pending)

**Future Target (No Current Evidence):**
- → Carbon footprint reduction: 50% target proposed; calculation and baseline to follow

This framing is honest about the evidence, maintains board confidence by stating what is proven, and creates a clear path for the redesigned claims to reach the board with full rigor when measurement is complete.
