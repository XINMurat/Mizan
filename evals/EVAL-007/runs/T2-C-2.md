# Audit Report: Building Retrofit Board Summary

**Audit Mode:** Retrospective  
**HARKing Status:** Retrospective selection — the building was chosen POST-RETROFIT because it showed favorable results ("the one building the facilities team chose to showcase"). The data itself is sound where measured, but the portfolio claim is selection bias dressed as evidence.

---

## Evidence Tier Analysis

### Claim 1: Portfolio-wide energy savings

**Atomized:** Two sub-claims: (a) retrofit saves energy; (b) savings apply across the portfolio.

**Claimed evidence:** One building, facilities-team-selected.

**Tier: [Y] — Misleading**

The retrofit saved energy in one building (true at face value, but see Claim 3 for the actual evidence). The portfolio claim is false. A single showcase building is the textbook selection-bias confound: you measure the winner and call it the system. No counter-example search is needed when the mechanism is transparent — facilities teams select buildings that look good. This claim flatters the hypothesis by the design of the evidence-gathering itself.

**Source:** Parenthetical revelation that one building was curated.

**Impact:** Tier drift from `[K]` (if accepted without reading the fine print) to `[Y]` (portfolio claim unsupported). Effort to fix: measure all buildings.

---

### Claim 2: Heat pumps quieter than boilers

**Claimed evidence:** "The installer said so."

**Tier: [H] — Plausible hypothesis**

No objective measurement provided. The installer has a financial incentive to praise the installed equipment and no independent judgment. The claim has theoretical grounding (heat pumps are generally quieter than older boiler models), but this document provides no empirical support. A sound-level meter reading, or tenant reports (separate from installer), or pre/post comparison would promote this.

**Source:** Installer statement (conflict of interest unacknowledged).

---

### Claim 3: 18% gas reduction, degree-day normalized, control building shows +1% change

**Claimed evidence:** Utility meter bills (two winters prior vs. retrofit winter), same building and control building, degree-day normalization applied.

**Tier: [K] — Proven**

This claim survives scrutiny. It has:
- Measured data (meter bills, direct evidence)
- Weather normalization (degree-days account for temperature differences)
- A control building to isolate retrofit effect from general trends
- Quantified differential improvement (retrofit –18%, control +1%, differential improvement ~19%)

The control building is on the same campus, reducing confounds from broader market conditions. This is the single well-supported claim in the summary.

**Source:** Utility meter bills, directly checkable.

---

### Claim 4: Tenants more comfortable

**Claimed evidence:** "Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit."

**Tier: [S] — Speculative**

The parenthetical is a confession of missing baseline data. Comfort is subjective; the only proxy mentioned is complaint volume. You cannot measure change without a baseline. "Complaints decreased" would require pre-retrofit complaint tracking. "Tenants report comfort improvement" would require a post-retrofit survey. Neither exists in this document.

**Source:** Absence of data (parenthetical reveals it).

---

### Claim 5: Total installed cost $2.4 million

**Claimed evidence:** Signed final invoice from contractor, reconciled by finance.

**Tier: [K] — Proven**

Cost is objective and verifiable. The invoice is a financial document; reconciliation by finance is the standard arbiter for cost claims. This is earned evidence.

**Source:** Signed invoice, finance reconciliation.

---

### Claim 6: Payback under 5 years

**Claimed evidence:** Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.

**Tier: [H] — Plausible hypothesis**

The parenthetical reveals the entire claim hangs on a price assumption. Payback is a derived calculation: (cost) / (annual savings) = years. The cost is locked (`[K]`). Annual savings are derived from the 18% reduction, but the assumption that gas prices remain at peak is:
- Not forecasted (no citation of energy-price projections)
- Historically fragile (gas prices fluctuate ±30% year-to-year)
- Not locked in (no hedge, no long-term contract mentioned)

A 5% drop in gas prices extends payback to 5.25 years, crossing the threshold. This is a forward prediction, not proven until year 5. In audit terms, payback that depends on an unjustified assumption is at best plausible until the assumption is validated or hedged.

**Source:** Implicit assumption, nowhere quantified or justified.

## Hit Rate and Coverage

**Proven claims (Tier [K]):** 2 of 6 (33%)  
**Plausible but unsupported (Tier [H]):** 2 of 6 (33%)  
**Speculative ([S]):** 1 of 6 (17%)  
**Misleading ([Y]):** 1 of 6 (17%)

The portfolio claim is selection bias presented as evidence. The comfort claim lacks baseline data by explicit admission. The payback claim rests on an unjustified price assumption. The team lead's "quick sign-off" on all claims would be premature.

---

## Missing Card

This summary omits:
- **Costs and risks not in payback.** Operating costs, maintenance, end-of-life disposal, financing costs, and tenant disruption during retrofit. Payback assumes cost is the full 2.4M divided by year-1 savings; it does not mention ongoing financing or maintenance burden.
- **What "comfortable" means operationally.** No thermal targets, humidity, acoustic thresholds, or tenant satisfaction measure.
- **Why one building.** Is portfolio retrofit underway, or is this a pilot? If a pilot, the board should know the extrapolation risk.

---

## Board Presentation Recommendation

**Do not sign off as-is.** Three of six claims do not survive the audit. Recommend:

1. **Separate the proven from the speculative.** Say: "Cost is locked at 2.4M (invoiced). Energy savings in the retrofitted building were 18% vs. a +1% drift in the control building (meter-verified). Payback is under 5 years IF gas prices stay at peak." The "if" is not a caveat to bury — it is the claim's actual scope.

2. **On portfolio savings.** Either measure all buildings, or say: "This retrofit is a proof-of-concept. Portfolio impact pending measurement of remaining buildings."

3. **On tenant comfort.** Track complaints prospectively. A "comfort survey" post-decision will be evidence of post-hoc reasoning, not an improvement.

4. **Price hedge consideration.** If payback depends on peak gas prices, a 2–3-year price floor hedge (futures, contracts) would protect the ROI and turn the assumption into an auditable control.
