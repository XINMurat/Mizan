## Atomized Claims and Tier Analysis

### 1. The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio.
**Source given:** "Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase."

**Claim breakdown:**
- **Subset A:** The retrofit saves energy in [at least] the one building tested.
- **Subset B:** This result generalizes to the whole portfolio.

**Tier: [Y] — Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

**Justification:** The evidence (one building, facilities team–chosen) supports Subset A narrowly but not Subset B. Subset B is presented as the headline while being unsupported. The facilities team's choice to "showcase" a building introduces selection bias—the team is unlikely to display a building where the retrofit failed or underperformed. No counter-example search conducted (did any retrofitted building show worse performance?). The framing implies portfolio-wide benefit when evidence is limited to a single, curated example.

**Next step:** Measure energy savings in *all* retrofitted buildings to upgrade this to [K], or reframe the claim to "The retrofit can save energy in the showcase building" ([K] tier but narrower scope).

---

### 2. The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers.
**Source given:** "The installer said so."

**Claim breakdown:**
- Factual assertion about comparative sound levels.
- No quantitative threshold (e.g., dB reduction).
- No independent measurement or third-party certification.

**Tier: [H] — Makul Hipotez / Plausible Hypothesis**

**Justification:** Theoretical grounding exists (heat pumps are often quieter than boilers in general). However, empirical support is weak: the installer has financial and reputational incentive to report positive results, and no independent measurement is cited. "The installer said so" is hearsay without verification. No counter-example search (did tenants report the heat pumps are loud?).

**Next step:** Measure noise levels pre- and post-retrofit (dB readings), or conduct tenant survey with a defined threshold (e.g., 90% report improvement) to reach [K].

---

### 3. The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period; figures are from utility meter bills.
**Source given:** Utility meter bills.

**Claim breakdown:**
- Quantitative comparison: retrofitted building −18% vs. control building +1% (net 19% differential).
- Pre/post comparison within the retrofitted building (18% reduction).
- Matched control building on same campus (reduces confounds from weather, regional pricing, demographics).
- Source is objective (utility meter data), not self-report.

**Tier: [K] — Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

**Justification:** Direct evidence supports the claim. Threshold met (measurable reduction in gas consumption). Source is documented (utility meter bills). The comparison against a control building strengthens the finding. However, note the scope: this measures *gas use only*, not total energy. If heating was shifted to electric resistance or heat pump electricity, total energy might not have declined. This claim is proven for gas consumption but does not support Claim 1's broader assertion about portfolio-wide energy savings.

**Caveat:** Assumes meter readings are accurate and utility bills were not adjusted mid-period for other reasons (e.g., rate changes, billing corrections).

---

### 4. Tenants are more comfortable.
**Source given:** "Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit."

**Claim breakdown:**
- Assertion: tenant comfort improved.
- Baseline: absent (no pre-retrofit complaint record).
- Post-retrofit data: unspecified (are complaints now being tracked? Are they lower than [unknown baseline]?).

**Tier: [R] — Reddedildi / Refuted** (as a supportable knowledge claim)

**Justification:** The very reason given for the claim—"complaints were not tracked before"—proves the claim cannot be verified. Without a baseline, no change can be established. The statement reveals a selection-bias structure: if complaints *are* now lower, that is selection bias (tracking only post-retrofit, with no control group of non-retrofitted buildings' tenants' complaint rates). If complaints are *not* tracked post-retrofit either, there is zero evidence. This claim has no refutation condition and no threshold; it is not auditable.

**Next step:** Establish a baseline of complaints pre-retrofit (e.g., tenant survey or facility records if available). Design a post-retrofit survey with a defined threshold (e.g., X% of tenants report improved comfort). Include a control building for comparison.

---

### 5. The total installed cost was 2.4 million: the signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance.
**Source given:** Signed final invoice from contractor, reconciled by finance.

**Claim breakdown:**
- Factual assertion about a documented financial transaction.
- Source is primary: signed invoice and finance reconciliation.
- No ambiguity in the threshold (exact amount).

**Tier: [K] — Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

**Justification:** Direct evidence supports it. The source is documentation (invoice and finance reconciliation). Threshold is met (an exact cost figure with dual verification). This is among the most defensible claims in the set.

---

### 6. Payback will be under 5 years.
**Source given:** "Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak."

**Claim breakdown:**
- Assertion: payback ≤ 5 years (forward-looking prediction).
- Dependency: gas price assumption (frozen at this winter's peak level).
- The assumption is acknowledged but buried parenthetically, not integrated into the claim's presentation.

**Tier: [Y] — Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

**Justification:** The claim is framed as a definitive fact ("Payback will be...") when it is contingent on an assumption about future prices. Gas prices are volatile and have historically fluctuated ±20–30% year-on-year; assuming they remain at a peak is speculative. The calculation itself (cost ÷ annual savings) may be sound and based on real energy-savings data (Claim 3), but the payback *prediction* depends on a price scenario that may not materialize. The critical control missing: sensitivity analysis showing payback under plausible alternative price scenarios (e.g., prices drop 10%, prices spike 20%). The claim contains a kernel of truth (IF prices hold) but implies a certainty that is not supported.

**Next step:** Reframe as "[H] Plausible under current assumptions" and provide sensitivity ranges: "Payback is 4.5 years at current peak prices, 6.2 years if prices return to 2024 averages, 3.1 years if they spike 25%." This converts a misleading headline into auditable scenarios.

## Missing Card: What the Board Summary Cannot Show

1. **Tenant comfort tracking:** No pre-retrofit baseline exists, so the summary cannot report on comfort as an outcome. Any claim here is unverifiable.
2. **Portfolio generalization:** Only one building was measured; the summary generalizes to "whole portfolio" without any data from other buildings.
3. **Non-gas energy use:** Heating shifted from gas to electric (heat pump) is not captured; total energy balance and emissions may differ from gas-only analysis.
4. **Maintenance costs and longevity:** No data on repair costs, downtime, or system lifespan compared to the old boilers.
5. **Tenant disruption:** No mention of installation impact (noise, access, occupation), downtime during retrofit, or tenant complaints *during* the project.
6. **Sensitivity to price and demand:** Payback is locked to one price scenario; robustness under price or demand changes is unstated.

## Recommendations, by criticality × (impact / effort)

1. **Highest priority:** Claim 1 (portfolio generalization). The headline claim fails scrutiny. 
   - **Mechanism:** Selection bias on one showcase building.
   - **Impact:** If the retrofit underperforms in other buildings, the portfolio decision is unjustified.
   - **Fix cost:** Medium (requires measuring all retrofitted buildings).
   - **Recommendation:** Downgrade to "The retrofit can save energy in favorable conditions (showcase building: −18% gas)" or measure remaining buildings before board presentation.

2. **High priority:** Claim 4 (tenant comfort). The absence of pre-retrofit data makes this claim unauditable.
   - **Mechanism:** No baseline, no control.
   - **Impact:** If tenant comfort is a decision criterion for the board, you have no evidence.
   - **Fix cost:** Low to medium (survey design and data collection).
   - **Recommendation:** Run a post-retrofit comfort survey now; retrospectively infer comfort from complaint data if archived. If no data exists, remove from board summary or mark as [S] (speculative).

3. **Medium priority:** Claim 6 (payback). Locked to a single price scenario.
   - **Mechanism:** Critical control (price sensitivity) missing.
   - **Impact:** If gas prices drop, payback extends beyond 5 years; decision calculus changes.
   - **Fix cost:** Low (spreadsheet sensitivity analysis).
   - **Recommendation:** Provide payback ranges under three price scenarios (current, −20%, +20%). Reframe as "Payback ranges from 3.1 to 6.2 years depending on price volatility."

4. **Medium priority:** Claim 2 (quieter heat pumps). Weak empirical support.
   - **Mechanism:** Installer opinion only.
   - **Impact:** If noise is a tenant-satisfaction factor, you have no measurement.
   - **Fix cost:** Low (sound meter, dB readings).
   - **Recommendation:** Measure baseline noise levels in comparable buildings; repeat in retrofitted building. Include tenant survey on noise satisfaction.
