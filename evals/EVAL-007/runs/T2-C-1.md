# Building Retrofit — Audit Report

## Summary

Review of six claims presented to the board for sign-off. This is a retrospective audit of claims made after measurements were selected. The board summary presents all claims as proven; the evidence tier does not support this assessment.

**Coverage & Scope**: All six claims audited. HARKing status: retrospective; the building was chosen by the facilities team to showcase ("the one building the facilities team chose"), indicating ex-post selection.

---

## Atomized Claims & Evidence Tiers

### Claim 1: The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio.
**Evidence presented**: Measured in one building the facilities team chose to showcase.

**Tier**: `[Y]` Misleading / Yanıltıcı

**Justification**: The claim's scope is "across the whole portfolio" but the evidence is restricted to a single building explicitly selected for visibility. This is selection bias masquerading as portfolio evidence. The parenthetical admission ("the one building the facilities team chose to showcase") reveals that no portfolio-wide sampling was performed. Presenting this as proof of portfolio-wide savings overstates the evidence base. The retrofit may have saved energy in that one building; it cannot be concluded to have done so across the whole portfolio from these data.

**Next step**: To recover this claim as `[K]`, measure energy savings in a representative random sample of portfolio buildings, or state the claim's scope as "in the showcase building" and archive this version as `[R]`.

---

### Claim 2: The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers.
**Evidence presented**: The installer said so.

**Tier**: `[H]` Plausible hypothesis / Makul Hipotez

**Justification**: Installer testimony is anecdotal and not independently verified. The installer has financial interest in the retrofit appearing successful. No objective acoustic measurements, no comparative decibel readings, no independent third-party assessment. The claim is plausible (heat pumps often are quieter) but the evidence is insufficient to meet the threshold for proof.

**Next step**: Conduct independent acoustic testing comparing heat pump noise levels to baseline boiler noise at comparable operational conditions. Lock the threshold (e.g., "≥5 dB reduction in steady-state operation at rated load") before testing.

---

### Claim 3: The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period; figures are from utility meter bills.
**Tier**: `[K]` Proven / Kanıtlanmış

**Justification**: This claim meets the threshold. Evidence: (a) direct measurement from utility meter bills (reliable primary source, institutional record); (b) proper weather normalization using degree-days (sound methodology for climate-corrected comparison); (c) within-building historical control (prior two winters establish trend); (d) between-building matched control (not retrofitted, same campus, establishes baseline for portfolio drift); (e) comparison of retrofit effect (18%) against control drift (+1%) is informative and separates signal from noise. The 18:1 magnitude difference makes a confound less likely. All elements are verifiable and primary-sourced.

---

### Claim 4: Tenants are more comfortable.
**Evidence presented**: Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit.

**Tier**: `[KKE]` Critical control missing / Kritik Kontrol Eksik

**Justification**: This claim has no evidence. Comfort is subjective and cannot be inferred from the absence of complaint data pre-retrofit. Without baseline data, the claim cannot be verified or falsified. A critical control is missing: either (a) retrospective tenant survey asking about comfort and thermal conditions before retrofit, or (b) systematic post-retrofit complaint tracking with comparison to a matched control building on campus. The fact that complaints "were not tracked before" is not evidence of comfort; it is a gap in the measurement system.

**Conditional recovery**: If retrospective survey of tenants can be conducted asking about comfort before retrofit with sufficient response rate and specificity (thermal comfort, noise, drafts, etc.), this could become `[H]`. To reach `[K]`, a matched control building with systematic tracking of the same complaints over the same post-retrofit period is required.

---

### Claim 5: The total installed cost was 2.4 million.
**Tier**: `[K]` Proven / Kanıtlanmış

**Justification**: This is a documented factual claim with primary-source evidence. Signed final invoice from contractor and reconciliation by finance are verifiable institutional records with accountability and audit trail. The claim is point-in-time and specific. No further evidence is needed.

---

### Claim 6: Payback will be under 5 years.
**Evidence presented**: Energy savings from Claim 3 (18% less gas per degree-day), cost from Claim 5 (2.4 million), assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.

**Tier**: `[H]` Plausible hypothesis / Makul Hipotez

**Justification**: The methodology for payback calculation is sound in structure. However, the claim is contingent on an assumption not supported by evidence: "gas prices stay at this winter's peak." This is a price forecast, not a measurement. Payback period is elastically sensitive to fuel costs; a 20% drop in gas prices extends payback significantly. The explicit caveat reveals that the threshold depends on a variable external to the retrofit's control and not preregistered. This is a prediction with an escape clause, not a verified outcome. The threshold is set to a commodity price, not to energy savings or cost data.

**Mechanism of error**: Threshold theatre (RR-449 in Mizan). A precise-looking number (5 years) attached to a claim whose arbiter is not a runtime verdict, an instrument, or a third party, but rather depends on an unprovable future external condition. This is not rigor, it is rigor cosplay.

**Next step**: (a) Preregister payback under three price scenarios: (i) prices hold at this winter's peak; (ii) prices return to 5-year mean; (iii) prices drop 20%. Or (b) split the claim into two: `[K]` for energy savings (Claim 3 already proved this), and `[H]` for the price-dependent payback with explicit sensitivity analysis. Do not present the payback prediction as a proven outcome.

## Recommendations (by criticality × impact / effort)

1. **Revise Claim 1** (medium effort, high impact): Change scope from "across whole portfolio" to "in the showcase building," or conduct sampling of 3–5 additional portfolio buildings. Current wording is misleading and will not survive external audit.

2. **Demote Claim 6** (low effort, high impact): Split payback into (a) energy savings `[K]`, and (b) time-to-payback with three price scenarios. Publish the price sensitivity. Stating payback as a single number under a "prices hold at peak" assumption is not a threshold; it is a forecast with a hidden escape clause.

3. **Design Claim 4 recovery** (medium-to-high effort, medium impact): Decide whether to pursue retrospective tenant survey or post-hoc matched control comparison. If neither is feasible, mark Claim 4 as `[R]` (refuted by absence of evidence) and do not present comfort as a retrofit benefit. Comfort without measurement is decoration.

4. **Fund Claim 2 verification** (low effort, low impact): Conduct independent acoustic testing if comfort is a board priority. If not, mark as `[H]` and do not overstate it in presentation.

## HARKing Status

**This audit is retrospective.** The building was chosen by the facilities team to showcase the retrofit *before* the audit was conducted. Evidence was gathered after the selection. Claim 1's explicit parenthetical ("the one building the facilities team chose") signals ex-post measurement. The board summary presents all claims as proven, which is the outcome the audit was expected to validate. This creates risk of confirmation bias in data collection, measurement selection, and threshold setting. Recommendation: treat this as a phase-one audit; design a phase-two independent verification of Claim 3 (energy savings) on a second building selected at random from the portfolio, to separate real signal from retrofit-announcement selection effects.

## Missing Card

The board summary is silent on:
- **Failure modes**: What could have gone wrong (equipment failure, installation defects, occupancy changes) and was not observed?
- **Confounds not measured**: Changes in building occupancy, tenant behavior, weather variability beyond degree-days, parallel upgrades (insulation, windows) — were these controlled?
- **Cost distribution**: Is the 2.4M figure (Claim 5) all-in or does it exclude engineering, project management, or soft costs?
- **Control building justification**: Why was the +1% control building deemed "matched"? Same square footage, occupancy, age, prior efficiency?
- **Measurement window**: Why exactly two prior winters for the within-building comparison? Was the window chosen after seeing the data?

These absences do not falsify the claims (Claim 3 survives scrutiny) but they constrain what can be concluded about generalizability.

---

## Tone Note

Claims 3 and 5 are genuinely strong evidence and deserve credit. The retrofit appears to have delivered the energy savings it aimed for, and the cost documentation is clean. The failures are in scope overreach (Claim 1), unsupported assertions (Claim 2), missing baselines (Claim 4), and threshold theatre on price-dependent outcomes (Claim 6). These are recoverable with revision. Recommend signing off on the retrofit itself and deferring board presentation of Claims 1, 4, 6 until they are retethered to evidence.
