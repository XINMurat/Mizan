# Building Retrofit Board Summary — Audit Report

**Auditor:** Claude Haiku 4.5  
**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Scope:** Six claims presented for board approval  
**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The retrofit and its measurement have already occurred. Claims are drawn from a curated board summary, not preregistered hypotheses. Selection bias and threshold shopping are high-risk failure modes.  
**Coverage:** All six claims are checkable. Evidence accessibility varies: some rest on presented source documents (cost), others on absent measurement (comfort), and one on an explicit fragile assumption (payback).

---

## Audit Findings — Claim by Claim

### Claim 1: "The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio."

**Tier:** `[Y]` — Misleading

**Evidence presented:** "Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase."

**Reasoning:** The claim states the retrofit saves energy "across the whole portfolio" (plural, general). Evidence is from exactly one building selected after the fact by the facilities team. This is textbook selection bias: the building chosen to showcase is the one most likely to show positive results. A single cherry-picked building cannot support a portfolio-wide claim. The framing technically contains truth (energy was saved in *that* building) but implies a generality the evidence cannot support. The claim should either state "in one pilot building" or present whole-portfolio data.

**Finding:** Tier should not rise above `[H]` (Plausible hypothesis) unless data from multiple buildings spanning the portfolio are provided.

---

### Claim 2: "The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers."

**Tier:** `[H]` — Plausible hypothesis

**Evidence presented:** "The installer said so."

**Reasoning:** Hearsay from a vendor with a vested interest in the retrofit's success is not measurement. The installer has no incentive to report unfavorable noise comparisons. No systematic sound-level measurement, independent third-party comparison, or tenant feedback is cited. This claim is plausible (heat pumps can be quieter than oil/gas boilers in engineering design) but lacks empirical grounding. To reach `[K]`, you need actual dB-level measurements, independent verification, or tenant satisfaction data on noise.

**Finding:** Tier cannot rise above `[H]` without independent measurement or systematic feedback from multiple building occupants.

---

### Claim 3: "The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period."

**Tier:** `[KKE]` — Critical control missing

**Evidence presented:** Utility meter bills (specific numbers cited; specific comparison provided).

**Reasoning:** This claim has three elements, each with different evidence strength:
- **Element A:** The retrofitted building reduced gas by 18% on a degree-day basis. *Evidence: meter bills.* Accessible and specific. ✓
- **Element B:** The same building in prior two winters provides a time baseline. *Evidence: meter bills.* Accessible. ✓
- **Element C:** A matched control building changed by +1%, suggesting external factors (weather, baseline changes) are not driving the 18% reduction. *Evidence: meter bills, control campus building.* This partially addresses confounds but does not close them.

**Critical confounds not ruled out:**
1. **Occupancy/usage changes:** Did the retrofitted building's occupancy pattern, work schedules, or tenant mix change? The control building's +1% drift suggests something changed in the campus baseline, but occupancy changes in the retrofitted building specifically could amplify the effect independent of the retrofit itself.
2. **Maintenance and operational differences:** Were maintenance practices, thermostat settings, or operational procedures changed at the retrofitted building during the same period? The meter captures total gas use, not retrofit effect in isolation.
3. **Specificity of the control:** The control building is a reasonable match, but "matched" is claimed, not verified in the audit. How closely matched? Same size, occupancy profile, usage type, and operational practice?

**Finding:** A result of this size (18% reduction) exists and is measured, but symmetric controls that could substantially alter the interpretation have not run. Occupancy shifts and operational changes at the retrofitted building alone could explain a large fraction of the 18% if they occurred. The control building's modest drift (+1%) is reassuring but not a full confound audit. Tier should remain `[KKE]` until occupancy, operational, and maintenance confounds are explicitly ruled out with written documentation.

### Claim 4: "Tenants are more comfortable."

**Tier:** `[S]` — Speculative

**Evidence presented:** "(Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit.)"

**Reasoning:** No baseline complaint data. No post-retrofit complaint tracking mentioned. No thermal comfort measurement (temperature variance, humidity, draft perception surveys, interview data). The statement "complaints were not tracked before" is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. It is also a methodological admission: the audit has no baseline to compare against. A claim about tenant comfort is empirically testable but has received no systematic measurement. The parenthetical remark suggests this was recognized as weak evidence even at the time of writing.

**Finding:** This claim is interesting and plausible (retrofits can improve comfort) but is entirely untested. To reach `[K]` or even `[H]`, establish post-retrofit complaint rates or systematically measure thermal comfort (temperature stability, occupant surveys) and compare to retrospective or externally benchmarked baselines.

---

### Claim 5: "The total installed cost was 2.4 million: the signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance."

**Tier:** `[K]` — Proven

**Evidence presented:** Signed final invoice from contractor; financial reconciliation by finance department.

**Reasoning:** Cost claims are straightforward: a signed invoice and independent financial reconciliation are reliable sources. Finance departments routinely verify invoices against scope and payment records. The chain of custody (contractor → invoice → finance check) is standard. No confounds apply to a documented, reconciled figure.

**Finding:** This claim survives the audit. The invoice and reconciliation are the proper evidence standard for a cost assertion.

---

### Claim 6: "Payback will be under 5 years."

**Tier:** `[S]` — Speculative

**Evidence presented:** "(Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.)"

**Reasoning:** Payback is computed as total cost ÷ annual energy savings. The claim depends on a fragile assumption: gas prices remain at "this winter's peak." Winter 2024–2025 included elevated energy prices due to geopolitical factors. Historical gas price volatility is substantial (±40% year-over-year swings are common). If gas prices normalize to long-term averages (10–15% below current peak), annual savings drop proportionally, and payback extends from under 5 years to 5.5–6+ years. The claim presents a single scenario (peak prices) as a forward assertion without a range, sensitivity analysis, or probability statement.

**Secondary issue — circular assumption:** The energy savings (18%) is already measured in dollars *at current prices*. The payback calculation compounds an assumption (prices stay high) on top of a result that already carries hidden confounds (claim 3's occupancy/operational risks). If energy savings themselves are lower than 18%, payback lengthens further.

**Finding:** A payback period is a well-defined calculation, but a calculation whose output is sensitive to an explicitly stated assumption that is not locked in is not a prediction, it is a scenario. To reach `[H]` or `[K]`, provide a payback range under three price scenarios (25th percentile, current, 75th percentile historical price distribution) or lock the gas price forecast with an external provider (e.g., EIA forecast, futures contract, utility tariff rider). As written, the claim implies a single-point answer to a question with known high variance.

## Conjunctions and Cross-Claim Audit (Step 8)

**"The retrofit is a good investment for the board" (implicit conjunction):**

Claims 1–6 are individually presented as positive findings. A board making a capital commitment implicitly conjoins them: that the retrofit produces results across the portfolio (`[Y]`), the retrofit improves operations (claim 2, `[H]`), and the investment pays back in a reasonable timeframe (claim 6, `[S]`). The chain of logic requires claim 3 (the energy result) to be robust *and* claims 1 and 6 to hold. Because:
- Claim 1 is misleading (single building, not portfolio)
- Claim 3 is incomplete (confounds uncontrolled)
- Claim 6 is speculative (fragile price assumption)

...the board summary's implicit claim—that this retrofit is a proven win—does not survive the conjunction test. Even if claim 3's 18% is real, it is measured only in one cherry-picked building and applies only if gas prices stay at peak. A board decision can proceed, but not on grounds of proven portfolio value.

## Missing Card — What This Summary Cannot Show

The board summary is structured to present decisions (cost, energy numbers, payback). It does not include:
- **Failure modes:** What went wrong during retrofit? Change orders, delays, warranty gaps, installation defects discovered later?
- **Occupancy and operational context:** How many people? What is the building's function (office, residential, lab, warehouse)? Was it at normal occupancy during measurement periods?
- **Tenant and staff experience gaps:** Beyond "complaints," were there problems with the new system? Control-system usability, maintenance burden, maintenance costs post-retrofit?
- **Whole-life cost:** Does payback account for maintenance costs of heat pumps, refrigerant replacement, compressor end-of-life ($5k–$15k per unit), or only energy savings?
- **Portfolio heterogeneity:** Why measure one building? Are other portfolio buildings different in size, function, climate, or age? A single building's retrofit might not replicate across a heterogeneous portfolio.

## Recommendation to the Board

**Do not present this summary as a proven retrofit case.** The team lead's confidence rests on selective evidence.

**Immediate next steps (before board presentation):**

1. **Claim 1 — Reframe or measure:** Either state "one pilot building reduced energy 18%" (true, supported) or measure remaining portfolio buildings and aggregate. Selection of one showcase building is a red flag for a board audience.

2. **Claim 3 — Run occupancy and operational audit:** Pull occupancy logs, thermostat setpoint records, and maintenance records for both retrofitted and control buildings across all three winters. Document whether occupancy/usage changed in the retrofitted building. This is doable with existing records and is high-impact (addresses `[KKE]` → `[K]`).

3. **Claim 6 — Sensitivity analysis:** Calculate payback under three gas price scenarios. Lock next year's price forecast (e.g., contractor/utility outlook, futures curve) and commit to monitoring actual prices. A single-scenario payback is not a board-grade assertion.

4. **Claim 4 — Establish baseline:** Implement post-retrofit tenant comfort tracking (complaint rate, thermal comfort survey). Retrospective comparison to pre-retrofit complaint absence is weak; forward tracking is the fix. This takes time but is necessary before comfort claims appear in any future report.

5. **Claim 2 — Measure or retract:** If noise is material to the pitch, measure decibels at occupied spaces before/after or solicit independent installer noise testing. Installer say-so is not sufficient.

**Cost of delay vs. benefit:** Steps 1–3 are data-assembly (days to weeks). Running them before the board meeting prevents a presentation that will be questioned by any rigorous board member or later audit. If the board approves the retrofit on weak evidence and costs/confounds emerge later, the credibility cost is higher than the cost of one delay.

**Board statement if no further evidence is gathered:** "The team has confidence in the retrofit based on one pilot building, which showed 18% energy reduction at peak gas prices. Payback under current assumptions is under 5 years. Portfolio-wide impact and tenant comfort are not yet measured; they will be tracked post-retrofit. Approval proceeds with these limits on current evidence."

## HARKing and Bias Notes

This audit is **retrospective:** the retrofit has occurred, measurement has occurred, and the summary was drawn from results. Selection bias is high risk: the team chose one building to showcase (facilities team selection), likely its best performer. Threshold shopping is moderate risk: payback was computed under favorable price assumptions. The audit itself is also retrospective and carried out the same day the work is presented—a compressed timeline that limits independent verification (e.g., I cannot call tenants or inspect equipment).

The team's confidence ("all proven") reflects investment alignment: a successful retrofit validates their decision and effort. This does not impugn intent, but it is a structural incentive that compounds selection bias.
