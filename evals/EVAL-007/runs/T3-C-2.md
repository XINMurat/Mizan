# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

## Overview

Six claims about a hiring screening tool are presented for board approval. The team lead indicates all are proven and seeks sign-off. This audit applies Mizan evidence-tiering to assess each claim's evidentiary support against the [K] (Proven) standard.

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates"

**Evidence offered:** Hiring managers who adopted it say the new hires "feel stronger."

**Assessment:** Subjective anecdotal testimony with no objective metric or control group.
- No comparison against hiring without the tool
- No measured quality metric (retention, performance rating, promotion speed)
- Susceptible to manager expectations bias and selection bias
- "Feel stronger" is an interpretation, not a refutation-testable outcome

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [H] Plausible hypothesis (manager confidence may indicate positive signal, but insufficient for [K])

---

### Claim 2: "It is unbiased"

**Evidence offered:** The vendor's white paper states it was trained on "diverse data."

**Assessment:** Training data diversity is not evidence of output fairness.
- Training on diverse data does not prevent biased predictions (e.g., can amplify historical hiring patterns)
- No independent bias audit provided
- No fairness metrics (FPR parity, ROC curves by protected groups, disparate impact analysis)
- No measurement of selection rates by demographic group

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [Y] Misleading (technically contains truth—diverse training is a component—but claims more than evidence supports)

---

### Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days"

**Evidence offered:** HR-system dates for every requisition in the 2 quarters before and after, across all 14 departments. Volume control: hiring freeze did not change open roles (212 vs 208).

**Assessment:** Quantitative change is measured and documented. Causation is not controlled.
- The 28% reduction in time-to-hire is a real measured outcome
- The volume comparison (212 vs 208) addresses one confound (whether the hiring rate changed) but not why speed improved
- Uncontrolled confounds:
  - Hiring freeze creates urgency (process pressure, not tool effect)
  - Candidate pool composition changed (quality, location, field)
  - Other operational changes (batching, parallel processing) concurrent with tool deployment
  - Economic/labor-market effects
- No comparison group: departments not using the tool, or cohorts hired without the tool during the same period
- No timing established: when exactly was the tool deployed; were the "before" and "after" periods truly pre/post?

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [H] Plausible hypothesis (speed improvement is real; tool causation is not established)

---

### Claim 4: "Tool's shortlist overlapped panels' consensus 81%, versus 79% agreement between two human panels"

**Evidence offered:** External firm audit, blinded, 600 anonymized applications. Tool and human panels scored each; tool overlap vs. consensus shortlist measured at 81%, human panels' mutual agreement at 79%.

**Assessment:** Quantitative comparison is measured by an external auditor. The comparison does not validate correctness.
- Agreement with consensus is not evidence that consensus (or tool) is correct
- Marginal difference: 2 percentage points (81% vs 79%) falls within typical measurement noise and statistical variance
- No significance test or confidence intervals provided
- Missing symmetric control: does the tool simply replicate human biases rather than improve them?
  - A 81% match could indicate the tool learned to mimic human decision-making, including human errors
  - The audit measures overlap, not whether overlapped selections succeeded
- No validation against ground truth: Which candidates actually performed better in role? Retention? Promotion?

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [KKE] Critical control missing (Measured agreement with human consensus, but consensus validity is unproven, and the difference is marginal; confound control—whether tool replicates biases—is absent)

---

### Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve"

**Evidence offered:** None. Explicitly stated: "It is too early to measure; the first cohort started 3 months ago."

**Assessment:** Predictive claim with zero empirical data.
- No baseline retention rate for prior (non-tool) hires provided
- Three months is insufficient for first-year retention measurement
- This is a forward-looking hypothesis, not yet a result
- "Will improve" is unquantified (improve by how much? vs. what baseline?)

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [S] Speculative (testable but not yet tested; legitimate preregistered prediction, but contains no result)

---

### Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week"

**Evidence offered:** Recruiter estimates in a team meeting.

**Assessment:** Self-reported subjective estimates without measurement.
- No time-tracking data (time logs, calendar analysis, work samples)
- Confounds in self-report:
  - Social desirability bias (team meeting context encourages positive testimony)
  - Anchoring bias (if a number was suggested, estimates cluster around it)
  - Availability bias (memorable tasks overweighted)
  - No baseline established (10 hours saved from what prior time?)
- No sample size stated; unclear how many recruiters were surveyed or whether this is one person's claim
- No distinction between perceived time savings and actual time re-allocated

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN
**Tier:** [H] Plausible hypothesis (recruiters likely benefit, but "save 10 hours" is quantified without measurement)

## Key Findings

1. **No [K] claims survive audit.** All six claims lack the evidence threshold required for board sign-off.

2. **Measurement vs. causation gap.** Claims 3 and 4 are quantitatively measured but do not establish causation or validate the mechanism (tool → better outcomes).

3. **Missing confound controls.** Time-to-hire and tool performance both have plausible alternative explanations (hiring urgency, process changes, consensus validity).

4. **Surprising positive bias.** Claims 1 and 4 are framed favorably without the symmetric control required before promotion to [K]. Claim 1 needs a retention/performance comparison; Claim 4 needs validation that consensus selections actually succeeded.

5. **Unverified vendor claims.** Claim 2 accepts the vendor's assertion about training data without independent bias audit.

6. **Self-report without measurement.** Claims 1, 2, and 6 rely on subjective testimony rather than objective measurement.

## Recommendations (ordered by impact/effort)

1. **Defer board presentation until evidence is collected.** A "prove all six" effort is single-threaded; parallelize:
   - Request external bias audit (FPR parity by demographic group, disparate impact analysis) — 2–3 weeks
   - Run time-tracking study in tool-using vs. control department — 2 weeks
   - Pull retention and performance data on first cohort after 12 months — wait until data available

2. **Validate Claim 4's consensus group:** Did the 81%-matched candidates actually perform better than the 19% where the tool and panels differed? Reconstruct 6-month and 12-month performance for both groups.

3. **Establish baselines before promotion.** If retention data becomes available, ensure a comparison group (hires from before tool deployment) is available.

4. **Convert self-reports to measurement:** Time-tracking for recruiters (2 weeks of logs from 3–5 recruiters) will replace the estimate.

5. **Commission the registry:** Create a Mizan registry with these six as forward-looking entries, thresholds locked, and the timetable above as the measurement schedule. This prevents claim drift and silent threshold revision.
