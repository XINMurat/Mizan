# Support Chatbot Board Claims — Audit Report

## Audit Metadata

**Audited document:** Support chatbot — board summary (6 claims)
**Audit mode:** Retrospective (claims presented after launch)
**HARKing status:** Full. This audit examines claims selected AFTER the chatbot was deployed and outcomes observed. No preregistered thresholds locked before measurement. The team lead's confidence that claims are "all proven" is post-hoc interpretation, not preregistered judgment.
**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims examined; all sources identified; evidence accessibility assessed.

## Individual Claim Analysis

### Claim 1: Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds

**Source stated:** Ticketing-system timestamps for all 38,000 tickets in 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by data platform team.

**Tier:** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven

**Justification:** 
- Evidence exists and is accessible (ticketing-system timestamps)
- Sample size is large (38,000 tickets)
- Time period is specified and symmetric (10 weeks before, 10 weeks after)
- Data pulled by independent team (data platform, not bot team)
- Metric is objective and verifiable
- Magnitude of change (4.2 hrs → 40 sec) is substantial and precisely stated
- **Confound check:** Time-to-first-response correlates with bot availability time-of-day patterns; no mention of whether before/after periods had equivalent distribution of ticket arrival times or seasonal effects. No counter-evidence provided; claim stands.

**Next step:** Request distribution check (time-of-day, day-of-week alignment between before/after periods).

### Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents

**Source stated:** Product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.

**Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis

**Justification:**
- Only evidence is qualitative coding of 30 transcripts by product team
- 30 is a very small sample for a preference claim
- "Prefer" requires systematic measurement; reading transcripts is interpretation
- No survey, no explicit preference question asked, no control (customers not asked directly)
- Product team has incentive bias (they built/support the bot)
- Selection of which 30 transcripts is not specified — likely bias toward bot-favorable conversations
- No metric is stated ("prefer on which dimension?": speed, resolution, clarity, politeness?)
- **Confound check:** Customers may prefer bot for speed but human for complex issues; these cannot both be true in a global preference ranking. No evidence that either dimension was measured.

**Verdict:** This is an inference from interpretation, not a measurement. Survives only as plausible hypothesis, not proven claim.

---

### Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own

**Source stated:** Vendor dashboard; "resolved" defined as customer closed the chat window.

**Tier:** `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading

**Justification:**
- Metric definition is unreliable: "customer closed chat window" is not evidence of resolution
  - Customers close chats due to frustration, abandonment, timeout, or successful resolution — signal is noisy
  - A follow-up ticket within 1 week would be the actual resolution signal, not window-close
- Vendor dashboard is self-reported by the chatbot vendor — direct incentive to inflate numbers
- No independent verification (analytics team did not audit this number)
- The claim presents a technical proxy ("window close") as if it were resolution measurement
- This is technically true (vendor does report this number) but framed to imply more than evidence supports
- **Confound check:** Repeat-contact rate (claim 4, independently verified) is 9.1% bot-first, meaning ~91% did NOT repeat. This is closer to true resolution than 70% window-close. Internal contradiction suggests claim 3 overstates resolution rate.

**Verdict:** The metric exists but does not measure what it claims to measure. The phrasing "resolves 70%" misleads readers to believe issues are solved, not that customers closed the window.

### Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts

**Source stated:** Randomized test, 5,000 customers, bot-first vs human-first for 6 weeks; repeat contacts within 7 days were 9.1% vs 11.4% (95% CI −3.0 to −1.6 points); computed by analytics team, not bot team.

**Tier:** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven

**Justification:**
- Study design is sound: randomized controlled trial, large sample (5,000), sufficient duration (6 weeks)
- Outcome is objective and verifiable: repeat contact within 7 days (binary, system-measured)
- Arbiter is runtime + third-party: analytics team computed the result independently
- Confidence interval is reported and does not cross zero (−3.0 to −1.6 points, both negative favoring bot-first)
- Effect size is modest (~2.3 percentage points) but real and reproducible in the stated design
- This is the strongest claim in the summary

**Confound check:** Bot-first may reduce repeat contacts because customers get faster answers (supported by claim 1) regardless of quality. No control for issue complexity or ticket type. Despite this, the experiment is sound enough to establish the effect existed under these conditions.

### Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year

**Source stated:** Projection from vendor's sales deck.

**Tier:** `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative

**Justification:**
- Source is the vendor's sales deck — maximally incentive-biased (vendor sells by projecting value)
- No data supports this: it is a prediction, not a measurement
- A year has not passed; claim is about future state
- No mechanism is shown: how does "70% resolved" (if that number were true) translate to "one-third headcount reduction"?
  - Headcount decisions depend on: ticket volume growth, SLA changes, new products, seasonal patterns, shift coverage, regulatory requirements
  - Bot impact on one metric does not determine staffing levels
- Threshold for "proves" this claim would require: (a) pre-deployment prediction with locked numbers, (b) actual headcount reduction at anniversary, (c) causal attribution to bot, not other staffing decisions

**Verdict:** This claim cannot be falsified yet (it's about future outcomes). It exists as speculation, not testable hypothesis.

---

### Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch

**Source stated:** No survey; attrition lower this quarter; hiring freeze started same month.

**Tier:** `[R]` Reddedildi / Refuted

**Justification:**
- No direct evidence (no survey, no interviews, no measurement of satisfaction)
- Proposed evidence (attrition) is affected by a confound: hiring freeze started the same month
  - Lower attrition when hiring freeze is active does NOT indicate happier agents; it indicates reduced job mobility
  - Agents cannot quit to take new jobs if hiring is frozen across the industry (or locally)
- This is a classic confound: two correlated events (bot launch + hiring freeze) make causation unidentifiable
- Even if attrition fell, it would support "fewer agents leaving" not "agents are happier"
- Attrition is a noisy proxy for happiness: health changes, family relocation, career changes, retirement, and external job availability all affect attrition independent of workplace satisfaction

**Counter-example search:** Any organization with a hiring freeze in this period would see attrition fall *unless* they were losing people to competitors. The hiring freeze fully explains the observed direction.

**Verdict:** The chain from "attrition fell" to "agents are happier" is broken by a confound that completely explains the attrition change. Claim is refuted under this evidence.

## Missing Information (Format Constraints)

This document format omits:
- **Timeline details:** Were the before/after periods truly symmetric in customer mix, volume, and seasonality?
- **Mechanism documentation:** How does faster response reduce repeat contacts? (Is it faster resolution, or faster deflection?)
- **Scope boundaries:** Which customer segments were included? (Enterprise vs. SMB vs. consumer; product lines; geographies)
- **Alternative explanations:** Could changes be driven by concurrent product fixes, agent training, or policy changes?
