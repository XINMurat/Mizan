# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**HARKing status:** This is a retrospective audit. The examples and figures were chosen by the team before my review, and my coverage is limited to what appears in the summary text. I had no access to the HR data, the external firm's report, the vendor white paper, or the recruiter time records.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims tiered. 0 of 6 checked against a primary source, because none was accessible. I checked internal consistency: 41→29 days is a 29% reduction, and 212 vs 208 open roles is a 2% difference.

**Bottom line:** I can't confirm that any of the six claims is proven as written. Claim 3's raw HR numbers may well be correct, but the summary uses them to imply a tool effect that the design can't support. I would not sign off on this slide as it stands.

---

### Claim-by-claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** → `[S]` + `[Y]`
Source: adopting managers say new hires "feel stronger." That is perception, with no outcome measure (performance ratings, retention, 90-day review). "Better" is never defined, so nothing can refute it. Presenting testimonials as a finding is misleading framing.
*Next step:* Define a quality-of-hire outcome now (e.g., 6-month manager rating), with a comparison group of non-tool hires and a pass threshold, before reporting anything.

**2. "It is unbiased."** → `[S]` + `[Y]`
Source: the vendor's white paper says "trained on diverse data." Training-data diversity is an input, not evidence about outcomes. No adverse-impact analysis by group appears anywhere in the summary. The 600-application audit (claim 4) can't detect bias either: if the human panels share a bias, agreement with them will look high.
*Next step:* Run a selection-rate analysis by protected group on real decisions, with a threshold set in advance (the four-fifths rule is a common screen), reviewed by an independent party.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** → `[KKE]`
Source: HR-system dates for all requisitions across 14 departments, two quarters before and after. This is a well-sourced measurement. The problem is attribution:
- There is no comparison group. If all 14 departments adopted the tool at once, there is no control.
- "Volume of open roles did not change" is not the same as "composition did not change." The freeze may have shifted recruiter effort toward faster-filling roles, or freed capacity for everything.
- The before/after windows may differ in seasonality and in the time it takes recruiters to learn a new tool.
- The summary doesn't define the start and end points of "time-to-hire."

The decline is a real observation. The implied cause is not supported.
*Next step:* Report the decline as an observation, without causal wording. To support a tool effect, compare adopting and non-adopting requisitions, or use a staggered rollout, and report time-to-hire by role type.

**4. "Blinded audit: tool 81% vs human panels 79% overlap."** → `[KKE]`
The design is good: external firm, anonymized applications, two independent panels, and a human-human baseline. Credit for that. Three problems:
- No confidence interval. With 600 applications, a 2-point gap is almost certainly within noise.
- "Consensus" is not defined. If the tool is scored against a consensus that includes the human panels, the comparison is not symmetric.
- Agreement is not accuracy. Two panels agreeing with each other doesn't mean either is correct about who will succeed.

The honest reading is "comparable to human-human agreement." It does not show "better." The presentation pairs 81% with 79% to suggest superiority, which is `[Y]` framing. I have not seen the firm's report, so this rests on the summary's figures.
*Next step:* Get the firm's full report, compute bootstrap CIs on both agreement rates, and define consensus explicitly. Then test agreement with eventual outcomes, not just with panels.

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** → `[S]`
Credit for the honest "too early to measure" note. But a prediction with no metric, no comparison cohort, no threshold, and no date is not a finding. It belongs in a roadmap, not a findings list. Note that the first cohort is 3 months old, so no retention data exists yet.
*Next step:* Preregister now: the retention metric (e.g., still employed at 12 months), the comparison cohort (pre-tool hires, same departments), the threshold, and the review date.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** → `[S]`
Source: estimates in a team meeting. There is no instrument (time logs, calendar data, ATS activity) and no baseline. Estimates made in a meeting where the tool is being promoted are not independent of the author's interest.
*Next step:* Two to four weeks of time logs before and after, with the arbiter being the logs, not recruiter recollection.

---

### Missing card
This summary is a set of positive results. It shows no outcome data, no bias analysis, no override rate (how often recruiters reject the tool's shortlist, and why), no candidates the tool screened out who were never reviewed, and no costs (vendor fees, training time, rework). The override rate and the screened-out population are especially important, since they show whether the tool is making decisions or just ranking.
