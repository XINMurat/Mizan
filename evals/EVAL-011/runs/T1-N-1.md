**Coverage:** I assessed all 6 claims from the summary and its stated sources. I could not reach the ticketing data, transcripts, vendor dashboard, or RCT data, so 0 of 6 are verified against raw sources. The judgments below rest on how each claim is measured, who measured it, and whether the numbers reconcile.

**Claim lines**

1. "Median time to first response fell from 4.2 h to 40 s" → [KKE] (Kritik Kontrol Eksik). The measurement is strong: timestamps from all 38,000 tickets, pulled by the data platform team, with no self-reporting. Two things are missing. First, "first response" is undefined. If the bot's automatic greeting counts, the 40 s figure measures the greeting, not a useful answer. Second, the comparison is pre/post with no control. Seasonality, ticket volume, and the hiring freeze that began the same month could all shift the median. Flip condition: confirm the definition, and show the same comparison on a non-bot channel or the prior-year period.

2. "Customers prefer the bot to human agents" → [S] (Speculative). Thirty transcripts read by the product team cannot measure preference. There is no denominator, no selection rule, and the reader is the product owner. No preference measure was run.

3. "The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own" → [Y] (Yanıltıcı, Misleading). The vendor defines "resolved" as the customer closing the chat window. Closing a window is abandonment as often as resolution. The vendor is also the arbiter. Flip condition: report the share of bot-handled tickets with no repeat contact within 7 days, from ticketing data, and label the vendor figure as "chat closed."

4. "The bot reduced repeat contacts" → [KKE]. This is the strongest design on the list: randomized, analyzed by a team independent of the bot. But the interval doesn't reconcile with the stated sample. The difference is 9.1 − 11.4 = −2.3 points, and the reported CI (−3.0 to −1.6) is centered correctly. The width is not. With about 2,500 customers per arm, the 95% interval is roughly ±1.7 points, not ±0.7. Reproducing ±0.7 would need about 14,500 per arm. Either the arms are much larger than 5,000 in total, or the interval used a different unit (tickets, not customers) or contains an error. Flip condition: send the arm sizes, the randomization unit, and the analysis method. If the interval reconciles, this becomes the first claim you can present as proven. The direction is credible; the precision is not yet checkable.

5. "It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year" → [S]. This is a forecast from the vendor's sales deck. It cannot be tested until next year, and the arbiter is the party selling the product. It should be registered now with a threshold, a baseline, and a finance-owned measure.

6. "Agents are happier since launch" → [Y]. No happiness measure exists. Lower attrition is a different construct, and it coincides with a hiring freeze that changes departures on its own. Presenting attrition as happiness is a substitution.
