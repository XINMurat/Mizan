**What survives**
- Claim 3's observed decline is the most credible number in the set. Its source is population-level and it includes a volume check. It needs a control before it can be presented as the tool's effect.
- Claim 4's design (blinded, external, two panels) is sound. The numbers only support parity, and that is still a useful result.
- The volume check in claim 3 is a real control. It is one of the few things in the summary that anticipates an objection.

**Next steps, in order of board risk**
1. **Claim 2 (bias):** This is the most serious exposure. Recruitment screening is high-risk under the EU AI Act and draws scrutiny in many jurisdictions. Before the board sees "unbiased," run an adverse-impact analysis: selection rates by gender, age, and other protected or relevant groups, with a threshold such as the four-fifths rule, locked in advance. Get the vendor's claim independently verified.
2. **Claim 3 (time-to-hire):** Present it as "observed decline, attribution pending." Then test it. Options include a difference-in-differences comparison across departments that did and did not adopt the tool, or a staggered rollout. Confirm that the time-to-hire definition (requisition open to offer accepted, or to start date) matches across both periods.
3. **Claim 4 (agreement):** Recast as "agreement at parity with human panels." If the board wants superiority, add a validity measure, such as a later performance or retention outcome, and report intervals alongside the point estimates.
4. **Claims 1, 5, 6:** Remove from the board sign-off or rephrase as open questions. For claim 1, define a quality outcome and a comparison group. For claim 5, register the retention baseline from pre-tool cohorts and a 12-month threshold now, before the data arrives. For claim 6, run a two-week time log against a pre-tool baseline.

**Suggested board framing:** "Time-to-hire fell by 12 days (41→29) across all departments over the same period, with volume unchanged. Causal attribution is under review. An external blinded audit found the tool's shortlists agree with trained human panels about as closely as the panels agree with each other. Bias and quality-of-hire evaluations are in progress."
