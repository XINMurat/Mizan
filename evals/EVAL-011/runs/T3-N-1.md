# Audit: Hiring screening tool board summary

**Bottom line:** I can't confirm that all six claims are proven. One is proven as a description (claim 3), and only if its definitions check out. Claim 3 is also the one that becomes misleading if presented as the tool's effect. Claims 1, 2, 5 and 6 are not supported by the evidence given. Claim 4 is probably a real measurement, but I haven't seen the report, and it shows parity, not superiority.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims checked against the summary text. I could not access the HR-system data, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, or the team-meeting notes. Every assessment below rests on how the summary describes those sources.

## Claim by claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates." → [S] — NOT PROVEN**
The only support is hiring managers saying new hires "feel stronger." That is an unmeasured impression with no outcome (performance, ramp time, retention) and no comparison group. Claim 4 doesn't rescue it, because agreement with panels is not job performance.

**2. "It is unbiased." → [Y] — NOT PROVEN**
The support is a vendor white paper saying the model was trained on "diverse data." Training-data diversity is not an outcome. No adverse-impact analysis by group is mentioned. Presenting the white paper as evidence of fairness is misleading framing. Testing this needs selection rates by group at each stage, compared with the four-fifths rule or a similar threshold, measured on the tool's real decisions.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days." → [K] for the description — PROVEN, conditional on definitions**
This is the strongest evidence in the set: all requisitions, 14 departments, HR-system dates, two quarters on each side. Before the board sees it, confirm three things:
- *Definition:* is time-to-hire measured from requisition open to offer acceptance or to start date? Is 41 and 29 a mean or a median?
- *Censoring:* are requisitions still open at period end excluded? If so, the "after" period is biased downward, because slow open roles drop out.
- *Freeze:* the volume check (212 vs 208) shows the freeze didn't change how many roles were open. It does not show that the freeze left the speed of hiring unchanged. Fewer, prioritized roles can move faster on their own.

**The causal headline is [KKE] — NOT PROVEN.** Nothing here separates the tool from the other changes that happened in the same window: the freeze, seasonality, and any process changes. The control that could flip this is a concurrent comparison, such as departments or requisitions without the tool, or a pre-trend check, run before the decline is attributed to the tool.

**4. "The tool's shortlist overlapped the panels' consensus 81%, vs 79% between the two panels." → [H] — NOT PROVEN (pending the report)**
If the firm's report says this, it is a reasonable parity result. Three limits:
- The two-point gap on 600 applications is well within noise. The standard error on each proportion is about 1.6 points, and this is a paired comparison, so the gap cannot be read as superiority without a confidence interval.
- Overlap with panel consensus measures agreement with human judgment. It does not measure candidate quality.
- The shortlist size and the blinding procedure are not stated in the summary.

It supports "the tool agrees with human panels about as well as the panels agree with each other." It does not support "better."

**5. "First-year retention will improve." → [S] — NOT PROVEN**
This is a prediction, and the first cohort is three months old. It is untestable today. It can become a registry entry with a locked threshold and a stated comparison group, but it can't be presented as an outcome.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week." → [S] — NOT PROVEN**
This is an estimate given in a team meeting. There is no baseline, no time log, and no measurement of before and after. Recruiters also have a stake in the answer. It needs a time-tracking sample or a before/after diary, with the number recorded as an estimate until then.

## Suggested board wording

- "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days over the two quarters after adoption, across all 14 departments. We have not yet isolated the tool's contribution from the concurrent hiring freeze."
- "An external blinded audit found the tool's shortlists agreed with panel consensus at roughly the same rate the two panels agreed with each other (81% vs 79%). This shows parity with human judgment, not superiority."
- "Retention and recruiter time savings are not yet measured. Both are tracked as open hypotheses."

Drop "picks better candidates" and "unbiased" until they have outcome-based evidence.

## Next steps (ordered by risk to the board)

1. **Bias test (highest risk).** Run adverse-impact analysis of the tool's decisions by group, before the board sees "unbiased" anywhere.
2. **Claim 3 definitions.** Confirm the time-to-hire definition, mean or median, and how open requisitions were handled at period end.
3. **Claim 3 control.** Find a comparison group that did not adopt the tool, or check pre-adoption trends, before attributing the drop to the tool.
4. **Claim 4 report.** Get the external firm's report and its confidence intervals, and ask whether the panels were blinded to the tool's output.
5. **Registry.** I can seed claims 1, 2, 5 and 6 as registry entries, each with a locked threshold, a named arbiter, and a refutation condition, so the board sees them as open questions rather than facts.

What is the source of the 41 and 29 figures? Are they medians or means, and were requisitions still open at the end of the second quarter included?
