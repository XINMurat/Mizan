# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**Bottom line: don't sign off on this as written.** None of the six claims is proven in the form the board would read it. Claim 3 has the strongest data, but as written it attributes the drop to the tool with no control. The other five rest on a vendor's wording, perception, an untested prediction, or a comparison that doesn't answer the question the board will ask.

## 2. Counter-example sweep
- **Claim 2 (unbiased):** I searched the summary for any subgroup, adverse-impact, or override breakdown. None present. Source documents were not accessible, so this is an empty result from the text, not from the data.
- **Claim 3 (speed):** I searched for anything that would explain a fall without the tool (process change, seasonality, freeze effects, definition changes). None addressed in the summary.
- **Claim 1 (better candidates):** no disconfirming outcome data exists in the document, which is itself the finding.

## 3. Hit rate
Not applicable. The summary contains no prediction record for the tool. The only accuracy-like figure (claim 4) is agreement with humans, which is not a hit rate against outcomes.

## 4. The missing card
This format shows only favourable measures. Missing from the summary:
- **Quality-of-hire outcomes** for tool-selected vs. otherwise-selected candidates.
- **Rejections by group** and the adverse-impact ratio.
- **Recruiter overrides** and how often the tool's shortlist was changed.
- **Who was screened out** and how fast the screening happened (the speed gain and the screening decisions may be the same thing).
- **Vendor cost, contract terms, and data-retention rules.**
- **Adoption timing** relative to the other process changes.

## 5. Structural diagnosis
The summary uses the strongest figure available for each claim, and a board summary format has no slot for "unmeasured." Each sentence is written by someone who owns one part of the story: the HR analyst owns the time data, the vendor owns the bias claim, and the managers own the anecdote. No single author needed to overstate anything for the combined document to read as more proven than it is. This is a format and selection effect, not evidence of intent.

## 6. What survives
- **Claim 3, descriptive part:** the 41 → 29 day fall is plausible and well-sourced as described. Once the extract is checked, it can likely move to `[K]` as a descriptive statement. It will still not support the attribution.
- **Claim 4, source type:** an external blinded audit with a third-party arbiter is the right instrument for this question. The design is good; the reporting of it is the problem.
- **Claim 6, direction:** a plausible benefit with a cheap way to test it.

## 7. Reachability
Not applicable: this is a non-software target.

## 8. Conjunctions (checklist item 13)
- **Speed × screening.** If the tool screens out candidates earlier, the time-to-hire gain could come from fewer candidates reviewed, which would also reduce quality (claim 1) and concentrate any bias (claim 2) at the point where the tool acts. Unchecked. The speed result cannot be used to support claim 1 until the rejection rate and shortlist size before and after are compared.
- **Tool × hiring freeze.** The freeze may have changed the candidate pool or approval steps during the same window. Unchecked.

## 9. Domain probe (asking, not inventing)
I can't write the realistic cases for this process myself. The owner (HR lead or recruiting ops) should answer these before the board:
- Do internal transfers, rehires, or career-gap applicants go through the tool the same way?
- Are accommodation requests and non-standard CVs (non-English, non-linear) handled, and by whom?
- What happens when a requisition is cancelled or re-opened mid-screen?
- What happens when a recruiter overrides the tool, and is the override recorded?
- Who can see the tool's score and rationale: candidates, hiring managers, auditors, or an export?

Record the answers and who gave them. Cases written after a problem is found don't count as coverage.

## 10. Next steps before tomorrow
1. **Replace claim 1 and claim 2 wording** with "Unvalidated; outcome and bias testing scheduled" (see suggested wording below). Do not present them as findings.
2. **Claim 3:** get the HR extract and confirm the clock definition and the adoption date. Ask whether any department did not adopt the tool, or staggered adoption, to create a comparison. Report as "time-to-hire fell 41 → 29 days over the period; effect of the tool not yet separated from other changes."
3. **Claim 4:** ask the external firm for the paired comparison (each panel against the consensus that excludes it, and the tool against the same consensus) with confidence intervals and the shortlist definition.
4. **Claim 5:** state as a prediction with a date and a baseline. Do not present it as an expected result.
5. **Claim 6:** label as "recruiter estimate; time diary under way."
6. **Bias:** commission an adverse-impact analysis by group, using the vendor's data but an independent analyst, before any "unbiased" statement appears in board material.

**Suggested board wording:**
> Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days over the two quarters after adoption (HR system, all requisitions). Other process changes in the same period have not yet been ruled out. An external blinded audit found the tool's shortlist agreed with the expert panel consensus at 81%, compared with 79% agreement between the two panels (report pending a paired analysis with confidence intervals). Quality-of-hire, bias, and retention outcomes are under evaluation and are not yet reported.

- **H1 — Tool improves quality-of-hire** `[S]` `[önkayıt / preregistered: DATE]`. Formal: hires selected by the tool have higher 6-month performance ratings than matched hires from the prior process. Arbiter: third_party (HR analytics, not the vendor). Threshold: to be locked before data collection. Refutation: difference ≤ 0 with CI excluding the minimum meaningful effect. Data status: not_collected. Stopping rule and exclusion rule: to be written before collection.
- **H2 — Tool is not adversely impacting any protected group** `[S]`. Formal: selection rate ratio for each group vs. the highest-rate group ≥ 0.8. Arbiter: third_party. Refutation: any group ratio < 0.8.
- **H3 — Time-to-hire reduction is attributable to the tool** `[KKE]`. Formal: adopting departments show a larger reduction than non-adopting or staggered-adoption departments. Arbiter: instrument (HR system). Refutation: no difference between groups.
- **H4 — Tool shortlist matches consensus at least as well as humans match each other** `[KKE]`. Formal: paired agreement difference ≥ −5 points with 95% CI. Margin to be locked before the analysis is re-run. Arbiter: third_party (external firm).
- **H5 — First-year retention improves** `[S]`. Formal: 12-month retention for tool-era cohorts exceeds the mean of the previous three cohorts. Arbiter: instrument (HR system). Matures around 12 months after the first hire.
- **H6 — Recruiters save ≥ 10 hours per week** `[H]`. Formal: time diary over two weeks, tool vs. matched non-tool weeks. Arbiter: instrument (time log, not self-estimate).
