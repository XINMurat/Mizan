**Mizan audit: six board claims (text review only, retrospective).** I checked the internal consistency of each claim and its stated source. I did not access the ticketing data, the vendor dashboard, or the transcripts, so the tags describe what the text supports, not what the underlying data shows. This is a retrospective review, and I chose the claims from the draft summary, so it cannot establish coverage of the bot's full record.

**1. Median first response fell from 4.2 h to 40 s.** `[K]` (with a wording fix)
Source: ticketing-system timestamps, all 38,000 tickets, 10 weeks either side, pulled by the data platform team. The measurement is direct and the source is independent of the bot team. The before/after design has no control group, so seasonal or volume changes are possible, but a drop of this size is unlikely to come from them alone.
*Wording risk:* if the bot's automatic acknowledgment is what counts as "first response," the board will read it as faster help. Say "median time to first automated reply."
*Next step:* reword, and add the ticket-volume comparison for the same weeks in the prior year.

**2. Customers prefer the bot to human agents.** `[S]` / `[Y]`
Source: the product team's reading of 30 transcripts. Transcripts do not record preference, the sample was not described as random, and the author is the team that built the product. Thirty hand-read chats cannot support a claim about customer preference.
*Next step:* add a post-chat preference question, or run a randomized comparison with a stated preference outcome.

**3. The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own.** `[Y]`
Source: vendor dashboard, where "resolved" means the customer closed the chat window. Closing a window does not show that the problem was solved. The vendor is also the source of the metric.
*Next step:* report "chat closed without handoff" instead. For resolution, use no repeat contact within 7 days, or a sampled independent review.

**4. The bot reduced repeat contacts (9.1% vs 11.4%, 95% CI −3.0 to −1.6 points).** `[KKE]`
This is the strongest evidence in the set: randomized, 5,000 customers, analysed by a team outside the bot team. The point estimate (−2.3) is the midpoint of the reported interval, so the arithmetic is consistent. The interval width is not.
With about 2,500 customers per arm and rates near 10%, the standard error of the difference is about 0.86 points, which gives a 95% CI of roughly −4.0 to −0.6. The reported interval is about 2.4 times narrower than a simple two-proportion calculation from the stated sample size would give.
*What would resolve it:* the analytics team should confirm the per-arm n, the unit of randomization (customer or contact), and whether the interval is clustered, covariate-adjusted, or computed some other way. If the true n is about six times larger than 5,000, the claim stands as written. If not, the direction is probably still right, but the interval on the slide is wrong.
*Next step:* do not present the interval until the analytics team has reconciled it. Ask whether the 7-day outcome and the 6-week window were specified before the test started.

**5. It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year.** `[S]` / `[Y]`
Source: a projection in the vendor's sales deck. It is a forecast with no test and a source that gains from the sale.
*Next step:* present it as a scenario with stated assumptions, or drop it from the board summary.

**6. Agents are happier since launch.** `[KKE]`
Source: none. There is no survey. Lower attrition coincides with a hiring freeze that started in the same month, so the freeze is a competing explanation that has not been ruled out.
*Next step:* run a short agent survey with a pre-launch baseline, and compare attrition between teams with high and low bot exposure, not just before and after.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims checked at the text level. Claim 1 is supported by its source, claim 4 is supported in design but its interval must be reconciled, and claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 are not supported as written.

**Recommendation for tomorrow:** one claim (1) can be shown with a wording change. Claim 4 is the strongest evidence but should not be shown until its interval is checked. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 should be removed or reworded as hypotheses.
