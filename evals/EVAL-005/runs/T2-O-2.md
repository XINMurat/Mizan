## Atomized Claims & Evidence Tiers

### Claim 1: Recall 0.94 on held-out 2025 data (independent audit team, unseen split)

**Claim atomized:**
- Specific metric: recall = 0.94
- Evaluation condition: held-out 2025 data
- Arbiter: independent risk-audit team
- Separation: modelling team did not see the test split

**Evidence required:**
- Actual held-out dataset (confirmed unseen by modelling team)
- Evaluation run by risk-audit team (documented)
- Result snapshot showing recall=0.94

**Accessible in audit materials:** No. Claim stated; no evaluation report, dataset documentation, or audit-team sign-off provided.

**Tier: `[H]` — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

The separation of auditor and modeller is sound methodology. The numeric threshold is locked. But the only evidence present is the claim itself. If the risk-audit team's report exists, this would promote to `[K]`; without it, the structure is plausible but unverified. Next step: produce the risk-audit team's signed evaluation report.

### Claim 2: Fraud losses fell 22% (Q3→Q4; no comparison group; Q4 usually higher than Q3)

**Claim atomized:**
- Measured difference: ΔLosses = −22%
- Period: Q3 → Q4 (calendar quarter)
- Comparison: none (model vs. baseline lost-to-time, not controlled)
- Confound flagged by team: seasonal Q4 elevation

**Evidence required:**
- Q3 fraud losses (with model live)
- Q4 fraud losses (with model live)
- Seasonal baseline: Q3/Q4 loss ratio in years prior to model launch (pre-model comparison group)

**Accessible in audit materials:** Partial. The claim includes the annual seasonal pattern ("Q4 losses are usually higher than Q3") but does not provide the historical baseline or the raw loss numbers. Without pre-model Q3/Q4 pairs, the 22% cannot be attributed to the model.

**Tier: `[KKE]` — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing**

A result exists (22% lower in Q4 vs Q3), but the confound that the team itself identified—seasonal elevation—has not been controlled. A pre-model Q3/Q4 ratio would tell whether 22% is a model effect or business-as-usual noise. The control is not speculative; it is within reach. Next step: compare Q3/Q4 loss ratios for the two years prior to model launch, compute the model-era difference against that seasonal baseline, and report both.

### Claim 3: Model is fair across customer groups (50 decisions reviewed by team; no problems seen)

**Claim atomized:**
- Scope: fairness (undefined operand—demographic parity? equalized odds? calibration?)
- Measurement: 50 decisions reviewed by modelling team
- Method: subjective visual inspection ("no problems")
- Sample: 50 (basis for sample size not stated)

**Evidence required:**
- Fairness metric (specific: e.g., max group-level false positive rate difference ≤ ε)
- Statistically justified sample size (50 is not justified by any stated threshold)
- Blind review (did modelling team know which group each decision belonged to?)
- Hit-rate reconstruction (how many "problems" would have been found if the threshold for "problem" had been set differently?)

**Accessible in audit materials:** No. Claim is stated; the review (50 decisions) is unquantified and subjective. "No problems seen" is not a measured outcome.

**Tier: `[S]` — Spekülatif / Speculative**

The review occurred (positive), but it is not testable as a fairness claim. The term "fair" is not operationalized. Eye-review of 50 cases is not a fairness audit. Selection bias: which 50? were they representative? Next step: choose a fairness metric, compute it across all customer groups with a preregistered threshold, and report the results and the sample size justification.

### Claim 4: Analysts trust the model (two senior analysts said so in a meeting)

**Claim atomized:**
- Measurement: trust (undefined)
- Arbiter: two senior analysts
- Evidence quality: reported speech ("said so")
- Sample: 2 individuals in one meeting

**Evidence required:**
- Structured trust measurement (e.g., validated survey instrument)
- Population sampled (all analysts using the model? a representative subset?)
- Stopping rule (survey until N responses? until saturation? one meeting with two people?)
- Hit rate (how many of the analysts who use the model trust it?)

**Accessible in audit materials:** No. Claim is hearsay (two anecdotes); no instrument, no population frame, no frequency data.

**Tier: `[S]` — Spekülatif / Speculative**

Positive signal (two analysts expressed trust), but no systematic evidence of adoption-level trust. Selection bias is severe: two analysts in a meeting are not a population sample. Survivorship bias: were other analysts consulted? which two, and why? Next step: conduct a brief structured survey of all analysts with regular model access (target: >80% participation); ask three binary/Likert items on trust, ease of use, and prediction accuracy; report count and percentage. Threshold: ≥70% "trust" rating to tier as `[K]`.

### Claim 5: False-positive rate (1.8%) lower than old rules engine (4.6%), shadow mode, 3 months live traffic, platform-logged

**Claim atomized:**
- New system FP rate: 1.8%
- Old system FP rate: 4.6%
- Comparison method: shadow mode (both systems evaluated on same data)
- Data: 3 months of live traffic
- Arbiter: payments platform (logged; runtime)
- Separation: platform made the measurement, not modelling team

**Evidence required:**
- Shadow-mode logs (both systems running in parallel on production traffic)
- False-positive definition (consistent between systems; in this domain, likely "flagged fraudulent but was legitimate")
- Sample size in log (number of transactions evaluated)
- Confidence intervals or statistical test (is 1.8% vs 4.6% significant, or within noise?)

**Accessible in audit materials:** Stated as "logged by the payments platform." The claim includes strong metadata (two systems, same data, same time window, external arbiter). However, the audit has not seen the logs themselves.

**Tier: `[H]` — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

The structure is sound: independent measurement, apples-to-apples comparison (shadow mode), runtime arbiter. But "logged by the payments platform" is asserted, not verified in this audit. If the platform logs exist and the 1.8% vs 4.6% figures check out, this would be `[K]`. The claim is specific enough to be falsifiable. Next step: retrieve the shadow-mode log from the platform, confirm the date range (3 months as stated), confirm FP definitions match, and report the sample size and any confounds in the traffic composition (e.g., was one period a holiday or a fraud spike?).

### Claim 6: Will scale to 10x current traffic (load-tested once, at 2x, on a laptop)

**Claim atomized:**
- Future capability claim: scalable to 10x
- Test performed: load test
- Test scale: 2x (not 10x)
- Test environment: laptop (not production hardware, networking, or database)
- Repetition: once (no replication)

**Evidence required:**
- Load test at 10x on production-grade infrastructure (cloud environment, production-like database, realistic network)
- Multiple runs (at least 2–3) to confirm consistency
- Latency, error rate, and throughput targets defined before test
- Post-test code review of any bottlenecks found at 2x

**Accessible in audit materials:** Partial. A load test exists (positive) at 2x. But the conditions (laptop, once, 2x) are far removed from the claim (10x production). The gap is flagged in the summary itself.

**Tier: `[S]` — Spekülatif / Speculative**

A positive signal (it did not crash at 2x) exists, but this is a 5× extrapolation on radically different hardware and network. Laptops have different resource profiles, no redundancy, and no database replication. A claim "will scale to 10x" requires testing at 10x or a measured analysis of the bottleneck (CPU? memory? database locks?) and a calculation showing headroom. One successful test at 2x on a laptop does not provide that. HARKing risk: was 10x chosen after the 2x test succeeded, or was it the original target? Next step: design a load test at 10x on staging infra that mirrors production, run it at least twice, and collect latency/error profiles. Threshold: P99 latency <2s, error rate <0.1%, sustained for 10 minutes under 10x peak load.

## Summary & Verdict

Six claims presented as "proven" for board sign-off. Audit result:

| Claim | Tier | Reason |
|-------|------|--------|
| 1. Recall 0.94 | `[H]` | Claim stated; evaluation report not provided. Arbiter separation sound; metric accessible. |
| 2. Loss reduction 22% | `[KKE]` | Result exists; critical seasonal confound (known but unchecked) blocks tier-K. |
| 3. Fairness | `[S]` | Eye-review of 50 cases ≠ fairness audit. No metric, no threshold, subjective assessment. |
| 4. Analyst trust | `[S]` | Two anecdotes; no survey, no population frame, no hit-rate data. |
| 5. FP rate 1.8% vs 4.6% | `[H]` | Claim structure strong (shadow mode, external arbiter, specific numbers); logs not accessed in audit. |
| 6. Scales to 10x | `[S]` | Tested at 2x on laptop; 10x claim is 5× extrapolation. Bottleneck analysis missing. |

### Missing Card

This summary cannot show:
- Which results were surprising (and thus need symmetric confound controls per the registry procedure).
- Whether thresholds (e.g., "trust," "fair," "will scale") were set before or after results were observed (HARKing risk is stated in the preamble, but individual claim timelines are not accessible).
- Hit rates: the team's track record on similar predictions is not provided.

### Recommendation to the Board

**Do not sign off as-is.** Three claims (`[S]`) are not testable in their current form. Two claims (`[H]`) are plausible but unverified in this audit (evidence exists elsewhere, not provided). One claim (`[KKE]`) has a measurable result but a known confound that can and must be checked before a production decision depends on it.

**Prioritized next steps (criticality × effort):**

1. **Claim 2 (seasonal confound): CRITICAL.** Lowest effort. Pull pre-model Q3/Q4 ratios (accounting data exists), compute seasonal baseline, re-report the model's Q3→Q4 improvement against that baseline. This resolves in one data query.

2. **Claim 1 (recall verification): HIGH.** Low effort if the audit exists. Request signed evaluation report from risk-audit team with dataset spec and held-out-set confirmation.

3. **Claim 5 (FP rate verification): HIGH.** Low effort if logs are accessible. Retrieve platform shadow logs, confirm sample size and FP definition, check for traffic-composition confounds in the date range.

4. **Claim 6 (scaling): HIGH.** Medium effort. Run load test at 10x on staging infra matching production, twice, with latency/error targets defined beforehand.

5. **Claim 3 (fairness): MEDIUM.** Medium–high effort. Operationalize "fairness" (recommend: demographic parity or equalized odds, pick one), compute across all customer groups, set preregistered threshold, report results and sample justification.

6. **Claim 4 (analyst trust): MEDIUM.** Low–medium effort. Brief survey (3–5 questions) of all analysts; target: 80% completion, 70% "trust" rating. Low-effort signal but not load-bearing for board decision.

**Board decision: conditional approval.** Model is worth deploying IF claims 1, 2, and 5 verify. Claim 2 verification is a prerequisite (seasonal baseline must rule out business-as-usual). Claims 3, 4, 6 should be re-presented after the next review cycle; they are not load-bearing for a go/no-go decision but are precursors to "analytics team adopts it daily" and "ops confidence scales with it."

## HARKing Disclosure

This audit is retrospective: the claims were chosen and framed by the team before this review, and the team lead framed the request as a "sign-off." Both facts increase selection bias and threshold-shopping risk. The audit has not seen raw data, logs, or model outputs—only the summary and the parenthetical flags. Claims that the team itself flagged as risky (Claims 2, 6) were rated lower by the audit, validating that the flags were sound; claims the team presented as proven (1, 3, 4, 5) split across four tiers. This pattern is consistent with motivated reasoning post-results, though the data are too sparse to be certain.
