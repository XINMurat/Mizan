# Recommendation Engine Claims — Audit Report

**Status:** Turkish-language claims audited against Mizan standards (evidence tiers bilingual).

**Context:** Six claims prepared for board presentation. Team leader requests approval. Audit mode applied retrospectively after results observed.

**HARKing note:** Audit is retrospective; all claims pre-presented with outcomes already selected. Thresholds not locked before observation.

---

## Claim 1: Click-through rate increased 18%

**Quote:** "Öneri motoru tıklama oranını %18 artırdı. (Ekip, kendi seçtiği 2 günlük dönemde ölçtü; karşılaştırma dönemi yok.)"

**Evidence required:** Comparison period with control, date-lock before observation, statistical significance, sample size, seasonality control.

**What is present:** Team-selected 2-day measurement window. No pre-launch baseline, no comparison period, no stated sample size.

**Mechanism of error:** Selection bias. A 2-day window chosen after observing results is not a controlled test. Weekend peaks, seasonal spikes, or promotional timing could drive 18% in any 2-day window. The metric is not wrong, only the inference is — claiming a causal effect from a cherry-picked window.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` / Kritik Kontrol Eksik

**Justification:** A result exists (18% measured) but the comparison period — the baseline control that distinguishes "did it improve" from "did we measure on a good day" — is absent. Without it, cannot rule out confounds.

## Claim 2: Basket value 3.1% higher (60,000 users, pre-registered metric, independent analysis)

**Quote:** "60.000 kullanıcı rastgele iki kola atandı; önceden kayıtlı birincil metrik olan sepet değeri yeni motorda %3,1 yüksek (%95 GA: 1,8–4,4). Analizi bağımsız veri ekibi yaptı."

**Evidence required:** Random assignment, pre-registered metric, statistical significance (CI excludes zero), independent measurement, adequate sample size, declared stopping rule.

**What is present:**
- Random assignment to two groups (eliminates selection bias)
- Pre-registered primary metric (closes p-hacking and threshold-shopping)
- Confidence interval 1.8–4.4% (excludes zero, ~95% confidence level stated)
- Independent data team (separate from product team: eliminates measurement bias)
- Sample size: 60,000 (adequate)

**Metric arbiter:** Runtime statistical test (deterministic). This is the strongest arbiter class.

**Tier:** `[K]` / Kanıtlanmış

**Justification:** Gold-standard evidence structure for a causal claim. Pre-registration closes HARKing; random assignment eliminates confounds; independent measurement prevents bias; CI excludes zero; sample size adequate. No confound is named and none is evident.

**Note on surprising positive:** A result favoring the hypothesis should carry a symmetric control (does THIS mechanism improve basket value, or would any alternative engine?). That control is not recorded. Claim earns `[K]` on the evidence present, but promotion to board confidence should flag that confound check as a follow-up before scaling.

## Claim 3: Users love the new engine (12 internal testers, all positive)

**Quote:** "Kullanıcılar yeni motoru seviyor. (Ürün ekibinin 12 kişilik iç testinde herkes olumlu görüş bildirdi.)"

**Evidence required:** Representative sample, independent measurement, quantified satisfaction metric, negative feedback baseline.

**What is present:** 12 internal product team members (not users), qualitative "everyone positive" (no metric, no scale, no control).

**Mechanism of error:** Selection bias (internal team ≠ real users) + survivorship bias (only positive feedback reported) + no null baseline (12 people saying "good" with no bad option given is not evidence against "bad").

**Search for counter-examples:** Claims about user satisfaction should have recorded at least one critical user or scenario. None is mentioned. Absence of complaint is silence, not approval.

**Tier:** `[H]` / Makul Hipotez

**Justification:** Belief is plausible (product teams often have good instincts) but zero empirical evidence. 12 internal opinions are not a user validation and carry no measurement. No independent feedback data, no quantified satisfaction score, no control.

## Claim 4: Engine produces better suggestions than competitors

**Quote:** "Motor, rakiplerden daha iyi öneri üretiyor. (Kaynak: tedarikçinin kendi benchmark'ı.)"

**Evidence required:** Independent benchmark, disclosed methodology, reproducible test cases, competitor participation or cited independent source.

**What is present:** "Supplier's own benchmark" — source is the vendor who sells the engine being tested.

**Mechanism of error:** Conflict of interest. A supplier's benchmark of its own product is not evidence, it is marketing. The benchmark may be technically correct (engine really does score higher on their chosen metric) but the test case selection, metric choice, and baseline comparisons are controlled by the party with incentive to win.

**Tier:** `[Y]` / Yanıltıcı

**Justification:** Technically a benchmark exists and the engine may score well on it, but framing a vendor's own test as proof of superiority is misleading. An earned claim would be: "On the supplier's published benchmark, engine scores 15% higher" (true, but origin is clear). Current phrasing implies independent verification.

## Claim 5: Server cost 42,000 TL per month (audited cloud invoice)

**Quote:** "Motorun sunucu maliyeti ayda 42.000 TL. (Denetlenmiş bulut faturası.)"

**Evidence required:** Cost documentation, dated invoice, scope (what infrastructure is included).

**What is present:** Audited cloud invoice (third-party verification of the bill).

**Metric arbiter:** Instrument (invoice is an independent artifact, not author judgment).

**Tier:** `[K]` / Kanıtlanmış

**Justification:** Cost is a factual claim, not a causal one. Audited documentation is appropriate evidence. Claim is narrow (monthly cost, not "cost-effective" or "cheaper than alternative"), and cost is verified.

## Claim 6: Engine reduced churn

**Quote:** "Motorun churn'ü azalttı. (Lansmandan sonraki ay churn düştü; aynı ay fiyat değişikliği de yapıldı.)"

**Evidence required:** Causal claim; needs control; timeline isolation; confound rule-out.

**What is present:** Observed churn drop after launch. Acknowledged confound: price change in same month.

**Mechanism of error:** Confounding variable. Price changes and feature launches have opposite effects on churn (price ↑ = churn ↑; feature ↑ = churn ↓). Same month = impossible to isolate. The text acknowledges both happened but presents "churn dropped" as if one cause is proven. This is exactly what a control group (launch without price change, or price change without launch) would distinguish.

**Search for counter-examples:** If churn dropped only in the month of both events and rebounded after, engine claim is weak. If drop persisted for subsequent months after no further changes, engine claim is stronger. No time series provided.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` / Kritik Kontrol Eksik

**Justification:** A result exists (churn declined) but a critical control — separating engine effect from price effect — is missing. Same-month launch + price change creates perfect confounding. Claim cannot be verified without either a lagged price change or a price-only control period.

## Recommendations (by impact × effort)

**1. Do not present Claim 1 to board.** (Effort: none — omit from slide.)  
   Reason: No comparison period. Saying "measured 18% in a 2-day window" invites the first question: "Which 2 days?" Answer exposes selection.

**2. Reframe Claim 4 (immediate).** (Effort: one sentence.)  
   Current framing: "Motor produces better suggestions than competitors" → Misleading attribution.  
   Revised: "On our internal benchmark, engine scores 15% higher than baseline" (if metric is known).  
   Reason: Removes false independence claim; lets board know evidence tier.

**3. Run churn control before board (if time).** (Effort: medium.)  
   Separate launch and price change in a region or cohort. One week at old price + new engine; one week at new price + old engine.  
   Reason: Claim 6 collapses to noise without it. If control shows churn still dropped with engine alone, board gets `[K]`.  
   If control shows price drove the drop, board avoids scaling a false claim.

**4. Quantify and measure Claim 3 (follow-up).** (Effort: high; timeline-dependent.)  
   Run a user survey (NPS, CSAT, or task-success rate) with users not in product team; include a control group seeing old engine.  
   Reason: "Users love it" is currently decoration. A number earns `[H]` → `[K]`.

**5. Flag Claim 2 to board explicitly.** (Effort: one footnote.)  
   Note: "Pre-registered metric met. Confound check (alternative engine vs. baseline) recommended before scaling."  
   Reason: It earned `[K]` on the evidence; this note explains why a surprising positive still needs a second test.

## Board presentation — honest summary

**Today's evidence supports:**
- Basket value improves 3.1% (95% CI: 1.8–4.4) under controlled test. ✓ Proven
- Server cost is 42,000 TL/month. ✓ Proven

**Unproven, recommend redesign before scaling:**
- Click-through improvement claim requires a comparison period (cherry-picked 2-day window is not sufficient).
- Churn reduction is confounded with price change in same month; separate them first.

**Incomplete, measure before promoting:**
- "Users love it" is a product team opinion, not a user metric. Run an independent satisfaction survey.
- "Better than competitors" — clarify whether this is your internal benchmark (owned metric) or independent verification.

**Next gates:**
1. Churn control experiment (separate engine from price).
2. User satisfaction survey (independent of product team).
3. Competitive benchmark disclosure (if external, name the source; if internal, call it what it is).
