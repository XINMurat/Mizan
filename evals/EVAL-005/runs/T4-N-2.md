# Security Product Claims — Audit Report

## Summary

The board presentation contains 6 claims of varying credibility. Two survive audit (`[K]`); four fail on selection bias, control deficiency, or source weakness. **Do not sign off without addressing claims 1, 2, 4, and 6.**

---

## Claims and Evidence Tiers

### 1. "The product blocks 99.9% of attacks."

**Source:** Vendor's own lab test; attack set chosen by vendor.

**Tier:** `[Y]` — Misleading

**Justification:** The 99.9% figure is selection bias disguised as precision. The vendor chose which attacks to test, so the attack set is a curated example, not a representative sample. This is "three confirming anecdotes" dressed in a percentage. The actual effectiveness against real-world attack diversity is unknown. A measured rate over a diverse external attack corpus would be required for `[K]`.

**Next step:** Commission an independent security firm to measure block rate over 30 days of real inbound attack traffic, with no filtering of the sample. If the number stays above 95%, report it as externally verified.

---

### 2. "We have had no breaches since we installed it."

**Source:** Four months; no breach detected.

**Tier:** `[H]` — Plausible hypothesis

**Justification:** Absence of observed breach is not evidence the product caused the absence. Confounds include: staffing changes, user behavior, attackers moving to other targets, other security controls added in the same window, and the short timeframe (four months). A comparable baseline rate from the 4-month period *before* installation is required to separate signal from noise. No such comparison is supplied.

**Next step:** Measure breach rate in the 12 months before and 12 months after installation, with all other variables held constant (or listed). If the rate genuinely fell, the product may be part of the cause; if constant, the claim is unfounded.

---

### 3. "Mean time to detect fell from 19 to 6 hours, measured by the external SOC provider over 11 months before and 11 after, with the same incident definitions and no other tooling change."

**Source:** External SOC provider measurement; 11 months before, 11 months after; same incident definitions; change log attached.

**Tier:** `[K]` — Proven

**Justification:** This claim meets the bar for direct evidence. The arbiter (external SOC provider) is independent, not vendor or author. The metric is numeric and specific (19 → 6 hours). The timeframe is long enough to smooth noise (11 months each direction). Key confounds are controlled: same definitions, no other tooling change stated in change log. The improvement is not marginal; a 68% reduction is substantial. No critical control is missing.

**Next step:** None required for this claim. Promotion of this single result to the headline is defensible.

---

### 4. "Staff find it easy to use."

**Source:** The IT team that chose it says so.

**Tier:** `[H]` — Plausible hypothesis

**Justification:** Self-report by a party with conflict of interest (the team that selected it has reputational investment in it being a good choice). "Easy to use" is not measured; no objective metric (task completion time, error rate, support tickets per user) is supplied. A survey of a representative sample without the team's presence, or measured usability data (e.g., mean task completion time vs. prior tool), would be required for `[K]`.

**Next step:** Run an independent usability study with quantified outcomes (task time, error count, SUS score). Alternatively, measure support ticket volume and resolution time before and after, with tickets tagged by reason.

---

### 5. "The licence costs $84,000 a year."

**Source:** Signed contract.

**Tier:** `[K]` — Proven

**Justification:** Direct factual claim with documentary evidence. A signed contract is a primary record that settles the question. No ambiguity or confound.

**Next step:** None.

---

### 6. "It protects against zero-day attacks."

**Source:** Marketing brochure.

**Tier:** `[S]` — Speculative

**Justification:** Marketing brochures are sources of promotion, not evidence. A zero-day attack is unknown to defenders by definition; how it is tested against is unexplained. The claim is not falsifiable as stated (no threshold, no test protocol, no arbiter). A marketing claim without evidence is decoration, not a tier-checkable hypothesis.

**Next step:** Replace this with a specific, testable claim — e.g., "mean time to patch zero-day-class variants fell to N days" measured independently. Or bin it and do not present to the board.

---

## Board Recommendation

**Do not sign off.** The presentation mixes proven facts (3, 5) with uncontrolled claims (1, 2, 4) and a non-falsifiable assertion (6). Claims 1, 2, and 4 require baseline data or external measurement; claim 6 is not auditable as written. Removing or correcting these four claims is the minimum bar for board-ready evidence.

The single strong claim — mean time to detect improvement (claim 3) — is genuine and should remain the headline if external measurement is confirmed.
