# Care App Board Summary — Evidence Audit

**Auditor note:** This is a retrospective audit of a prepared board summary where claims are presented without qualification. The format itself pressures toward uniform acceptance, and the parenthetical evidence notes contain confessions of the very problems that should demote the claims below [K]. This audit applies the full procedure despite the brevity request.

**HARKing status:** These claims were written before board presentation; the trial in claim 2 was preregistered. Claims 1, 3–6 are presented without preregistration and contain no third-party evidence controls.

---

## Audit findings

**Claim 1: The app reduces hospital readmissions by 30%.**

**Problem:** The parenthetical itself discloses the fatal confound: "Users compared with non-users; users were younger and healthier." This comparison is not controlled — younger, healthier populations have lower readmission rates regardless of app use. The claimed 30% reduction is observational selection bias, not a treatment effect. The confound is not a minor caveat; it is the entire explanation.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` — Critical control missing. The fundamental comparison lacks any adjustment for age and health status.

**Next step:** Ignore this claim in the board summary. Only claim 2 addresses readmissions in a controlled way.

---

**Claim 2: In a preregistered randomized trial (n = 1,240) with an independent data monitoring board, 90-day readmission was 11.2% with the app vs 13.9% with usual care.**

**Assessment:** Preregistration locks the hypothesis before results; randomization controls confounds; n=1,240 is adequate for a difference of 2.7 percentage points; independent data monitoring board provides external oversight. The absolute reduction (2.7 pp) is the actual finding; the relative reduction implied by "30%" in claim 1 is not derived from this trial — it refers to the uncontrolled comparison.

The claim as stated is accurate and properly sourced.

**Tier:** `[K]` — Proven. This is a preregistered, externally monitored trial.

**Caveat:** Only 2.7 pp reduction is supported, not 30%. The trial shows the app works; the magnitude claim does not.

---

**Claim 3: Doctors recommend it.**

**Evidence:** "The company's own medical advisory board endorses it."

**Problem:** A company's internal advisory board endorsing the company's own product is not independent evidence. Advisory boards are established to advise the company, not to audit it. This is internal validation, not external judgment. The claim conflates "an advisory board exists and was told about the app" with "doctors recommend it" (which implies external, arm's-length endorsement).

**Tier:** `[S]` — Speculative. Without independent physician surveys, prescribing data, or external medical organizations' positions, this is not evidence.

**Next step:** Commission an independent physician survey or report prescribing adoption rates.

---

**Claim 4: Patients love it.**

**Evidence:** "4.8 stars in the app store."

**Problem:** App store ratings are subject to severe selection bias — people who love an app are more likely to rate it; people who deleted it cannot rate it; detractors may leave one-star reviews but in lower proportion. The sample is self-selected, not representative of all users. The claim also lacks context: How many reviews underpin the 4.8 stars? 100 reviews or 10,000? A 4.8 with 50 reviews is different from 4.8 with 50,000.

The rating exists and is high, but it is not evidence that patients broadly "love" the app.

**Tier:** `[H]` — Plausible hypothesis. Users who rate the app give it a high score, but selection bias is severe and sample size is unspecified.

**Next step:** Report the number of reviews, the distribution (what % gave 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 stars), and ideally measure retention and active-use duration as alternative indicators of satisfaction.

---

**Claim 5: The app has 52,000 monthly active users.**

**Evidence:** "Analytics platform, standard MAU definition."

**Assessment:** Monthly active users is a defined, measurable metric. If the analytics platform is correctly configured and the standard definition is applied, this is a factual measurement. The claim is specific and sourced.

**Tier:** `[K]` — Proven. The metric is directly measurable.

---

**Claim 6: It is safe.**

**Evidence:** "No adverse events were reported to the company's support email."

**Problem:** Absence of spontaneous reports to a support email is not evidence of safety. This is passive surveillance, which detects only serious, obvious events reported by motivated users. It does not detect:
- Mild or transient harms (headache, dizziness, sleep disruption from app use)
- Events users attribute to other causes
- Harms that develop over time
- Users who simply stop using the app without reporting
- Events users experience but don't contact support

A claim of safety requires active adverse-event monitoring, regular check-ins with users, symptom screening, or medical record review. None of these are mentioned.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` — Critical control missing. Passive email reporting is not a safety surveillance system.

**Next step:** Implement active adverse-event monitoring (surveys, interviews, or medical records) before making any safety claim.

## Summary for the board

| # | Claim | Tier | Status |
|---|-------|------|--------|
| 1 | Reduces readmissions by 30% | `[KKE]` | Uncontrolled; confound admitted |
| 2 | RCT shows 2.7 pp reduction | `[K]` | **Approved; this is the evidence** |
| 3 | Doctors recommend it | `[S]` | No independent evidence |
| 4 | Patients love it | `[H]` | High app-store rating; selection bias |
| 5 | 52,000 monthly active users | `[K]` | **Approved** |
| 6 | It is safe | `[KKE]` | No active safety monitoring |

**Recommendation:** Present the board with only claims 2 and 5. Claims 1, 3, and 6 should not appear in the summary — each admits or requires evidence controls that are absent. Claim 4 can appear only if sample size and rating distribution are reported, and the language downshifted from "love" to "high app store rating."

The trial result is genuine and meaningful. Do not weaken it by surrounding it with unsupported claims.
