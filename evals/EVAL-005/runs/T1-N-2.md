# Yeni Öneri Motoru — Denetim Raporu
## New Recommendation Engine — Audit Report

**Denetim statüsü / Audit status:** Retrospective analysis of pre-launch claims presented for board approval.

**HARKing deklarasyonu / HARKing declaration:** This audit is retrospective: we examine claims after implementation outcomes are partially visible (launch period results are in; full business impact is still developing). The audit does not predict board approval timing or acceptance thresholds — it assesses evidential tiers only.

---

## 1. Claim Atomization and Evidence Inventory

Six factual assertions presented for board approval. Each requires separate verification.

---

## 2. Tiered Findings

### **Claim 1: Recommendation engine increased click rate by 18%**

**Formal statement:** Metric improved by 18 percentage points.

**Evidence cited:** "Ekip, kendi seçtiği 2 günlük dönemde ölçtü; karşılaştırma dönemi yok" (Team measured it in a self-chosen 2-day period; no comparison period).

**Verification steps:**
- No pre-registered measurement window (post-hoc date selection introduces HARKing risk)
- No control group, baseline, or prior-period comparison
- 2-day window is too short to exclude noise, learning effects, or cyclical patterns
- Click rate is engagement metric; sensitive to time-of-day and user-cohort biases

**Confounds not addressed:**
- Network effects: did new press/comms drive traffic?
- Selection: did team choose the period because results looked good?
- Seasonality: are these peak traffic days vs. typical?
- Competing features: did another launch coincide?

**Tier: `[H]` — Makul hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

"Self-measured window without control period cannot support published claim." The mechanism is unclear (traffic surge vs. engine improvement), and the threshold (18% improvement) is stated but not independently verified.

---

### **Claim 2: Pre-registered basket value metric: 3.1% higher, 95% CI [1.8–4.4]**

**Formal statement:** Basket value in new engine cohort is 3.1 percentage points higher than control, with 95% confidence interval 1.8–4.4.

**Evidence cited:** "60.000 kullanıcı rastgele iki kola atandı; önceden kayıtlı birincil metrik olan sepet değeri yeni motorda %3,1 yüksek (%95 GA: 1,8–4,4). Analizi bağımsız veri ekibi yaptı." (60,000 users randomly assigned to two cohorts; pre-registered primary metric: basket value 3.1% higher in new engine, 95% CI 1.8–4.4. Analysis by independent data team.)

**Verification steps:**
- Random assignment of 60k users eliminates selection bias for the cohort allocation
- Pre-registered metric locks threshold before results were observed (satisfies R18 requirement: data-status = seen but threshold pre-locked)
- Independent analysis team is institutional third-party arbiter (satisfies R9: named arbiter class)
- CI excludes zero; lower bound (1.8%) is material and non-trivial
- Confidence interval appropriate for large-N experiment

**Controls present:**
- Randomization provides symmetric control (A/B group comparison)
- No confound variables noted; independent team selected metric and threshold

**Weakness noted (not disqualifying):**
- No stated stopping rule (R18 requires stopping rule; if "run until n=60k" was preset, that is compliant; if flexible, see R18)
- One metric survives; other metrics not reported (selection possible if multiple metrics tested)

**Tier: `[K]` — Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

"Pre-registered random trial, independent third-party analysis, threshold met, CI excludes zero, sample size adequate. Threshold-meeting evidence sufficient for tier K on this metric."

---

### **Claim 3: Users love the new engine**

**Formal statement:** User satisfaction is high / positive.

**Evidence cited:** "Ürün ekibinin 12 kişilik iç testinde herkes olumlu görüş bildirdi." (Product team's 12-person internal test: everyone gave positive feedback.)

**Verification steps:**
- Sample size: n = 12 (internal team members)
- No control group; no pre-launch baseline; no external or representative cohort
- Selection bias: internal team members may have motivation to view product favorably
- Measurement: "olumlu görüş / positive feedback" is subjective and unquantified
- No metric (NPS, rating scale, engagement, retention) supplied; no threshold defined

**Missing confounds:**
- Did feedback come from people who chose to opt in?
- Did briefing influence their responses (priming)?
- No counterfactual: what feedback did competitors' engines receive from the same group?

**Tier: `[S]` — Spekülatif / Speculative**

"Unquantified subjective feedback from small, non-representative, self-selected internal sample. No metric, no control, no threshold. Interesting signal but not testable at current form. Upgrade to [H] only if a quantified metric (NPS ≥ X, retention ≥ Y) is pre-registered and tested on external users."

---

### **Claim 4: Engine produces better recommendations than competitors**

**Formal statement:** Recommendation quality exceeds competitors.

**Evidence cited:** "Kaynak: tedarikçinin kendi benchmark'ı." (Source: supplier's own benchmark.)

**Verification steps:**
- Arbiter: supplier (engine vendor)
- Bias: supplier has financial and reputational incentive to show own engine favorably
- Control: no independent benchmark, no third-party measurement
- Metric: not specified ("better" is undefined; by what measure?)
- Threshold: not specified
- Reproducibility: benchmark criteria and methodology not shared for verification

**Red flags:**
- Self-serving benchmark: this is the weakest arbiter class (`author`; see R9)
- No symmetric control ("worse" outcome is unavailable or unreported)
- No prior art or competitive analysis disclosed
- "Benchmark" is a term of art in ML; exact protocol not stated

**Tier: `[Y]` — Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

"Supplier's own benchmark technically contains truth (engine may outperform some competitors on some metrics) but is framed to imply comprehensive superiority without independent verification. Authority is the claimant. Arbiter class = `author` blocks tier K (R9); claim remains promotion, not evidence. Upgrade path: commission independent benchmark by third party, or pre-register reproducible test on held-out evaluation set."

---

### **Claim 5: Server cost is 42,000 TL per month**

**Formal statement:** Monthly server cost = 42,000 TL.

**Evidence cited:** "Denetlenmiş bulut faturası." (Audited cloud bill.)

**Verification steps:**
- Source: audited bill (external financial document)
- Arbiter: accounting/audit function (institutional third-party, independent of engine team)
- Metric: objective (TL spent, from invoice)
- Reproducibility: cost is recurring and measurable each period

**No confounds or missing controls:**
- Cost is cost; no causal claim being made
- Audit trail is documented in cloud provider's billing system
- No threshold mentioned but cost is fact, not forecast

**Tier: `[K]` — Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

"Objective cost stated in audited financial document. Arbiter is independent (accounting). Tier K on factual cost assertion."

---

### **Claim 6: Engine reduced churn**

**Formal statement:** User churn rate declined after engine launch.

**Evidence cited:** "Lansmandan sonraki ay churn düştü; aynı ay fiyat değişikliği de yapıldı." (Churn dropped in month after launch; same month a price change also occurred.)

**Verification steps:**
- Observation: churn ↓ after launch month
- Control: ABSENT — price change happened the same month
- Causal attribution: CONFOUNDED

**Critical confound:**
- Price change is a known driver of churn (price up → churn up; price down → churn down)
- No statement of whether price went up or down (inferred: if churn dropped and price changed, likely price went down)
- Symmetric control needed: measure churn change in cohort WITHOUT price change, or measure separately in A/B where price is held constant

**Missing evidence:**
- No pre-registered metric or stopping rule
- No statement of churn magnitude (churn ↓ 0.1% vs. ↓ 5% are different)
- Temporal correlation (both in same month) is not causation
- Rival hypotheses: price change alone explains the drop

**Tier: `[KKE]` — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical Control Missing**

"Causal claim (engine reduced churn) cannot be isolated from confounding price change. A symmetric control is possible (churn trend in cohort without price change, or experimental separation of price from engine effect) but was not run. Result exists but the confound-check is mandatory before claiming causation. Pre-register control: measure churn change when only engine changes, price held constant."

## 3. Missing Card — What This Board Summary Cannot Show

- **Cost-benefit analysis:** cost (42k TL/month) is stated; benefit monetization is not (does 3.1% basket-value lift outweigh the cost? On what timeframe?).
- **Engagement trade-offs:** click rate up 18% (unverified) may indicate more noise/distraction, not more value; basket value up 3.1% is the relevant metric but tells a different story.
- **Survivor bias in user feedback:** 12 internal testers cannot predict external-user satisfaction. No retention, adoption rate, or support-ticket volume disclosed.
- **Competitive threat:** benchmark claim offers no context: if competitors' engines are equally good, the advantage is temporary.
- **Failure modes:** no mention of recommendation diversity, cold-start behavior, or edge-case handling.

---

## 4. Conjunctions — Guarantee Pairs Checked

- **Price change + engine launch (claim 6):** Churn outcome guaranteed to hold? **Unchecked.** Either change can drive the outcome; pair must be separated.
- **Click rate + basket value (claims 1 & 2):** Can both improve? Hypothetically yes (more clicks + higher value per click = both rise). **Not tested jointly.** A/B showed basket value; click rate came from different measurement window. Contradiction possible but not addressed.
- **Internal satisfaction (claim 3) + external basket value (claim 2):** Do internal testers represent the broader user satisfaction reflected in metrics? **Unchecked.** 12-person opinion ≠ 60k-user cohort behavior measurement.

---

## 5. Coverage and Audit Scope

All six claims examined. Evidence assessed against available documentation. No access to underlying data, trial protocols, or full benchmark criteria — audit is at document/claim level only.

---

## 6. Recommendations (by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Do not present claim 6 (churn reduction) to the board without a symmetric control.** The price change occurred the same month; causation is masked. Either re-analyze churn in price-unchanged cohort, or pre-register a follow-up A/B and defer the churn claim. **Critical; effort = low if data exists.**

2. **Correct claim 1 (click rate) on the board slide.** State it as a self-measured observation during a non-random window, not a verified finding. If the team insists on citing it, note it was measured in a 2-day period and include the date range. Alternatively, omit it — basket value (claim 2) is the stronger evidence. **Critical; effort = minimal.**

3. **Add monetization math to claim 2.** "3.1% basket value lift on 60k users = $X monthly revenue. Server cost is 42k TL (~$Y USD). ROI is Z months." Decision-making requires this; evidence tier is separate from business impact. **High; effort = medium.**

4. **Commission independent benchmark for claim 4.** Do not present supplier's own benchmark as competitive evidence. If board asks, acknowledge the bias and offer a plan (third-party eval, published benchmark replication, or statement that competitive analysis was deprioritized). **Medium; effort = high.**

5. **Claim 3: replace subjective feedback with a metric.** If user satisfaction is a goal, pre-register NPS or CSAT among external users and run the test. "12 internal testers all positive" is not evidence. **Medium; effort = medium.**
