## Atomized Claims & Tier Assessment

### Claim 1: Recommendation engine increased click rate by 18%

**Claim (atomized):** The new recommendation engine produced an 18% increase in click rate.

**Evidence provided:** Team measured click rate over a self-selected 2-day period. No comparison period; no control group mentioned.

**Verification:**
- No baseline specified (18% vs. what previous state?).
- Time window self-selected ("2 günlük dönem" / "2-day period") — strong selection bias signal. Two days can capture anomalies, promotions, time-of-week effects, or seasonal spikes unrelated to engine quality.
- No comparison arm or concurrent control mentioned.
- No independent measurement.

**Tier:** `[H]` / Makul Hipotez  
**Justification:** Theoretical mechanism plausible (better recommendations → more clicks). Empirical threshold NOT met. Missing baseline, comparison period, and random assignment. Selection of measurement window after observing success is textbook HARKing.

---

### Claim 2: Cart value increased 3.1% (95% CI: 1.8–4.4) in new engine arm

**Claim (atomized):** Users assigned to the new recommendation engine spent 3.1% more on average (measured by cart value), with 95% confidence interval 1.8–4.4%.

**Evidence provided:**
- 60,000 users randomly assigned to two arms (A/B test structure).
- Primary metric pre-registered (stated explicitly).
- Independent data team conducted analysis (auditor ≠ implementer).
- Confidence interval reported (allows assessment of precision).

**Verification:**
- Random assignment controls for confounds.
- Sample size (60k) adequate for detecting small effects.
- Pre-registration locks threshold before observing data.
- Independent analysis team reduces author-side bias.
- Confidence interval excludes zero and is narrow relative to effect size.

**Tier:** `[K]` / Kanıtlanmış  
**Justification:** Meets preregistration standard (R9, R18). Arbiter = runtime (test framework reports statistical verdict). Threshold numeric and pre-locked. Effect crosses significance and is precise. This is the only claim in the set that matches rigorous preregistered experimentation.

---

### Claim 3: Users love the new engine

**Claim (atomized):** Users have positive sentiment toward the new recommendation engine.

**Evidence provided:** 12-person product team internal test; all gave positive feedback.

**Verification:**
- Sample is employees (internal team), not external users — severe selection bias. Product team has incentive to view their own work favorably.
- Sample size (n=12) is anecdotal, not representative.
- Measurement is subjective verbal report ("olumlu görüş" / "positive feedback"), not a scored instrument.
- No control group (did old engine also get positive feedback?).
- No statistical test.

**Tier:** `[H]` / Makul Hipotez  
**Justification:** Sentiment is real among this group. Evidence does NOT generalize to user base. Three confirming anecdotes from biased sample = selection bias (checklist item 6). This is proof of existence, not proof of acceptance.

---

### Claim 4: Engine produces better recommendations than competitors

**Claim (atomized):** The recommendation engine's quality exceeds that of competing engines.

**Evidence provided:** Supplier's own benchmark.

**Verification:**
- Conflict of interest: supplier benchmarked their own product — identical to asking a contestant to score their own competition.
- No independent verification.
- No methodology disclosed (which metrics? which competitor versions? which user scenarios?).
- No third-party arbiter.

**Tier:** `[Y]` / Yanıltıcı  
**Justification:** Technically contains truth (supplier did run *something*), but framed as "better than competitors" on basis of self-supplied evidence. Benchmark results are marketing material unless independently reproduced. This fails the third-party arbiter rule (R9 in schema 1.4).

---

### Claim 5: Engine server cost is 42,000 TL/month

**Claim (atomized):** The monthly server operating cost of the new engine is 42,000 TL.

**Evidence provided:** Audited cloud bill.

**Verification:**
- Financial fact verified by audit function (independent of product team).
- Invoiced amount is directly observable.
- No confounds (cost is cost).
- Verifiable and immutable (invoice dated).

**Tier:** `[K]` / Kanıtlanmış  
**Justification:** Direct documentary evidence from authoritative source (cloud provider invoice). No threshold-setting required; this is factual data. Arbiter = instrument (billing system return verdict deterministically).

---

### Claim 6: Engine reduced churn

**Claim (atomized):** The new recommendation engine caused a reduction in user churn.

**Evidence provided:** Churn dropped in the month after launch. Price change also implemented in the same month.

**Verification:**
- **Confound present and acknowledged:** Price change is a known alternative explanation for churn reduction. Churn is sensitive to pricing.
- No control group that received the engine WITHOUT the price change.
- No control group that received the price change WITHOUT the engine.
- Causal attribution impossible.
- This is exactly the situation Mizan procedure step 5 forbids: a surprising positive (churn reduction) without the symmetric confound control.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` / Kritik Kontrol Eksik  
**Justification:** Result exists. Confound identified. The claim "engine reduced churn" cannot be separated from "price drop reduced churn" without a secondary experiment. Procedure R26 (Mode 7 security logic applied to causality): confound + no control = the control must run before promoting to [K]. R15 (bug/feature entries must name rival hypotheses) is violated here — "price change" is the rival hypothesis, uncontrolled.

## Recommendations for Board Conversation

**Immediate:**
1. **Lead with Claim 2.** This is the only preregistered, randomized, independently analyzed claim. Present it as the core evidence, with confidence interval and sample size.
2. **Isolate Claim 6 for follow-up experiment.** The churn signal is real but confounded. Run a limited cohort that sees the engine WITHOUT the price change, or vice versa. Until then, frame churn as "observed but not yet attributed to engine."
3. **Retire Claims 1, 3, 4 from board slide.** They do not meet approval threshold. Claims 1 and 3 are anecdotal; Claim 4 is vendor self-report. Keep them as internal notes ("promising signals not yet confirmed").

**Cost/benefit framing:**
- Claim 5 is verified: cost is known. Claim 2 shows cart value +3.1%. A simple ROI sketch: if the +3.1% cart effect holds across the user base, does it exceed 42k TL/month? That is a different claim (threshold: yes if ROI > K%, no otherwise). That calculation should be preregistered before the board meeting, not after.

**Process for next cycle:**
- Document the price-change confound in your bug registry (Mode 4) and run Claim 6 as a preregistered second experiment.
- For Claim 1 (click rate), define the baseline, establish a minimum comparison period (at least 1 week), and lock the threshold before measurement.
- For user sentiment (Claim 3), commission an external user survey with a scored instrument, not internal verbal reports.
