# Security Product Claim Review — Audit Report

**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective; the claims were selected after outcomes were known and are being presented as established facts before review.

---

## Claim-by-claim analysis

**1. The product blocks 99.9% of attacks.**
- **Source:** Vendor's own lab test; attack set chosen by vendor.
- **Tier:** `[KKE]` | Kritik Kontrol Eksik | Critical control missing
- **Finding:** The attack set was chosen by the vendor, not an independent party. This introduces selection bias: the vendor has no incentive to test against attacks that expose gaps. A 99.9% figure on vendor-chosen tests is selection bias masquerading as measurement. Before promotion to `[K]`, this requires: (a) independent testing by a third party with an attack set not chosen by the vendor, or (b) testing against a published, widely-recognized attack taxonomy (e.g., MITRE ATT&CK) with published results showing how the product performs on the full taxonomy, not a curated subset. The vendor's test is evidence of vendor confidence, not evidence of product effectiveness.

**2. We have had no breaches since we installed it.**
- **Source:** Four months of operation; no breach detected.
- **Tier:** `[H]` | Makul Hipotez | Plausible hypothesis
- **Finding:** This is survivorship bias without a control. "No breach detected in four months" could mean the product works, or it could mean: (a) the organization was not attacked during those four months, (b) attacks occurred but were too subtle to detect with existing monitoring (independent of this product), or (c) the breach detection threshold is too high. Before calling this proven, require: (a) a baseline: what was the breach rate in the same four-month period BEFORE installation, (b) isolation: what other security changes occurred in the same window, (c) a control: do peer organizations using similar attack surfaces without this product have comparable breach rates? The claim conflates "no detections" with "no incidents." No active counter-example hunting documented.

**3. Mean time to detect fell from 19 to 6 hours.**
- **Source:** External SOC provider measured over 11 months before and 11 after; same incident definitions; no other tooling change; change log attached.
- **Tier:** `[K]` | Kanıtlanmış | Proven
- **Finding:** This claim is earned. The measurement is by an independent third party (the SOC provider) with aligned incentives (faster detection is better), the time windows are equal and long enough to smooth noise, the definitions were held constant, and the confound (other tooling changes) was explicitly ruled out and documented. The attached change log is the required evidence artifact. No further control needed; this meets the threshold for `[K]`.

**4. Staff find it easy to use.**
- **Source:** The IT team that chose it says so.
- **Tier:** `[Y]` | Yanıltıcı | Misleading
- **Finding:** The IT team is biased: they selected the product and have reputational investment in the choice being correct. Asking the people who picked it whether it was a good pick is not evidence of usability. The claim is technically true (they said it), but framed to imply broader satisfaction than the evidence supports. Before calling this proven, require: (a) independent usability testing with users not involved in the purchase, (b) a post-implementation survey of the full user base (not a sample of advocates), or (c) support ticket volume / user complaints as an objective proxy. "The IT team that chose it says so" is evidence of their optimism, not evidence of ease of use.

**5. The licence costs $84,000 a year.**
- **Source:** Signed contract.
- **Tier:** `[K]` | Kanıtlanmış | Proven
- **Finding:** Contract is the primary evidence; this claim is directly verifiable and unambiguous. `[K]` is earned.

**6. It protects against zero-day attacks.**
- **Source:** Marketing brochure.
- **Tier:** `[S]` | Spekülatif | Speculative
- **Finding:** Zero-day protection is not currently testable by definition (you cannot test against attacks you do not yet know about). A marketing brochure is not evidence; it is a vendor claim. This entry is interesting but remains speculative. It could move to `[K]` only if the product's mechanism is evidence-based (e.g., a published security proof or architectural property) AND independently verified. Mere vendor confidence is not evidence.

## Coverage and next steps

**Checkable claims:** 6 of 6.
**Claims earning `[K]`:** 2 (claims 3, 5).
**Claims below threshold:** 4.

The team lead's assertion that all six are proven **does not survive audit.** Four of six have material gaps:

- Claims 1, 2, 4 require independent evidence or controls not yet present.
- Claim 6 is not testable in its current form.

**Recommendation:** Before board presentation, prioritize this work order:
1. **Highest impact:** Commission independent testing of claim 1 (third-party attack lab, or testing against MITRE ATT&CK framework). Selection bias is the largest credibility risk in the summary.
2. **Medium:** Conduct post-implementation usability survey (claim 4) or gather support ticket data as a proxy. User experience failures can derail otherwise strong adoption.
3. **Lower:** Measure claim 2 against the pre-installation breach baseline (four months before) and document any other security changes in that window. This may already be in logs; do not rebuild it.
4. **Lowest:** Treat claim 6 as what it is — a marketing positioning statement, not a security claim — or, if the claim is central to the pitch, request the vendor's architecture docs or published security model. "Protects against zero-day" as stated cannot be made proven; "uses architectural principles that resist zero-day attack vectors [list]" could be.

**Honesty annex:** This audit covered all accessible claims. Three of the four failing claims are constrained by the absence of independent evidence or control conditions, not by findings of harm. The product may work well; the evidence presented does not yet establish it.
