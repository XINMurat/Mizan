# Fraud Model Claims Audit

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective review of board summary)  
**HARKing Status:** This audit is retrospective — claims were selected and framed after the model was built and results observed. The claims are presented without method preregistration (thresholds, stopping rules, comparison groups were not locked before the work).

---

## Claims Review

### Claim 1: Recall 0.94 on held-out 2025 data, independent audit team evaluation

**Tier: [H] — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Evidence:** The board summary cites an independent evaluation on held-out data that the modelling team did not see.

**Why not [K]:** We do not have access to the independent audit report itself — only a claim about it in a summary. A proven claim requires the evidence to be available for inspection. We cannot verify:
- The actual recall metric value and confidence interval
- The definition of "held-out" (temporal? stratified? by risk segment?)
- The evaluation criteria (binary classification? threshold-aware?)
- Whether this 0.94 is the primary metric or one among many tested (which would suggest selection bias)

**Next step (criticality: high, effort: low):** Obtain and review the full independent audit report before board presentation.

### Claim 2: Fraud losses fell 22% since launch (Q3 vs Q4, no comparison group)

**Tier: [Y] — Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

**The number is technically true; the framing is not.** The summary itself flags the confound: Q4 losses are typically higher than Q3 seasonally. Presenting a Q3→Q4 drop without seasonal adjustment or a control group (what would loss reduction be without the model?) implies a causal claim the data cannot support.

**Evidence:** A 22% drop exists in the numbers.

**Missing (critical controls):**
1. **Seasonal baseline:** What is the typical Q3→Q4 change? If Q4 is usually +15% higher, then a -22% change might reflect a 37% model effect, OR it could reflect data recording artifacts, product changes, or customer mix shifts. This is [KKE] — critical control missing.
2. **Comparison group:** No control group means we cannot rule out concurrent changes (e.g., new fraud patterns, payment method adoption, CX changes, competitive pressure).
3. **Post-hoc framing:** The team lead "is confident these are proven" — but the metric was selected after observing the favorable direction (Q3 baseline rather than, say, month-on-month). This is HARKing.

**Verdict:** The 22% drop is not baseline-adjusted and offers no evidence of causal impact.

**Next step (criticality: critical, effort: medium):** Before presenting, provide:
- Seasonal decomposition (Q4 vs historical Q4 losses, Q3 vs historical Q3)
- Month-on-month loss curves before/after launch
- Loss trajectory for a control segment (e.g., a customer cohort not yet using the model)

### Claim 3: The model is fair across customer groups (50 decisions reviewed, no problems seen)

**Tier: [H] — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Evidence:** A review of decisions occurred; reviewers saw none they deemed unfair.

**Why not [K]:** "Reviewed 50 decisions and saw no problems" is not a fairness metric. It is a subjective sample review vulnerable to:

1. **Selection bias:** Which 50 decisions? Were they the clearest cases (which would inflate confidence)? Stratified by customer segment and model score percentile?
2. **No metric definition:** "Fairness" is undefined. Equal false positive rates across groups? Equal false negative rates? Demographic parity? Equalized odds? Each has different implications.
3. **No measurement:** "No problems" is a qualitative judgment, not a threshold-based verdict. One reviewer's "no problem" is another's "needs investigation."
4. **Inadequate sample:** 50 decisions across multiple customer groups is too small to detect even large disparities. For a true fairness proof, you need stratified sampling (e.g., 50+ per group) and a pre-defined fairness metric.

**Missing:**
- Definition of fairness (which metric, which customer groupings)
- Measurement instrument (confusion matrix per group, with CIs)
- Blind or double-blind review
- Benchmark (what FP rate disparity would be acceptable? What was acceptable in the old rules engine?)

**Next step (criticality: critical, effort: high):** 
- Define fairness metric and threshold (e.g., "false positive rate within 5 percentage points across all segments")
- Measure: compute FP rates, FN rates, precision, recall BY customer segment using the actual model output
- Compare to baseline (old rules engine fairness on the same segments)
- Report sample sizes and CIs per segment

### Claim 4: Analysts trust the model (two senior analysts said so in a meeting)

**Tier: [S] — Spekülatif / Speculative**

**Evidence:** Two individuals reported trust verbally in a meeting.

**Why not [K] or [H]:** 
1. **Selection bias:** Were these two analysts picked because they favor the model? Was there an open poll of all analysts?
2. **No measurement:** "Trust" is unquantified. On what scale? Compared to what baseline?
3. **No sample:** Two people are anecdotal evidence, not a finding. Even "two out of two" at a meeting is selection bias (who attended? who didn't?).
4. **No instrument:** What would "distrust" sound like? Did the team ask them? Did they volunteer?

**Missing:**
- A representative survey (all analysts, not two at a meeting)
- A quantified trust scale (e.g., "I would use this as the primary decision for X% of cases")
- A comparison to baseline (trust in the old rules engine)
- Behavioral evidence (do they actually use it, or did they say yes in a meeting?)

**Verdict:** This is closer to folklore than evidence.

**Next step (criticality: medium, effort: low):**
- Structured survey: all fraud analysts, same questions, scored response
- Behavioral check: usage logs (how many analysts use the model regularly, vs. the old system?)

### Claim 5: False-positive rate 1.8% vs 4.6% (old rules engine), shadow mode, same 3 months

**Tier: [H] — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis** (borderline [K], pending verification)

**Evidence:** Specific metrics from shadow mode on the same time period, logged by the payments platform (third-party record).

**Why not [K]:** We do not have access to the actual logs or the run configuration. We have a claim in a board summary that:
- Both systems ran in shadow mode (not live, so not operational impact)
- Same 3-month window (good, controlled for seasonality)
- FP rates are 1.8% vs 4.6% (specific and favorable)
- Logged by the payments platform (third-party source, good credibility)

This COULD be [K] if:
- The logs are independently verifiable (can we see them?)
- The FP definition is pre-defined (what is a false positive? Blocked-but-legit? Flagged-but-OK?)
- The shadow mode test was not selected post-hoc (were there other shadow tests that showed worse numbers?)

**Missing or unverified:**
- Direct access to the logs or a third-party audit of the comparison
- Definition of FP (may differ between systems if thresholds differ)
- Confirmation this was the planned comparison (not one of several tried and then presented)

**Verdict:** The claim has a strong evidence trail (payments platform logging) but we have not verified it. It is plausible but not confirmed.

**Next step (criticality: high, effort: low):** Provide a screenshot or extract of the payments platform logs showing the 1.8% vs 4.6% metrics, or certify that a third party (the risk-audit team from claim 1?) has verified them.

### Claim 6: Will scale to 10x current traffic (load-tested at 2x on a laptop)

**Tier: [R] — Reddedildi / Refuted** (or [S] at best)

**The evidence directly contradicts the claim.** Load-testing at 2x on a laptop does not support a 10x scaling claim.

**Problems:**
1. **2x ≠ 10x:** Linear extrapolation from a 2x test to a 10x prediction is not engineering practice. Load growth is often superlinear (disk I/O, GC pauses, queue saturation amplify).
2. **Laptop ≠ production:** A laptop is not production infrastructure. No redundancy, no failover, no cache coherence across nodes.
3. **Shadow mode ≠ live:** None of these tests were live at scale. Live deployments encounter pathological cases the test suite missed.
4. **No stopping rule or exclusion rule:** Was there a test plan? Pass criteria? Or is "10x" a marketing guess?

**Verdict:** This claim failed its own evidence. A laptop test at 2x is [R] against a 10x production claim.

**Corrected claim tier:** [H] — "We have evidence of stable operation at 2x in a test environment; production performance at higher scales is untested."

**Next step (criticality: critical, effort: high):** Do not present the 10x claim to the board. Instead, state: "The model has been tested at 2x current traffic in shadow mode. Production scaling to 10x has not been tested and should be gated on a phased rollout with monitoring."

## Board Recommendation

**Do not present these claims as proven.** The team lead's confidence is not calibrated to the evidence. 

**What CAN be presented with confidence:**
- We have run an independent audit that produced a specific recall metric (claim 1 after verification)
- We have measured performance on held-out data (shadow mode, specific FP comparison)
- We have evidence the model performs better than the prior system on tested scenarios

**What CANNOT be presented:**
- Causality on fraud losses (no control group, seasonal confound)
- Fairness (no systematic measurement)
- Analyst trust (no measurement)
- 10x scaling (tested at 2x only)

**Cost of board approval of unproven claims:** Regulatory risk (if fairness is assumed and later audited), operational risk (if 10x scaling fails), and credibility damage when reality diverges from claims.

**Next steps (order by criticality):**
1. Drop or reframe the 10x scaling claim immediately
2. Provide seasonal decomposition for the fraud loss claim and a control group if possible
3. Verify the FP rate logs (claim 5)
4. Design and run a fairness audit with pre-defined metrics (claim 3)
5. Obtain the independent audit report (claim 1)
6. If needed, survey analysts on actual usage and trust (claim 4)
