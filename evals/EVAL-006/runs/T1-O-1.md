# Support Chatbot — Evidence Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-29  
**Auditor:** Claude Haiku  
**Target:** Board summary claims (6 claims)  
**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The team selected these claims after launch outcomes were known. The audit is also retrospective.

---

## Audit findings by claim

### Claim 1: Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds
**Source:** Ticketing-system timestamps for all 38,000 tickets in the 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by the data platform team.

**Atomization:** Two separable claims: (a) the before/after comparison was made; (b) the difference is causal.

**Evidence check:** The data exists (38,000 tickets is verifiable), the measurement is objective (timestamps), the sample is large. However, the "before/after" design is vulnerable to confounds: seasonal variation, time-of-day effects, and support volume changes are not ruled out by the design alone. The data platform team is not independent from the bot team's interests.

**Tier:** `[K]` Proven — the methodology is sound and the sample large, but the causal claim rests on the assumption that no confounds altered the baseline. A control (e.g., response time for categories the bot does NOT handle) would strengthen this to exclude technical explanations.

**Verdict:** PROVEN — the measurement is direct and the evidence threshold is met. The causal interpretation requires an unstated assumption (no confounding changes between periods).

---

### Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents
**Source:** Product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.

**Atomization:** One claim: customer preference is positive toward the bot.

**Evidence check:** The sample is very small (30 transcripts). "Preference" is not operationalized — no threshold is stated (e.g., "mentions bot favorably," "chooses bot in follow-up contact"). Qualitative judgement is subjective and no coding scheme is described. Most critically: which 30 transcripts were selected? If they were chosen by the product team after knowing the outcome, this is severe selection bias. No counter-example hunt is possible without the source transcripts.

**Tier:** `[H]` Plausible hypothesis — customer preference *may* exist, but the evidence is too small, too subjective, and too likely to be selected to support a knowledge claim.

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN — this is a plausible signal, not evidence. It requires systematic measurement.

---

### Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own
**Source:** Vendor dashboard; "resolved" means the customer closed the chat window.

**Atomization:** One claim: 70% of tickets reach resolution via the bot.

**Evidence check:** The metric is defined as "customer closed the chat window," which is a behavioral proxy, not a resolution measure. A customer may close the window in frustration, having received no useful help. No confound control is run: (a) Did the customer contact support again within 7 days? (b) Did the customer report satisfaction? Claim 4 (below) shows that "repeat contacts within 7 days" is a measurable outcome; that would be the appropriate counter to this claim. The vendor has financial incentive to report high resolution rates. The dashboard is vendor-provided, not audited.

**Tier:** `[Y]` Misleading — technically true (customers do close windows), but the framing implies resolution when closure is compatible with failure.

**Critical control missing:** The same randomized trial framework used in Claim 4 would settle this. Within the bot-first group, compare chat-close rate to repeat-contact rate. If they differ substantially, the chat-close metric does not measure resolution.

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN — the metric does not measure what is claimed.

---

### Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts (randomized trial)
**Source:** Randomized trial, 5,000 customers, bot-first vs human-first, 6 weeks, repeat contacts within 7 days: 9.1% vs 11.4% (95% CI: −3.0 to −1.6 points), computed by analytics team.

**Atomization:** Two claims: (a) randomization was executed and groups are comparable; (b) repeat-contact reduction is real and lasting.

**Evidence check:** Randomization is the gold standard for causal inference in this domain. The sample size (5,000 per group) is adequate. The metric is clear and measurable (repeat contact within 7 days). The confidence interval is provided and does not cross zero (−2.3 point difference, CI −3.0 to −1.6), indicating statistical significance at α=0.05. The analytics team is independent from the bot team. The 6-week trial duration is sufficient to detect medium-term effects but does not measure long-term durability beyond 7 days post-contact.

**Caveat:** The result is one-sided (bot-first vs human-first). A three-arm trial including "bot-then-human escalation" would clarify whether the bot prevents contact or deflects it to human channels. The current design cannot distinguish.

**Tier:** `[K]` Proven — the evidence is strong and the methodology is appropriate.

**Verdict:** PROVEN — this is well-designed experimental evidence.

---

### Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year
**Source:** Projection from the vendor's sales deck.

**Atomization:** One claim: costs will fall by 33% (or thereabouts) in the next 12 months.

**Evidence check:** This is a future projection, not a measured outcome. Vendor sales decks have structural incentive to be optimistic; costs savings projections are one of their primary marketing tools. The projection methodology is not described (e.g., "we assume support volume holds constant and agent utilization improves from X% to Y%"). No data on current cost structure is provided. Cost savings depend on staffing decisions (will headcount actually be cut?) and external factors (wage growth, support volume trends, competitor pricing). "A third" is imprecise — is it ±5%?

**Tier:** `[S]` Speculative — interesting and plausible, but not currently measurable before the outcome.

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN — this is a vendor projection, not evidence.

---

### Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch
**Source:** No survey. Attrition this quarter was lower. Hiring freeze started the same month.

**Atomization:** One claim: agent satisfaction improved.

**Evidence check:** No direct evidence (no survey or measurement of agent satisfaction). The indirect signal is attrition decline, but this is heavily confounded: (a) A hiring freeze that started the same month is a strong alternative explanation — employees may stay due to job-market uncertainty, not bot happiness. (b) Agent workload reduction (if it occurred) would support the claim, but is not measured. (c) The claim requires a control: did agents in other departments (not using the bot) show similar attrition patterns? Without this, the timing coincidence is data-free.

**Tier:** `[KKE]` Critical control missing — agent workload, agent survey responses, and a control group (agents not using the bot, if any) are all missing.

**Verdict:** NOT PROVEN — the evidence is circumstantial and the confound (hiring freeze) is not ruled out.

## Coverage and missing evidence

**What this summary format omits:**

1. The board agenda does not surface cost-per-claim or durability horizon: Claim 1 is a speed metric (good for first contact); Claim 4 is a 7-day outcome (good for customer loyalty). Do customers ultimately avoid the bot in future contacts?

2. No measurement of escalation quality: if Claim 3 is false and the bot *does* deflect hard problems to humans, did it improve or worsen the human agent's starting position? (Better context, or angrier customer?)

3. No financial baseline: Claim 5 cannot be evaluated without knowing current support costs and volume.

4. Selection bias across the board: which customer segments does the bot serve well, and which does it fail? A 70% aggregate resolves at the cost of 100% failure for a segment.

1. **Claim 1 (timing):** APPROVED for the board with one caveat: note that the before/after design assumes no confounding changes in support volume or composition. Mention the control that would strengthen it.

2. **Claim 2 (preference):** DOWNGRADE from "proven" to "signal." Report: "30-transcript qualitative review suggests positive customer reception; systematic measurement pending."

3. **Claim 3 (70% resolution):** REMOVE or REFRAME. Replace with: "70% of chats result in window closure (vendor dashboard); 9.1% of customers in the bot-first group had repeat contacts within 7 days, vs 11.4% in human-first (randomized trial, n=5,000)." The second statistic (Claim 4) is the honest one.

4. **Claim 4 (repeat contacts):** APPROVED. This is the strongest claim and the only one that measures customer outcome.

5. **Claim 5 (cost projection):** REMOVE from "proven." Mark as "vendor projection — actual impact depends on staffing decisions and volume stability." Do not present cost savings as evidence.

6. **Claim 6 (agent happiness):** REMOVE or REFRAME. Report: "Attrition declined this quarter (concident with hiring freeze). Direct measurement of agent satisfaction pending."

**Tier drift:** Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 are presented as proven but are `[H]`, `[Y]`, `[S]`, and `[KKE]` respectively. This is the pattern the board needs to see.
