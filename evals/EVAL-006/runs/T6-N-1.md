# Audit Report: Route Optimization Claims

**Audit Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Target:** Route optimization board summary (6 claims)  
**Mode:** Audit Mode (retrospective)  
**HARKing Status:** Retrospective analysis; examples selected after outcomes were observed. Some claims reference data collection methods chosen post-facto relative to stated results.

---

## Summary

Of 6 claims presented to the board, 2 survive audit as proven. Three lack independent verification or face confounds that undermine them. One is untestable speculation. The board summary conflates proven findings with self-reported or vendor-supplied assertions without attribution.

---

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### Claim 1: Daily fuel consumption per vehicle decreased 9%
**Tier: `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

**Claim:** "Araç başına günlük yakıt tüketimi %9 azaldı"

**Evidence structure:**
- Treatment: Random half of fleet ran new routes for 8 weeks
- Control: Other half ran old routes for 8 weeks
- Measurement: Fuel card records (primary)
- Independence: Analysis by fleet finance, not route team
- Verification: Available and checkable

**Assessment:** The design is sound — randomized allocation, objective data source (fuel card transaction records), and analysis by a unit with no stake in the route team's reputation. The 9% threshold is specific and the measurement arbiter (finance department) is independent. No stated confounds; external factors (weather, vehicle maintenance) affect both groups equally under randomization.

**Justification:** Direct evidence supports it; threshold met; independent analysis.

---

### Claim 2: Deliveries now always on time
**Tier: `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Claim:** "Teslimatlar artık hep zamanında"

**Evidence:** "Sürücülerin kendi bildirdiği teslim saatleri" (Drivers' self-reported delivery times)

**Problems:**
1. Self-report bias: Drivers have incentive to report times favorably, especially if instructed to use new routes.
2. No definition of "on time" (promised window, requested time, baseline?).
3. Absolute claim ("always") cannot survive a single counter-example and is weaker than data justifies.
4. No independent verification (e.g., customer-confirmed timestamps, GPS-derived vs. reported).
5. No comparison to prior self-reported times to rule out measurement change.

**Assessment:** Plausible that routing improved delivery speed, but the evidence is compromised by self-report and lacks a control group. The data may exist (GPS logs of arrival, promised delivery windows), but the audit was handed only the self-report channel.

**Justification:** Theoretical grounding exists; empirical support missing or below threshold. Independent verification required before promotion.

---

### Claim 3: Customers more satisfied
**Tier: `[R]` Reddedildi / Refuted**

**Claim:** "Müşteriler daha memnun"

**Evidence offered:** Complaint numbers decreased

**Confound:** "Şikâyet formu web sitesinden kaldırıldı" (Complaint form was removed from website during the same period)

**Critical failure:** The reduction in complaints coincides with removal of the complaint channel itself. No counterfactual. Complaint volume is a dependent variable on form availability — fewer visible forms → fewer recorded complaints, independent of satisfaction. This is a selection-mechanism failure, not evidence of satisfaction change.

**Alternative interpretations:** 
- Satisfaction improved and complaints fell (claim's hypothesis)
- Satisfaction constant, fewer channels → fewer recorded complaints (confound)
- Satisfaction declined, fewer channels masked it (opposite of claim)

No data separates these. Removing the form makes the metric unreadable.

**Justification:** Tested against evidence that falsifies it. The measurement method changed mid-trial; complaint reduction is an artifact of form removal, not evidence of satisfaction. Marked `[R]` and archived.

### Claim 4: Software 30% more efficient than competitors
**Tier: `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Claim:** "Yazılım rakiplerinden %30 daha verimli"

**Evidence:** "Tedarikçinin kendi karşılaştırma testi" (Supplier's own comparison test)

**Problems:**
1. Non-independent source: The vendor running the comparison test has a direct financial and reputational stake in the outcome.
2. No methodology disclosed: Which competitors? Which efficiency metric (CPU, latency, throughput, cost)? Test conditions? Baseline versions?
3. No auditable artifact: The audit was handed only the number, not the test itself.
4. No external replication: No third-party benchmark referenced.
5. Selection bias in competitor choice: Vendor may have selected weaker competitors.

**Assessment:** A number with no chain of custody is not evidence. Plausible that the software is efficient; not proven that it outperforms competitors by 30% under neutral testing.

**Justification:** No independent verification; supplier-conducted test without disclosed methodology or external audit. Field requires third-party benchmark or neutral testing conditions.

### Claim 5: Fleet drove 1.82 million km this quarter
**Tier: `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

**Claim:** "Filo bu çeyrekte 1.82 milyon km yol yaptı"

**Evidence:**
- Primary source: Vehicle tracking system, raw GPS records (automatic, timestamped)
- Validation: Independent auditor sampling
- Objectivity: GPS is independent of fleet operations' reporting

**Assessment:** This is a factual claim about distances, grounded in a reliable automated measurement system. The independent auditor's sample validation adds a second layer of confidence. No incentive for GPS system to misreport; distance is not strategically interesting to any party.

**Justification:** Direct evidence supports it; source cited; threshold met (if threshold is "accurate distance"); measurement is objective and independently spot-checked.

### Claim 6: Carbon footprint will be cut in half
**Tier: `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative**

**Claim:** "Karbon ayak izimiz yarıya inecek"

**Evidence:** "Hedef; hesaplama yok" (Target; no calculation)

**Problems:**
1. Future claim: Not testable in the present; presented as if it were proven.
2. No calculation: No methodology for projecting carbon savings from the route changes.
3. No assumptions stated: Depends on energy sources (e.g., grid decarbonization, fuel type), fleet size, driving patterns post-implementation — none disclosed.
4. No baseline: Current carbon footprint not provided, so "half" is unmeasured.
5. Presented as fact, not as a goal: The board summary omits the "(Hedef)" qualifier, framing a target as an achieved outcome.

**Assessment:** This is a goal, not a finding. Interesting and plausible if routing does reduce kilometers and fuel burns fewer (from claim 1). But moving from "9% fuel reduction" to "50% carbon reduction" requires:
- Carbon per unit fuel (accounting for fuel type, boiling point, energy content)
- Baseline carbon footprint
- Projection of maintenance/manufacturing/distribution carbon (often omitted)
- Stability of assumptions over time (grid mix, vehicle fleet composition)

None of these appear in the record.

**Justification:** Interesting; not currently testable or no test designed. Refutation condition is undefined (what would falsify "50%"?). Threshold is decorative; lacks numerical justification. Remains speculative until a calculation is published.

## Missing Card: What This Summary Cannot Show

This format omits:
- **Failed routes or segments:** Claims present successes; no data on whether some routes worsened.
- **Costs:** Fuel saving is savings only if rerouting did not increase maintenance, driver overtime, or customer service costs.
- **Causality direction:** Did routing improve because routes are better, or because drivers were more careful under observation?
- **Duration stability:** 8-week trial does not guarantee 8-month or 8-year stability (seasonality, system load, driver turnover).
- **Alternatives not tried:** No baseline against simply incentivizing fuel efficiency without routing changes.

## Next Steps (Ordered by Criticality × Impact/Effort)

1. **Before board presentation (CRITICAL — 15 min):** Reclassify claims 2, 3, 4, 6 in the summary before delivery. Presenting `[H]`, `[R]`, and `[S]` claims as proven is a credibility hazard to the leadership team and to the route optimization program.

2. **Claim 3 must not appear as supporting "customer satisfaction" until confound is resolved (1–2 weeks).** Run parallel complaint tracking: keep form live for one cohort, remove it for another, 4 weeks, compare both to prior baseline. Only then can complaint reduction be attributed to satisfaction vs. form availability.

3. **Claim 2 requires independent verification (1–2 weeks).** Cross-check driver-reported delivery times against customer-confirmed receipt times (email, signature, delivery notification) and GPS-derived arrival times. Define "on time" before analysis.

4. **Claim 4 needs methodology disclosure and third-party benchmark (2–4 weeks).** Obtain the supplier's test protocol, identify competitors, run a neutral independent benchmark (e.g., third-party SaaS benchmarker, university lab) on the same metrics. Document arbiter class (currently self-judged).

5. **Claim 6 requires a carbon calculation (2–3 days).** Multiply claim 1's fuel savings by:
   - Carbon intensity of fuel in use (kg CO₂ per liter),
   - Baseline quarterly fuel consumption (needed to compute "half").
   State assumptions on scope (Scope 1 only? Including supply chain?). Refutation condition: "Scope 1 carbon will drop ≤ 40% OR ≥ 60% when measured at Q1 close."

6. **Long-term:** Establish a registry for route optimization (Mode 2 / Registry Mode). Pre-register next quarter's metrics (fuel %, on-time rate, satisfaction) with thresholds, stopping rules, and arbiters before the quarter runs. Prevents tier drift and HARKing on the next review.

The board can present claims 1 and 5 without qualification. Claims 2, 3, 4, and 6 should be downgraded in the presentation or supported with the next steps above before board discussion.
