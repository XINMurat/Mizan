# Hiring Screening Tool — Board Summary Audit

**Audit mode: Retrospective review of six claims.** All claims are presented as proven; the team lead seeks board sign-off.

**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective; results are already known. No preregistered threshold exists for any claim.

---

## Claim-by-claim audit

**1. The tool picks better candidates.** (Hiring managers who adopted it say the new hires "feel stronger.")

- **Evidence source:** Anecdotal reports from hiring managers who self-selected to adopt the tool.
- **Problems:** No objective definition of "stronger"; no measurement of job performance, retention, or any other outcome; self-reported feedback from users with selection bias (they chose to use it). Expectation effects: managers told the tool is better will perceive new hires as better.
- **Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis — User sentiment exists, but no performance evidence.
- **Missing:** Objective performance metrics (e.g., performance reviews, promotion velocity, attrition), control group of candidates rejected by the tool, control for hiring manager expectations.

**2. It is unbiased.** (The vendor's white paper states it was trained on "diverse data.")

- **Evidence source:** Vendor white paper claim about training data.
- **Problems:** Training data diversity is not proof of algorithmic unbiasedness. Bias can emerge from label definitions, feature selection, optimization objectives, or model architecture regardless of input diversity. "Diverse" is unquantified and unverified. This is a vendor assertion, not independent evidence.
- **Tier:** `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative — No test exists; only a vendor claim about preparation.
- **Missing:** Bias audit (differential outcomes by protected class), fairness metrics, independent verification of training data, testing against realistic hiring scenarios.

**3. Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days.**

- **Evidence source:** HR-system requisition dates, 2 quarters before and after, 14 departments. Volume held steady (212 vs 208 open roles).
- **Strengths:** Objective time series with documented source and sample scope.
- **Problems — critical confounds not controlled:** A hiring freeze nearly halted new openings (208 vs 212). This signals external labor-market pressure, hiring plan changes, or process urgency unrelated to the tool. No control group (departments that did not adopt the tool). No before/after comparison of other recruitment metrics (pipeline quality, offer acceptance rate, source mix). No accounting for role-type shifts (faster roles fill faster regardless). Time can drop for reasons orthogonal to tool quality: higher urgency, pooled candidates from a larger source, higher hiring bar (fewer offers sent, each one accepted faster).
- **Tier:** `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing — Metric changed, but confounds could explain it equally well.
- **Next step:** Compare time-to-hire in departments that did not use the tool (same quarters). If they also fell, the freeze or market conditions, not the tool, drive the result. Repeat within role categories and experience levels.

**4. In a blinded audit by an external firm, 600 anonymized applications scored by tool and two trained human panels; tool shortlist overlap with panels' consensus 81%, versus 79% agreement between the two human panels.**

- **Evidence source:** External audit, blinded, objective scoring.
- **Strengths:** Independent third party, large sample (600), blinding reduces observer bias, specific numbers.
- **Problems — critical control missing:** No evidence that tool-selected candidates perform better on the job. Agreement with human panel consensus is flattering but uninformative; humans can systematically select wrong candidates and agree with each other doing it. The 81% vs 79% difference (two percentage points on 600 cases ≈ 12 additional overlapping scores) is likely below statistical significance and could reverse in a different sample. The tool may have been trained or tuned on similar data to the audit pool. Most critically: **outcome bias missing.** Did tool-selected hires perform better, retain longer, or contribute more than human-selected hires? If not, agreement means nothing.
- **Tier:** `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing — Result from a credible source, but the claim conflates "agrees with humans" with "selects better candidates."
- **Next step:** Measure first-year performance and retention of tool-selected vs. human-selected hires (from this audit's pool), controlling for role, level, hire date.

**5. First-year retention of hires will improve.**

- **Evidence source:** None. "It is too early to measure; the first cohort started 3 months ago."
- **Tier:** `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative — Future-oriented, not yet testable. No threshold, no data.
- **Next step:** This is a valid hypothesis. Preregister the threshold (e.g., "retention will be ≥ 95% at 1 year, vs. baseline 92%"), the test population, and the measurement date before seeing 12-month data. Lock it now or concede it to [H] pending evidence.

**6. Recruiters save 10 hours a week.**

- **Evidence source:** Recruiter estimates in a team meeting.
- **Problems:** Self-reported time savings are subject to expectation bias (told the tool should help, report that it did) and anchoring (asking "how much time saved?" yields inflated estimates). No baseline measurement before tool adoption. No objective audit (time tracking, task logs). No control for confounds (new process steps, clearer job descriptions, or training that accompanied the tool).
- **Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis — Reported by users, but no objective support.
- **Next step:** Measure before-after using time-tracking logs, task counts (e.g., outreach emails, phone screens per week), or a two-week objective time audit.

---

## Summary findings

**No claim meets the `[K]` / Proven threshold.** All six are `[H]` (plausible but unsupported), `[S]` (speculative), or `[KKE]` (result exists but confounds are uncontrolled). The strongest claim (4) rests on an external audit of agreement, not job performance.

**Board-level risk:** Presenting these as proven inverts the hierarchy — the team lead's confidence is in the framing, not the evidence. If any claim becomes visible to regulators, plaintiffs, or the public (especially claim 2 on bias), the gap between stated certainty and actual evidence becomes a credibility liability.

**Recommendation:** Do not present these as proven to the board. Instead, frame them as hypotheses and report what is known:
- Time-to-hire dropped measurably (`[KKE]`—result real, cause uncertain).
- External panel preferred the tool's shortlist by small margin in one audit (`[KKE]`—agreement noted, job impact untested).
- Users report time savings and stronger hires (`[H]` for both—self-reported, not verified).
- Bias claim is unsubstantiated (`[S]`).
- Retention outcome pending (`[S]`).

Propose a postlaunch registry (Mode 6 / Meta-review) to track which claims hold up under scrutiny over the next 6–12 months, with job-outcome data as the primary arbiter.
