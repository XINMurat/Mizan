# Support Chatbot Board Summary — Audit Report

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective)  
**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective, conducted after the launch. The document presents results selected because they are favorable; no methodology for identifying or reporting unfavorable results is stated. The audit itself is also retrospective.

---

## Claims Reviewed

### Claim 1: Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds

**Tier:** [K] Proven / Kanıtlanmış

**Source:** Ticketing-system timestamps, all 38,000 tickets in 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by data platform team.

**Justification:** Direct measurement on complete population, large sample, clear before/after comparison. The claim is about the *fact* of the metric change, not causation. The evidence demonstrates the metric fell. ✓

---

### Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents

**Tier:** [H] Plausible hypothesis / Makul Hipotez

**Source:** Product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.

**Justification:** No sampling methodology stated. 30 transcripts is a small sample; selection bias is likely (which 30 were chosen?). "Prefer" is not operationalized — no clear criteria for what counts as preference. Subjective interpretation by product team without independent raters or formal coding scheme. No counter-examples searched. Cannot verify.

**Next step (criticality: high):** If this claim matters for the board decision, conduct a structured analysis: random sample of 200+ recent chats, define preference operationally (e.g., customer explicitly states preference, issue resolved without escalation, sentiment score), code independently by two raters, report inter-rater agreement. Expect a more modest true preference rate if this is done.

---

### Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own

**Tier:** [Y] Misleading / Yanıltıcı

**Source:** Vendor dashboard; "resolved" defined as "customer closed the chat window."

**Justification:** The definition of "resolved" does not match the claim. Customers close chat windows for many reasons: issue solved, frustration with bot, misclicked, lost connection, gave up. "Customer closed chat" ≠ "issue resolved." This is technically true by the vendor's definition but frames a weaker claim as stronger. Vendor dashboard has undisclosed financial incentive (vendor profits if bot is perceived as successful). No independent audit of resolution definition or rate. No spot checks of actual resolutions.

**The mechanism of error:** Selection of definition after seeing the dashboard would be HARKing (choosing the definition that makes the result look best). Even if unintentional, the definition chosen is the one that flatters the hypothesis.

**Next step (criticality: critical):** Audit 200 closed chats randomly sampled from the past week. For each, check whether the underlying customer issue was actually resolved (requires access to tickets, not just chat transcripts). Recompute the true resolution rate. Expect a substantial downward revision.

---

### Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts (9.1% vs 11.4%, 95% CI −3.0 to −1.6 points)

**Tier:** [K] Proven / Kanıtlanmış

**Source:** Randomized controlled trial, 5,000 customers, bot-first vs human-first support assignment, 6 weeks, repeat contacts within 7 days measured by analytics team (not bot team).

**Justification:** Proper randomized assignment eliminates selection bias. Large sample (n=5,000). Clear outcome metric (repeat contact within 7 days). Confidence interval provided and does not cross zero — effect is statistically significant. Analytics team (not bot team) computed the result, reducing incentive bias. Symmetric control exists: human-first is a proper alternative. ✓

---

### Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year

**Tier:** [S] Speculative / Spekülatif

**Source:** Projection from vendor's sales deck.

**Justification:** Forward-looking projection, not evidence. Vendor has direct financial incentive to overestimate impact (sales success depends on this claim). No model shown. No assumptions stated (e.g., how many FTEs, at what salary, with what reduction in volume?). No external validation. Cannot be tested before board decision. This is not a refutation; it is an untestable assertion framed as a prediction.

**Next step (criticality: medium, for budget planning):** Request vendor's cost model with all assumptions exposed. Cross-check against your own labor data: given actual observed reductions in repeat contacts and time-to-resolution (claim 1 and 4), model the expected headcount reduction using your own staffing and cost data. That is a testable hypothesis. The vendor projection is sales material, not audit evidence.

---

### Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch

**Tier:** [KKE] Critical control missing / Kritik Kontrol Eksik

**Source:** Attrition this quarter was lower; hiring freeze started the same month as launch.

**Justification:** Attrition is a proxy for happiness, but a confounded one. A hiring freeze causes attrition to fall for reasons unrelated to happiness: employees who might have quit become less likely to do so if they fear a layoff wave or if job market perception shifts. The confound (hiring freeze) is the dominant signal, not bot-induced happiness. Without a comparison group insulated from the hiring freeze, or a pre-planned separation of the two effects, the claim is unverifiable. No survey of actual agent sentiment exists.

**The symmetric control needed:** Measure agent happiness directly (survey or interviews) in a random sample of agents, comparing before/after launch, and separately compare those exposed to the hiring freeze to those not (if possible). Or: report attrition in a cohort that did not experience the hiring freeze (e.g., new hires or different region), if the freeze was localized. Neither control was run.

**Next step (criticality: high, if retention is a success metric):** Conduct an agent sentiment survey now (before the freeze settles into baseline). Ask about specific aspects (workload, satisfaction with support tools, interactions with chatbot). Compare self-report happiness to tenure and attrition risk. Do not report attrition alone as happiness evidence.

## Board Recommendation

**Do not sign off as stated.** Two claims are proven ([K]). Two more are speculative or misleading ([S], [Y]); they can be presented as such. Two collapse under basic scrutiny ([H], [KKE]).

If the board's concern is uptime and customer friction (claims 1 and 4), the product is supported by solid evidence. If the narrative requires universal success (cost, happiness, universal preference, resolution rate), it fails this audit.

Recommend:
1. Split the headline: "We reduced response time and repeat contacts (proven); we reduced resolution time (measured but definition needs audit); and we project cost savings (estimate subject to sensitivity analysis)."
2. Deprioritize the vendor's cost projection (claim 5) until you have done the model with your own data.
3. Audit claim 3's definition and true resolution rate before a board reference to "70% self-resolution."
4. Separate agent attrition from agent happiness (claim 6). Attrition is down; happiness is unknown and confounded by hiring freeze.
