# Building Retrofit — Evidence Audit

This audit applies Mizan evidence-tiering against the six claims presented to the board. The team lead requested "quick sign-off"; the discipline here requires specificity about which claims earn which tier before any sign-off is given.

## Claim 1: "The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio."

**Claim scope:** Whole portfolio (plural buildings)  
**Evidence provided:** "Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase"  
**Assessment:** [Y] Misleading

The framing claims portfolio-wide impact, but evidence is explicitly from a single building *selected by the facilities team to showcase*. This is selection bias: the team chose the best-performing example to present. A portfolio claim requires either (a) measurement across multiple buildings or (b) honest scope-reduction to "the retrofitted building."

**Tier basis:** The claim exceeds its evidence by a structural margin. This is classic selection pressure: single curated example presented as system-wide pattern.

---

## Claim 2: "The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers."

**Claim scope:** Comparative noise level  
**Evidence provided:** "The installer said so"  
**Assessment:** [H] Plausible Hypothesis

The installer has direct financial interest in the retrofit's success. No independent measurement is provided. No threshold for "quieter" is stated—no dB level, no methodology, no before/after measurement. Installer testimony is anecdotal and biased.

**Tier basis:** Assertion without independent measurement. Plausible given heat pump technology, but unverified.

---

## Claim 3: "The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period; figures are from utility meter bills."

**Claim scope:** Quantified energy savings with control  
**Evidence provided:**
- Retrofitted building: 18% less (year-over-year)
- Control building (not retrofitted, same campus): +1% change
- Data source: utility meter bills

**Assessment:** [KKE] Critical Control Missing

Strengths:
- Specific quantitative claims (18% vs. +1%) invite verification
- Control building comparison addresses "was there a general energy trend?"
- Degree-day normalization handles weather variation correctly
- Data source (utility meter bills) is auditable

Critical gap:
- R5 (Mizan procedure): "Surprising positives get a symmetric control before a headline. A result that flatters the hypothesis is the one most in need of a confound check."
- 18% savings is a strong, positive result. The control building confirms it's not an environmental trend (ruling out weather/seasonal driver).
- **However:** occupancy and operational confounds are not checked. Did the retrofitted building's usage patterns change? Did tenant count, hours of operation, or behavior shift? These could account for part or all of the 18% differential, independent of the retrofit's thermal performance.

The evidence presented does not show that occupancy, usage, or behavioral confounds were actively examined for this specific building. Mizan step 4 requires actively hunting counter-examples; silence on this front means the verdict holds at [KKE].

**Tier basis:** Control exists; weather normalization applied. A critical confound—occupancy/usage—remains unchecked and could flip the result. Before promotion to [K], measure: occupancy levels, operating hours, tenant count pre/post retrofit.

---

## Claim 4: "Tenants are more comfortable."

**Claim scope:** Tenant comfort (subjective)  
**Evidence provided:** "Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit"  
**Assessment:** [S] Speculative

The logical leap: "complaints not tracked" does not equal "tenants are more comfortable." Comfort is unmeasured. No survey, no standard metric, no before/after baseline. The statement could mean "we have no evidence either way," but it is presented as affirming comfort.

**Tier basis:** No measurement methodology; no baseline; inference only. Speculative.

---

## Claim 5: "The total installed cost was 2.4 million: the signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance."

**Claim scope:** Historical cost (factual)  
**Evidence provided:** Signed final invoice; reconciliation by finance  
**Assessment:** [K] Proven

This states a past fact (what was paid), not a performance claim. The evidence is documentary and reconciled. Utility meter bills and contractor invoices are audit-standard sources.

**Tier basis:** Factual claim with documentary evidence. No confounds apply to a completed transaction.

---

## Claim 6: "Payback will be under 5 years."

**Claim scope:** Future financial return  
**Evidence provided:** "(Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.)"  
**Assessment:** [S] Speculative

This is a future-oriented prediction. The explicit caveat—"assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak"—is the entire claim's load-bearing assumption. Winter peak prices do not sustain. If prices normalize, the payback window extends, possibly beyond 5 years. The threshold lives downstream of an uncontrolled variable (commodity price).

**Tier basis:** Conditional on an assumption unlikely to hold. No threshold arbiter (what sets the 5-year target?). Speculative.

---

## Summary: Ready for Board?

**No.** Only one claim ([K] cost) survives the audit. Four are below threshold (plausible, speculative, or uncontrolled assumptions). One ([Y] portfolio savings) is affirmatively misleading in scope. The third ([KKE] energy savings) has good structure but a critical confound unchecked.

**Next steps (criticality order):**

1. **Claim 3 confound audit.** Obtain: tenant count, occupancy rates, operational hours for the retrofitted building and control building, pre/post retrofit. Measure whether 18% is thermal or behavioral.
2. **Claim 1 scope.** Measure energy savings across the full portfolio of buildings, or reframe the claim to the single retrofit.
3. **Claim 4 baseline.** Run a tenant comfort survey pre/post retrofit (or at lease renewal). Quantify: satisfaction score, complaint category, resolution time.
4. **Claim 6 sensitivity.** Model payback across gas price scenarios ($1–$5/MMBTU range). State the price assumption explicitly in board materials.
5. **Claim 2.** Independent acoustic test, or label as installer opinion.

The team lead's confidence that all claims are "proven" is post-hoc and selects examples to present. This audit's role is to name what survived skeptical review, not to affirm what was decided before scrutiny began.
