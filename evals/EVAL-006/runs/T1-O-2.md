# Audit Report: Support Chatbot Board Claims

## HARKing Status
This audit is retrospective. The team lead selected these claims after the chatbot's deployment and is seeking board approval; selection bias toward positive findings is structural and must be assumed. The claims presented were not preregistered before the chatbot launched, so every signal could have been shaped by outcomes observed.

## Atomized Claims & Evidence Tier

### Claim 1: Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds
**Quote:** "Median time to first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds: ticketing-system timestamps for all 38,000 tickets in the 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by the data platform team."

**Tier:** `[KKE]` — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing

**Evidence:** The data source exists (ticketing-system timestamps), sample is large (38,000 tickets), metric is measurable. However, causation is not established. The time delta could result from changes in ticket composition (customer volume, complexity, category mix), support staffing patterns, or parallel changes to the system unrelated to the bot. No confound check isolates the bot's contribution from other variables that changed in the 10-week window.

**Missing controls:** (a) Did ticket volume, routing, or complexity distribution change between periods? (b) Did staffing, shift patterns, or triage process change? (c) Did the measurement system itself change? (d) Is the 4.2h → 40s delta robust to removal of outliers, or is it driven by a small fraction of tickets?

---

### Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents
**Quote:** "Customers prefer the bot to human agents. (Product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.)"

**Tier:** `[H]` — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis

**Evidence:** A small, non-random sample of 30 transcripts was read by the product team. "Prefer" is subjective and requires interpretation; no rubric is stated, no inter-rater reliability reported, and no method is described for selecting which 30 transcripts to read. This is below the threshold for `[K]` because: (i) sample is tiny (30 out of how many?), (ii) judgment is subjective with no scoring method, (iii) no statistical test separates preference from noise.

**Counter-example search:** Anecdotal reports of customer frustration with the bot are typical for new chatbots; the absence of any such transcripts in the sample of 30 should be flagged. It is possible the 30 were selected from cases the bot handled well, or the product team's framing of "preference" was unconsciously biased toward positive signals.

---

### Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own
**Quote:** "The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own. (Vendor dashboard; 'resolved' means the customer closed the chat window.)"

**Tier:** `[Y]` — Yanıltıcı / Misleading

**Evidence:** The vendor provides a dashboard metric. However, the definition of "resolved" is a customer closing the chat window, not an objective measure of problem resolution. A customer may close the chat because: (i) they are frustrated, (ii) they were disconnected, (iii) they gave up and will contact support another way, (iv) the bot confused them. The 70% headline implies the bot solved problems; the evidence supports only that it achieved window-closes.

**Mechanism of the error:** The vendor dashboard defines success in a way that benefits the vendor (high completion rate). The framing conflates two measures: "customer interaction closed" with "problem solved." This is technically true on its face but misleads about capability. The actual resolution rate is unknown; the 70% is an artifact of how "resolve" was defined.

---

### Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts in a randomized trial
**Quote:** "The bot reduced repeat contacts: in a randomized test, 5,000 customers were assigned to bot-first or human-first support for 6 weeks; repeat contacts within 7 days were 9.1% vs 11.4% (95% CI for the difference −3.0 to −1.6 points), computed by the analytics team, not the bot team."

**Tier:** `[K]` — Kanıtlanmış / Proven

**Evidence:** (i) Randomized assignment eliminates selection bias. (ii) Large sample (5,000 customers per arm, implied). (iii) Clear, measurable endpoint (repeat contacts within 7 days). (iv) Quantified result with confidence interval (95% CI: −3.0 to −1.6 points), which excludes zero and shows the difference is real, not noise. (v) Independent computation (analytics team, not bot team, reduces bias). The threshold for `[K]` is met: direct evidence, source cited, threshold met.

**Caveat:** The CI width (1.4 percentage points) is narrow, suggesting precision; however, this is the only claim in the set that withstands scrutiny.

---

### Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year
**Quote:** "It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year. (Projection from the vendor's sales deck.)"

**Tier:** `[H]` — Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis

**Evidence:** This is a future projection, not a current measurement. The vendor's sales deck is not independent evidence; it is a vendor predicting the financial benefit of their own product, with clear incentive to overstate. No methodology is described (how was "one-third" computed?), no sensitivity analysis is shown, and no stopping rule or refutation threshold is stated. The claim is plausible (if the bot truly reduced repeat contacts by ~2 percentage points, some labor savings are likely), but no current empirical evidence supports the specific "one-third" figure.

**Missing data:** actual planned headcount reductions, cost per FTE, utilization assumptions, and whether the vendor's model accounts for bot maintenance cost.

---

### Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch
**Quote:** "Agents are happier since launch. (No survey; attrition this quarter was lower, and a hiring freeze started the same month.)"

**Tier:** `[KKE]` — Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing

**Evidence:** Lower attrition is cited as a proxy for happiness. However, a critical confound is present: a hiring freeze started the same month. Agents who might have left are now less likely to do so because they fear unemployment or believe the external labor market is weak. This confound completely undermines the causal inference. Attrition can fall for reasons unrelated to job satisfaction (economic fear, reduced exit opportunities). No survey measure of actual sentiment was collected.

**Mechanism:** The hiring freeze is a rival hypothesis that explains the attrition drop equally well or better than "agents are happier." Without separating these, the claim cannot reach `[K]`.

---

## Missing Card: What This Document Omits

The summary format omits:
1. **Failure cases:** Do we know of tickets the bot handled poorly? What is the failure rate?
2. **Customer effort:** Lower first-response time does not mean lower total effort; customers may have to escalate more often.
3. **Cost of the bot:** Vendor license, maintenance, integration — no ROI calculation.
4. **Temporal dynamics:** Did improvements hold over 10 weeks, or did they fade after the first few days?
5. **Agent workload:** If repeat contacts fell but customers are now in queue longer, total agent workload might have increased.

## Recommendations

**Immediate (criticality × impact / effort):**

1. **Before board presentation:** Acknowledge that claims 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6 are not yet proven. Present only Claim 4 (the RCT) as evidence and frame the others as "promising indicators requiring further work."

2. **For Claim 3:** Redefine "resolution" as customer self-reported satisfaction or absence of repeat contact (already measured in Claim 4), not window-close. The current metric is misleading.

3. **For Claim 6:** Run a brief anonymous survey of agent sentiment, or separate attrition into voluntary and involuntary categories. The hiring freeze confounds must be accounted for.

4. **For Claim 1:** Control for ticket composition (stratify by category or complexity) to isolate the bot's causal effect. A decomposition would show how much of the 4.2h → 40s drop comes from faster triage vs. actual handling speed.

5. **For Claim 5:** Publish the vendor's cost model so assumptions can be audited. Require an independent cost-benefit analysis before headcount decisions are made.

**Follow-up (preregistration for next audit):**
- Define thresholds for success metrics before decisions are made (e.g., "repeat contacts < 10%" vs. measured 9.1%).
- Set a cadence (e.g., monthly) to track these metrics going forward so tier drift is visible.
- For any future "agent happiness" claim, use a validated instrument (survey with established psychometric properties), not proxies.

---

## Tone & Mechanism

The skew toward positive framing is structural, not intentional: a vendor-supplied dashboard, a product team curating transcripts, and retrospective selection all apply pressure in the same direction. The claims are not deceptive, but they exceed their evidence, and the board needs to know how much.
