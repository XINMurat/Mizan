# Building Retrofit Board Summary — Audit Report

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective)  
**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Status:** Retrospective analysis — claims already made; HARKing risk: board summary selected examples after retrofit completion to support approval narrative.

---

## Executive Summary

Of the six claims, two are PROVEN by direct evidence, two are NOT PROVEN for lack of sufficient evidence, one is PROVEN but overstated, and one is NOT PROVEN because it predicts the future under volatile assumptions. The most critical finding is **Claim 1 (portfolio-wide savings)**, which uses a single cherry-picked building as proof — a selection-bias trap that renders it misleading despite evidence that this one building performed well.

**Recommendation:** Present Claim 3 (single-building energy performance) as the board's primary confidence point, add baseline tenant-comfort data before endorsing Claim 4, and reframe Claim 1 to name which buildings have been measured and which have not. Payback (Claim 6) depends on price assumptions that lie outside the retrofit's control.

## Atomized Claims & Tier Assignments

### Claim 1: Portfolio Energy Savings
**Stated:** "The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio."  
**Evidence cited:** "Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase."

**Atomized:**
- Sub-claim 1a: Energy savings occurred in *at least one building*.
- Sub-claim 1b: This finding generalizes to *the whole portfolio*.

**Tier: [Y] MISLEADING**

**Justification:**  
Sub-claim 1a is supported (see Claim 3 for the building's data). Sub-claim 1b is NOT supported. The phrase "the facilities team chose to showcase" flags selection bias: this one building was selected *because it performs well*. Measuring only the best-case building and attributing the result to the portfolio is survivorship bias. The claim as written — "saves energy across the whole portfolio" — is technically true only for the one building tested; framing it as portfolio-wide implies coverage the evidence does not support.

**Counter-example search:** No data provided for other buildings in the portfolio. The audit cannot confirm whether other retrofits match this performance, underperform, or (implausibly) overperform. 

**Impact:** A board vote based on this claim will expect portfolio-wide results; delivery against only one building will appear as a failure even if that building succeeds.

### Claim 2: Heat Pump Quietness
**Stated:** "The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers."  
**Evidence cited:** "The installer said so."

**Tier: [H] PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**

**Justification:**  
The installer is a named source, but is not independent: the installer has a financial stake in the retrofit's perceived success. The claim relies on subjective assessment ("quieter") without baseline measurements (e.g., decibel levels of old system vs. new system) or post-retrofit tenant feedback. The theoretical basis is sound — modern heat pumps are typically designed for lower noise — but this particular retrofit's performance is not verified.

**What would promote this to [K]:**
- Decibel measurements of boilers before removal, heat pumps after installation.
- Tenant survey on noise satisfaction before and after, with response rates ≥70% and neutral framing.
- A third-party measurement (not installer).

**Missing card:** Tenant annoyance with noise is a common retrofit failure mode (heat pumps can be audible at night if sited near occupied spaces). Absence of evidence here is notable because noise complaints are often a *lagging* indicator — they arrive 6–12 months post-retrofit once seasonal use normalizes.

### Claim 3: Energy Reduction (Single Building)
**Stated:** "The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period; figures are from utility meter bills."

**Tier: [K] PROVEN**

**Justification:**  
- **Evidence:** Utility meter bills (primary, independently auditable documents).
- **Comparison:** Same building, two winters before vs. post-retrofit (temporal control).
- **Confound check:** Matched control building (+1% drift, minor) demonstrates that campus-wide trends (weather, occupancy patterns, maintenance) are accounted for. The retrofitted building's 18% reduction vs. control's +1% drift yields an estimated **17 percentage-point delta attributable to the retrofit** (conservative estimate; could be higher if control-building efficiency declined).
- **Threshold:** 18% reduction is substantial, well-above typical measurement noise (±2–3%).
- **Refutation condition:** Clear. If meter data is falsified, if control building is not truly comparable, or if the same-building pre-period included an anomaly (e.g., boiler malfunction hiding high usage), the claim fails.

**Caveats to record (not blockers):**
- Only one building measured. Tier [K] applies to *this building*, not the portfolio (Claim 1 conflates the two).
- Two-winter window is short; seasonal anomalies (mild vs. harsh winter) could skew results. Longer history would strengthen the finding.
- Behavioral change by tenants (lower setpoints after retrofit, occupancy habits) is not ruled out but is less likely given the control building's stability.
- Degree-day adjustment is sound methodology, but if the control building's heating setpoint or insulation differ materially from the retrofitted building, the comparison is weakened. The claim assumes the buildings are truly "matched" — audit cannot verify this from the summary.

**Verdict:** This claim passes the threshold. It is the only claim in this summary with direct, auditable, independent evidence.

### Claim 4: Tenant Comfort
**Stated:** "Tenants are more comfortable."  
**Evidence cited:** "Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit."

**Tier: [H] PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**

**Justification:**  
The evidence provided is *negative evidence* — an absence of data — which cannot support a positive claim ("more comfortable"). Without a baseline complaint rate or post-retrofit comfort survey, there is no measurement of change. The lack of tracking is itself a finding: it means the retrofit's success on this dimension is not measurable from historical records.

**What would be required for [K]:**
- Pre-retrofit tenant satisfaction survey on thermal comfort, with response rate ≥60%, baseline score recorded.
- Identical survey post-retrofit (6+ months after to avoid novelty effects), same response rate.
- Difference statistically significant or practically meaningful (e.g., mean shift ≥0.5 on 5-point scale).
- Or: complaint-tracking system implemented before retrofit, showing a drop post-retrofit.

**Missing card:** This is a known retrofit failure mode — heat pump noise, unfamiliar controls, or seasonal inefficiency can *reduce* comfort despite energy savings. The absence of tracking before the retrofit means this retrofit cannot be claimed to improve comfort even if post-retrofit tracking eventually begins. **HARKing risk:** Once post-retrofit complaints are eventually logged, the team may be tempted to compare them against a (fabricated or vaguely remembered) pre-retrofit baseline. Establishing a formal baseline now is urgent.

**Mechanism vs. motive:** Comfort improvements are theoretically plausible (better temperature control, radiant heating via heat pumps). The absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — it reflects a gap in measurement, not a failure to improve comfort. However, the claim cannot be approved based on untracked data.

### Claim 5: Installed Cost
**Stated:** "The total installed cost was 2.4 million: the signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance."

**Tier: [K] PROVEN**

**Justification:**  
- **Evidence:** Signed contractor invoice (binding document) + independent finance reconciliation (second party).
- **Source:** Both documentary and third-party verified.
- **Refutation condition:** Clear. If the invoice is disputed or reconciliation uncovers discrepancies, the figure changes.
- **Threshold:** This is a factual claim, not a statistical one. The invoice is the arbiter.

**Caveat:** "Total installed cost" should be verified to include all costs (labor, materials, permits, contingencies, change orders). Finance reconciliation suggests this has been done, but the audit cannot verify from the summary alone. Assume finance performed this due diligence.

**Verdict:** Claim passes. This is the most straightforward claim in the summary.

### Claim 6: Payback Period
**Stated:** "Payback will be under 5 years. (Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.)"

**Tier: [H] PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**

**Justification:**  
This is a prediction (future claim), not a measurement of past performance. It depends on:
1. Continued energy savings (supported by Claim 3, but only for one building).
2. Gas price assumption (stated as "peak"; volatile and outside the retrofit team's control).

**Refutation condition:** If gas prices fall or energy savings decline, payback extends beyond 5 years.

**Threshold and arbiter:** The claim names a numeric threshold (5 years) but the arbiter is *market conditions*, not a test or measurement under the team's control. Per Mizan schema, an arbiter outside the team's influence (market price) is properly classified as [H] until the 5-year period expires and actual payback is measured.

**Caveats:**
- One-building energy savings (Claim 3) do not guarantee portfolio-wide payback performance.
- Gas price assumption is volatile. Winter 2023–2024 saw historic energy prices; current-year projections are speculative.
- Maintenance costs, equipment lifespan, and potential replacement cycles are not mentioned.
- If financing was used, interest costs should be accounted for in payback calculation. Claim does not state whether 2.4M is equity or financed.

**What would promote this to [K]:**
- Actual payback measured at 5-year mark by comparing cumulative cost savings (from meter bills) to installed cost.
- Or: independent energy audit predicting payback with clear assumptions, published before retrofit completion, with ≥2-year track record of accuracy.

**Verdict:** Claim is plausible but predictive. Do not present as "proven" until the 5-year period is observed or a transparent model is audited and locked.

## Synthesis & Missing Card

**What this summary cannot show:**
1. **Multi-year stability:** Energy savings may not persist (equipment degradation, tenant behavior drift, maintenance gaps). Only one winter of post-retrofit data is provided; comparison needs 3+ years to rule out anomalies.
2. **Portfolio performance:** Only one building measured. No roll-out timeline, cost, or performance data for other buildings.
3. **Non-energy benefits:** Water usage, indoor air quality, occupant health — often the true return on retrofit investment. Not mentioned.
4. **Tenant satisfaction trend:** No pre-retrofit baseline; post-retrofit complaints are not yet tracked. Success here is not measurable.
5. **Costs of ownership:** Maintenance, warranties, spare parts supply, technician training. Not addressed.
6. **Confounds:** Occupancy shifts, tenant demographics, usage patterns (remote work uptake, heating setpoint changes) — not isolated.

**Structural bias:** This summary cherry-picks one high-performing building ("showcase"), omits portfolio-wide data, and includes forward-looking claims (payback, comfort) that rest on unmeasured assumptions. This is common in retrofit presentations and is the reason HARKing risk is significant.

## Tier Drift Audit

Comparison against typical retrofit presentations:
- **Claim 1** drifted from "[H] Portfolio retrofit may save energy" (before retrofit) to "[K] Proven across portfolio" (this summary), supported by one cherry-picked building. **Finding:** Tier drift without new evidence of portfolio coverage.
- **Claims 2, 4** were likely stated as [H] before retrofit ("should be quieter, should improve comfort"); now presented as [K] without new evidence to support the promotion. **Finding:** Tier inflation.
- **Claim 3** was [S] before retrofit (theoretical); now [K] based on meter data. This is correct tier drift (justified by evidence).

## Tone & Framing

This retrofit has succeeded on its most measurable dimension: **one building's energy performance is real and substantial (18%)**, verified by independent meter data. That is a genuine achievement and should be presented plainly.

The risk is that Claim 1 (portfolio savings) and Claim 6 (payback) will be taken as proven when they rest on extrapolation and price assumptions. Removing that extrapolation does not diminish the retrofit's value — it clarifies what the board is voting on: a proven single-building retrofit model, not a portfolio-wide rollout.

Tenants have not yet reported whether they are more comfortable; the facilities team should not claim comfort until they measure it. This is not a failure (comfort may improve once data arrives); it is honesty about the current state.

## Audit Status

This is a **retrospective audit of a forward-facing summary** ahead of a board vote. The summary presents outcomes selectively (one building, not portfolio), assumptions as facts (price stability), and unmeasured claims (comfort) as proven. Tier assignment reflects what the evidence currently supports, not what the retrofit's designers hoped for.

No claim should be removed from the board summary; instead, each should be **reframed to match its tier**. The board deserves to know which claims are locked (Claim 3, Claim 5) and which depend on future outcomes or further measurement (Claims 1, 2, 4, 6).
