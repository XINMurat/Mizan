# Audit Report: Building Retrofit Board Summary

**Audit mode:** Retrospective audit of board-ready claim set  
**Auditor note:** This document presents six forward claims with evidence. Applying Mizan evidence tiers to support informed board decision. Each claim is atomized, sourced, and assessed against the provided evidence.

---

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### Claim 1: The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio.
**Evidence cited:** Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase.

**Issue:** Scope mismatch. The claim asserts portfolio-wide energy savings; the evidence is deliberately selective ("the one building the facilities team chose to showcase"). This is explicit acknowledgment of selection bias in the cited evidence itself.

**Tier:** `[H]` Plausible hypothesis — The retrofit may save energy in the retrofitted building (supported by Claim 3's specific data), but no evidence supports the portfolio-wide claim. The parenthetical actually contradicts the claim by naming the selection mechanism.

**Next step:** Measure energy use in all retrofitted buildings in the portfolio before board presentation, or reframe the claim to "pilot building demonstrates energy savings, portfolio impact pending."

---

### Claim 2: The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers.
**Evidence cited:** The installer said so.

**Issue:** Source bias and no objective measurement. The installer is the party selling and installing the equipment and has direct financial and reputational incentive to report favorable results. No decibel measurement, no third-party acoustic assessment, no tenant surveys.

**Tier:** `[H]` Plausible hypothesis — Heat pumps may be quieter (they operate differently than boilers), but anecdotal endorsement from an interested party is not evidence. A qualitative claim requires qualitative verification (tenant comfort feedback) or quantitative verification (acoustic measurement).

**Next step:** Conduct independent acoustic measurement or survey tenants on noise levels post-retrofit vs. memory of pre-retrofit.

---

### Claim 3: The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters; a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period; figures are from utility meter bills.
**Evidence cited:** Utility meter bills.

**Tier:** `[K]` Proven — This claim meets rigor standards. It is specific (18% vs. +1%), measurable, sourced from objective records (meter bills), includes a before/after comparison (prior two winters), and includes a matched control group on the same campus. The control accounts for campus-wide factors (weather normalization via degree-day, campus-level utility changes). The margin between retrofit building (−18%) and control (+1%) is substantial and unlikely to be measurement error. This is the strongest claim in the set.

---

### Claim 4: Tenants are more comfortable.
**Evidence cited:** Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit.

**Issue:** Absence of evidence is not evidence. The provided evidence actually proves the opposite: no pre-retrofit baseline exists. Without knowing complaint volume or tenant satisfaction before retrofit, "more comfortable" cannot be verified. Tenants may have the same comfort; complaints may have stopped because the complaint mechanism changed; or new occupants (who never experienced boilers) may have no comparison.

**Tier:** `[H]` Plausible hypothesis — Heat pumps may improve comfort (more even temperature, less noise), but no measurement supports this. The cited evidence (no pre-retrofit tracking) means the claim is untestable against data. Reframe as aspiration pending post-retrofit tenant survey or drop until data exists.

**Next step:** Conduct post-retrofit tenant comfort survey; attempt to reconstruct pre-retrofit baseline from facility maintenance logs or prior tenant feedback if it exists.

---

### Claim 5: The total installed cost was 2.4 million.
**Evidence cited:** The signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance.

**Tier:** `[K]` Proven — Financial claim tied to a concrete, auditable document (signed invoice, finance reconciliation). Cost is a factual matter of record. Assumes the invoice reflects actual work completed and finance reconciliation was accurate; no contrary evidence presented.

---

### Claim 6: Payback will be under 5 years.
**Evidence cited:** Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.

**Issue:** Forward prediction resting on an explicit assumption. The evidence (assumption) is conditional. If gas prices fall significantly (historically plausible), payback extends beyond 5 years. This is not an observation or measurement; it is a scenario. The scenario may be reasonable but is speculative.

**Tier:** `[H]` Plausible hypothesis — Payback *under current gas prices* may be under 5 years (though the calculation is not shown, only the assumption stated). As a predictive claim, it requires sensitivity analysis: what gas price triggers 5-year vs. 7-year payback? The assumption itself should be justified (is winter peak likely to persist?). Present as scenario-dependent, not as proven.

**Next step:** Show payback calculation and sensitivity table (payback vs. gas price); state confidence interval on price assumption.

---

## Summary for Board Presentation

**Claims ready for board:** 2 of 6 are proven and sourced.
- Claim 3 (energy savings in the retrofitted building, measured) is fully supported.
- Claim 5 (cost) is a matter of financial record.

**Claims requiring revision before presentation:** 4 of 6.
- Claim 1: Reframe from portfolio-wide to pilot building, or measure the portfolio.
- Claim 2: Add acoustic measurement or tenant feedback.
- Claim 4: Add post-retrofit tenant survey or clarify that comfort claim is aspirational.
- Claim 6: Show payback calculation and gas-price sensitivity.

**Recommendation:** Present Claim 3 and 5 as approved. For Claims 1, 2, 4, 6: choose between (a) collecting the missing evidence before presentation, (b) reframing to match the evidence that exists, or (c) labeling them prospective and deferring to post-retrofit measurement. Presenting all six as proven to the board exposes the organization to audit findings and tenant/stakeholder misalignment when reality diverges from these claims.
