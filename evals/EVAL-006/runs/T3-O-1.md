# Audit: Hiring Screening Tool — Board Summary

**Audit mode (retrospective).** This is a Mizan evidence-tier audit of six claims presented for board approval. The user has requested sign-off; I have reviewed each claim against the evidence provided.

**HARKing status:** This audit is retrospective — the hiring tool has been in use, outcomes are known, and the board summary was written with those outcomes visible. The audit itself is also retrospective. No new evidence was collected during this review; all claims were assessed against provided evidence only.

---

## Claim-by-Claim Analysis

### Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates."
**Evidence provided:** "Hiring managers who adopted it say the new hires 'feel stronger.'"

**Tier: [H] Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Finding:** This claim rests on subjective impressions only. "Feel stronger" is not a measurable outcome. No job performance data, no retention metrics, no competency assessments, no comparison to hires made without the tool are provided. The claim cannot be promoted from hypothesis to proven without:
- Objective performance metrics on hired candidates (e.g., project delivery speed, quality ratings, promotion velocity)
- A control cohort of hires made without the tool
- A threshold that defines "better" numerically

**Next step:** Conduct a retrospective cohort analysis comparing performance ratings of tool-selected vs non-tool hires over their first year. Cost: medium (HR system data + manager ratings). Impact: critical (falsifiable evidence for value claim).

---

### Claim 2: "It is unbiased."
**Evidence provided:** "The vendor's white paper states it was trained on 'diverse data.'"

**Tier: [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

**Finding:** Training on diverse data is a necessary but not sufficient condition for unbiased outputs. The claim conflates training data composition with output fairness. No testing is reported:
- No disparate-impact analysis by demographic category
- No threshold accuracy rates by protected class
- No audit of shortlist composition by demographics

The evidence states what was CLAIMED in a vendor document, not what was TESTED. Vendors have structural incentive to claim fairness. The distinction is material.

**Next step:** Commission an independent bias audit on 200+ applications with demographic labels removed, analyzing shortlist composition and acceptance rates by demographic groups. Report both false-negative rates (false rejections) and false-positive rates per group. Cost: high (requires external firm). Impact: critical (bias claims require proof, not vendor promises).

---

### Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days: HR-system dates for every requisition in the 2 quarters before and after, across all 14 departments, while a hiring freeze did not change the volume of open roles (212 vs 208)."
**Evidence provided:** HR-system requisition dates (before: 41 days average; after: 29 days); role volume control (212 vs 208 open roles).

**Tier: [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing**

**Finding:** The metric change IS documented and significant (−29% time-to-hire). The attempt to control for hiring freeze (role volume stable) shows some rigor. However, causation to the tool is NOT proven. Multiple uncontrolled variables could explain the improvement:
- Process changes (e.g., hiring team restructure, new interview format) introduced simultaneously
- Candidate pool changes (market conditions, employer brand, recruiter activity level)
- Selection bias in which departments adopted the tool first
- Interaction effects: the hiring freeze itself may have accelerated hiring for roles that remained open

**Critical gap:** The evidence does not state WHEN the tool was introduced, whether adoption was simultaneous across departments, whether any OTHER process changes occurred in the same period, or whether time-to-hire fell uniformly across all departments or only where the tool was used.

**Tier promotion path:** Conduct a department-level analysis. If the tool was rolled out gradually, compare time-to-hire pre- and post-adoption within each department, controlling for role type and market conditions. If rollout was simultaneous, find a matched control cohort (peer company, similar size/industry) where hiring volume changed similarly but the tool was not used.

**Next step:** Reconstruct the implementation timeline; identify departments where the tool was NOT used by end of measurement period; run a difference-in-differences analysis (tool-adopting vs non-adopting departments). Cost: medium (data analysis). Impact: high (rules in or out tool causation).

---

### Claim 4: "In a blinded audit by an external firm, 600 anonymized applications were scored by the tool and by two trained human panels; the tool's shortlist overlapped the panels' consensus shortlist 81%, versus 79% agreement between the two human panels."
**Evidence provided:** Blinded external audit; 600 applications; anonymized; 81% vs 79% overlap.

**Tier: [K] Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

**Finding:** The audit claim itself — with methodology and specific percentages — is documented and supported by evidence. An external firm, blinded protocol, and two-panel comparison constitute sound methodology. The 81% vs 79% comparison is stated with precision.

**Caveat — interpretation:** This proves the TOOL PERFORMS AT HUMAN LEVEL (81% vs 79% is a small gap). It does not prove the tool picks BETTER candidates overall, only that its rankings agree with human consensus at similar rates that humans agree with each other. This audit result is compatible with Claim 1 being false (the tool performs comparably, not better).

**Note — statistical significance:** The evidence does not report confidence intervals or p-values. A 2 percentage point difference on 600 applications may not be statistically significant. However, the claim AS STATED (the overlap percentages observed) is proven; the statistical meaning of that difference is a separate question.

---

### Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve."
**Evidence provided:** "It is too early to measure; the first cohort started 3 months ago."

**Tier: [S] Spekülatif / Speculative**

**Finding:** This is an explicit prediction about the future with zero evidence. No baseline retention rate is provided; no retention data from the first cohort has been collected; the measurement period (first-year) has not elapsed for any cohort.

This claim cannot be measured until the first cohort completes 12 months of employment. Presenting it as a board-approval point is premature. It is a hypothesis, not a finding.

**Next step:** Remove from board summary, or reframe as "We will measure first-year retention and compare to historical baseline (XX%) at Q3 2027." Lock a threshold and acceptance rule now; measure later.

---

### Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."
**Evidence provided:** "Recruiter estimates in a team meeting."

**Tier: [H] Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis**

**Finding:** Recruiter estimates are subjective and not measured. Time savings claims are highly prone to:
- Anchoring bias (someone suggests "10 hours" and consensus forms around that number)
- Novelty effect (new tool feels faster in first weeks; settles down)
- Unmeasured work shift (tool automates screening but recruiters spend time on other tasks)

No before-after time-tracking data is provided. No method for measuring time is described. No control is possible after the fact.

**Next step:** Implement time-tracking for one month (baseline without tool) and one month (with tool). Track hours per hire, hours per screen, hours per offer. Measure variation within recruiters, not just averages. Threshold decision: "≥5 hours saved per hire, measured by logged time entries, or claim demoted to [H]."

## Board Summary Recommendation

**Do not approve as presented.** Of six claims, only one ([K] tier) survives scrutiny. The evidence package conflates training data with output fairness, anecdote with measurement, and correlation with causation.

The tool shows **one solid result**: it screens applications at human-level accuracy (Claim 4). That is valuable and true. However:

- **Claim 1 (better candidates)** requires performance data on hired individuals.
- **Claim 2 (unbiased)** requires bias testing by demographics.
- **Claim 3 (time-to-hire)** requires ruling out other causes; currently [KKE].
- **Claim 5 (retention)** is future prediction; remove or table.
- **Claim 6 (time savings)** requires measured time data; currently anecdote.

**Next steps, ordered by impact:**
1. **Immediate:** Remove Claims 5 and 6 from board presentation. Replace with interim findings: "Audit confirms tool screens at human-level accuracy. Measurement of hiring speed and quality improvements in progress."
2. **Before next board meeting (4 weeks):** Run department-level causation analysis on Claim 3; commission bias audit on Claim 2.
3. **Before Q2 2027:** Collect performance data on hired candidates (Claim 1); measure recruiter time-savings (Claim 6).

The tool is not proven harmful, and one claim IS proven. The ask is not to kill the project; it is to stop presenting hypothesis as fact.
