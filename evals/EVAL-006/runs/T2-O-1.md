# Building Retrofit Board Summary — Audit Report

**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Mode:** Audit (retrospective claim review)  
**HARKing status:** Retrospective; examples selected by facilities team for portfolio showcase. The audit also conducted retrospectively.

---

## Executive Summary

The board is being asked to sign off on six retrofit claims. This audit applied Mizan's evidence-tiering method to each. **Result: 2 of 6 are proven [K]; 2 are critically undermined by missing controls [KKE] or misleading framing [Y]; 2 require conditional qualification [H].**

The team lead's confidence in "all proven" does not match the evidence. Proceeding with full-portfolio claims based on a curated single building is a board-level risk.

---

## Individual Claim Audits

### Claim 1: The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio.

**Evidence cited:** Measured in the one building the facilities team chose to showcase.

**Analysis:**  
The claim makes a portfolio-wide assertion. The evidence is explicitly selection-biased: one building chosen to showcase results. This is survivorship/selection bias in textbook form — the facilities team selected the building most likely to show positive results, not a representative sample or random draw.

**Counter-example search:** No data provided on retrofit performance in other buildings across the portfolio. The disclosure itself — "the one building chosen to showcase" — proves there is at least one building NOT in the showcase set, meaning the portfolio-wide claim is untested elsewhere.

**Tier:** `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading  
**Justification:** The headline claim ("across the whole portfolio") contradicts its evidence ("one building selected for showcase"). Technically true that this building saved energy, but framed to imply coverage it does not have.

---

### Claim 2: The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers.

**Evidence cited:** The installer said so.

**Analysis:**  
No objective measurement provided. No decibel readings, no acoustic testing, no independent verification. The sole source is the equipment supplier, who has economic incentive to make the product sound favorable. This is hearsay, not measurement.

**Counter-example search:** No attempt to measure baseline boiler noise or post-retrofit heat pump noise reported. No third-party acoustic assessment.

**Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis  
**Justification:** Installer testimony provides weak grounding (plausible that pumps could be quieter), but no empirical evidence. Threshold for "proven" requires objective acoustic measurement; this has none.

---

### Claim 3: The retrofitted building used 18% less gas per degree-day than the same building in the prior two winters, and a matched control building on the same campus, not retrofitted, changed by +1% over the same period.

**Evidence cited:** Utility meter bills (objective measurement).

**Analysis:**  
Strong evidence structure:
- **Objective source:** Utility meter data (not survey, not installer claim)
- **Before/after comparison:** Two prior winters vs. post-retrofit winter
- **Control building:** Matched campus building, no retrofit, +1% change over same period
- **Quantified result:** 18% vs. +1% is a clear, measurable difference
- **Degree-day normalization:** Accounts for weather variation, a proper confound control

The comparison (-18% retrofitted vs. +1% control) suggests the retrofit caused the improvement, not external factors affecting both buildings equally.

**Counter-example search:** No contrary evidence presented. Meter data is objective and publicly auditible (utility records).

**Tier:** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven  
**Justification:** Direct evidence from objective meters, control building comparison eliminates weather confound, specific quantified threshold met, source cited.

---

### Claim 4: Tenants are more comfortable.

**Evidence cited:** Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit.

**Analysis:**  
This is a critical control missing problem. The claim is "more comfortable" — a comparative statement requiring before/after data. The disclosure reveals there is NO before-retrofit baseline: "Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit."

Without baseline complaint data, it is impossible to determine whether comfort improved, stayed the same, or (if complaints were simply not recorded) whether the situation is actually worse but undocumented.

**Possible confounds not addressed:**
- Placebo effect (new equipment feels better even if performance is unchanged)
- Reporting bias (post-retrofit, tenants expect to report satisfaction)
- Tenure changes (different tenants before and after)
- Seasonal variation (retrofit happened in which season relative to when complaints were recorded?)

**Tier:** `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing  
**Justification:** A comfort claim requires before/after comparison. Baseline measurement explicitly absent. The missing control could completely flip the conclusion (satisfaction might be reporting bias, not comfort).

---

### Claim 5: The total installed cost was 2.4 million.

**Evidence cited:** The signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance.

**Analysis:**  
Straightforward documentary evidence:
- **Primary source:** Signed invoice (contractual document)
- **Verification:** Finance reconciliation (independent check)
- **Factual claim:** A sum of money, verifiable against records
- **No assumption:** This is historical fact, not forecast

This is a factual claim with proper documentary evidence and third-party (finance) review.

**Tier:** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven  
**Justification:** Direct documentary evidence (signed invoice), verified by independent reconciliation (finance), clear source.

---

### Claim 6: Payback will be under 5 years.

**Evidence cited:** Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.

**Analysis:**  
This is a forward-looking claim dependent on an assumption about future conditions.

- **Known:** Actual energy savings (18% from Claim 3)
- **Known:** Actual cost (2.4M from Claim 5)
- **Unproven:** Gas prices staying at this winter's peak

Gas prices are volatile. Winter 2025–2026 prices peaked due to geopolitical factors; they do not typically stay at winter peaks through summer and fall. The assumption is unstated as optional; it is presented as THE assumption the claim depends on.

If gas prices drop 20% from peak (common within 2 years), payback extends beyond 5 years. The calculation's sensitivity to this assumption is high.

**Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis  
**Justification:** The calculation method is sound (savings ÷ cost), but it depends on an unproven assumption about future market conditions. Payback under 5 years is plausible IF the assumption holds; without that proof, it is conditional, not proven. Tier-K would require either (a) locked price contracts, or (b) sensitivity ranges showing payback remains under 5 years across a realistic price range.

## Missing Card

This board summary is structured around six positive claims. What it structurally cannot show:

1. **Negative results:** Were any buildings retrofitted and saw NO energy savings? Are any excluded from the portfolio?
2. **Cost overruns or delays:** Were there retrofit projects that came in over budget or behind schedule?
3. **Maintenance issues:** Post-retrofit, have heat pumps required more service calls than boilers did? (Relevant to actual cost of ownership.)
4. **Tenant complaints about cold/heat:** Comfort goes both ways; if anyone reported inadequate temperature control, that is absent.
5. **Comparison to alternative solutions:** Could different retrofit approaches achieve the same savings at lower cost?

A board summary format naturally omits failures and deferrals. This particular summary also omits the price sensitivity of payback and the selection bias in sampling.

## Conjunction Check (Step 8)

Do these claims hold together when placed in context?

- **Claim 3 + Claim 5 together:** The energy savings (18%) and cost (2.4M) are compatible and support the payback claim.
- **Claim 3 + Claim 6 together:** Payback depends on sustained high gas prices. If prices fall, the payback calculation fails even though the energy savings (Claim 3) remain true. These are not contradictory, but Claim 6's time horizon creates risk to Claim 3's narrative.
- **Claim 1 + Claim 3 together:** Claim 1 (portfolio-wide savings) rests on Claim 3 (one showcased building). If only the showcased building shows 18% savings, Claim 1 is false, not proven.

**Finding:** Claim 1 (portfolio-wide) is only as strong as evidence from non-showcased buildings. That evidence is absent.

## Procedure Step 7: Domain Scenarios

Building retrofit claims typically encounter:

1. **Weather variation year-to-year** — Addressed in Claim 3 via degree-day normalization. ✓
2. **Tenant-side behavioral changes** — Not addressed (Claim 4 lacks baseline; occupant use patterns not discussed).
3. **Measurement artifacts (meter calibration, billing changes)** — Utility bills are assumed accurate but not validated.
4. **Deferred maintenance (old boilers dying anyway)** — Retrofit timing relative to equipment end-of-life not addressed.
5. **Energy price volatility** — Explicitly flagged in Claim 6 but not quantified (how far must prices drop before payback exceeds 5 years?).

**Scenarios supplied by:** Audit (self-report). Domain expert (facilities team) was not interviewed on which scenarios actually occurred during retrofit period. This is `supplied_by: none` in Mizan schema, which means a tier-K coverage claim on "all scenarios handled" cannot proceed.

## Final Stance

The team lead is confident in "all proven," but three of six claims cannot support a sign-off at tier [K]:
- **Claim 1** is misleading (portfolio claim rests on showcase sample).
- **Claim 4** is impossible to verify (baseline data deleted).
- **Claim 6** is conditional (price assumption untested).

The **two [K] claims (3, 5)** are solid. **Claim 2** needs one acoustic test.

**Recommendation to the board:** Approve the retrofit on the strength of Claim 3 (measurable energy savings with control) and Claim 5 (documented cost). Reframe Claim 1 as "Retrofit saved 18% energy in showcased building; plan to measure other buildings before declaring portfolio-wide impact." Postpone Claim 4 (comfort) to post-retrofit survey. Condition Claim 6 (payback) on gas price scenarios: "Under current prices, under 5 years; at $X prices, under 7 years."

A honest, tiered presentation is stronger with the board than an inflated one.
