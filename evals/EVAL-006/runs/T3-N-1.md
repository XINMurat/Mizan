# Audit Report: Hiring Screening Tool Board Claims

**Audited:** Board summary of claims for hiring screening tool (6 assertions)
**Audit date:** 2026-09-29
**HARKing status:** Retrospective. Claims selected after tool was already deployed and metrics chosen to show favorable outcomes. No pre-audit registry exists; this is a post-deployment summary audit.
**Coverage:** All 6 claims audited. No inaccessible sources.

---

## Summary

Of the 6 claims presented for board approval, **none reach [K] (Proven) status**. Three are [KKE] (critical controls missing), two are [H] (plausible but unverified), and one is [S] (speculative). A sign-off on these claims cannot be justified. The tool's value may still be real, but these specific statements do not meet the evidence standards the board should expect.

---

## Claim-by-claim audit

### Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates"
**Evidence presented:** "Hiring managers who adopted it say the new hires 'feel stronger.'"

**Tier:** [H] Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis

**Justification:** 
- Source: Subjective impressions from hiring managers in unstructured feedback
- No definition of "better" (stronger at what? by what metric?)
- No baseline: no measurement of candidate quality before the tool
- Selection bias: only managers who adopted the tool provide feedback; non-adopters' views absent
- No control: no comparison to candidates hired without the tool in the same period
- The claim cannot fail: any newer hire can be said to "feel stronger," and the self-selection of adopting managers makes critical feedback unlikely

**Next step:** If this claim matters, define "candidate quality" (job performance at 6/12 months? manager rating scale? technical skill score?) and measure it for the same requisitions across adopt/non-adopt teams, controlling for department and role.

---

### Claim 2: "It is unbiased"
**Evidence presented:** "The vendor's white paper states it was trained on 'diverse data.'"

**Tier:** [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading

**Justification:**
- Source: Vendor marketing material, not independent evidence
- "Diverse data" is vague; no definition of what dimensions of diversity or how it was measured
- Vendor claims about their own product are known to be unreliable (vendor interest in favorable findings)
- No audit of the tool's actual outputs for bias
- No comparison to human panels or baselines
- The claim conflates training data diversity (input) with bias-free decision-making (output) — different claims requiring different evidence
- The statement technically contains a fact (vendor did write that) but frames it as proof of the broader claim of unbiasedness, which it does not

**Next step:** If bias risk is important to the board, conduct an independent bias audit. Rescore the same 600 applications (from Claim 4) by demographic group; compare tool's selection rate by gender, race, and age to: (a) the human panels' rates, and (b) the underlying applicant pool. Report gaps larger than 5 percentage points as [KKE] (control needed: is this due to the tool or to different applicant pools by group?).

---

### Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days"
**Evidence presented:** "HR-system dates for every requisition in the 2 quarters before and after, across all 14 departments, while a hiring freeze did not change the volume of open roles (212 vs 208)."

**Tier:** [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing

**Justification:**
- The time reduction itself is proven (41→29 days): HR data are hard records, measured consistently, full population (not a sample), and the 27% reduction is substantial
- Confounds not isolated: The statement asserts the time fell AND notes roles were stable, but does not establish that THE TOOL caused the reduction
- Alternative explanations not ruled out:
  - Other hiring process changes during this period (job descriptions, screening criteria, interview process, offer speed)?
  - Did recruiters shift effort or hiring team composition?
  - Did the labor market improve (applicant pool quality increased)?
  - Did compensation/offer terms change?
  - The "hiring freeze did not change volume" supports the tool's association but does not isolate its causal role
- Missing baseline: time reduction in a control group (a subset of departments or a peer company not using the tool)
- The claim as presented to the board implies the tool caused the reduction, but causation is not established

**Next step:** Extract time-to-hire by department for the post-deployment period. For a subset that did NOT use the tool (if one exists), compare their time-to-hire trend. If all departments adopted, compare the tool's timing within requisitions: do roles that went to the tool first close faster than those screened manually afterward? (This is weaker but may be available in your data.)

### Claim 4: "The tool's shortlist overlapped the panels' consensus shortlist 81%, versus 79% agreement between the two human panels"
**Evidence presented:** "In a blinded audit by an external firm, 600 anonymized applications were scored by the tool and by two trained human panels; the tool's shortlist overlapped the panels' consensus shortlist 81%, versus 79% agreement between the two human panels."

**Tier:** [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing

**Justification:**
- Measurement is executed: 600 applications, external firm (third-party arbiter), blinded (anonymized), quantified (81% vs 79%)
- Critical confounds:
  1. **Comparison direction:** 81% (tool ↔ panel consensus) vs 79% (panel ↔ panel) are different pairs. Tool-to-consensus agreement ≠ tool superiority. The tool is being compared to the consensus it was designed to serve; this is expected to be high.
  2. **Margin of error:** No significance test reported. 81% vs 79% is a 2-percentage-point difference on n=600. Confidence intervals likely overlap; this may not be meaningful.
  3. **What does "overlap" mean?** Are these binary (shortlist: yes/no), ranking-based, or threshold-based? If shortlist is "top 10," a 2-point difference could reflect rounding or borderline candidates.
  4. **Interpretation gap:** Does 81% mean the tool is *better* than panels, or just similar? The board summary frames it as a strength ("the tool's shortlist overlapped...") but similarity to trained humans is not proof of superiority.
  5. **Hidden alternative:** No comparison of tool+panels vs. panels alone. Would a human panel with access to the tool's scoring make better decisions? Would tool scores improve panel accuracy, or is the tool just redundant?

**Next step:** Before claiming this as evidence of tool quality, compute 95% confidence intervals around 81% and 79%. If they overlap, they are not distinguishable. Then rank all 600 applications by tool score and by average panel score; compare top-k hiring lists (top 20, top 50) and report which sublist sizes show meaningful agreement. Finally, design a test: In a next cycle, can you flag low-confidence borderline applicants (tool and panels disagree) and measure their on-job performance? If the tool and panels diverge on borderline cases, which signal is stronger for actual job success?

### Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve"
**Evidence presented:** "(It is too early to measure; the first cohort started 3 months ago.)"

**Tier:** [S] Spekülatif / Speculative

**Justification:**
- No data exist. The parenthetical is an explicit admission of absence of evidence.
- The claim is forward-looking (future-tense "will improve") but the reason given ("too early to measure") confirms it is not yet measured.
- This is a plausible hypothesis (better candidates might retain longer) but not testable today.
- Presenting this to the board without data is premature. It is not a finding; it is an aspiration.
- The threshold is not defined: what is "improve"? Baseline retention unknown. Success metric not locked.

**Next step:** Defer this claim from the board summary. Define first-year retention baseline (current tool-naive cohort, or historical average). Set a locked threshold (e.g., "≥65% 12-month retention, vs. 60% baseline") before the first cohort completes its first year. Measure and report by Q4 2027 at the earliest. If the user is concerned retention may suffer (e.g., if the tool brings in very different candidate profiles), set the metric to detect both improvement AND decline equally (two-sided informativeness).

---

### Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week"
**Evidence presented:** "(Recruiter estimates in a team meeting.)"

**Tier:** [H] Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis

**Justification:**
- Source: Self-reported estimates, not measured time
- Self-estimates are known to be biased and unreliable; a "quick chat" in a meeting is especially prone to inflation and to social desirability bias (recruiters reporting what the meeting asker wants to hear)
- No baseline: how long did tasks take before the tool? No measurement.
- No instrument: estimates are subjective; no time tracking, no log-based measurement, no controlled sample
- The number is plausible (10 hours is ~12% of a 40-hour week; tool savings in recruiting are realistically in this range) but unverified

**Next step:** Measure actual time spent on screening for a 1-week sample before and after the tool, or have recruiters use a time tracker for the same roles with/without the tool. If that is disruptive, ask one pilot department to measure and extrapolate. A measured 8–12 hours/week is valuable; an estimate of 10 is speculation. Report the measured value with caveats (sample size, single week or department, depends on role/volume mix).

## Closing: HARKing status and board readiness

**Retrospective analysis:** This audit examined claims chosen *after* seeing the tool's deployment outcomes. No pre-audit registry prevented threshold shopping or cherry-picking favorable metrics. Every claim that is quantified (3, 4, 6) arrives with a positive point estimate, which is consistent with post-hoc selection of metrics that flatters the tool.

**Board sign-off recommendation:** NOT READY. Reframe the board conversation:
- **Tier-K claims (Proven):** None of the 6 claims meet this standard.
- **Tier-H claims (Plausible):** Claims 1 and 6 are reasonable hypotheses worth pursuing but require measurement before decisions depend on them.
- **Tier-S claims (Speculative):** Claim 5 is too early to assess.
- **Tier-KKE claims (Control missing):** Claims 3 and 4 show promise (time reduction is real, tool agreement with panels is high) but causation and significance are not yet isolated.
- **Tier-Y claims (Misleading):** Claim 2 conflates vendor marketing with independent audit.

The tool may ultimately be valuable, but these claims do not justify the framing of "all proven." A responsible board summary would say:
> "Time-to-hire declined 27% in the first two quarters. Tool adoption is associated with this change, but we have not isolated its contribution from other process and market changes. We are measuring recruiter time savings and candidate retention outcomes; results will be available by [date]. Initial third-party audit found tool agreement with human panels is high (81%), though not distinguishably better than panel-to-panel agreement. We are planning a bias audit for [date]."

This is honest, data-grounded, and does not oversell. It is stronger with the board, not weaker, because it shows rigor.
