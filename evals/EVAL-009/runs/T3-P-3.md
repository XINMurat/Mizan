## Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims assessed, but only against the summary text. I had no access to the HR data, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, or the adoption timeline. Each verdict below says what would change it.

**Bottom line:** I can't confirm the summary as written. Two claims fail outright, two are measured but mis-framed, and two are predictions with no data. The team lead's confidence isn't supported by the evidence provided, and signing off on these lines would put unsupported claims in front of the board. A reworded version is below.

### What survives, with specificity

- **Claim 3 (descriptive part):** the HR-system decline across all 14 departments, with volume held roughly constant, is a credible observation. Its strength comes from the population coverage and the volume check.
- **Claim 4 (the blinding):** an external firm scoring 600 anonymized applications against human panels is the strongest evidence in the set. It is the right design; it just hasn't been analyzed to the standard the claim needs.

### Board-safe rewording

- "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days across all 14 departments in the two quarters after rollout, with open-role volume unchanged. The tool's contribution is not yet isolated."
- "In an external blinded audit of 600 applications, the tool's shortlist agreed with the panel consensus 81% of the time, against 79% agreement between the two human panels. The difference is within sampling noise; the audit did not measure candidate quality."
- "Bias has not been tested. The vendor's claim rests on training data composition, not on outcome parity."
- "Retention and recruiter time savings are hypotheses under measurement."
