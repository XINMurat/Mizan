# Review of the support chatbot board summary

I can't confirm that all six claims are proven. Two are adequately supported (1, with a caveat, and 4). The other four go beyond their evidence.

**1. Median first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds.** *Supported as a description of the metric, with a definition caveat.*
- Strengths: the data come from system timestamps covering the full population of 38,000 tickets, pulled by an independent data team, so measurement error is low.
- Caveat 1: "first response" may now include the bot's automated acknowledgment. If so, the 40-second figure measures how fast a message appears, not how fast a customer gets help. The board should see the exact event definition before and after launch.
- Caveat 2: this is a before/after comparison with no control. Seasonality, ticket volume, staffing, and the hiring freeze that began the same month could all shift response times. The number is accurate, but attributing the whole change to the bot is not shown.

**2. Customers prefer the bot to human agents.** *Not proven.*
- The evidence is the product team's reading of 30 transcripts. No selection method is stated, so the sample may be cherry-picked, and nothing measures preference: there is no survey, no choice experiment, and no comparison with human-handled chats.
- The reviewer is the team that built the product, which is a conflict of interest for a qualitative read.
- Even a fair reading of 30 transcripts could not support a claim about customers in general.

**3. The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own.** *Not proven.*
- "Resolved" is defined as the customer closing the chat window. Closing a window is not resolution. It also counts customers who give up, who leave for another channel, or who close the window while still waiting for help.
- The source is the vendor's dashboard, not an audited measure, and the vendor has a commercial interest in the figure.
- There is no baseline (what share of tickets were resolved without the bot?) and no check against outcomes such as repeat contact or reopened tickets.

**4. The bot reduced repeat contacts.** *Supported.*
- The design is a randomized test with 5,000 customers over 6 weeks, analyzed by a team independent of the bot team. Randomization is the right tool for a causal claim.
- The reported numbers are internally consistent: 11.4% − 9.1% = 2.3 points, which sits inside the 95% CI of −3.0 to −1.6.
- Items to confirm before the board sees it: that the analysis followed the original assignment (intention-to-treat), not just customers who stayed in their arm; that the 7-day outcome and 6-week window were prespecified; and that repeat contacts were captured across all channels in both arms. If bot-first customers contact support through a channel the system doesn't track, the difference could be overstated.
- The scope is the effect on repeat contacts within 7 days in this test population. It does not show effects on satisfaction or long-term retention.

**5. The bot will cut support headcount costs by a third next year.** *Not proven.*
- The source is a projection in the vendor's sales deck. It is a forecast, not an observed result, and it comes from a party that gains if the claim is believed.
- No model, assumptions, or staffing data are provided. It also ignores the hiring freeze and any change in ticket volume.

**6. Agents are happier since launch.** *Not proven.*
- There is no survey or other measure of agent wellbeing.
- Lower attrition is at most a weak proxy. It is confounded by the hiring freeze, which reduces departures for reasons unrelated to happiness (for example, people who might leave have no other jobs to move to).

**Recommendation for the board:** present claim 4 as the strongest result, and present claim 1 as a measured change in the response-time metric, with the definition clarified and without a causal attribution. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 should be removed or reframed as hypotheses to test, for example with a preference survey, an audited resolution metric that counts reopened or repeat contacts, and an agent survey.
