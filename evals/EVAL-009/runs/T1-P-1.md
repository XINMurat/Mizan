# Mizan Audit: Support Chatbot Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims reviewed. Claims 1, 3, and 4 are checkable against the data cited. Claims 2, 5, and 6 are not checkable with the evidence provided.

**Overall:** Only one claim is fully board-ready as written. The team lead's "all proven" is itself tier drift: the claims were stated with more confidence than their evidence supports.

---

**1. Median first response 4.2 h → 40 s.** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven, with a labeling condition
Full ticket population, timestamps, and an independent data team. The measurement is sound. Two caveats:
- **Definition:** If "first response" includes an automated acknowledgment, the 40 s figure measures the bot's greeting, not a substantive reply. Confirm the field definition and label it on the slide ("first reply, human or automated").
- **Attribution:** The data is a 10-week before/after comparison with no control for volume or seasonality. Report the change as descriptive, not as caused by the bot.

**2. Customers prefer the bot.** `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
Thirty transcripts from the product team, with no sampling method and no preference measure. Transcripts show what was said, not what customers preferred. Remove this claim or replace it with a preference survey.

**3. Bot resolves 70% of tickets.** `[Y]` Misleading / `[KKE]` Critical control missing
The vendor defines "resolved" as the customer closing the chat window. Closing a window is abandonment as often as resolution. The arbiter is the vendor, which is the threshold-theatre pattern. Before this goes to the board, define resolution as no repeat contact within 7 days (the metric from Claim 4), and audit a random sample of bot-closed tickets by hand.

**4. Bot reduces repeat contacts.** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven, with one verification item
This is the strongest claim: randomized, an independent analytics team, a CI that excludes zero, and a point estimate (−2.3 points) that sits at the center of its interval. Verify one thing before presenting it:
- **Sample size vs. CI width.** Under simple assumptions (independent customers, a binomial comparison at ~10%), 5,000 customers split into two arms gives a 95% CI half-width of roughly ±1.7 points. The reported half-width is ±0.7. The CI would require about 14,000 units per arm. The likely explanations are that the analysis unit is different, the CI is cluster-adjusted, or the reported n is wrong. Ask the analytics team which applies. The conclusion may still hold, but the interval as printed needs to be reconciled before it's shown.

**5. Headcount costs cut by a third next year.** `[S]` Speculative
A projection from a vendor sales deck. No baseline model or independent estimate is provided. Present it as a vendor estimate, or drop it.

**6. Agents are happier.** `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
No survey was run. Lower attrition coincided with a hiring freeze, which reduces attrition independently of morale. This is a confound that could fully explain the observation. If the board needs this, run an agent survey and compare attrition by team against teams unaffected by the freeze.

---

**Recommended board framing:**
- Lead with Claim 4 (randomized, reconcile the CI first) and Claim 1 (descriptive, with the definition stated).
- Drop or relabel Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 as `[S]` or `[Y]` until the listed checks run.

**Next step:** Before tomorrow, get three things: the first-response field definition (Claim 1), the per-arm n and analysis unit from the analytics team (Claim 4), and a hand-audit of 50 bot-closed tickets (Claim 3). If you can't get them by then, present Claim 4 and Claim 1 as the only findings and label the rest as open.
