## Mizan audit: board summary, retrofit building

**Bottom line:** Do not sign this off as written. Two claims are proven (3 and 5), one is proven only for the building it describes, and three are not supported by any evidence in the summary. Claim 6 depends on an assumption that makes it misleading. The board version should be reworded before tomorrow.

**Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims reviewed. Claims 3 and 5 have checkable sources. Claims 1, 2, 4 and 6 have no measurement behind them, so they can be assessed only for what the summary cites.

---

**1. Portfolio-wide energy savings** `[Y]` Misleading / `[KKE]` Critical control missing
The only evidence is one building that facilities chose to showcase. A hand-picked sample of one cannot support a portfolio claim. Nothing shows the other buildings saved energy.
*Verdict: NOT PROVEN.* Rewrite as "one building showed X% savings." For a portfolio claim, meter every building with a pre-set threshold, or run a random sample of retrofits with matched controls.

**2. Heat pumps quieter than old boilers** `[S]` Speculative
The only source is the installer, who has a commercial interest. No sound measurement is cited.
*Verdict: NOT PROVEN.* Measure dB(A) before and after at the same positions and operating conditions, using someone independent of the installer. Set the threshold before measuring.

**3. 18% less gas per degree-day, control +1%** `[K]` Proven (scoped)
This is the strongest claim. It uses utility meter data, normalises for weather with degree-days, and compares against a matched building on the same campus. The implied difference-in-differences effect is roughly 19%.
*Verdict: PROVEN*, for this building only. Before the board sees it, confirm three things:
- Pre-period parallel trends: check that the two buildings moved together in the two prior winters, not just in the retrofit winter.
- Control building: one control is a small comparison. Confirm nothing else changed there (occupancy, operating hours, plant work).
- Year-to-year variation: state how much the control's gas use varies normally, so the board can see whether +1% is noise.

Also confirm that "the retrofitted building" is the same building as claim 1. If it is, say so.

**4. Tenants more comfortable** `[S]` Speculative
Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit, so there is no baseline. This cannot be shown retrospectively.
*Verdict: NOT PROVEN.* Run a short tenant survey now, and state that the result is a post-retrofit snapshot with no before-and-after comparison. If the claim matters, the next retrofit should record comfort before and after.

**5. Installed cost 2.4 million** `[K]` Proven (scoped)
This is the signed final invoice, reconciled by finance. Source and reconciliation are both cited.
*Verdict: PROVEN.* One scope check: "total installed cost" should be stated as the contractor's invoice total. If design fees, internal labour, commissioning or incentives are excluded, say so.

**6. Payback under 5 years** `[Y]` Misleading
The claim assumes gas stays at this winter's peak price. Peak prices are not a steady state. The arithmetic is not shown anywhere in the summary, so it cannot be checked.
*Verdict: NOT PROVEN.* For a 5-year payback on 2.4 million, the building needs net savings of about 480,000 per year. At an 18% reduction, that requires a baseline gas spend of roughly 2.7 million per year. Check whether the 2.4 million covers this building alone or the portfolio, because the answer changes completely. Present payback as a range using average prices, not peak prices.

---

**Suggested board wording**

- "One showcase building, metered against a matched control, used about 18% less gas per degree-day than its prior two winters (control: +1%)." Scope: one building.
- "Total installed cost was 2.4 million (final contractor invoice, finance-reconciled)." Scope: state what the total includes.
- "Portfolio-wide savings, heat pump noise, and tenant comfort are not yet measured."
- "Payback is pending. It depends on gas price and on the cost scope; a range using average prices will be provided."

If the team lead wants a portfolio-level sign-off tomorrow, the honest answer is that the evidence does not support it yet. The next step is to decide which buildings to meter and set the threshold for "savings" before the data comes in.
