# Review of Support Chatbot Board Summary

Claims 1 and 4 are adequately supported as measured. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 are not, and should be removed or reworded before the board sees them. The team lead's view that all six are proven does not hold.

## Claim 1: Median first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds

**Supported as a measured change, with one definitional check.**

- **Evidence:** Ticketing-system timestamps covering all 38,000 tickets over 10 weeks before and after launch, pulled by the data platform team. This is a complete census from a system of record, not a sample, and the team that pulled it is independent of the bot.
- **Limitations:**
  - It is a before/after comparison with no control group. Seasonality, ticket volume, staffing changes, or routing changes in the same window could contribute to the drop. The claim as written does not attribute the change to the bot, so this is acceptable for the statement itself, but the board may read causation into it.
  - The key question is what "first response" means. If the bot's instant auto-acknowledgment now counts as a first response, the 40-second figure is technically accurate but describes a canned reply, not a substantive answer. The definition must be the same in both periods, and the slide should say what counts.

**Verdict: PROVEN**, as a statement about the recorded median. Confirm the definition and avoid implying the bot caused the change unless a control supports it.

## Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents

**Not supported.**

- **Evidence:** The product team's reading of 30 chat transcripts.
- **Problems:**
  - Thirty transcripts are too few and are not described as random. Selection could easily favor positive examples.
  - Transcripts show what customers said in a chat. They cannot show preference, because no comparison with human agents was made and no customer was asked which they would choose.
  - The reviewers are the product team that built and wants to promote the bot. No blinding, no coding rubric, and no inter-rater check.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.** Remove, or replace with a preference survey of matched customers who experienced both channels.

## Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own

**Not supported.**

- **Evidence:** A vendor dashboard.
- **Problems:**
  - "Resolved" is defined as the customer closing the chat window. Closing a window is not resolution. Customers abandon chats when frustrated, leave for a phone call, or give up. Those cases would count as "resolved" under this definition.
  - The metric is from the vendor, who has a financial interest in a high number. There is no independent audit.
  - Claim 4's repeat-contact data is a more direct test of resolution, and it does not show the 70% figure.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.** Replace with a resolution metric that means "issue did not recur within X days" and is measured independently. Otherwise drop the number.

## Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts

**Supported.**

- **Evidence:** A randomized test with 5,000 customers assigned to bot-first or human-first support for 6 weeks. Repeat contacts within 7 days were 9.1% vs 11.4%, with a 95% CI of −3.0 to −1.6 points. The analytics team computed the result, not the bot team.
- **Strengths:**
  - Randomization gives a valid control and supports a causal reading.
  - The analyst is independent of the people who built the bot.
  - The point estimate (−2.3) sits centered in the CI, and the entire interval is below zero.
- **Items to confirm before presenting:**
  - **The CI width needs a check.** If 5,000 customers were split evenly, a simple two-proportion calculation at these rates gives a standard error near 0.86 points and a 95% CI of roughly −4.0 to −0.6. The reported CI is narrower, implying roughly 14,000 per arm. The gap could be explained by covariate adjustment, clustering, or the 5,000 being a different unit (e.g., contacts rather than customers). The analytics team should confirm arm sizes and the estimator. The conclusion holds either way, since both intervals exclude zero, but the stated numbers should be reproducible.
  - Confirm the analysis was planned in advance, and whether it was intention-to-treat.
  - Six weeks and a 7-day window are short. The result applies to that population and period.

**Verdict: PROVEN.** This is the strongest evidence on the slide. Fix the CI reconciliation before the board sees it.

## Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year

**Not supported.**

- **Evidence:** A projection in the vendor's sales deck.
- **Problems:** This is a forecast from an interested party, not a result. It has no stated assumptions, no link to measured handling time or ticket volume, and no account of hiring freezes, wage changes, or the cost of the bot itself. Attributing a future cost change to the bot requires a model built on observed data.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.** Present only as a scenario with stated assumptions, labeled as an estimate, or remove.

## Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch

**Not supported.**

- **Evidence:** No survey. Attrition this quarter was lower, but a hiring freeze began the same month.
- **Problems:**
  - No direct measure of happiness exists.
  - Lower attrition has an obvious alternative explanation: a hiring freeze reduces external job options and internal moves, and it changes workload. Attrition also has no pre-launch baseline in the summary.
  - Even if attrition fell for reasons tied to the bot, it does not measure happiness.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.** Run an agent survey with a pre-launch baseline, or drop the claim.

- **Reword or remove:** Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6. Claim 3 can be kept only if "resolved" is redefined and measured independently.
- Do not present all six as proven. Two are supported, and four are not.
