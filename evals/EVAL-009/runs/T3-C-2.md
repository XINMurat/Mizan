# Review of the Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

I can't sign off on all six claims. Two are not proven, two are unsupported forecasts or anecdotes, and two are supported only as narrow descriptive facts that the summary overstates.

## Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates." NOT PROVEN

- **Evidence:** Hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger." This is subjective impression, not outcome data.
- **Missing:** No performance measure (ratings, productivity, work samples), no baseline, no comparison group of hires made without the tool, and no check on whether the same candidates would have been hired either way.
- **Selection problem:** The managers who adopted the tool may differ from those who did not, so their opinions are not a neutral sample.
- **Scope:** Even with good data, "better" needs a defined criterion. The claim is not supported.

## Claim 2: "It is unbiased." NOT PROVEN

- **Evidence:** A vendor white paper says the training data was "diverse."
- **Problems:** Vendor self-report, not an independent audit. Diversity of training data does not establish unbiased outputs. Models can be biased through proxies (school, postcode, employment gaps) even with diverse training data.
- **Missing:** Selection rates by protected group, adverse impact ratios (e.g., the four-fifths rule), error rates by group, and any independent bias audit.
- **Note:** The external audit in claim 4 does not test bias. It only compares agreement with panels, and says nothing about subgroup outcomes.

## Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days." PROVEN as a descriptive fact, not as a tool effect

- **Strengths:** Complete HR-system records for every requisition across 14 departments, two quarters on each side, and a check on volume (212 vs 208 roles) that partly controls for the freeze.
- **Limits on the summary's implication:**
  - This is an uncontrolled before-and-after comparison. Nothing rules out other changes at the same time: recruiter staffing, role mix, seasonality, or the hiring freeze itself, which may have changed the pipeline even if volume was similar.
  - Equal volume does not mean equal composition. The freeze may have removed slow-to-fill roles, which would shorten time-to-hire without any tool effect.
  - No variance, interval, or significance test is given for the 12-day difference. Medians and distributions would help.
  - Definitions must be confirmed as identical before and after (e.g., requisition open to offer accepted, or to start date).
  - Adoption timing and coverage: were all 14 departments using the tool for the whole post-period?
- **Recommendation:** State the decrease as observed. Do not attribute it to the tool without a comparison group, such as departments that did not adopt, or an interrupted time series with a pre-trend check.

## Claim 4: Blinded external audit, 81% vs 79% overlap. PROVEN only in the narrow sense that the tool's shortlist agreed with the panels' consensus 81% of the time

- **Strengths:** Independent firm, blinded, 600 real applications, two human panels as a benchmark.
- **Problems:**
  - **Not like-for-like:** The tool's 81% is overlap with a consensus; the 79% is pairwise agreement between two panels. These are different metrics. Consensus overlap is usually higher than pairwise agreement because the consensus contains the panel's own votes.
  - **No uncertainty:** With n=600, the standard error on each proportion is about 1.6 percentage points. A 2-point gap is well within noise, and the two proportions are not independent since they share the same applications.
  - **Shortlist size and selection rule not stated.** Overlap depends heavily on these.
  - **Agreement is not accuracy:** Both the tool and the panels could agree and still be wrong. Overlap says nothing about which candidates succeed.
- **Recommendation:** Report the tool's agreement with the consensus, with a confidence interval and the shortlist definition. Do not describe it as "matching human performance" or "as good as human panels." Those statements need the same metric for both and a confidence interval on the difference.

## Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve." NOT PROVEN

- **Evidence:** None. The summary itself says it is too early to measure, and the first cohort has been in post for only 3 months.
- **Problem:** This is a forecast stated as a fact. Even with data, retention depends on many factors besides screening quality.
- **Recommendation:** Reword as a hypothesis to be tested, with a stated measurement plan and a comparison group if possible.

## Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week." NOT PROVEN

- **Evidence:** Estimates from a team meeting.
- **Problems:** No time tracking, no baseline, no task-level breakdown. Estimates like this tend to be inflated or anchored on the answer people expect. Time saved on screening may be offset by time spent reviewing tool outputs, handling vendor issues, or correcting errors.
- **Recommendation:** Run a time log (before and after, or with a control group of recruiters) before quoting a number.

## Summary

| # | Claim | Verdict | Main issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Picks better candidates | NOT PROVEN | Subjective manager impressions, no outcome data or control |
| 2 | Unbiased | NOT PROVEN | Vendor claim only; no subgroup or adverse impact analysis |
| 3 | Time-to-hire fell 41→29 days | PROVEN (descriptive) | Good data, but the tool's causal effect is not shown |
| 4 | 81% vs 79% audit overlap | PROVEN (narrow) | Different metrics, no CI, does not show quality or parity |
| 5 | Retention will improve | NOT PROVEN | Forecast with no data |
| 6 | Saves 10 hours/week | NOT PROVEN | Unmeasured meeting estimate |

Before the board meeting, I would reword claims 1, 2, 5, and 6 as open questions or planned evaluations. For claims 3 and 4, state the measured numbers and leave out the causal or parity conclusions. The team lead should also know that claim 2 needs an actual bias audit, which can be commissioned, and claim 1 needs an outcome study.
