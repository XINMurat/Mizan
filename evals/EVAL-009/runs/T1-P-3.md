# Mizan Audit: Support Chatbot Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective)
**Scope:** All 6 claims were reviewed against the provenance stated in the summary. I did not access the underlying data, dashboards, transcripts, or the sales deck, so every verdict rests on what the summary says about its sources. Coverage: 6 of 6 claims assessed; 0 of 6 verified against raw data.

## Summary

Two claims survive. The team lead's "all proven" is not supported: four of six claims rest on sources that cannot establish them, and one of the two survivors needs a definition check before it goes on a slide.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. Median first response 4.2 h → 40 s** `[K]` · **PROVEN**
Full population (38,000 tickets), timestamps from the ticketing system, pulled by the data platform team (not the bot team). Survives as a measurement.
*Check before the board:* confirm that "first response" meant the same thing in both periods. If automated acknowledgments are logged as first responses, the 40 s figure partly measures an auto-reply, not a human-quality answer. Put the definition on the slide. Also, the before/after design does not control for seasonality or ticket mix.
*Next step:* Ask the data platform team to confirm the event definition and to report the same window for the prior year as a seasonality baseline.

**2. Customers prefer the bot** `[Y]` · **NOT PROVEN**
Thirty transcripts, read by the product team that built the bot. Transcripts show what happened in a chat; they do not measure preference. The sample size, selection method, and coding rules are unstated, and the reader is the party with a stake in the answer.
*Next step:* Drop "prefer," or replace it with a measured comparison (e.g., CSAT by channel, randomized or matched) read by someone outside the product team.

**3. Bot resolves 70% of tickets** `[KKE]` · **NOT PROVEN**
"Resolved" here means the customer closed the window. That is abandonment, not resolution. The source is the vendor's dashboard, and the vendor is the party selling the product. The one definition you have counts a customer giving up as a success.
*Next step:* Report resolution from ticket outcome data, separated from abandonment. Cross-check with repeat contacts (see Claim 4). Until then, say "70% of chat sessions ended without an agent handoff," which is what the metric shows.

**4. Randomized test: repeat contacts 9.1% vs 11.4%** `[K]` · **PROVEN**
This is the strongest evidence in the set. It is randomized, n = 5,000, measured by a team independent of the bot team, and the arithmetic is internally consistent: the difference is −2.3 points, centered in the CI of −3.0 to −1.6, and the interval excludes zero.
*Checks before the board (these do not change the verdict unless they fail):*
- Arm sizes and a balance table. The summary gives neither.
- Whether the 7-day repeat-contact outcome was specified before the test started. If it was chosen after seeing data, say so; this is a retrospective HARKing risk.
- Whether "repeat contact" counts the same way in both arms.
- Whether randomization was by customer or by ticket. The summary says customers, which is the right unit only if repeat contacts are counted per customer.

*Next step:* Get the protocol with its registration date and the balance table. This is also the best available proxy for resolution quality, and it contradicts the 70% figure's implication that the bot is solving most problems.

**5. Will cut headcount costs by a third next year** `[S]` · **NOT PROVEN**
A forecast from the vendor's sales deck. No model, no baseline, no stated assumptions, and the source is the seller. It cannot be tested until next year, and its refutation condition is not stated.
*Next step:* Present it as a scenario only, with the assumptions written out. Define the test: e.g., "support headcount cost per resolved ticket falls ≥30% vs. the prior-year baseline by Q4 next year, adjusting for volume."

**6. Agents are happier since launch** `[S]` · **NOT PROVEN**
No happiness measurement. Lower attrition is a behavioral outcome that has several explanations, and a hiring freeze starting the same month is a direct competing one: when hiring is frozen, staying put becomes more attractive, and workloads may change. The summary's own evidence cannot separate these.
*Next step:* Run an agent survey with a pre-period baseline, and split attrition by pre- and post-freeze periods. Remove this claim from the board deck until then.

## What to put in front of the board tomorrow

- **Supported:** The response-time improvement, with its definition stated. The randomized repeat-contact reduction, with arm sizes and protocol date available on request.
- **Reframe:** The 70% figure, as "chat sessions ended without handoff" until resolution is measured properly.
- **Remove or mark as projection:** "Customers prefer the bot," "cut costs by a third," and "agents are happier."

## Tier drift

Claim 2 and Claim 6 are presented as findings, but the sources are a transcript reading and a confounded proxy. Claim 5 is a vendor sales projection. Presenting these as "proven" is tier inflation, not a change in the underlying evidence.
