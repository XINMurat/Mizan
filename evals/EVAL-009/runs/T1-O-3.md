# Board-summary audit (retrospective)

**HARKing status:** This is a retrospective audit of a summary written after the conclusions were reached. I had only the text. I did not access the ticketing data, transcripts, vendor dashboard, or the RCT file. The checks below are design review and internal-consistency arithmetic. Coverage: 6 of 6 claims assessed on the text; 1 numerical check run (claim 4); 0 of 6 verified against source data.

**Bottom line:** None of the six claims is proven as worded. Claims 1 and 4 are strong candidates, each with one specific check that could settle it. Claims 2, 3, 5 and 6 should not go to the board as findings.

---

### 1. First response 4.2 h → 40 s
**Tier: [KKE] Critical control missing / Kritik Kontrol Eksik**

- **Source:** Ticketing timestamps, all 38,000 tickets, pulled by the data platform team. This is a strong instrument, and the arbiter is independent of the bot team.
- **Missing control 1, definition:** If the bot's automatic acknowledgment counts as "first response," the drop is partly built into the metric. The bot sends an instant reply by design. The summary does not say which definition was used.
- **Missing control 2, concurrent change:** The hiring freeze began the same month (see claim 6). Staffing changes move human queue times directly. A before/after comparison cannot separate the launch from the freeze or from seasonal volume.
- **Could flip it:** If the human-first-response median barely moved, the headline is mostly a definitional effect.
- **Next step:** Report the median split by first-response type (bot vs. human), and add the same 10-week window from the prior year as a seasonal baseline.

### 2. Customers prefer the bot
**Tier: [Y] Misleading / Yanıltıcı**, arbiter `author`

- Thirty transcripts read by the product team, with no sampling rule and no denominator. A transcript shows what happened in a chat, not what the customer preferred. Preference requires a choice or a survey.
- Permanent `[KKE]` on any preference reading from this source.
- **Replacement:** Among RCT customers, the share of bot-first customers who asked for a human, plus a survey with a stated response rate.

### 3. Bot resolves 70% of tickets
**Tier: [Y] Misleading**, arbiter `third_party` (vendor, with a commercial interest)

- "Resolved" is defined as the customer closing the window. Abandonment counts as resolution.
- The RCT repeat-contact figure (9.1% within 7 days) is a partial cross-check, but it is population-level and does not measure resolution. It cannot confirm 70%.
- **Replacement:** Resolution = no contact on the same issue within 7 days and an explicit customer confirmation, measured in-house.

### 4. Bot reduced repeat contacts (RCT)
**Tier: [KKE]**, arbiter `instrument` (analytics team, independent of the bot team)

- **Design strengths:** Randomized, 5,000 customers, six weeks, analysed by a team outside the product. This is the strongest evidence in the set.
- **Arithmetic check:** 9.1% − 11.4% = −2.3 points, which matches the midpoint of the reported CI (−3.0 to −1.6). Internally consistent so far.
- **The problem is the CI width.** With about 2,500 customers per arm, the standard error of the difference is about 0.86 points, so the 95% CI would be roughly **−4.0 to −0.6**. The reported half-width of 0.7 points needs about 15,000 independent customers per arm, roughly six times the stated sample. Two explanations are possible:
  - The analysis treats tickets as independent units when several tickets come from the same customer. This understates variance, and the reported CI is too narrow.
  - The CI is computed incorrectly.
- **Also check:** the denominator for the repeat rate (all assigned customers, or only those who contacted support); whether the analysis is intention-to-treat; whether the 7-day outcome window was fixed before the test; and whether the arms were balanced at baseline.
- **Could flip it:** A customer-clustered CI that includes zero would make the result inconclusive. The effect may still be real; the current interval just does not show it at the stated precision.
- **Next step:** Recompute the CI clustered by customer and state the denominator. If the CI still excludes zero, this claim is the strongest one in the set and can be promoted to `[K]`.

### 5. Support headcount costs cut by a third next year
**Tier: [S] Speculative / Spekülatif**, arbiter `third_party` (vendor sales deck, commercial interest)

- A projection from a sales deck. No baseline, no cost model, and not testable until next year.
- **Replacement:** Frame as a hypothesis with a stated threshold and a date, judged by finance against actual headcount cost.

### 6. Agents are happier
**Tier: [S] Speculative**

- No survey, so there is no happiness measure. Attrition is a proxy, and the hiring freeze is a confound that can lower attrition on its own (people stay when external options are scarce).
- **Replacement:** A survey with a baseline taken before launch, or drop the claim.

### Summary for tomorrow

- **Do not sign off "all proven."** Presenting the six claims with equal confidence would give the board a uniform `[K]` that the evidence does not support.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Present claim 1 with the definition caveat (or with the split by first-response type, if it can be run first).
  - Present claim 4 with the CI as currently reported and a note that the clustered recalculation is pending. Promote it once that check is done.
  - Move claims 2, 3, 5 and 6 out of the findings and into a "hypotheses under test" section with their replacement measures.
- **Why:** Claim 4 is the only one that could carry a causal statement, and it depends on a CI that does not match the stated sample. Presenting it unchecked risks a board decision built on an overstated precision.
- **Cost if I am wrong:** If the CI holds up, presenting claim 4 with the caveat costs a sentence of hedging. If I am wrong to be cautious on claim 1 and the split confirms the effect, the headline is stronger with the split than without it.
- **Who should do the two checks:** The analytics team owns the RCT file and can recompute the clustered CI. The data platform team can produce the first-response split. Both are a short job if the raw files are accessible. I can draft the exact queries if you share the schema.
