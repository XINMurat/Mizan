# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims are checkable in principle. I reviewed only the summary text. I did not see the raw HR data, the external audit report, or the white paper, so the verdicts rest on what is written here.

**Bottom line:** Do not sign off as written. One claim is supported, and it is narrower than the board will likely read it. Four claims have no outcome evidence, and one has a causal attribution the data cannot yet carry.

---

### 1. "The tool picks better candidates." `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
- **Evidence:** Hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger."
- **Problem:** This is a subjective impression with no outcome measure, no baseline, and no comparison group. The blinded audit in item 4 measures agreement with human panels, not quality of hire. Agreement with humans cannot show the tool is *better* than them.
- **Verdict:** Not supported.
- **Next step:** Register a prediction with a refutation condition, e.g., "Hires scored in the top tier by the tool achieve a supervisor-rated performance score at least X points above the baseline cohort at 6 months. If not, the claim is refuted." Measure it.

### 2. "It is unbiased." `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
- **Evidence:** The vendor's white paper says the model was trained on "diverse data."
- **Problem:** Training-data diversity does not establish unbiased outputs. "Unbiased" needs adverse-impact analysis: selection rates by gender, age, ethnicity, and disability status, compared across groups, with an independent auditor. The vendor is an interested party. This is a marketing claim, not a test result.
- **Verdict:** Not supported.
- **Next step:** Commission a selection-rate analysis on your own applicant data. Use a preset threshold (the US four-fifths rule is a common reference; check the equivalent under your jurisdiction's employment and data-protection law, e.g., KVKK in Turkey). Ask the vendor for independent bias-audit results, not white-paper language.

### 3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days." `[KKE]` Critical control missing
- **What holds:** The data source is strong: HR-system dates, every requisition, 14 departments, two quarters on each side. Open-role volume was similar (212 vs 208), so workload is roughly constant. The decline in the records is probably real.
- **Problems:**
  - The summary presents the decline as a tool effect. A before/after comparison with no control group cannot separate the tool from other changes during the same window (seasonality, recruiter staffing, other process changes).
  - It is unclear whether all 14 departments adopted the tool on the same date. If so, there is no within-sample control.
  - Timestamp definitions may have changed when the tool was introduced, for example when a requisition is marked "opened" or "filled." That would shift the measurement itself.
  - It is unclear whether 41 and 29 are means or medians. A few long requisitions can drive a mean.
  - A hiring freeze occurred in the same period. The volume figures show the open-role count was similar, but the summary does not explain what the freeze did to hiring pace. Ask this before the board does.
- **Verdict:** The decline is probably real in the records. The claim that the tool caused it is not proven.
- **Next step:** Check whether the decline starts at the tool's rollout date, not at a calendar boundary. Compare against the same quarters a year earlier (seasonality). Use a difference-in-differences design if any departments adopted later or not at all. Confirm timestamp definitions did not change. Report median and distribution.

### 4. Blinded audit: 81% vs 79% overlap `[K]` Proven (narrow scope) / `[Y]` if read as superiority
- **What holds:** This is the strongest design in the summary. The audit was external and blinded, with 600 anonymized applications and independent scoring.
- **Limits:**
  - The 2-point difference is well within sampling noise for 600 applications. The confidence interval is not reported. A rough estimate, assuming a shortlist of about 20%, puts the interval on the difference at roughly ±10 points.
  - The result shows *parity* with human agreement. It does not show superiority, and the 79% baseline is itself an agreement rate, not an accuracy rate.
  - The consensus shortlist is built from human judgments, so it is not ground truth.
- **Verdict:** The numbers are supported as stated. They support "comparable to human panels." They do not support "better than human panels."
- **Next step:** Report the confidence interval. Define "overlap" precisely (set overlap, Jaccard, or Cohen's kappa). State the non-inferiority margin in advance if parity is the claim.

### 5. "First-year retention of hires will improve." `[S]` Speculative
- **Problem:** The first cohort started three months ago. Nothing can be measured yet. The claim is a forecast, and no threshold or refutation condition is stated.
- **Verdict:** Not supported. This is correctly labeled as too early, but it cannot go on the board as a finding.
- **Next step:** Register it now: "12-month retention of tool-screened hires is at least X percentage points above the pre-tool baseline of Y. If it is equal to or below Y at 12 months, the hypothesis is refuted." Record the outcome whether or not it is favorable.

### 6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week." `[S]` Speculative
- **Problem:** The figure comes from recruiter estimates in a team meeting. Self-reported time savings from a tool the team is enthusiastic about are a known overestimate pattern. No baseline exists.
- **Verdict:** Not supported.
- **Next step:** Run a four-week time log (task-level, pre-tool vs post-tool, same recruiters). Report the measured number, even if it is smaller than 10.

---

### What you can present tomorrow
- **Supportable:** The time-to-hire decline appears in the HR records, with volume held roughly constant. Describe it as "observed," not "caused by the tool," and say the attribution is under investigation.
- **Supportable:** In a blinded external audit, the tool's shortlist agreed with the consensus about as often as two human panels agreed with each other.
- **Not supportable yet:** Better hires, unbiased selection, retention gains, and time savings. Present these as pending, with the tests you will run and their refutation conditions.

I'd recommend recording these six as registry entries, with the four open tests as the preregistered hypotheses, so the board sees a tracked plan rather than unproven claims.
