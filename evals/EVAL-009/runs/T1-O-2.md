# Mizan Audit: Support Chatbot Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **HARKing status:** The six claims were selected by the team, and I reviewed only the text and the sources described in it. I could not open the ticketing data, the transcripts, or the randomization logs, so every tier below rests on the descriptions given.

**Bottom line:** These are not all proven. Claim 4 is the strongest claim in the set. Claims 1 and 4 are each one check away from proven. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 should not appear on a "proven" slide.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. Median first response 4.2 h → 40 s. Tier: [KKE]**
- *Credit:* The source is strong: all 38,000 tickets, system timestamps, pulled by a data team, not the chatbot team.
- *Problem 1:* A before/after comparison has no control. Ticket volume, seasonality, staffing, and the hiring freeze all changed during the same window. The number is real, but it does not isolate the bot.
- *Problem 2 (could flip the headline):* If the bot's instant acknowledgment counts as "first response," the 40 s figure measures an auto-greeting, not a human-quality answer. The summary does not say which definition was used.
- *Check:* Recompute the median with first *substantive* response (first non-automated agent or bot answer). Then compare with the same weeks of the prior year, or with a non-launched product line, as a baseline.
- *Say tomorrow:* "Time to automated acknowledgment fell from hours to seconds. Time to substantive answer: [pending]."

**2. Customers prefer the bot. Tier: [Y]**
- Thirty transcripts, chosen by the product team, with no stated sampling rule and no human-agent comparison arm. "Prefer" was not measured; it was read into the transcripts.
- *Say tomorrow:* Drop it, or replace it with a CSAT comparison across bot and human conversations.

**3. Bot resolves 70% on its own. Tier: [Y]**
- The vendor defines "resolved" as the customer closing the window. That measures abandonment, and a customer who gives up and leaves is counted as resolved. The figure comes from the party selling the product.
- *Say tomorrow:* "70% of chats ended without escalation" (the accurate description), plus the repeat-contact result from claim 4.

**4. Bot-first reduced 7-day repeat contacts (9.1% vs 11.4%, CI −3.0 to −1.6). Tier: [KKE]**
- *Credit:* This is the only randomized comparison in the set, with 5,000 customers and 6 weeks of follow-up. The analytics team, not the bot team, ran it. The arithmetic checks out: 9.1 − 11.4 = −2.3, which sits in the CI midpoint.
- *Open check that could flip it:* Is a repeat contact counted the same way in both arms? If bot-first conversations do not create tickets, the bot arm can look better simply because its contacts were recorded in a different place. This is a measurement-channel confound, and nobody has reported ruling it out.
- *Other items to report:* Whether the analysis was intent-to-treat, whether the 7-day window and the contact definition were fixed before the test, and whether the randomization split was 1:1.
- *Say tomorrow (now):* "In a randomized test, bot-first routing was associated with a 2.3-point reduction in 7-day repeat contacts." Upgrade to "reduced" once the channel check is done.

**5. Support headcount costs cut by a third next year. Tier: [S]**
- A vendor's sales-deck projection. No model, no baseline, no cost data in the summary.
- *Say tomorrow:* Remove it, or present it as a scenario the finance team has modeled.

**6. Agents are happier. Tier: [Y]**
- No survey was run. Lower attrition is not a happiness measure, and a hiring freeze can change attrition and morale independently of the bot.
- *Say tomorrow:* Remove it, or run a short agent survey before the next board cycle.

## What this summary cannot show

- **Cost and quality tradeoffs:** Escalation accuracy, bad bot answers, and customer satisfaction are absent. A faster and more repetitive channel can look good on these four numbers.
- **Failure cases:** Which ticket types the bot handles poorly, and whether those customers are the ones who churn.
- **Domain coverage:** I have not run the domain probe. Scenarios such as a customer with two open issues, a bot handover mid-conversation, or an agent who leaves during a queue backlog all need to be supplied by someone who works in support operations. I will not invent them.

## Next steps (ordered by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Check the repeat-contact channel definition (claim 4).** Low effort, decisive. *My recommendation:* have the analytics team confirm in writing that bot-first and human-first contacts were counted identically. This is the one check that could make your strongest claim the headline.
2. **Re-define "first response" (claim 1).** Low effort. *Recommendation:* report both the automated and substantive figures, side by side.
3. **Replace claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 on the slide.** Zero effort. *Recommendation:* use the rewording above. If the team lead wants the original wording kept, the decision is theirs, but the board should be told which claims are unverified.
4. **Register these claims as tracked hypotheses** so the board follow-up has a record: claims 1 and 4 as open entries with their checks, and 2, 3, 5, and 6 as `[S]` or `[Y]` items with named evidence needed.

If the channel check and the substantive response-time figure both come back clean, claims 1 and 4 can be promoted to proven and the verdicts below updated.
