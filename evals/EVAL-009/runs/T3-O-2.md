# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool, Board Summary

**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The summary was written after the outcomes were known. I had only the summary text. I did not have the HR extracts, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, or the recruiter time data, so every tier below is provisional on the source. Coverage: 6 of 6 claims read; 1 of 6 partly checkable from the text (claim 3's design); 0 of 6 verified against source files.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** `[S]` → verdict NOT PROVEN
The only support is hiring managers saying new hires "feel stronger." That is self-report with no outcome measure (performance rating, quality-of-hire, manager scorecard) and no comparison group. The claim names an outcome the evidence never measured. Test: compare a defined quality-of-hire metric for tool-shortlisted versus non-shortlisted candidates, with the metric and threshold fixed before the comparison.

**2. "It is unbiased."** `[Y]` / `[S]` → verdict NOT PROVEN
The white paper's "trained on diverse data" describes training inputs, not outputs. Diverse training data does not establish unbiased scoring. The external audit measured agreement with human panels, not adverse impact by group, so it cannot test this either. Test: selection rates by protected group at each funnel stage, with the four-fifths ratio or a pre-set equivalent as the threshold, using the vendor's own data plus your applicant data.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** `[KKE]` on the causal reading; the descriptive figure stands → verdict PROVEN
This is the strongest claim. It uses HR-system dates, covers every requisition in all 14 departments, and checks volume (212 vs 208) to rule out the freeze as a volume explanation. That is a real confound check. What it does not show is that the tool caused the drop. The before/after design has no concurrent control: no departments that did not adopt the tool, and no check of when adoption began relative to other process changes. Present it as "time-to-hire fell 12 days across the period" and not as "the tool cut time-to-hire by 12 days" unless a control is added.

**4. "Shortlist overlap 81% vs 79%."** `[KKE]` → verdict NOT PROVEN
The numbers may be accurately reported, but the comparison is not like-for-like. The tool's 81% is overlap with a consensus shortlist. The human 79% is pairwise agreement between two panels. Those are different statistics. Also, a 2-point gap on 600 applications is well inside sampling noise (roughly ±3–4 points at this n), so the data cannot separate tool from humans. Finally, agreement with human panels measures similarity to human judgment, which inherits whatever bias those panels have. It is not evidence of better selection. Correct framing: "the tool's shortlists were broadly consistent with human panels; the difference is not meaningful."

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** `[S]` → verdict NOT PROVEN
This is a forecast with no data. The first cohort is 3 months old, and first-year retention cannot be observed yet. Nothing in the summary states a threshold, a comparison group, or a date. It should not appear as a board claim. Register it as a hypothesis with a locked metric (e.g., 12-month retention of tool-hired versus non-tool hires), a comparison cohort, and a measurement date.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** `[S]` → verdict NOT PROVEN
The source is an estimate given in a team meeting. The arbiter is the author's own recollection (`author`), so under the method this cannot reach `[K]` and is permanently `[KKE]` until measured. Test: time logs before and after, or task-level timing sampled across recruiters, with the saving defined as a specific number of hours.

## Recommendation on sign-off

Do not sign off the summary as written. Two claims (1 and 2) are the claims the board is most likely to act on, and both are unsupported. Claims 5 and 6 are forecasts or estimates presented as results.

Recommended wording for tomorrow:

- Keep **3**, reworded as a descriptive finding (time-to-hire fell 41 → 29 days over the period; no control group yet, so attribution to the tool is not established).
- Reword **4** as "tool shortlists were consistent with human panels (81%); human panels agreed with each other at 79%; no meaningful difference."
- Move **1, 2, 5, 6** to a "hypotheses under test" section, each with the test named above.

**Why this recommendation:** if the team lead wants a sign-off on the full list, the cost is that a board decision rests on a bias claim with no adverse-impact test and a productivity claim with no outcome measure. If the recommendation is wrong (the team has bias and quality data not included in this summary), the rewording costs nothing, because the tests can be added and the claims promoted with dated results.
