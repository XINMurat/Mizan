# Mizan Audit: Support Chatbot Board Summary

**Verdict for the board:** Do not sign off on all six as proven. Two claims survive (1 and 4, with corrections). Four are not supported by the evidence given.

**Scope and constraint:** I audited the text alone. I did not have the raw data, the analysis code, any pre-registration for the trial, or the vendor's metric definitions. Each verdict below is conditional on what the text states.

---

### 1. Median first response 4.2 h → 40 s
**Tier: [K] Kanıtlanmış / Proven**

Ticketing-system timestamps for all 38,000 tickets, pulled by the data platform team, are direct evidence. The effect size is too large for seasonality or volume shifts to explain.

**Condition to state on the slide:** "First response" must mean the same thing before and after launch. If the bot's automatic acknowledgment counts as a first response, the 40 s figure measures a canned reply, not a substantive answer. That is a definition problem, not a confound, so the metric is correct but the headline must say what it measures.

**Next step:** Confirm with the data platform team that auto-acknowledgments are excluded or reported separately. Add the median time to first *human-substantive* response as a second line.

---

### 2. Customers prefer the bot to human agents
**Tier: [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading** (the evidence is [S] at best)

Thirty transcripts read by the team that built the bot cannot measure preference. They show what happened in 30 conversations, not what customers chose or would choose. The reader also has a stake in the result, and the sample's selection is not described.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.**

**Next step:** Replace with a measured result. Options: a post-chat preference or CSAT item, or a randomized choice between bot and human. Until then, drop "prefer" from the slide.

---

### 3. The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own
**Tier: [Y] Yanıltıcı / Misleading**

The definition is the problem. "Resolved" here means the customer closed the chat window. Closing a window is also what abandonment looks like. A customer who gives up and leaves is counted as a resolution. The vendor's dashboard is also not an independent measure.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.** The 70% figure could be true as an abandonment-plus-success rate, but the text does not establish that it is a resolution rate.

**Next step:** Define resolution as a confirmed outcome, such as no repeat contact within 7 days plus a positive or neutral closing response. Audit a random sample of 100 to 200 "resolved" chats by hand. Report the agreement rate. Source the dashboard metric from your own data, not the vendor's.

---

### 4. The bot reduces repeat contacts
**Tier: [K] Kanıtlanmış / Proven** (direction), with a [KKE] flag on the reported interval

This is the strongest evidence in the set: a randomized design, 5,000 customers, a six-week window, and analysis by a team independent of the bot team. The direction survives. Reported rates of 9.1% vs 11.4% give a 2.3-point difference, and the stated interval (−3.0 to −1.6) is centered on it.

**Two problems to fix before the board:**

- **The interval looks too narrow for the stated sample.** For a two-proportion comparison with about 2,500 customers per arm, the 95% CI half-width is roughly ±1.7 points. The reported half-width is ±0.7. The sample size, unit of analysis (customers, tickets, or contacts), or clustering must be explained. My estimate is rough, but the gap is large enough to check. I checked whether the conclusion survives plausible sample sizes: it does. Even at roughly 2,500 per arm, the interval excludes zero. The magnitude estimate is what is unreliable, not the sign.
- **Was the analysis pre-registered?** The outcome (repeat contact within 7 days) and the comparison were presumably fixed in advance. If they were chosen after seeing results, state that.

**Also check:** Whether bot-first contacts are logged the same way as human-first contacts. If bot interactions create fewer ticket-level "contacts" by design, the lower repeat count may be a measurement artifact. This would not flip the result, but the board should know.

**Next step:** Ask the analytics team for the n per arm, analysis unit, and the registration date of the analysis plan. Re-state the effect with the corrected interval. Quote it as "2.3 points lower (CI …)," not as a percentage reduction.

**Verdict: PROVEN** (direction). Correct the interval before presenting.

---

### 5. Cuts support headcount costs by a third next year
**Tier: [S] Spekülatif / Speculative** (the source is a sales projection)

The source is the vendor's sales deck. The vendor has a financial interest in the figure, and the deck does not state its assumptions, baseline, or method. No cost model appears in the text. A headcount-cost projection based on the bot's current performance also depends on claims 3 and 6, which are not established.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.**

**Next step:** Build an internal cost model using your own staffing and ticket-volume data. Model the bot's effect using the measured numbers from claims 1 and 4 only. Run sensitivity analysis on containment, which is currently uncertain because of claim 3. Present the result as a range with the assumptions listed.

---

### 6. Agents are happier since launch
**Tier: [KKE] Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing** (no direct measurement exists)

No survey was run. The proxy, lower attrition this quarter, is confounded by the hiring freeze that began the same month. Freezes reduce outside job options and cut the number of open positions, which typically lowers voluntary attrition regardless of how happy agents are. The data cannot separate the two.

**Verdict: NOT PROVEN.**

**Next step:** Run an agent wellbeing or engagement survey before and after, with a comparison group if possible. Track attrition separately for periods with and without the hiring freeze. Until that exists, remove the claim from the board deck.

---

## Recommended board framing

| # | Claim | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Response time | Present, with definition stated |
| 2 | Customer preference | Remove or measure |
| 3 | 70% resolution | Redefine and re-measure, or relabel as "chat closures" |
| 4 | Repeat contacts | Present, after correcting the interval |
| 5 | Headcount savings | Replace with an internal model or remove |
| 6 | Agent happiness | Remove or survey |

**Bottom line:** The team lead's confidence is justified for claims 1 and 4, which the data supports. The other four would not survive a board member's first question about how they were measured. Fixing the interval in claim 4 and the definition in claim 1 takes a day or two. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 need actual measurements before they can go on a slide.
