## Notes

**Claim 1 (NOT PROVEN).** The portfolio claim has no evidence. You have one building, and it was selected for good results, so it cannot represent the portfolio. Reword to "the showcase building used X% less gas" or measure a random sample of buildings.

**Claim 2 (NOT PROVEN).** The installer's statement is a sales claim, not a measurement. Get a dB(A) reading at a stated distance, before and after, or drop the claim.

**Claim 3 (PROVEN, scoped to this building).** This is the strongest claim, and it is the one I would stand behind. It has a control, it uses meter data rather than estimates, and it normalizes for weather. Before the board sees it, check three things:
- The control building was fixed before the results were in, not chosen afterward.
- The post-retrofit period is the same length and season as the baseline winters. A partial first winter would distort the result.
- The degree-day base temperature was the same for both buildings.

It is still one building. Say so on the slide.

**Claim 4 (NOT PROVEN).** Without a baseline, you cannot show a change. If comfort matters to the board, run a short tenant survey now, and label it as a baseline for future comparison.

**Claim 5 (PROVEN, scoped).** The signed invoice and finance reconciliation are good evidence. Check whether "total" includes design fees, surveys, and internal labour. If it does not, the total is understated and the slide should say "contractor invoice total."

**Claim 6 (NOT PROVEN).** Rerun the payback at the forward or average gas price, and show the break-even gas price. The board needs to see how far prices can fall before the 5-year target is missed.
