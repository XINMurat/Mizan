# Audit Report: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**Mode:** Audit (retrospective). **Coverage:** 6 of 6 claims reviewed. I checked the logic of the stated evidence only. I did not have the underlying data, the vendor white paper, or the external audit report, so every "data not seen" item below is a request, not a finding.

**Result: 0 of 6 claims are proven as written.** Two are close to defensible if reworded, and item 3 has the strongest evidence in the set.

---

## Claim-by-claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
Evidence is that managers say new hires "feel stronger." That is subjective, unmeasured, and comes from managers who chose to adopt the tool. No outcome (performance rating, ramp time, 6–12 month quality) is measured against a baseline. Item 4 measures agreement with human panels, not quality of hire, so it does not rescue this claim.
*Refutation condition:* the tool-hired cohort's performance ratings at 6 months are no better than a matched pre-tool cohort or a human-screened control.

**2. "It is unbiased."** `[S]` Speculative / `[Y]` Misleading
"Trained on diverse data" describes training inputs, not outcomes. A vendor white paper is a marketing source, and no adverse-impact analysis is cited. Bias can appear at the screening stage even with diverse training data.
This is also a legal exposure, not just a wording issue. NYC Local Law 144 requires an independent bias audit for automated employment decision tools used on NYC candidates, and the EU AI Act classifies recruitment AI as high-risk. Please confirm with counsel which jurisdictions apply.
*Needed:* selection rates and impact ratios by gender, race/ethnicity, age, and disability at each funnel stage (the four-fifths rule is the usual first screen), from an independent auditor.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** `[KKE]` Critical control missing
This is the strongest evidence here: HR-system dates, all requisitions, 14 departments, two quarters on each side. The descriptive fact survives. The problem is attribution and method:
- **Uncontrolled pre/post.** Seasonality, process changes, and staffing changes can explain a 12-day drop. No comparison group (departments or periods without the tool) is cited.
- **Volume is not composition.** Equal volume (212 vs 208) during the freeze does not show equal role mix or urgency.
- **Right-censoring.** Requisitions still open at the end of the period may be excluded from the after-period average, which biases it downward. Please check how open requisitions were handled.
- **Adoption date.** The summary does not say when the tool went live relative to the window.

*Verdict logic:* as written, the board reads this as a tool benefit, so it is NOT PROVEN. The sentence "time-to-hire fell by 12 days over the two quarters after rollout" would be supported by the data as reported. Presenting it that way is the honest version.

**4. Blinded external audit, 81% vs 79% overlap.** `[KKE]` Critical control missing
The design is good: external firm, anonymized applications, 600 cases. Three problems:
- **The difference is two percentage points with no confidence interval.** Depending on shortlist size, that is one or two candidates. It is very likely within noise. Bootstrap or a paired test would settle it.
- **Agreement is not accuracy.** Matching a consensus of human panels measures similarity to those panels, including their biases. It cannot show the tool is better.
- **"Blinded" needs definition.** Anonymization can leave proxies (school names, employment gaps, locations) in the text.

*Supportable reading:* "agreement with human panels is in the same range as panel-to-panel agreement." That is not a superiority claim.

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** `[S]` Speculative
A forecast about an outcome that cannot yet be measured. The first cohort is 3 months old and no retention data exists. It can be preregistered now, but it cannot be sign-off material today.
*Preregister:* 12-month retention for tool-hired cohorts vs. the prior-year cohort, same retention definition, threshold locked now (e.g., "at least X percentage points higher"), measured from HR records. Expected test date: about 9 months out.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** `[Y]` Misleading
The source is recruiter estimates in a team meeting. Self-reports of time savings are anchored, unmeasured, and come from people who advocated for the tool. The estimate also ignores time spent checking or overriding tool output, which may offset savings.
*Needed:* time logs or ATS activity data before and after, with a comparison group. Net of review time.

Bias testing, outcome-quality measurement, and retention are in progress and preregistered.
