# Review of Building Retrofit Board Summary

I can't confirm all six claims. Two are adequately supported as stated, and four are not. The team lead's view that everything is proven doesn't hold up.

## Claim-by-claim review

**1. "The retrofit saves energy across the whole portfolio."**
*Not proven.* The only evidence is one building that facilities chose to showcase. That is a selection problem: a showcase building is likely to be one where the retrofit went well, so it is not a random or representative sample. A sample of one also cannot establish a portfolio-wide effect, and the portfolio's buildings may differ in age, use, fabric, and occupancy. The claim would need either data from a representative sample of buildings or a clear statement that the result comes from one building.

**2. "The heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers."**
*Not proven.* The only source is the installer, who has a commercial interest in the outcome. No noise measurements are reported, no before-and-after or comparison data exist, and "quieter" is not defined (dB(A) at what distance, which operating modes, which times of day). This is a vendor claim, not a finding.

**3. "18% less gas per degree-day than the prior two winters; the control changed +1%; figures from utility meter bills."**
*Proven, for the narrow statement, with caveats.* This is the strongest claim. It uses a pre/post comparison with a matched, non-retrofitted control building on the same campus, which helps separate the retrofit from weather and campus-wide changes. Meter bills are a primary, verifiable source, and normalising by degree-days is appropriate. The 18% versus +1% gap is a reasonable basis for attributing the difference to the retrofit. Caveats the board should hear:
- There is only one control building, so the comparison is noisy. No confidence interval or uncertainty range is given.
- The base temperature used for degree-days is not stated, and it matters for the normalisation.
- Occupancy, operating hours, and any other changes in the retrofitted building are not described.
- It is one building, so the result cannot be extended to the portfolio (see claim 1).

**4. "Tenants are more comfortable."**
*Not proven.* Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit, so there is no baseline. Without one, nothing can show a change. Complaint counts after the retrofit tell us nothing about whether comfort improved. Comfort is also subjective and would need a survey or structured measurement to assess. Lower complaints could reflect fewer tenants reporting, not better conditions. The claim should be dropped or reframed as "no data on comfort change is available."

**5. "Total installed cost was 2.4 million: signed final invoice, reconciled by finance."**
*Proven as a statement of the contractor's final invoice total.* A signed final invoice reconciled by finance is a good source. The caveat is scope: "total installed cost" may not include design fees, separate electrical or structural works, owner's costs, or any rebates. If those were procured separately, the true all-in cost is higher. The wording should say "contractor final invoice: 2.4M" unless the team confirms that all project costs are included.

**6. "Payback will be under 5 years. (Assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak.)"**
*Not proven.* Several problems:
- It is a projection, not a measured result.
- It rests on an assumption that the gas price will stay at a peak. That is an optimistic assumption, since savings from gas reduction are worth more at higher prices. Using a peak price rather than a central or forward-looking price is a choice that flatters the result.
- It relies on the savings figure from claim 3, which is a single building, and then appears to assume it applies to the whole portfolio.
- No payback calculation is shown, and no cost-side items (heat pump electricity costs, maintenance, replacement cycles, rebates) are included.
- No sensitivity analysis is presented for lower gas prices.

The board should see the payback under a central gas price scenario, with a range, before accepting any figure.

## Summary

| # | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Portfolio-wide savings | Not proven (single, selected building) |
| 2 | Heat pumps quieter | Not proven (vendor assertion only) |
| 3 | 18% gas reduction vs control | Proven for this building, with caveats (one control, no interval) |
| 4 | Tenants more comfortable | Not proven (no baseline) |
| 5 | 2.4M installed cost | Proven as final invoice total; confirm scope |
| 6 | Payback under 5 years | Not proven (peak-price assumption, unverified inputs) |

**Recommended action before the board:** Present claim 3 and claim 5 (with scope wording fixed). Reword claim 1 to refer to the one showcase building. Remove or qualify claims 2 and 4. Replace claim 6 with a payback range built on a central gas price, or present it explicitly as a scenario.
