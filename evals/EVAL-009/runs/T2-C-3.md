# Review of the retrofit board summary

Only claims 3 and 5 are adequately supported as stated. Claims 1, 2, 4 and 6 are not proven, so the team lead's view that all six are proven does not hold. Each claim is assessed below.

**1. Saves energy across the whole portfolio: NOT PROVEN.**
The evidence is one building, which the facilities team chose to showcase. That selection is likely biased toward a good result, and a sample of one cannot establish a portfolio-wide effect. Buildings differ in fabric, systems, occupancy and climate exposure, so the result may not transfer. The claim would need either a random or representative sample of buildings, or a justified argument that the showcase building is typical.

**2. Heat pumps are quieter than the old boilers: NOT PROVEN.**
The only source is the installer, who has a commercial interest in the product. No sound measurements (dB levels, measurement location, time of day, operating mode) or comparison to the boilers are presented. This is an unverified sales claim.

**3. 18% less gas per degree-day, with a matched control at +1%: PROVEN (with caveats).**
This is the strongest claim. It uses utility meter bills, which are an objective source. Normalising by degree-days handles weather, and the comparison to a non-retrofitted building on the same campus controls for shared conditions such as weather, tariffs and campus-wide policy changes. Comparing the retrofit and control changes gives a relative reduction of roughly 19 percentage points, which is consistent with the headline figure.
Caveats the board should hear:
- There is only one control building, so its +1% carries large noise. Confidence intervals or a multi-year variance estimate would strengthen the claim.
- The method for matching the control and the degree-day normalisation (base temperature, method) should be stated.
- The claim is scoped to one building. It says nothing about electricity, comfort or other sites.
- Any concurrent changes in the retrofitted building (occupancy, operating hours, other works) should be ruled out or disclosed.

**4. Tenants are more comfortable: NOT PROVEN.**
Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit, so there is no baseline. Without a pre-retrofit measure, or a survey with a comparison group, there is no evidence of change. Current complaint levels alone do not show improvement. Reporting may also have changed because of the retrofit itself.

**5. Total installed cost was 2.4 million: PROVEN (with a scope caveat).**
The figure rests on a signed final invoice from the contractor, reconciled by finance. That is good documentation of what was paid to the contractor. The word "total" needs definition. It may exclude design and engineering fees, internal staff time, commissioning, monitoring equipment, or any grants or rebates received. The board should be told what the 2.4 million covers.

**6. Payback under 5 years: NOT PROVEN.**
This is a projection, and its key assumption is that gas prices stay at this winter's peak. That is the most favourable assumption available, and it is likely to overstate savings. The calculation also appears to rest on the single-building savings from claim 1, which is not representative. No sensitivity analysis, discount rate, maintenance cost, or savings uncertainty is shown. A payback under 5 years at peak prices may not hold at average prices. The claim should be restated with a range of gas prices and a savings figure drawn from claim 3's evidence, with the assumptions shown.

**Recommendation for tomorrow:** Present claim 3 (with its single-control caveat) and claim 5 (with its cost scope defined) as findings. Reword or remove claims 1, 2, 4 and 6, or label them as unverified, anecdotal, or projections, and state their assumptions. Claim 1 should be limited to "one showcase building," and claim 6 should show a payback range rather than a single figure.
