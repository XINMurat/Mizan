# Review of the Building Retrofit Board Summary

Only two of the six claims are supported as written: claim 3 (gas savings against a control) and claim 5 (installed cost). The team lead's view that all six are proven does not hold. Claims 1, 2, 4, and 6 need either different evidence or substantial rewording before the board sees them.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. Portfolio-wide energy savings. NOT PROVEN.**
The only evidence is one building, which the facilities team chose to showcase. That selection is likely biased toward a good result, and a sample of one cannot describe a portfolio. Nothing in the summary shows the other buildings were measured, or that they resemble the showcase building in age, use, or envelope. The claim should be restated as applying to one building, or the rest of the portfolio should be measured.

**2. Heat pumps quieter than the old boilers. NOT PROVEN.**
The only source is the installer, who has an obvious interest in the outcome. No noise measurements, no before-and-after comparison, and no stated method are given. It may be true, but it is an unverified sales claim. It needs a measurement (for example, dB(A) readings at defined distances and times, before and after) or it should be dropped.

**3. 18% less gas per degree-day, against a matched control. PROVEN, with caveats.**
This is the best-supported claim. It uses utility meter bills, which are a primary record. It normalises for weather with degree-days, compares against the building's own prior two winters, and includes a matched control on the same campus. The control moved +1%, so the difference-in-differences is roughly 19 percentage points, which is hard to explain by weather or a general trend alone. Caveats the board should be aware of:
- The result rests on one retrofitted building and one control. No confidence interval or uncertainty is reported.
- Any change in occupancy, operating hours, or set-points over the period is not mentioned and should be ruled out.
- The claim is correctly scoped to this building. It should not be read as portfolio-wide (see claim 1).

**4. Tenants are more comfortable. NOT PROVEN.**
There is no baseline. Complaints were not tracked before the retrofit, so a comparison is impossible. Current complaint levels, if they are low, say nothing about change. A valid claim would need a pre-retrofit measure (complaints, survey, or comfort logs) and a comparable post-retrofit measure, ideally with a control building. Otherwise the statement should be removed or presented as an unmeasured impression.

**5. Total installed cost of 2.4 million. PROVEN.**
The source is a signed final invoice, reconciled by finance. That is an adequate record of what was paid. One wording point: "total installed cost" could be read to include design fees, internal staff time, or change orders not on the contractor's invoice. If those are excluded, the label should say "contractor final invoice" or the total should be reconciled to a full project cost.

**6. Payback under 5 years. NOT PROVEN.**
This is a projection, and its key assumption is weak. It assumes gas prices stay at this winter's peak for the whole payback period. Peak prices are the most favourable case for savings and are unlikely to persist. The projection also appears to depend on the portfolio-level savings in claim 1, which is not supported, and on single-building savings. No sensitivity analysis is given. The claim should be recalculated at average or forward gas prices, with the savings figure limited to the one measured building, and shown with a range. It may still come out under 5 years, but that has not been shown.
