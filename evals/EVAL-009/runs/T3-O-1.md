# Audit Report: Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

**HARKing status:** Retrospective. The claims were written before this review, and I audited them after seeing the summary's framing. This review is also retrospective.

**Coverage:** I audited the six claims as written. I did not have access to the HR-system extract, the vendor white paper, the external audit report, or the recruiter time data. Every tier below rests on the summary's description of those sources. Items marked as verifiable need the source opened before the board sees them.

**Bottom line:** Only claim 3 survives as stated, and only as a description of what happened, not as proof that the tool caused it. I cannot give a blanket sign-off. Claims 1, 2, 5 and 6 should come off the slide or be rewritten as hypotheses.

---

### Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates." (Hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger.")

- **Tier:** `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative
- **Justification:** The only evidence is a subjective impression from adopting managers. No outcome metric (performance rating, ramp time, retention) was defined, no comparison group exists, and the sample is self-selected adopters. Nothing here tests "better."
- **Verdict:** NOT PROVEN

### Claim 2: "It is unbiased." (Vendor white paper: trained on "diverse data.")

- **Tier:** `[Y]` Yanıltıcı / Misleading, with `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing
- **Justification:** "Trained on diverse data" describes an input. It says nothing about outputs. Unbiasedness would need an adverse-impact analysis of the tool's pass rates by protected group, run on your own applicant data. No such analysis appears in the summary. The external audit in claim 4 measured agreement with human panels, not fairness, so it cannot stand in for this. A human-panel agreement rate can be high while the tool reproduces the panels' own biases.
- **Risk note:** Biased screening carries legal exposure. Putting "unbiased" in front of a board without a subgroup analysis is the highest-stakes error in this set. Use the fairness test your jurisdiction and counsel require, not a vendor sentence.
- **Verdict:** NOT PROVEN

### Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days." (HR-system dates, all requisitions, 2 quarters before and after, 14 departments; open roles 212 vs 208.)

- **Tier:** `[K]` Kanıtlanmış / Proven for the descriptive fall, `[KKE]` for the implied causal attribution
- **Justification:** This is the strongest evidence in the set. It covers the full population from a system of record, not a sample, and the open-role volumes are nearly equal across periods. The stated claim is descriptive, so it survives.
- **What the summary does not show:**
  - *Causation.* This is a before/after design with no control group. Anything else that changed in the same window (process changes, seasonality, recruiter staffing, the freeze's effect on hiring pace) could produce the same drop. The freeze check only compares volumes. It does not rule out the freeze changing how quickly the remaining roles moved.
  - *Definition.* Confirm that time-to-hire was measured the same way in both periods (same start and end events, same treatment of withdrawn requisitions).
- **Required wording:** "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days over the period the tool was deployed." Do not write "because of the tool."
- **Verdict:** PROVEN

### Claim 4: "The tool's shortlist overlapped the consensus 81% vs. 79% panel agreement." (Blinded external audit, 600 applications.)

- **Tier:** `[KKE]` Kritik Kontrol Eksik / Critical control missing
- **Justification:** The external blinding is real and this is the most credible design in the set. But the conclusion the board will draw ("the tool is as good as our panels") does not follow from these numbers:
  - *No interval.* A 2-point difference on 600 applications may or may not be noise. Report a confidence interval (bootstrap is fine) for both figures.
  - *No equivalence margin.* "Not worse" requires a margin set before the test, such as "within 5 points." None is stated.
  - *Asymmetric comparison.* The consensus is built from the two panels, so each panel is partly compared against itself. The tool is compared against a reference that includes human judgment. A fair comparison is each panel against the consensus of the other panel plus the tool.
  - *Concordance is not quality.* Matching human shortlists measures agreement, not whether the hires perform. That is why claim 1 cannot be inferred from this one.
- **Verdict:** NOT PROVEN

### Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve."

- **Tier:** `[S]` Spekülatif / Speculative
- **Justification:** This is a forecast. The first cohort is three months old, so the outcome cannot yet be observed. The claim has no threshold, no baseline, and no stated test. The summary itself says it is too early to measure. A forecast presented as a board claim should be labeled as a forecast.
- **Verdict:** NOT PROVEN

### Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week." (Estimates in a team meeting.)

- **Tier:** `[H]` Makul Hipotez / Plausible hypothesis, with `[KKE]` (no instrument)
- **Justification:** This is an unaudited self-estimate, collected in a group setting where anchoring and social desirability are both likely. No time logs, no pre-deployment baseline, and no measure of rework (checking the tool's output, correcting errors) that would offset the saving. The arbiter is the team's own opinion, so this cannot reach `[K]` in its current form.
- **How to test it:** Two weeks of time logs per recruiter, before and after, including rework time.
- **Verdict:** NOT PROVEN

---

## The Missing Card

The summary is built from speed and agreement. It cannot show:

- **Adverse impact:** pass rates by demographic group. This is the claim with the largest risk.
- **Quality of hire:** performance, ramp, and retention outcomes for tool-selected versus not-selected candidates. Claim 1 depends on this.
- **Rejected-candidate outcomes:** candidates the tool screened out. If nobody checks them, errors are invisible.
- **Cost:** license fees, implementation, and recruiter time spent on oversight.
- **Failure cases:** any requisitions where the tool was overridden or caused a delay.

## Next Steps (ordered by criticality × impact/effort)

1. **Remove "unbiased" from the board material, or commission a subgroup adverse-impact analysis on your own applicant data.** Highest risk, moderate effort.
2. **Reword claim 3 to describe timing, not cause.** Verify the HR-extract definitions. Low effort, prevents an overclaim.
3. **Re-run the external audit's comparison with confidence intervals and a pre-stated equivalence margin, using a symmetric panel comparison.** The data already exists, so this is analysis, not new collection.
4. **Mark claims 1, 5 and 6 as hypotheses in the deck.** Claim 6 can be measured with two weeks of time logs.
5. **Set up a registry for this rollout.** Each of the unproven claims above becomes an entry with a locked threshold, a named arbiter, and a refutation condition, and the board's next review checks them against recorded results. I can draft that registry from these six claims if you want it.

I can't sign off on the set as written. What I can sign off on is claim 3 as a description of what happened, and claim 4 as a measurement that needs its interval before anyone interprets it. The rest should go to the board as open questions with a plan to answer them.
