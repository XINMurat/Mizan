# Mizan Audit: Hiring Screening Tool, Board Summary

**HARKing status:** Retrospective. I audited the summary text only. I had no access to the vendor white paper, the HR-system export, the external audit report, or the hiring-manager feedback. Every tier below is therefore based on how each claim is described, and each one that depends on an unopened source is marked as such. My own audit is also retrospective, and I chose the framing after seeing the claims.

**Bottom line:** None of the six claims can be signed off as proven as written. Two have strong designs behind them, but the board sentence goes further than either design supports. Three have no measurement at all. The team lead's confidence is not evidence, and I did not treat it as such.

## Claim-by-claim

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."** `[S]` Speculative
Evidence offered: hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger." That is an opinion with no outcome measure (performance, retention, or quality of hire), no comparison group, and a self-selected sample of managers who chose to adopt. No test was designed. Reword it as a hypothesis, or preregister a quality-of-hire measure with a threshold.

**2. "It is unbiased."** `[Y]` Misleading, and `[KKE]` on the substance
"Trained on diverse data" describes an input, not an output. Fairness is an outcome property, and the summary reports no adverse-impact analysis (selection rates by group, e.g. the four-fifths ratio), no error rates by group, and no independent test. The source is also the vendor, which is an interested party. Only the vendor's own assertion is on the table.

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."** `[H]` for the measurement, `[KKE]` for attribution
- *Strengths:* The data source is the full population (every requisition, both periods, all 14 departments) from system dates, not a sample. The volume check (212 vs 208 open roles) is a sensible control for workload.
- *Missing control:* All 14 departments adopted the tool, so there is no concurrent untreated group. The hiring freeze is a period-specific shock that can change fill times through channels other than volume (for example, which requisitions were kept open, or how dates were recorded). Nothing shows the freeze did not alter the process.
- *Unverified:* I have not seen the export, so the 41 and 29 figures are `[H]` until someone checks them. Confirm the date-field definitions did not change between periods.
- *Could flip it:* A difference-in-differences against a comparable non-adopting unit, or a staggered rollout, would test attribution. Pre-period trend data would show whether 41 days was already falling.

**4. "Blinded external audit: 81% shortlist overlap vs 79% for humans."** `[H]` for the numbers, `[KKE]` for the comparison
- *Strength:* This is the best design in the set: external, blinded, 600 anonymized applications, and an arbiter other than the vendor or the team.
- *Not like-for-like:* The tool is compared against the consensus of two panels, but the human figure is panel-vs-panel agreement. Agreement with a consensus built from both panels is not the same quantity as one panel's agreement with the other, so the two numbers measure different things.
- *No uncertainty:* A 2-point gap on 600 applications is well inside sampling noise. Without a confidence interval, "81 beats 79" is not a finding. Even the stronger reading, "matches humans," is not established.
- *Overlap is not quality:* Two judges can agree and both be wrong, and this also says nothing about claim 1.

**5. "First-year retention will improve."** `[S]` Speculative
The first cohort started three months ago, so there is no data. The claim also has no baseline ("improve" relative to what?) and no threshold. It is a forecast and cannot be proven tomorrow. It can be restated as a preregistered prediction with a baseline cohort and a threshold, to be checked at 12 months.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."** `[S]` Speculative
The only source is an estimate in a team meeting. The arbiter is the author's estimate, with no instrument. Per the registry rules, an entry with no instrument stays at `[S]`. Time logs for four weeks, compared against a pre-adoption baseline, would turn this into a measurement.

## What the board can say tomorrow

- **Can say, with sources:** Time-to-hire in the HR system fell from 41 to 29 days across both quarters, with open-role volume roughly unchanged (pending export verification). An external blinded audit found 81% shortlist overlap with human consensus on 600 applications.
- **Should not say:** That the tool *caused* the speedup, that it is unbiased, that it picks better candidates, that retention will improve, or that it saves 10 hours a week.
- **Should say explicitly:** Bias and quality of hire have not been measured.

## Next steps (ordered by criticality × impact / effort)

1. **Claim 2, bias:** Request adverse-impact data (selection rates by group) from the vendor, and have an independent party run it. This is the highest-stakes gap, because an unbiased claim in a hiring context is a legal exposure if it is wrong.
2. **Claim 3, attribution:** Pull pre-period trend data and find or construct a comparison unit. Check the freeze dates against the adoption date.
3. **Claim 4, restatement:** Recompute tool-vs-consensus against a matched human baseline, and add confidence intervals. Until then, present it as "agrees with external consensus at 81%."
4. **Claim 1, quality of hire:** Preregister a quality measure (e.g., 6-month performance rating, with a threshold) before anyone reads the results.
5. **Claim 6, time:** Four weeks of time logs.
6. **Claim 5:** Set a baseline cohort and a 12-month check date now.

**Registry seed:** I cannot write a file from here. This audit should be re-run at the 12-month retention checkpoint and whenever the tool's configuration or the requisition process changes, and the six entries above belong in the project registry with the thresholds listed in each step.

**Missing card:** This summary cannot show the cost of errors. False negatives (qualified candidates screened out), the speed-versus-quality tradeoff of faster hiring, and any change in offer acceptance or early attrition are all absent. Those are the outcomes a board most needs to see before a sign-off.
