# Review of the Hiring Screening Tool Board Summary

I cannot confirm that all six claims are proven. Only Claim 3 is supported, and only as a description of the data. The team lead's request for a quick sign-off should be declined for the other five.

## Claim 1: "The tool picks better candidates."

**Not proven.** The only evidence is that hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger."

- There is no outcome measure of candidate quality, such as performance ratings, probation pass rates, or manager assessments collected on a defined schedule.
- There is no baseline or control group comparing tool-selected hires with hires made without the tool.
- The judges are the managers who chose to adopt the tool, so they have a stake in the result. Their impressions are also not a measure of quality.

## Claim 2: "It is unbiased."

**Not proven.** The only evidence is the vendor's white paper saying it was trained on "diverse data."

- Training data being diverse does not show that the outputs are unbiased. Bias is an outcome property and needs to be measured.
- No adverse-impact analysis is cited, such as selection rates by gender, race, age, or disability, or the four-fifths rule.
- The source is the vendor, not an independent auditor.
- The board summary also does not say whether the tool's outputs have been tested on this organization's applicant pool.

## Claim 3: "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."

**Supported as a description of the data; causal attribution to the tool is not established.**

Strengths:
- The data come from HR-system dates, not self-report.
- The sample covers every requisition in all 14 departments over two quarters on each side, so sampling error is not the main concern.
- The freeze check (212 vs 208 open roles) addresses one obvious confounder, which is volume.

Weaknesses:
- This is an uncontrolled before-and-after comparison. Any other change over the same period could explain the drop: seasonality, changes in applicant supply, a new recruiter, a change in how requisitions were opened or closed, or a different job mix.
- The volume check controls for number of roles, not for their difficulty or type. A freeze can also change which roles stay open and how fast managers respond, so "no change in volume" does not mean "no change in the hiring process."
- The summary does not say when the tool went live relative to the two periods, or whether the freeze fell entirely within one period.
- No variability or significance test is reported. With full census data this matters less, but the board should still see the spread across requisitions or departments.

The board can be told that time-to-hire fell by 12 days. It should not be told that the tool caused the fall without a concurrent control, such as departments without the tool, or an interrupted time-series analysis that accounts for trend.

## Claim 4: Blinded external audit (81% vs 79% overlap)

**Not proven.** The design is a strength: an external firm, blinded, 600 anonymized applications. The conclusion is the problem.

- **Wrong target.** The audit measures agreement with human panels' consensus, not accuracy. Matching human judgment can be wrong in the same way humans are, and it cannot show that the tool picks better candidates. This is the same gap as Claim 1.
- **No uncertainty.** A 2-point difference (81% vs 79%) on 600 items has a confidence interval wide enough to include no difference or a small deficit. The summary reports no CI and no test. The only defensible reading is that agreement is comparable.
- **Unclear comparison.** It is not stated whether the tool's 81% is against the consensus of both panels, or whether the 79% is inter-panel agreement on the same items. Those two figures need to be computed on the same basis to be compared.
- **Missing details.** The shortlist size, how the consensus was formed, and who was blinded to what are not reported.
- **No bias component.** The audit says nothing about subgroup performance.

## Claim 5: "First-year retention of hires will improve."

**Not proven.** This is a forecast. The first cohort started three months ago, so no retention data exist. The summary acknowledges this, but the claim is still presented as a result. It should be reworded as a hypothesis to be tested, with a stated measure and a comparison group.

## Claim 6: "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."

**Not proven.** The only source is recruiter estimates in a team meeting. Self-estimates of time saved are known to be unreliable, and there is no baseline timesheet, time log, or task-level measurement. Any savings should be measured before and after, or through time logs, before they are reported. Note also that the time saved may be offset by time spent reviewing or overriding tool output, which was not measured.

## Recommendation

- **Claim 3** can go to the board as a descriptive finding, with the causal caveat stated.
- **Claims 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6** should be removed or reworded as hypotheses or goals, with the evidence gap stated.
- Before the tool is presented as effective or fair, the organization should commission an independent adverse-impact analysis and an outcome study with a comparison group and a retention or performance measure.
