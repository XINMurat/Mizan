# Review of Support Chatbot Board Summary

**Bottom line:** Claims 1 and 4 are adequately supported. Claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 are not, and should not go to the board as stated. The team lead's view that all six are proven does not hold.

---

**Claim 1: Median first response fell from 4.2 hours to 40 seconds.**
*Supported, with two caveats.*
- The evidence is strong for what it measures: system timestamps for the full population of 38,000 tickets, pulled by an independent data team, over 10 weeks on each side of launch.
- *Definition risk:* If the bot's automatic greeting counts as a "first response," the 40-second figure measures acknowledgment, not help. The board should be told how "first response" is defined and whether it means a human or substantive reply.
- *Design limit:* This is a before/after comparison with no control group. Seasonality, ticket volume, or staffing changes could contribute. The claim as written is descriptive ("fell"), which the data supports. Attributing the whole change to the bot is an inference.
- A median can also hide a worse tail. Reporting the 90th percentile would help.

**Claim 2: Customers prefer the bot to human agents.**
*Not supported.*
- The evidence is a product team's reading of 30 transcripts. The sample size is small, the selection method is not stated, and there is no comparison arm.
- Reading transcripts shows what happened in those chats. It does not measure what customers prefer. Preference would need a survey, a choice experiment, or a satisfaction comparison between bot and human conversations, with a representative sample.
- Suggested wording: "Transcript review of 30 chats showed no obvious customer complaints about the bot. Customer preference has not been measured."

**Claim 3: The bot resolves 70% of tickets on its own.**
*Not supported.*
- "Resolved" is defined as the customer closing the chat window. Closing a window is not resolution. It could mean the problem was solved, the customer gave up, or they went to a different channel. Abandonment would count as success under this definition.
- The figure comes from the vendor's dashboard. The vendor has a commercial interest in the number, and the team has not verified it independently.
- Claim 4's data suggests a useful check: whether these "resolved" chats generate repeat contacts. That check hasn't been done here.
- Suggested wording: "The vendor reports that 70% of chats end with the customer closing the window. This is not yet validated as resolution."

**Claim 4: The bot reduced repeat contacts.**
*Supported, with caveats.*
- This is the strongest evidence in the set. It is a randomized test with 5,000 customers, a defined outcome (repeat contacts within 7 days), an effect estimate with a confidence interval, and analysis by a team independent of the bot.
- The reported numbers are internally consistent. The difference is 9.1 − 11.4 = −2.3 points, which is the midpoint of the interval (−3.0 to −1.6).
- *Caveats to confirm before presenting:*
  - Was the analysis intention-to-treat? Were all assigned customers counted, including those who never used the bot?
  - Could bot-first customers have fewer *recorded* contacts because they gave up or moved to another channel that isn't tracked? If so, the reduction is partly measurement, not improvement.
  - Was the outcome and 7-day window pre-specified, or chosen after the data came in?
  - Does a 6-week test population represent the full customer base and the post-launch period?
- The claim is supported for the tested population and period as stated.

**Claim 5: It will cut support headcount costs by a third next year.**
*Not supported.*
- This is a forecast taken from the vendor's sales deck. It is not evidence of future savings. The source has a commercial incentive to inflate it.
- There is no cost model, no stated assumptions about ticket volume or staffing, and no account of the hiring freeze or attrition.
- Suggested wording: "Vendor projects cost savings; the internal estimate is pending."

**Claim 6: Agents are happier since launch.**
*Not supported.*
- There is no survey or other direct measure of agent wellbeing.
- Lower attrition is a weak proxy, and it coincides with a hiring freeze. The freeze is a plausible alternative explanation: it changes the labor market and internal mobility, and may affect who leaves and when. The data cannot separate the two.
- Suggested wording: "Attrition fell this quarter; agent sentiment has not been measured, and a hiring freeze began at the same time."

---

**Recommendation:** Present claims 1 and 4 as findings, with the caveats above. Reword claims 2, 3, 5, and 6 as either open questions or clearly labeled vendor or unverified figures. Before the board meeting, confirm the definition of "first response" (claim 1) and the analysis details of the randomized test (claim 4).
