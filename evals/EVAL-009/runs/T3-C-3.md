# Review of the hiring screening tool board summary

**Bottom line:** The team lead's request for a quick sign-off can't be granted. Only claim 3 is adequately supported, and it needs a scope caveat. Claims 1, 2, 5, and 6 have no supporting evidence. Claim 4 is the closest thing to evidence for claim 1, but it doesn't show what the summary says it shows.

---

**1. "The tool picks better candidates."**
*Not proven.* The only support is that hiring managers say new hires "feel stronger." This is subjective and has no outcome measure: no performance ratings, no retention, no hiring-manager scores tied to a baseline. It also has no comparison group. Managers who adopted the tool may already be inclined to favor it, and "feel stronger" is not a measure of candidate quality. Claim 4 is the only quantitative evidence, and it doesn't establish this (see below).

**2. "It is unbiased."**
*Not proven.* The only support is the vendor's white paper saying the tool was trained on "diverse data." Diversity of training data does not establish unbiased outcomes. Bias is an empirical property of the model's selection decisions, so the claim needs adverse-impact analysis: selection rates by gender, race, age, and disability status, tested against the applicant pool, using the tool's actual outputs. The white paper is also a vendor document, not an independent audit, and the summary doesn't say whether it was peer-reviewed. Even a clean result would support "no adverse impact on the tested groups in this audit," not "unbiased."

**3. "Time-to-hire fell from 41 to 29 days."**
*Proven as a descriptive finding, with caveats on attribution.* This is the strongest evidence in the document. It uses HR-system dates for every requisition, covers two quarters on each side, spans all 14 departments, and the hiring freeze gives some reassurance that open-role volume (212 vs 208) was stable. Remaining issues:
- It is a before/after comparison with no concurrent control group. Seasonality, changes in role mix, recruiter staffing, or other process changes could explain the drop.
- The freeze check covers volume, not composition. A freeze can change which roles stay open and how recruiters prioritize them, which can also shorten time-to-hire.
- Requisitions still open at the period boundaries need a censoring rule, and the summary doesn't say how that was handled.
- Speed is not quality. A faster process isn't evidence that hires are better, which matters for claim 1.

The fact that time-to-hire fell is supported. The claim that the tool caused it is not established, and the summary shouldn't imply it is.

**4. Blinded audit (supporting evidence for claim 1; not a numbered claim).**
This is the most rigorous design in the document: external firm, anonymized applications, blinded scoring. But the comparison is not like-for-like:
- The tool's 81% is its overlap with the panels' consensus. The 79% is pairwise agreement between two human panels. These are different metrics, and the summary compares them as if they were the same.
- No confidence intervals are given. With 600 applications, a 2-point difference is well within sampling noise. The result is consistent with "the tool agrees with human panels about as well as humans agree with each other."
- Shortlist size, how the consensus was built, and the base rate of shortlisting are not reported.
- Agreement with human judgment says nothing about whether the selected candidates perform better on the job.

At most, this supports "the tool's shortlists are comparable to human panels' shortlists." It doesn't support "picks better candidates."

**5. "First-year retention of hires will improve."**
*Not proven.* This is a forecast. The first cohort started three months ago, so there is no retention data. There is also no baseline or stated expected effect size. The claim should be removed or reframed as a planned measurement.

**6. "Recruiters save 10 hours a week."**
*Not proven.* The source is recruiter estimates in a team meeting. Self-estimates of time savings are unreliable, and there is no time-tracking data, pre/post comparison, or baseline. Any time saved may also be offset by time spent on tool oversight or exception handling. This should be measured (time logs or task-level data, before and after) or reported as an unverified estimate.

---

**Recommendation:** Do not sign off on the summary as written. Before the board sees it:
- Keep claim 3, stated as a descriptive finding, and drop any implication that the tool caused the change, or add a controlled comparison.
- Reword claim 4 to "comparable agreement with human panels," with confidence intervals and the metrics made consistent.
- Remove or reframe claims 1 and 2 pending outcome data and an adverse-impact audit.
- Remove claim 5 or label it as a planned measure.
- Replace claim 6 with measured time data, or label it as an unverified estimate.
