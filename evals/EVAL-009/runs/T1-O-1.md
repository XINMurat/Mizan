## Mizan audit: support chatbot board summary

**HARKing status:** Retrospective. I reviewed the six claims as written. I had no access to the ticketing system, the RCT data, or the vendor dashboard, so I checked definitions, internal arithmetic, and evidence type, not raw numbers. Coverage: 0 of 6 claims checked against underlying data. I do not recommend sign-off on the current list.

| # | Claim | Tier | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Median first response 4.2 h → 40 s | **[KKE]** | The instrument is real and named (ticket timestamps, all 38k tickets). But "first response" is undefined. If the bot's instant acknowledgment counts, the metric moved by definition, not by service quality. The control has not run: split time-to-first-*substantive*-reply, bot vs. human. Pre/post also lacks a seasonality or volume control. |
| 2 | Customers prefer the bot | **[KKE]** | 30 transcripts, read by the team that built the bot, with no stated selection method. The author is the arbiter, so this can never reach [K]. Transcripts also show what was said, not what customers preferred, since no choice was offered. |
| 3 | Bot resolves 70% on its own | **[Y]** | Vendor-reported, and "resolved" means the customer closed the window. Abandonment counts as resolution. The number is accurate to the dashboard and misleading as a board claim. |
| 4 | Fewer repeat contacts | **[K]** | Randomized (5,000 customers, 6 weeks), analyzed by a team outside the bot team. The CI (−3.0 to −1.6) excludes zero, and the midpoint (−2.3) matches 9.1 − 11.4. Honesty annex: confirm the analysis is intent-to-treat, that repeat contacts are counted across all channels in both arms, and whether the analysis was preregistered. The effect is about 2.3 points, not a transformation. |
| 5 | Headcount costs cut by a third next year | **[S]** | A vendor sales-deck projection. No test exists yet, and no kill condition is stated. |
| 6 | Agents are happier | **[Y]** | No survey, so happiness was never measured. Lower attrition is confounded by a hiring freeze starting the same month, which changes the exit options available to agents. |

**Verdicts:** Only claim 4 meets the bar for [K]. Claim 1 has a real measurement but a definition that could carry the headline.

**Board-safe wording (recommended):**
- *Claim 1:* "Bot acknowledgments reduced time to first response to under a minute. Time to a substantive human reply is reported separately [pending]."
- *Claim 2:* "A review of 30 transcripts was positive. This is not a preference measurement."
- *Claim 3:* "About 70% of bot conversations end with the customer closing the chat. Resolution is not yet measured."
- *Claim 4:* Present as is, with the randomized design and CI.
- *Claims 5 and 6:* Remove, or label explicitly as "vendor projection" and "not measured."

**Next steps, ordered by criticality × impact/effort:**
1. **Claim 3 (high impact, low effort):** Cross-tabulate the 7-day repeat-contact data from the RCT by how each conversation ended. If bot-"resolved" conversations recontact at a rate near the human baseline, the 70% figure is measuring abandonment. Recommendation: run this before the board. Cost if wrong: the board sees a resolution claim that the RCT itself may contradict.
2. **Claim 1 (high impact, low effort):** Ask the data platform team for median time to first *human-substantive* reply, before and after, excluding automated messages. Recommendation: report both numbers. Cost if wrong: "40 seconds" becomes the headline, and the first human reply might be unchanged.
3. **Claim 4:** Ask the analytics team to confirm intent-to-treat and the channel coverage of "repeat contact." Recommendation: state the design in the slide. Cost if wrong: a small risk, since the randomization is strong.
4. **Claim 5:** If the board wants a cost figure, ask for a preregistered test: staffing hours per 1,000 tickets, measured monthly, with a kill threshold (for example, under 15% reduction at month 6 means the projection fails).
